# G_yt_900fXN3dPUM_en — first meeting

## Term

The first meeting is the first time you meet a client, and it is important because a lot of people ask about it: this is how you figure out whether you're going to do the job or not, it sets the tone for the engagement, and here is where you find out what they want. It is a meet and greet, after they say they like you and are looking to work with you, either on the phone or in person. The phrase appears 10 times in the source, beside question, client, proposal, and first. The concrete questions are the business driver — what is driving the decision and the start of the whole project — and the purchasing trigger, which he also calls the magic question: if your deity of choice came down and gave you the perfect partner to do this project, what would that look like. Remember you're not a designer, you're a consultant: you're sending out a proposal to get a website for $20,000, not a website project for $500.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as just an intro call: the first meeting sets the tone for the whole engagement and decides whether you take the job.
- Read as asking about price first: clients often ask about price first because they're gathering information for an RFP — the designer's questions are about the business driver and the purchasing trigger.
