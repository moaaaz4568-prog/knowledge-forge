# G_yt_8TzzCHyZY0k_en — emotional responses

## Term

Emotional responses are the feelings a designer deliberately aims for in the viewer: the redesign starts by choosing which emotions the audience should feel, and only then picks layout, color, typography, and imagery to make those responses happen. The speaker takes average-looking designs and applies advanced graphic design theories to improve their effectiveness through emotion. The phrase appears 2 times in the source, beside design, viewers, layout, and typography. His concrete cases are a tech poster meant to spark a sense of exploration and a salmon menu meant to create a craving, with the salmon as focal point and top-quality imagery.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as manipulating feelings: each response is tied to the audience and the product — exploration for new tech, craving for food — not a random sentiment pasted onto any design.
- Read as decoration: the responses drive the layout, color, and typography choices themselves, so they are the brief for the redesign, not a coat of paint on top of it.
