# G_yt_9K1aLtAjGBY_en — slave class

## Term

The slave class is the dependent class, in Nick's history of the classes: he did one video with the proletariat class and genteel poverty, and this time he's talking about the dependent class, which for most of human history was regarded as the slave class. The phrase appears 16 times in the source, beside class, slave, slavery, and society. Child slavery is not really a thing in modern society: the conventional view of slavery — a person being somebody's property — was effectively abolished at the turn of the 20th century, the American Civil War was fought to end it in the 1860s, and Brazil was really the last major country to abolish slavery outright in the 1890s. So if slavery is over on paper, why does he still talk about a slave class? Because that's not really the true definition of what a slave class is: the true definition of a slave class is somebody who works for wages below subsistence. The overwhelming majority of the population has been in the slave class for the entire history of civilization: if you go back to the beginnings of ancient civilization, some sort of labor in bondage or coercive labor was almost a requirement to operate any sort of advanced society, and in the age of farming it was the first time you really had surpluses in food or reserves that lasted more than a season. His concrete modern case is Walmart, which has controversially gotten in trouble for giving guides to help people collect benefits, because they know their ability to pay the wages they do is dependent on their workers being supplemented through the government social safety nets. And even if legal slavery is starting to go away, de facto wage slavery was replacing it for most people: if you make less money than the cost of living to keep you alive every month, whether you have legally an owner or not, you are not free really in the sense of it.
