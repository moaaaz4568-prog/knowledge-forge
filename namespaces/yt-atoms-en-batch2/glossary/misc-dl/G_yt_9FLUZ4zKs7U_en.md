# G_yt_9FLUZ4zKs7U_en — strategy

## Term

Strategy is what Max Traylor — the consultant's consultant — says agencies should sell instead of implementation: his book The Agency Survival Guide has a chapter entitled "burn your implementation business to the ground," and this conversation is about the three steps to start selling strategy that you're already capable of delivering. The word appears 46 times in the source, beside going, agency, about, and content. Agency clients often want more strategic consulting from the agencies they hire, while many agencies feel the services they're delivering are often undervalued. His concrete case is the churn program: 20 clients had already said they were going to cancel HubSpot, and the agency had two employees, burnt out, already at 200% capacity. The only thing they could do for these 20 companies was to run a very efficient strategic process — giving them a technology strategy and documenting how content, people, and technology fit together to actually accomplish their business goals. They came up with a 5-hour process. They sold the first one for $2,500, then said, we're going to make this specifically for higher education clients — and by positioning it for a certain type of someone, they charged $30,000, multiplied the price by 10 plus, and ran the same process. Hitting his goals as an agency owner just led to more exhaustion and burnout: he wanted a personal life, so he had to carve out something for it — at an agency, return on effort, value for hours put in, is not in implementation. Ultimately, in the face of disruption — AI, nearbound, things like that — your relationship with a client's executive team will save you, and they do not care about marketing: your ability to speak their language, to consult with them, to help them make decisions, decide where to spend money and with whom — that's where your power comes from.
