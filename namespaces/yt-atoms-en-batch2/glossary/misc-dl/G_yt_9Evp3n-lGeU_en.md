# G_yt_9Evp3n-lGeU_en — agency

## Term

An agency is what Kevin Howell and Josh Annish built as the co-founders of 329: Kevin primarily runs the web design and development arm, and Josh handles all things strategy when it comes to marketing. They started with two different agencies. Kevin was a teacher — at the end of the year he told his ex-wife he was going to stop teaching, and if he couldn't make what he made teaching in his first year of being a web designer, he'd go back to teaching; if he made more or the same, he'd stay. He went through graphic design school and worked in the agency world for a while, and got to the point where the agency he was working for was shutting down its web division, around 2010 — they were doing video and creative, focused on home builders, and just didn't have the web chops. He came home and had what he expected to be a long, drawn-out conversation with his wife — "Hey, I want to start an agency" — and it was a 30-second conversation. The word appears 34 times in the source, beside going, out, doing, and kind. He calls himself a typical small business owner: he wasn't trained to run a business — he knew how to do a thing, and then he went and started an agency. Their concrete rules are: you would think when you start an agency, making people feel dumb means you're really smart and they're going to hire you — but they're really good at sales because of all the stories they know, based on the problems they've helped, which they can explain. They have to have a meeting, and if a client won't meet with them, they're not going to do it. And they work more because they're doing their job, the client's job, and managing them — which doesn't seem really fair.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a planned business: he wasn't trained to run a business — he knew how to do a thing, and then he went and started an agency, with teaching as the fallback.
- Read as making clients feel dumb meaning you're smart: they're good at sales because of the stories they know, based on the problems they've helped — not because clients feel dumb.
