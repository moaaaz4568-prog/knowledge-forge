# G_yt_9P1-Z5tb-hc_en — golden rule

## Term

A golden rule, in this video, is one of the super important rules that only a few designers are taking notice of — and today he's not covering the basics that everyone's mentioned a billion times before, things like keep your design simple or don't use too many fonts. The phrase appears 7 times in the source, beside design, work, designs, and rules. If you follow today's first golden rule, your designs will keep up to date with the constant evolution and shaping of interest and preferences of the general public: as a designer, you should probably try and keep up with pop culture, because by staying current with pop culture trends, graphic designers can create artwork that resonates with their target audience, making that work more relevant, relatable, and engaging. Incorporating elements from popular movies, music, fashion, or even internet memes can actually help designers connect with their audience on a more profound and a more deep level: this clever set of designs uses Pantone cards mixed with pop culture references, and the design is instantly more interesting and intriguing to audiences — and websites like fubis have dedicated pop culture sections on the website, so you can keep up to date. When you are researching pop culture, you likely realize that you become inspired whilst conducting that research — though it won't be relevant to all your briefs; it's no good just slapping Walt Disney onto a brochure for a funeral business, as an example. Being popped with culture is a powerful weapon a designer can have in their toolkit, ready to use whenever. The next rule is to break rules — but that's not the full story: as a graphic designer, you should learn all the principal rules, like hierarchy, balance, and alignment, not only learn them but master them. Jesse Nyberg has mastered the rule in his work, and also Elliot in his work — two awesome design channels. The golden rule that he's mastered is to carve out your own designer style. And research will always be a golden rule on any list, because it is about making emotions and forging lasting impressions. AI, meanwhile, is not a replacement for human creativity but a powerful ally that can amplify the creative process, elevate design outcomes, and redefine the boundaries of graphic design.
