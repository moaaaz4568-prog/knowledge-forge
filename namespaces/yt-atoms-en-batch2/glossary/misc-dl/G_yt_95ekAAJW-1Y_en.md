# G_yt_95ekAAJW-1Y_en — Capital Genesis

## Term

Capital Genesis is the economic model a figure from the book It's Insanity would have used to create a new economy and self-finance his plan of world domination. The book, by Matthew B Cox, tells the story of a lawyer who is very gifted but also very emotionally unstable, and who plans on using mergers and acquisitions and corporate workarounds in the legal system to finance a military to take over the world: he had started a private security firm as one of his many companies and almost succeeded in a coup in both the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Tajikistan. The phrase appears 3 times in the source, beside economy, companies, new, and world. This is basically how it works: he acquires a company out of bankruptcy and uses the company to become the customer and the supplier to other companies in his portfolio, so all of them use each other's services to propel each of their businesses — and using the proceeds from all these intra-party related-party transactions, you'd be able to buy more companies and create a bigger micro-economy under your umbrella. His concrete downfall is the payroll: he was arrested for tax fraud because he failed to pay over 200 million dollars worth of payroll taxes, and he spent the money on his own lifestyle, saving it to buy things such as fighter jets.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a workable business model: it is the plan of a megalomaniac from a book called It's Insanity — he broke the first rule of Fight Club for super villains and was arrested for tax fraud.
- Read as plunder meaning theft only: the plunder economy is how the ancient Roman economy worked — growth from taking wealth through conquest and flowing new wealth into the existing economy — and once they stopped plundering, it was dead.
