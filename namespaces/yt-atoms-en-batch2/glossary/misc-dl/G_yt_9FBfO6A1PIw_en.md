# G_yt_9FBfO6A1PIw_en — expertise

## Term

Expertise is the business of David C. Baker: he studied language, history, and theology — a really interesting path into what he's doing — and he's considered by many as the experts' expert. He's advised over 900 firms and he's written five books, including The Business of Expertise, and he's the co-host of Two Bobs with Blair Enns. The word appears 24 times in the source, beside about, clients, book, and business. He taught motorcycle racing and drives a tractor — and he has no office: his workshop, with woodworking and metalworking equipment, is where he works, with sawdust at his feet, making furniture, turning things on a lathe, building really ornate boxes. His concrete test of expertise is the $10,000 day fee: what does a person have to do to warrant a $10,000 day fee? He kept obsessing over this idea, and several weeks later it turned out Fabian Ghalter did hire him — and his rates were much higher than that. His rule is: if they are respecting you for your very carefully defined expertise, and if you should be working for this client, there's very little you can do to wreck it. And the way to sell it is to pull the strategy out of the proposal you were giving away: the first step in the process is to figure out how to solve this problem, and then, if you want, you can solve the problem too. Writing clarifies it: if you just try to think about it so that it becomes clear, and then you'll write about it, you'll never clarify it as much as you could.
