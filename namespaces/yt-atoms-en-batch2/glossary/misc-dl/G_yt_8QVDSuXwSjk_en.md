# G_yt_8QVDSuXwSjk_en — right seat

## Term

The right seat is the speaker's test for whether a person fits the work they are doing: work that drives focus, growth, and enjoyment, rather than busywork. His exercise is a sheet of paper with two columns — every person on the team on the left, and one honest question beside each name about whether they are in the right seat. The phrase appears 2 times in the source, beside team, focus, growth, and enjoyment. He stresses honesty and fairness in answering, because most people are not bad employees — usually they are simply in the wrong seat.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a chair or job title: the right seat is a fit between a person and the work — focus, growth, and enjoyment — not a desk, a role label, or a promotion.
- Read as a verdict on the person: the exercise judges the fit, not the employee, and the speaker insists on honesty and fairness instead of branding people as bad.
