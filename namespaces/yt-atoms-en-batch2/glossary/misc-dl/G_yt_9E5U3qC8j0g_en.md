# G_yt_9E5U3qC8j0g_en — earnout

## Term

An earnout is the part of the acquisition that Kelsey Dixon warns about: did they have any earnout, were they able to hit it — yes, they had a successful first year of integration. But that's the one thing she always tells everybody: an earnout, a lot of times, is designed for you to fail. Looking back, they lost millions from it, though they still did really well, and they had two years of it. The word appears 10 times in the source, beside agency, out, clients, and kind. Kelsey is one of the co-founders of the former Davies and Dixon, an organic social media agency based out of Seattle, serving clients globally, launched in 2014 and acquired in early 2022. She never envisioned herself as an entrepreneur; her co-founder McKenzie was one of the people who unearthed the full potential out of her — they had interned together in 2010 in New York City. She was 24 years old in 2014 and had no idea what she was doing, which was probably a lot to her benefit: the naivety of starting a business gave her so much gumption to learn. At first they were very much everything, because they were young and hungry and wanted to earn the trust of clients, so they said yes to a lot of things — then, through trial and error and iteration, they niched down. They trusted their gut and intuition and used data to analyze decisions and opportunities, so they could pivot and iterate and be open-minded. They would have thrived either way, she says — there was no wrong answer in this scenario, which is one of the reasons they chose it. And they stayed very much embedded in the business: an intentionally small boutique agency with very close-knit relationships with clients.
