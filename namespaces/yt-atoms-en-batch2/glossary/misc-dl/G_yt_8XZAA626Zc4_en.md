# G_yt_8XZAA626Zc4_en — identity

## Term

Identity is what you actually achieve, in the speaker's argument: you do not get the life you want, the car, the bank account, or the house — you become a person who already has those things. The rule is to act like the version of yourself that has already achieved success, and to throw away any input that does not align with your goals or your image of that person. The word appears 7 times in the source, beside version, beliefs, success, and yourself. His concrete proof is his own anger: a school friend called his drive to improve insecurity, and his fury proved his identity was unwavering — he calls that self-awareness, not insecurity.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as faking it: acting like the achieved version means aligning what you take in with your goals and discarding what does not fit — not pretending to own things you do not.
- Read as ego: his anger story frames identity as an unwavering belief in who he is, tested when someone imposes their limits on him, and he names the feeling self-awareness rather than insecurity.
