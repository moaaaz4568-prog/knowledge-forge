# G_yt_9BmSQ6xXf8s_en — agency

## Term

An agency is what he has run for about 20 years: he got in the business as an unpaid intern, when a PR firm stopped him in the interview and asked if he had heard of Steve McKee — Steve, the founder of McKee Wallwork, offered him the unpaid internship, and he bought the firm from the founders a little over 10 years ago. Steve approached him 15 years ago about owning and running a firm, and they started the conversation a long time ago. The word appears 16 times in the source, beside ai, business, doing, and going. Steve ran strategy and sales and some account management, and Pat Wallwork is a nationally respected media planner; they had their hands in everything, and succession was hard but good, very difficult. Steve had gone through a very poor attempt at buying a firm before he started McKee Wallwork, and learned a lot of lessons from it. One of their main mantras was don't be greedy. His concrete point about selling is that it's a tough business to sell: you don't have inventory, you might have intellectual property and that is worth something, but unless it's famous, it's not worth a ton — you're really worth your current book of business, and maybe if you can tie down some top talent, that's worth something to a buyer. And when people come to agencies, think about why agencies exist: they exist to solve a problem that someone is having.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as buying an agency meaning buying assets: there is no inventory — you're worth your current book of business, and maybe top talent tied down.
- Read as AI replacing the agency: you can always find a cheaper option and get AI slop, but the agency exists to solve a problem someone is having — and the traditional integrated advertising agency model is not as fun, and hard to make money in, if you follow all the conventions of the industry.
