# G_yt_8QyY2NyohrM_en — 24 hours

## Term

24 hours is the speaker's rule, borrowed from coach Don Shula, for how long a team gets to feel a result: celebrate a victory or bemoan a defeat for a day, then get back to practice. He learned the lesson in London the morning after the attacks, watching people get on with stuff the next day instead of staying in panic and drama. The phrase appears 6 times in the source, beside victory, defeat, practice, and celebrate. His concrete case is the sports team after a game: an incredible game or an awful game, the team had 24 hours either way, and at the end of it, back to practice.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a deadline for grief: 24 hours is a discipline for moving on, not a ban on feeling — the speaker still calls the attacks awful and horrible, and the point is getting on with stuff, not pretending nothing happened.
- Read as forgetting in a day: the rule is about the next action, not the memory — after the 24 hours the team is back to practice, game on, with the result still counted.
