# G_yt_8XjHcLFxK9A_en — leverage

## Term

Leverage is the decision frame the speaker wishes he had learned earlier: make decisions from a place of leverage and opportunity cost, not from what you can afford with the money you have. Being broke, he says, only teaches you how to be broke, while leverage is about compounding — finding a business that is a value creation machine at its core. The word appears 5 times in the source, beside opportunity cost, decisions, wealthy, and money. His concrete regret is priced: focusing on leverage, he says, would probably have made him 50 to 100 million dollars in three years instead of 15 million.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "If you want to become wealthy, learn to make decisions from a place of leverage and opportunity cost, not from a place of what can you afford with the money that you have." — sources/S_yt_8XjHcLFxK9A_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as borrowing or debt leverage: leverage here is a decision frame — opportunity cost, compounding, and a value creation machine — not financial leverage or taking on loans.
- Read as spending money you do not have: the point is how the decision is framed, not reckless spending — the speaker still tracks cost per booked call and the upfront a client will actually pay.
