# G_yt_94KixZgf7p4_en — top tax rate

## Term

The top tax rate is the number Nick compares between the US and Europe: is Europe really a high-tax place where people simply can't get ahead on income, and is the US really a relative tax haven compared to the rest of the developed world? On the surface, every developed country has about the same level of taxation if you just look at solely top tax rates — every major country has a top tax rate of about 40 to 50%. The phrase appears 4 times in the source, beside tax, taxes, top, and countries. His concrete numbers are: the US top combined rate is about 50%, with top state brackets of 10% to 13% in Hawaii and Oregon; Canada's top federal rates are in the high 20s and the provincial taxes are also in the high 20s, so the top rate exceeds 50% in most of the populated provinces; Switzerland's top rate is 12%, depending on the canton. The 47% bracket only kicks in at £125,000, while the 42% bracket kicks in at £43,000 — less than $60,000.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the US being a tax haven: the US top combined rate is about 50%, the same level as Europe — the US only looks lower because a federal system is not factored in like a unitary one.
- Read as the headline rate being the whole story: what really kills European taxpayers is the higher tax brackets on the lower end of income, and the VAT, which is an attack on consumption.
