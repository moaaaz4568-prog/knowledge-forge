# G_yt_8UAsN9wvePE_en — western culture

## Term

Western culture is the speaker's label for the cultures that read from left to right — Northwestern Europe and the countries shaped by it — used to show how culture changes the way brains process information and design things. His hexagon test makes the point: if the shape looks indented or concave, odds are you are from a western culture, while a right-to-left reader, like an Arabic reader, is more likely to see it bumping out. The phrase appears 7 times in the source, beside eastern, design, reads, and left to right, with the plural western cultures 4 more times. His concrete cases are a Japanese rice cooker that looks totally different from an American design, and traditional Japanese joinery against European joinery.

## Synonyms

- western cultures (plural form of the term, used in the source)

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a fixed geography: the speaker's western is about reading direction and shared history, and he notes that quirks and even local laws can shift the trajectory of a culture and its design.
- Read as a value judgment: the video contrasts how brains process information — Americans naming the big fish while Japanese subjects describe the whole underwater scene — not which way of seeing is better.
