1. "I SAID THIS ON THE FIRST CALL AND I WILL SAY IT ON THE LAST ONE."

The two planted voices said green on all thirty-six slides. Not most voices said green on one of them. All of them. all thirty-six Moscovici found that the sameness of the answer, not its volume, was what moved the majority.

Pick the one claim your deal rests on and repeat it in the same words every time it comes up. Buyers read a claim that never changes as a fact and a claim that drifts as a tactic.

Consistency is what a minority has instead of numbers.

2. "I AM NOT GOING TO SOFTEN THAT, BECAUSE THEN I WOULD BE LYING TO YOU."
When the two were allowed to waiver on twelve of the slides, the majority almost stopped moving. Green answers fell from more than eight percent to barely one.
When a buyer leans on your central claim, hold it in the same words with the same calm. The moment you round it down to be agreeable, the room stops taking it seriously.
One retreat costs more than a hundreds more than a hundred repetitions earn.

3. "NOBODY SENT ME. I AM TELLING YOU THIS BECAUSE I THINK IT IS RIGHT."
The majority only moved when it believed the minority spoke for itself, from conviction, and not from a script or from someone behind the glass.
Say where your position comes from before you say what it is. A rep who is visibly following orders gets tolerated. A rep who owns the position gets considered.
A room will argue with a company. It will listen to a person.

4. "I HAVE PUT A LOT INTO BEING SURE ABOUT THIS."
In Moscovici's model the minority that visibly pays a price for its view is the one that gets believed. Cost signals sincerity in a way that fluency never can.
Let the buyer see the work behind the claim. Name the calls you made, the numbers you pulled, the hours it took. A position that cost you something cannot be dismissed for free.
What you visibly paid for your view is what they quietly credit it.

5. "YOU CAN HAVE EVERYTHING EXCEPT THE POINT."

Rigid minorities got dismissed as cranks. The ones that held the core claim and gave ground on everything around it were the ones that converted the room.

Concede the timing, the wording, the order of steps, the size of the fist order, anything that is not the claim itself. Generosity everywhere else is what makes one hard line credible.

Be soft on everything so you can be hard on one thing.

6. "YOU DO NOT HAVE TO AGREE WITH ME IN FRONT OF THEM."
The strangest result came later. People who had said blue out loud shifted toward green who had on their own. Minorities do not win the vote. They change what people see afterward.
Stop trying to win the meeting. Plant the claim, hold it, and let the buyer carry it out of the room. The decision that matters is the one they make when nobody is watching.
Compliance happens in the room. Conversion happens in the car.

7. "ASK YOURSELF IN A WEEK WHETHER I WAS RIGHT."
The minority effect was slow. It showed up in later trials and later conversations, long after the two green voices had left the room.
End on a claim the buyer can check without you, then leave it alone. A prediction that comes true a week later closes deals your follow-ups never could.
The best close is the one the buyer finishes for you.
