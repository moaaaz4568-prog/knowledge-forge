This is what I learned from spending a weekend with Charlie Morgan and other seven and eight-figure entrepreneurs. The most successful ones had one thing in common, and that was that they were all client-obsessed. Look, most agency owners are like Toyotas. They pick the offer which is the easiest to fulfill, for example, just editing videos for their clients, and they try to be as systemized and affordable as possible to work with virtually everyone. And I'm not saying that that can't be profitable, but the product that comes out of it is usually something that is very replaceable and everybody can replicate it, which is why it becomes very hard to scale in the first place because you have so much competition. And the people that are actually crushing it are more like Bugatti. They go through crazy pain and suffering while building their products and services because they do not cut corners and they always put the client experience first. Keep in mind, the harder it is to build a product, the easier it will be to sell because nobody is competing with you. Most people don't want to follow your steps and go through that whole pain. And sure, your price might be higher than the Toyotas, but as long as the product is actually worth it, there will be people who buy, just like with Bugattis.
