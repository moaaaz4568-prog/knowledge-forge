Core value #1 at Zappos, painted on the wall in the call center.

Every Zappos call had one goal: make the customer’s day. Not move them through fast. Not upsell. Just create one moment they’d tell other people about. That was the entire pitch.

Zappos didn’t sell shoes. They sold a moment. The shoes were the souvenir.

2. "There's no script. Have a real conversation."

What Tony told every new Zappos rep on day one.

He refused to script his reps. He told them to be themselves and connect with the human on the other end. The longest call on record was over 10 hours. The rep helped a customer pick out shoes for their wedding.

Scripts protect the rep. Conversations move the customer.

3. "Connect with the customer first. Sell second."

From Delivering Happiness, page 146.

He insisted reps spend the first portion of every call learning about the customer's life, not their order. The connection drove the lifetime value far beyond any single sale. Repeat purchases tripled.

One conversation. Decades of orders.

4. “Your culture is your brand.”

His thesis after selling LinkExchange to Microsoft and watching the culture collapse.

He believed culture was the ONLY long-term moat. Products get copied. Prices get matched. Culture takes a decade to build and competitors can’t take it. Zappos’s culture WAS the product.

Marketing is what you say. Culture is what you actually do when no one’s watching.

5. “Spend more time on the call than the competitor will.”

His running argument with industry consultants.

Most call centers measured success by call brevity. Tony measured by depth. Longer calls meant deeper customer relationships, which drove referrals and lifetime value. He bet against the entire industry. He won.

Patience scales. Hustle plateaus.

His operating philosophy for the Zappos call center.

His rule: every customer call should produce a story the customer would tell their friends. Free overnight shipping. Sending flowers when a customer's mom died. Personal handwritten notes. Make them want to talk about you.

Customer service is marketing nobody pays for.
