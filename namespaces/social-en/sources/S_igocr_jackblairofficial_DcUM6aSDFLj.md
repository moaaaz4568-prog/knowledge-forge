1. "WHAT CHANGED IN THEIR WORLD SINCE WE LAST TALKED?"

Deals move when something in the buyer's world moves. New boss, new quarter, new fire. Most reps dial with three-week-old context and open the call already behind.

Sixty seconds. Their site, their LinkedIn, your last note. You're not researching, you're catching up. One changed fact rewrites your whole opener.

Stale context sounds exactly like not caring.

2. “IF THIS CALL GOES PERFECTLY, WHAT DID THEY AGREE TO OUT LOUD?”
A call without a named finish line drifts into a conversation. Conversations feel good and close nothing. The difference is decided before the phone rings, not during.
Write the yes down in one sentence. Not “build rapport.” A date, a number, a name, a next step someone can put on a calendar.
Nobody closes by accident.

3. "WHAT'S THE ONE QUESTION I'M HOPING
THEY DON'T ASK?"
Every rep carries the one. The price gap, the missing
feature, the reference you can't give. Hope is the
plan most calls run on, and buyers can smell it.
Say the dreaded question out loud, then draft the
answer cold, before the adrenaline. If it comes,
you're ready. If it never comes, you sold the whole
call without flinching.
The question you fear is the call you win.

4. "WHAT HAVE THEY ALREADY TOLD ME THAT I'M ABOUT TO ASK AGAIN?"
Buyers forgive almost anything except repeating themselves. Making them re-explain their problem tells them the last call evaporated, and that this one will too.
Read your notes back before you dial and quote them one line from last time. Watch the temperature change when they realize somebody was actually listening.
Remembering is the cheapest trust you'll ever buy.

5. "WHICH THREE THINGS AM I NOT GOING TO
BRING UP?"
The feature dump is a nerves problem, not a sales
problem. Everything you add past the buyer's actual
issue dilutes the one thing that closes.
Cut the pitch to what this buyer, on this day, needs
to hear. Park the rest. If they want the tour, they'll
ask for the tour.
What you leave out is also a pitch.

6. "WHAT AM I BRINGING THAT THEY HAVEN'T SEEN BEFORE?"
The just-checking-in call is the most hated call in sales. It asks the buyer to do your job: find a reason this conversation should exist.
No new number, no new proof, no new idea? Don't dial yet. One fresh piece of value turns a nag into a visit worth taking.
"Just checking in" means "I brought nothing."

7. "WHAT ARE THE LAST TEN SECONDS OF THIS CALL?"
Reps rehearse openers and improvise endings, which is why so many calls die at "so yeah, let me know." The close of the call sets the speed of the deal.
Script the final line before you dial: the ask, the date, who sends what. End it on purpose. Every time.
Calls don't end. They're ended.
