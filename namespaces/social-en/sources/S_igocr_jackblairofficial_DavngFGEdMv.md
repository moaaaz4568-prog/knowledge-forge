This isn't a prediction. It's payroll data.
Stanford's Digital Economy Lab partnered with ADP, the company that processes paychecks for roughly one in six American workers, and analyzed monthly records for millions of people across tens of thousands of firms.
Not surveys. Not forecasts. Actual paychecks, appearing and disappearing, tracked in near real time. The lead economist's verdict this summer: whatever this is, it's not going away.

The replacement already started. It just started at the bottom.
Since late 2022, workers aged 22 to 25 in the jobs most exposed to AI are down 13 percent relative to the older workers beside them. By some measures, 16. Junior software developers, down around 20.
And it's happening through headcount, not paychecks. Companies don't cut your pay. They quietly stop replacing the desk.

Here's the line that should stop you cold.

The same data shows experienced workers in the same exact occupations held steady or grew. Same job title. Same company. Opposite outcomes.

The researchers' explanation is brutal: what junior workers' knowledge overlaps with what AI can replace. Book knowledge. Scripts. The predictable, trainable part of every job. The part you can write down is the part a machine can learn.
