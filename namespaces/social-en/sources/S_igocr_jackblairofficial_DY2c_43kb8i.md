1. "Self-discipline. Do the hard thing whether you feel like it or not."

The trait Iannarino puts at the top of his list.

He argues motivation is unreliable and feelings come and go. Self-discipline is doing the prospecting, the follow-up, the hard call, on the days you do not feel like it. That is most days.

Discipline is just doing it when the motivation did not show up.

2. “Stay optimistic. The buyer borrows your belief.”

Iannarino on why attitude is not soft.

He says a buyer who is unsure will lean on the rep's certainty. If you do not believe the outcome is good, neither will they. Optimism is not naive. It is part of the job.

If you do not believe it, do not expect them to.

3. “Take full accountability. No excuses, no blaming the lead.”

Iannarino on the habit that separates pros.

He has zero patience for reps who blame bad leads, the territory, or the market. The pro owns the lead, good or bad. Accountability is uncomfortable, and it is the only way you actually improve.

The moment you blame the lead, you stop getting better.

4. "Be resourceful. Find a way around the no."
Iannarino on the trait that creates opportunities.
He says resourceful reps treat an obstacle as a puzzle, not a wall. No budget, no access, no clear path. The resourceful rep finds the angle the average rep never looks for.
Average reps see a wall. Resourceful ones look for the door.

5. "Show initiative. Move before someone tells you to."

Iannarino on not waiting to be managed.

He values the rep who acts without being prompted, who follows up, who creates the next step, who does not wait for a manager. Initiative is what makes a rep worth keeping and worth promoting.

Waiting to be told is how you stay average on purpose.

6. "Outcare the competition. Genuinely."

Iannarino on caring as a real edge.

He believes caring more about the client's result is a genuine advantage, and one that cannot be faked for long. The rep who actually cares asks better questions and builds deeper trust.

Care more than the next rep and the buyer feels it.

7. “You are the value proposition. Become worth buying from.”

Iannarino’s central idea about modern selling.

When products look similar, he says the rep is the difference. The insight you bring, the way you handle the process, the trust you create. You are not selling the product. You are the reason they buy.

Make yourself the best part of the deal.
