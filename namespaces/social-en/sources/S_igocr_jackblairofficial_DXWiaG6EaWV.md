1. "I am going to tell you something about your situation that nobody else who has sat across from you has said."

Daniel Plainview never opened with what everyone else opened with.
He arrived with something the room did not have before he walked in and the room felt it immediately.

This phrase signals exclusive insight before you have delivered a single word of it.
The buyer stops comparing you to the last rep and starts wondering what you know that the others did not.
That curiosity is the most valuable attention you will ever earn on a cold or competitive call.

Arrive with something nobody else brought. The room will reorganise itself around you.

2. "I have done this in conditions harder than yours and the result was the same every time."

Plainview's conviction was never theoretical. It was carved from experience so real and so documented that the room had no reasonable basis for doubt.

This phrase places your track record in the room before you have to justify it. The buyer stops questioning your capability and starts wondering why they would risk anyone else. Experience delivered without arrogance is the single most credible thing a rep can put on a table.

Let your history do the work your pitch cannot. It always closes harder.

3. "I am not going to dress this up. Here is exactly what I am offering and here is exactly what it will do for you."

Plainview never performed. He presented reality with such directness that the room had no space left for doubt. In a world of polished pitches, brutal clarity lands like nothing else available.

This phrase cuts through every expectation a buyer has built about what a sales conversation sounds like. They stop bracing and start receiving the information. A buyer who is receiving instead of bracing is a buyer who is already moving toward yes.

Strip the performance entirely. What remains is the most powerful version of your offer.

4. "I want you to understand what you are actually deciding here. This is not a small choice."

Plainview always elevated the stakes of every conversation he was in.
Not through manipulation but through the genuine weight he placed on every commitment he made.

This phrase reframes a routine purchasing decision as the significant choice it actually is.
The buyer who was treating this as a low-stakes vendor selection suddenly feels the real gravity of it.
Elevated stakes demand a more serious decision process and a more serious rep, and you just established which one you are.

Make the decision feel as significant as it actually is. Buyers close harder on things that matter.

7. "I am going to leave you with one thing. Whatever you decide, decide it knowing you had access to the best option available."

This is Plainview's final move, the statement of total conviction delivered so cleanly. It carries it into every other conversation they have about this decision.

Plant the standard. Let every competitor be measured against it while you are not in the room.
