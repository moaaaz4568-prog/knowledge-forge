1. "Before I sell you anything, let me help you win."

Converse hired him as a traveling salesman in the early 1920s. For nearly 30 years he ran free basketball clinics in high school gyms and YMCAs, teaching a new sport to coaches and players. Local stores sold the shoes to the crowd afterward. Open your next call by solving one real problem before you pitch.

Nobody argues with the person who helped them win.

2. "Keep this, whether you buy or not."

At his clinics he handed out pocket-sized instruction books on how to play the game. Free, useful, and still in the coach's pocket long after he drove away. Leave your buyer something they'll use without you: a one-page plan, a checklist, the numbers they asked for.

What stays in their hands keeps your name in their head.

3. "Watch it work first. Then decide."

His clinics were a live demo: a game, a lesson and a gym full of people watching the shoes do their job. Store reps took the orders at the door. Before you ask for anything, show the result working: a case study, a walkthrough, a client on the line.

Seeing it work beats hearing that it will.

4. "Tell me what's wrong with it. I'll take it to the people who build it."

He listened to the players who wore the shoes, passed what they told him back to Converse, and helped improve the All Star. When a buyer finds a flaw, don't defend it. Write it down, own the fix, and come back with what changed.

An objection you fix becomes a reason they stay.

5. "I'll be there when it matters, not just when you sign."

He didn't vanish after the sale. His biographer compared it to buying a basketball signed by LeBron James, then having LeBron show up to pump it up for you. That personal touch is what made the difference. Show up after the yes: the first week, the first win.

The rep who shows up after the sale gets the next one.

6. "Tell me what you're up against first."

his method: go to a small town, win over
the coach, then put on the clinic. He sold
the person before the product. Spend the
first minutes on their world, their goals

7. "I'm not going anywhere."

He sold for Converse from the early 1920s until he retired in 1968. Coaches trusted him because he kept coming back, season after season. Say it when a buyer is scared of being left alone after they sign.

Now look at what he built. Teach to you. Show up after the sale. It's the same playbook online companies run today.

He just had to drive to every buyer. You don't.
