3. "NOT THE MARKET. NOT YOUR BUDGET. WHAT IN HERE STOPS IT?"

Her work locates the obstacle inside the person, not the environment. External blame is comfortable and it never gets solved.

Push once, gently, past the market conditions and the CFO. The real block is usually a fear of being blamed for choosing wrong.

Outside obstacles are excuses. Inside obstacles are solvable.

4. "WHEN EXACTLY DOES THAT GET IN THE WAY?"
Vague obstacles cannot be planned around.
Oettingen's protocol forces the obstacle down to a moment you could put on a calendar.
Ask when it shows up, and who is in the room when it does. You are building the exact scene where your deal dies.
A named moment is the only thing you can prepare for.

5. "SO WHEN THAT HAPPENS, HERE'S WHAT WE DO INSTEAD."

The last step of her method is an if then plan tied to the obstacle. If this moment shows up, then this is the response.

Give them the sentence to use in that exact meeting, in their words. They are not buying your product. They are buying a plan for a moment they dread.

Sell the plan for the bad moment, not the picture of the good one.

6. "SAY BOTH BACK TO ME. WHAT YOU WANT, AND WHAT'S IN THE WAY."
Mental contrasting only works when both halves are held together. Separated, the dream relaxes and the obstacle discourages.
Make the buyer say the pair out loud, in that order, before you talk price. Whoever says it owns it.
Held apart they're a wish and a worry. Together they're a plan.

7. "BEFORE WE TALK MONEY, IS THIS ACTUALLY WORTH THE FIGHT?"

Ask it straight, and be willing to lose the buyers who say yes to this question stop needing to be chased.
A goal that survives the obstacle stops needing your follow up.
