1. "Totally fine if this isn't a fit."

Braun lowers the sales pressure on purpose, right at the start.

The harder you push, the harder people pull back. Giving them a clean way out removes the resistance, and a buyer who doesn't feel cornered actually leans in instead of bracing.

Take the pressure off and they stop defending.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "I'm not sure we can help yet. Can I ask a few questions?"

Braun leads with doubt instead of a confident pitch.

Certainty makes buyers suspicious; honest uncertainty makes them curious. Admitting you don't know yet earns the right to ask, and the questions are where the real selling happens.

Lead with honesty, not a hard pitch.

3. "What made you take the call today?"

Braun gets the buyer to say their own
reason for being interested.

When the buyer names the problem out
loud, it becomes real and urgent in their
own voice. You're not pushing a reason on
them, you're surfacing the one they
already have.

Let them tell you why they're here.

Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "How are you handling that now, and how's it going?"

Braun pokes gently at the status quo instead of attacking it.

People defend a decision you attack and reconsider one you simply ask about. A neutral question about the current way lets them find the cracks themselves.

Question the status quo, don't bash it.

@jackblairofficial

5. "A lot of people in your seat worry about... is that on your mind?"

Braun names the unspoken objection before the buyer has to.

Saying the quiet concern out loud builds instant trust, because it proves you understand their world. An objection you raise first is one you get to handle calmly.

Name the fear before they have to.

6. "Out of curiosity, what would need to be true for this to make sense?"

Braun lets the buyer define their own conditions for yes.

When people set the terms, they're far more committed to meeting them. You stop guessing at what would close them and let them hand you the answer directly.

Let them build the path to yes themselves.

7. "Want me to lay out what working together would look like?"

Braun asks permission instead of launching into the close.

A pitch the buyer invited lands completely differently from one they endured. Getting the yes to hear it means they're already leaning toward the yes to buy.

Get permission to close and the close is half done.
