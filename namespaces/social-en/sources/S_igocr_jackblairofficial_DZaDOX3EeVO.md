1. He treated the cold call as a way to get the meeting, not the sale.

At Xerox, Becker stopped trying to sell copiers over the phone.

He realized the only job of the call was to earn five minutes in person, so he stopped pitching the product and started selling the appointment. One small yes is far easier to get than one big one.

Ask for the meeting, not the money.

2. He learned to want objections instead of fearing them.

Most reps freeze the second a buyer pushes back.

Becker flipped it. He read an objection as proof of interest, because a person with zero interest doesn't bother to argue. A price complaint means they want it, just not at that number yet.

When they object, lean in. Silence is the real no.

Becker heard no more than almost anyone alive.

Keep your composure at no and you stay in the game long enough to win.

7. He found a way to get six days of work done in five.

Protect the hours that make money and cut the ones that just fill time.
