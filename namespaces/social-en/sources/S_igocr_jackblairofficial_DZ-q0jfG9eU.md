1. “Before we talk your price, here's what this usually runs.”

Kahneman proved the first number a person hears quietly sets the frame for every number after it. He called it anchoring.

Drop a real, higher reference point before the discussion on and your actual price lands as a relief, not a shock. The first number wins.

Set the anchor before they set it for you.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. “Let’s look at what staying where you are is already costing you.”

Kahneman showed that losing something hurts about twice as much as gaining the same thing feels good. Loss does the heavy lifting.

Frame the decision around what they keep bleeding by doing nothing, not just what they’d gain. The pain of the leak moves people.

Make the cost of staying louder than the cost of moving.

Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "It's not an expense. It's the thing that pays you back."

Same fact, two frames, two different decisions. Kahneman called it the framing effect, and it changes the answer every time.

Never present a number raw. Wrap it in the outcome it buys, and the brain evaluates the outcome instead of the cost.

Control the frame and you control the choice.

Now imagine handing it back.”

endowment effect.

before they buy it, and walking away starts
to feel like a loss they won't take.

Let them own it in their head first.

5. "You've actually handled harder calls than this one."
Kahneman showed the brain trusts what feels familiar and easy. Cognitive ease reads as truth and safety.
Make the next step feel like something they already know how to do, and the fear of deciding quietly drops away.
Easy to picture is easy to say yes to.

6. "Before you decide, here's the best part."

Kahneman proved people judge an experience by its peak and its ending, not the whole thing. The peak-end rule.

End every call on the strongest point, not the admin. The last thing they feel is the thing they remember when they choose.

Finish on the peak, because that's what they keep.

Kahneman described the halo effect, where one strong impression colors how people judge everything else about you.

Lead with quiet, specific competence early and it makes the whole offer feel safer. One clear strength carries the rest.

Be the calm expert and the doubt has nowhere to sit.
