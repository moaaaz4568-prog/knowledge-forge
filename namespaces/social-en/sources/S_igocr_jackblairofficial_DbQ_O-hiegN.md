1. "THAT ONE'S ON ME, START TO FINISH."
1
Roy Lewicki tested apologies on 755 people at Ohio State and one component buried the other five: acknowledgment of responsibility. Not regret. Not explanation. Ownership.
So when the demo breaks or the deadline slips, say it before they've decided whose fault it was. First sentence of the call. The longer ownership waits, the more it looks negotiated.
Ownership is the apology. Everything else is delivery.

2. "HERE'S WHAT HAPPENED. NONE OF IT IS AN EXCUSE."
Explanation made de Lewicki's list too, but it only works after ownership, never instead of it. Order is everything.
Own it first and the same facts sound like transparency. Lead with them and they sound like a lawyer. So give your buyer the two-sentence version, flag it as context, and stop talking before it grows a defense.
Explain the failure. Never defend it.

Offer of repair ranked second of six in the study,
right behind ownership. Feeling sorry is private.

5. "AND HERE'S WHAT CHANGES SO IT CAN'T
HAPPEN TWICE."
Lewicki's list calls this repentance, the declared
change. On a deal it's the difference between an
incident and a pattern, and your buyer is deciding
which one you are right now.
So show them the new system, not a promise to try
harder. Name what got removed, added, or
automated. Patterns kill accounts. Incidents just
bruise them.
The second failure is the one nobody forgives.

6. "YOU DON'T HAVE TO BE FINE WITH THIS YET."

Of all six components, requesting forgiveness scored last. It asks the wounded party to do the work.

This line does the opposite, it hands them the timeline, and anger with nowhere to push against runs out of fuel fast. Give the room. You'll usually get the pardon you never asked for.

Forgiveness shows up faster when nobody asks for it.

7. "HOLD ME TO ALL OF IT. GRADE ME IN TWO WEEKS."
Run Lewicki’s sequence and this is the close: ownership, facts, cost, repair, change, room, and now a date.
A burned client can’t believe promises anymore, so don’t offer one. Offer a checkpoint they control. Two weeks, their calendar, their scorecard. Deals have survived worse than your mistake. Almost none survive a rep who disappears after it.
A kept promise after a miss outsells a clean record.
