McDonald's Isn't Actually a Fast Food Company?
Opening Line
McDonald's is a fast food company, right? Well, not exactly. In fact, McDonald's isn't a fast food company at all. It's a real estate company.
The Ray Kroc Revelation
In 1954, Ray Kroc, a milkshake machine salesman, met the McDonald brothers, Richard and Maurice, in San Bernardino, California. He was impressed by their efficient fast-food operation, the "Speed Service System," and saw potential to franchise it.
The Real Business Model
Ray Kroc didn't just want to sell burgers; he wanted to own the land on which the restaurants were built. He realized that the real money was in real estate, not in selling food.
So, he created a separate company, McDonald's Corporation, which would buy the land and lease it to the franchisees. This way, McDonald's Corporation earned money from two sources: the franchise fees and the rent.
The Franchisee's Role
The franchisees, on the other hand, were responsible for running the restaurants, including hiring staff, buying supplies, and cooking the food. They paid rent to McDonald's Corporation and a percentage of their sales as franchise fees.
Why It Matters
This business model allowed McDonald's to expand rapidly without the huge capital investment of building and owning every restaurant. It also gave them a steady stream of income from rent, regardless of how well each individual restaurant was performing.
So, the next time you grab a Big Mac, remember: you're not just buying a burger, you're participating in a real estate empire.
