1. "YOU'RE RIGHT. IF I WERE SITTING WHERE YOU SIT, I'D PUSH BACK TOO."
In September 2015 the Bard Prison Initiative debate team met Harvard, the reigning national champions, inside a New York maximum security prison. They opened by conceding the obvious instead of fighting it.
Do the same the second an objection lands. Agree with what is true in it before you touch what is not. Resistance has nothing to grip.
Meet them where they're right. Lead them where they're right. Lead them where they're wrong.

3. "I DON'T NEED YOU TO LIKE IT. I NEED YOU TO LIKE IT. I NEED YOU TO WEIGH IT."
The team said openly they disagreed with their own side. Then they argued it to a 2 to 1 win in front of three world class judges. Position and conviction are separate muscles.
Stop selling your enthusiasm. Put the evidence on the table and ask for a verdict on the evidence, not on you.
Conviction is cheap. Discipline reads as truth.

7. "SCORE IT LIKE A JUDGE. WHERE DOES THIS
ACTUALLY STAND?"
The match ended on scorecards, 2 to 1, not
applause. Three neutral judges, criteria, a verdict.
That structure is why the upset counted.
End calls the same way. Ask for the standing, the
criteria, and what moves the score. A number you
can work beats a feeling you can't.
Feedback invited never arrives as an ambush.
