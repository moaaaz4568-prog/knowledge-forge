SAY WHY YOU'RE CALLING IN TEN SECONDS
Friedstad and Wright's model: every buyer runs a background process hunting for the moment this became selling.
An undeclared agenda keeps that detector running hot.
State the commercial purpose up front, plainly, before the small talk. Once there is nothing left to detect, the detector quiets.
A named agenda is boring. A hidden one is loud.

KILL THE PIVOT
The flip fires hardest at the turn, the moment warm
chat becomes pitch. The paper calls it a change of
meaning: everything before the turn gets reread as
setup.
Stop building rapport as a runway. Open with the
business, and let the human part happen inside the
work, where it cannot be reread as a move.
Warmth before the ask gets audited. Warmth inside
the work does not.

PRAISE ONLY WHAT YOU CAN POINT AT
Flattery sits on the model's tactic list. The instant praise reads as technique, everything after it gets discounted.
That question tells me you've done this before, works because it has an object. Compliment their question, their prep, their diligence, and only when it is true.
Empty praise costs trust. Specific praise builds it.

REAL DEADLINES ONLY, REASON ATTACHED
Manufactured urgency is the model's loudest trigger.
One fake countdown and the buyer reprices every
future sentence as tactic.
If the deadline is real, give its mechanics: the
implementation slot, the team's capacity, your
quarter. If there is no reason, there is no deadline.
Say so.
Urgency with a reason is information. Without one it
is pressure.

SHOW THE MATH, SKIP THE ADJECTIVES
Superlatives trip the schema. Unverifiable claims are
exactly what the detector evolved to catch.
Replace best in class with the two numbers and the
source the buyer can check without you. What they
verify alone never gets audited again.
Claims invite audits. Sources end them.

TELL THEM WHAT HAPPENS AFTER THE YES

The model says buyers audit hardest near the close, hunting for the thing being slipped past them. The unprompted tours the audit before it starts.

Walk them through the unglamorous first sixty days, the onboarding lift, the real workload, without being asked.

Nothing calms a suspicious buyer like the boring truth.

"LET ME STOP SELLING FOR A SECOND."
The model allows re-entry. When answers go short
and polite, the flip has already fired, and more pitch
only feeds it. Naming the dynamic resets the frame.

whatever comes back with no segue to the deck.
The detour is the close.
You cannot out-pitch suspicion. You can only out-
honest it.
