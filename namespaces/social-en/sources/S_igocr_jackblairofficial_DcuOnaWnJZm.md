1. "WALK ME THROUGH THE LAST TIME YOU
ACTUALLY BOUGHT SOMETHING LIKE THIS."
1957. Ford launches the Edsel after a decade of
research, polls, ches the Edsel after a decade of

it the 250 million dollar flop. The research asked
what people wanted. Buying answered differently.
Opinions are free and spent like it. History costs
money, so it tells the last purchase,
not the preference. Receipts are true.

What they've tried is what they truly believed. The graveyard of past fixes tells you the real problem and the real budget.

3. "IF THIS SHOWED UP FREE TOMORROW, WOULD YOU ACTUALLY USE IT?"
People told pollsters they wanted a medium priced statement car. Then 1958 arrived, a recession bit, and America bought small, plain, and cheap. Stated love, absent wallet.
Free strips price out of the answer. A no at free means the problem isn't real. A yes at free means you're only negotiating value now.
