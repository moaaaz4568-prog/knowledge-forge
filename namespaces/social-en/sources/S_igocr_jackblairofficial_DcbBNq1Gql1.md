1. "BEFORE I PUT ANYTHING ON THE TABLE, WHAT WOULD YOU PUT ON IT?"
Lee Ross at Stanford named it reactive devaluation: the identical offer dropped from 90 percent approval to 44 the moment people heard it came from the other side.
So stop being the author. Open by asking for their version. A term is already worth more to them than the same term you volunteer.
The offer they write is the offer they defend.

2. "BE SUSPICIOUS OF ANYTHING I HAND YOU FOR FREE."
The ugly half of the finding: a concession nobody asked for reads as a tell. The buyer's brain asks what you know that they don't.
Say it before they think it. Naming the suspicion out loud drains it, and it tells them the next thing you give was pulled out of you, not planted.
Nobody trusts a gift from across the table.

3. "IF THIS WERE YOUR IDEA INSTEAD OF MINE, WOULD IT WORK?"
In Ross's survey the terms never changed. Only the name on the proposal did, and that alone moved the vote by half.
Take the concession you planned to make anyway and hand it over to them as a hypothetical of theirs. Let them say yes to it as their own thought before you confirm it.
Same terms. Different author. Different answer.

4. "WHAT WOULD MAKE YOUR VERSION BETTER FOR YOU?"
The drop tracked the source. A neutral name cost the proposal ten points. The adversary name cost it nearly fifty. Every edit the buyer makes moves you off the adversary line.
Ask them to improve their own draft before you touch it. Two edits bits in, it is their deal, and people stop fighting a deal they helped write.
Nobody devalues their own handwriting.

5. "RUN IT PAST YOUR TEAM. TELL ME WHAT
THEY CUT."
The neutral messenger in Ross's survey lost almost
nothing. The adversary lost half. The most neutral
messenger in any deal is the buyer's own side.
Send the draft into their building and ask for cuts,
not additions. Whatever survives their own people
has been endorsed by a source they cannot
discount.
Their cuts are worth more than your additions.

6. "YOU BUILT THIS. I JUST PRICED IT."
Ross's work shows the value of an offer travels with
its author. Handing the buyer authorship of the final
terms is not modesty. It is pricing strategy.
When you present the final version, walk through
every line they touched and credit it back to them.
Your number rides in on their structure.
Credit is the cheapest concession there is.

7. "YOU WROTE IT. IS THERE ANYTHING IN IT
YOU'D STILL ARGUE WITH?"
The last trap is the close itself. The second you
push, you become the close and the support
heads back toward that 44.
So do not push. Invite the final argument against a
deal they built. Most buyers defend it to you. The
ones who object just told you the real price of yes.
Let them close the deal they wrote.
