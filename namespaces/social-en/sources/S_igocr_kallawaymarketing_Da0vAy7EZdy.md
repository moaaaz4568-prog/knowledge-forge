LEVEL 1: STORYTELLING
The Reporter
The reporter includes the core facts of the story
but sequences them in a super predictable way.
This happened,
then this happened,
then this happened.
Level 1
Level 1 is like a newscaster reading off a teleprompter.
it's informative but not that engaging. Graduate
from this level as soon as possible.
<!-- image 3 -->
LEVEL 2: STORYTELLING
The Illusionist
You keep the core facts,
but now you add hooks
to the intro and body of the story. Great
hooks are just misdirections.
•
•
•
•
• Level 2
•
You lean somebody one way, then snap them back in a
different direction. Do this even a couple
times and you're at level 2.
<!-- image 4 -->
LEVEL 3: STORYTELLING
The Champion
In this level keep the facts and the hooks, but now add the stakes.
Stakes are use cases, problems, or characters that make the
viewer feel personally connected to the story.
Level 3
Without these, the story falls flat and
never truly resonates
<!-- image 5 -->
LEVEL 4: STORYTELLING
The Architect
From here, we're getting into advanced storytelling
mode. You start adding arcs and loops.Loops are
mini subplots that open and close.
Level 4

LEVEL 5: STORYTELLING
The Translator
Take everything from the first 4 levels and add comprehension
and clarity, so the viewer is clear on what you are
saying 100% of the time.
Level 5
That means reframing things, adding better
visuals, and an overall layer of polish.

LEVEL 6: STORYTELLING
The Maestro
This level is all about adding your unique fingerprint to the story. This is Your signature style and whatever makes your storytelling uniquely yours.
Level 6
Beginners won’t have this fully built out yet, but you develop it over time and it becomes the layer that differentiates you.
