1. He researched every prospect before knocking.

Ogilvy walked the village before he knocked the door. He learned the husband's profession, the family size, the kitchen layout, the income.

He arrived knowing what they needed before they did.

Reps cold-call. Closers warm-research. The rep introduces themselves. The closer arrives already knowing the answer.
<!-- image 2 -->

2. He sold the cooker as the centerpiece of the home.

Most cooker reps sold the cooker. Ogilvy sold what the cooker did to family meals, to mornings, to the way the kitchen smelled.

The buyer wasn't buying iron and steel. They were buying Sunday dinner that the in-laws would talk about.

Reps sell the product. Closers sell what the product replaces in the buyer's life.

3. He told the buyer what was wrong
with the product first.

The cooker was expensive. It took 2 weeks
to install. It needed maintenance.

Ogilvy named all of it before the buyer
could ask. By the time they got to the
close there was nothing left to push back
on.

The closer who names the negatives first
kills the negotiation cycle before it starts.

4. He matched his pace to the buyer's.

If they spoke slowly, he slowed down. If they spoke quickly, he accelerated. If they paused, he paused longer.

The match dropped the buyer's defenses without them noticing.

Reps run their own pace. Closers run the buyer's. Same conversation, different result.

5. He asked questions that made the buyer answer for him.

"How does your current stove handle a Sunday roast?" The answer was always a complaint. He listened. Wrote it down. Read it back at the close.

The buyer who hears their own complaint quoted back can't argue with the solution.

The closer who lets the buyer build the case wins every objection round before it begins.

6. He documented every sale in a notebook and reviewed it weekly.

What worked. What didn't. Which line landed. Which one fell flat. Which objection killed which deal.

By year 2 he had a system. By year 3 he was unbeatable.

Reps grind harder when the numbers slip. Closers track the slip and find the fix.

The closer who can systematize their craft scales it. The rep who keeps the craft in their head keeps the income in their head too.
