1. “You cannot outsell your own emotional intelligence.”

The core finding behind Stanley’s work.

Technique gets you in the room. Composure closes it.

2. “Desperation has a smell. Buyers catch it instantly.”

Stanley on what reps leak without knowing.

She taught that a rep who badly needs the deal gives it away in their tone, their pace, their eagerness. The buyer senses it and the leverage shifts. Need is felt long before it is said.

The buyer smells desperation before you finish the sentence.

3. "The gap between knowing and doing is emotional, not technical."

Stanley on why training alone does not fix reps.

She pointed out reps already know they should ask harder questions and hold their price. They do not, because fear and discomfort stop them. The missing piece is rarely knowledge. It is nerve.

Most reps do not need to learn more. They need to flinch less.

4. "Empathy is a selling skill, not a soft one."

Stanley on a misunderstood word.

She argued empathy is not about being nice. It is the hard skill of truly reading what the buyer feels and needs. A rep who genuinely understands the buyer asks sharper questions and earns deeper trust.

Empathy is not softness. It is accurate reading of the room.

5. "Manage your reaction before you manage the call."

Stanley on the first thing that has to be under control.

She taught reps to notice their own reaction, the flash of panic at an objection, the urge to discount, and pause before acting on it. The rep who controls themselves controls the conversation.

Get a grip on yourself first. The call follows.

6. “A need for approval will talk you out of the close.”
Stanley on a quiet, common weakness.
She found reps who needed to be liked would avoid the hard ask, soften the price, and let the buyer off the hook. Wanting approval feels harmless. It quietly costs you the deal.
If you need them to like you, you will not push when it matters.

7. "Stay calm under pressure. The composed rep wins."
Stanley on the trait that holds it all together.
When the deal gets tense, the objection lands hard, the price gets challenged, the composed rep keeps thinking clearly. The rattled one reacts and concedes. Calm is a competitive advantage.
Whoever stays calm in the hard moment usually walks out with the deal.
