1. "Let me walk you through what actually goes into delivering this."

Claude Hopkins toured the Schlitz brewery, saw bottles washed with live steam, and built the whole campaign on it. Every brewery did the same thing. He said it first, and Schlitz went from fifth to the top. Describe your ordinary process out loud. The first one to say it owns it.

Whoever explains the process first owns the category.

2. "It's not faster. It's eleven days faster."

Hopkins proved generalities bounce off people and specifics stick. Supreme quality means nothing. An exact number reads as fact, because people assume you can't fake precision. Strip every vague claim out of your pitch and replace it with the real figure, however unglamorous.

Vague claims get ignored. Specific ones get believed.

3. "There's a reason this deal looks the way it does. Let me tell you."

Hopkins found that an offer without a reason breeds suspicion. A discount with no explanation reads as desperation or a defect. Attach the honest logic to every number, every deadline, every concession, and suspicion turns into confidence.

An unexplained deal smells wrong. Give the reason.

4. "If this doesn't do what I said, here's exactly what happens."

Hopkins pioneered the free sample and the guarantee because he knew risk kills more deals than price. The buyer isn't afraid of the number. They're afraid of being the one who chose wrong. State the safety net before they ask and the fear has nowhere to live.

Buyers don't fear the price. They fear the regret.

Anchor the problem to their morning and you never leave.

Everyone else sold toothpaste on decay and fear. Hoe sold toothpaste on pretty teeth, and his campaign moved a nation. People stretch toward a gain longer than they run from a warning. When the call goes heavy, flip to the version of their life this builds.
