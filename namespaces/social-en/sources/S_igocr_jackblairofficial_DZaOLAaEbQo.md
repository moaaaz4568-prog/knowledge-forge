1. "The reason for my call is..."

Weiss teaches reps to say why they are
calling in the first breath.

Most callers stall with chitchat and trigger
suspicion, but stating the reason immediately answers the one question
every prospect is asking, so they stop
bracing and start listening. Clarity beats
warm-up.

Name the reason for the call before you
say anything else.

2. "Do you have a quick minute? This is a business call."

Weiss never pretends a cold call is a friendly catch-up.

Asking for a defined minute and naming it as business gives the prospect control and respect, and the honesty buys more time than any clever hook does. People give time to those who ask for it straight.

Request a specific minute and tell them what it's about.

3. "No one is born knowing how to do this."

Weiss heard the same excuse for decades, that some people just have the gift.

She killed it flat. Cold calling is a skill like any other, built by reps through repetition, not handed out at birth. The natural is just someone who practiced.

Stop waiting to feel ready and start running the reps.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Don't make the script your own. Make it work first."

When a beginner wanted to rewrite the proven script in week one, Weiss stopped them cold.

A new rep doesn't yet know which words are carrying the call, so personalizing too early strips out the power language that makes it land. Earn the right to improvise.

Run the proven words exactly until the results show up, then adjust.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Warm up, rehearse, then perform."

Weiss carried one thing from ballet straight into sales.

No dancer walks on stage cold, and no closer should dial cold either, so she drilled the script until it was muscle memory before it ever mattered on a live call. Practice removes the fear.

Rehearse out loud before the call so the real one runs on instinct.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Start with your worst leads."

Weiss told reps to dial their weakest prospects first.

The early calls are where you're rustiest, so spending them on low-stakes names lets you find your voice before you reach the ones that count. Protect the calls that matter.

Burn the rust off on the throwaways, then hit your best on the leads sharp.

7. "Go through the no's until someone says yes."

Weiss treated rejection as the toll on the road, not a roadblock.

A fixed number of no's stands between a rep and each appointment, so the job is simply to move through them without flinching instead of stopping at the first one. The no's are the path.

Keep dialing through the no's and the yes shows up on schedule.
