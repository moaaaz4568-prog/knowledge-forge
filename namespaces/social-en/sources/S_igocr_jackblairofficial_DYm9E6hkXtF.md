1. "I'm not sure we're a fit. But let me ask you a few questions."

Sandler taught reps to take pressure off by suggesting the deal might not happen.

Most reps push to close. Sandler taught the reverse. The buyer who senses you might walk starts trying to qualify themselves.

The closer who is willing to lose the deal controls the deal.

2. "Tell me more about that."
Sandler called this the most powerful phrase in selling.
Whenever a buyer raised a concern, an objection, or a hint of pain, the rep said it. Tell me more.
Every time the buyer kept talking, they revealed the real reason they would or would not buy.

3. "How long has that been a problem?"
Sandler taught reps to dig into the buyer's
pain instead of rushing past it.
The phrase made the buyer sit with the
cost of doing nothing. A problem they had
carried for years suddenly felt urgent.
The closer who lets the buyer feel the pain
does not have to manufacture urgency.
<!-- image 4 -->

4. "What have you tried to fix it?"
Sandler taught reps to map every failed
solution the buyer had already attempted.
It did two things. It showed the rep what
not to pitch. And it reminded the buyer
that the problem had survived every
previous attempt.

5. "What happens if you don't fix this?"
Sandler called it the pain question. The
buyer answered by describing their own
worst case out loud.
The rep did not have to threaten or
pressure. The buyer scared themselves
into action with their own words.

6. "It sounds like this might not be a priority. Should we leave it?"

Sandler taught reps to offer the buyer an exit.

The buyer who was stalling either took the exit, which saved everyone time, or pushed back and admitted it mattered.

The closer who offers the exit gets a real answer instead of a slow no.

7. "Does it make sense to move forward, or not?"

Sandler taught a close with a built-in permission to say no.

The buyer felt no pressure, because no was an honest option. That safety is exactly what made the buyer comfortable saying yes.

The closer who makes no easy gets a faster yes.
