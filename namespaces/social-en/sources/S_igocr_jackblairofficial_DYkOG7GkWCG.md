ELMER LETTERMAN SOLD A $1 MILLION LIFE INSURANCE POLICY TO A MAN HE MET ON A NEW YORK SIDEWALK. HERE ARE THE 7 PHRASES HE USED ON EVERY DEAL.
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "The sale begins when the customer says no."

Letterman's most famous line. He put it on the cover of his book.

Most agents heard "no" and packed up. Letterman heard "no" as the moment the real conversation started.

Every objection was an invitation to understand the buyer better. The reps who quit at "no" never found out what the buyer actually wanted.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "It's not what you say. It's what they hear."

Letterman rehearsed every pitch by imagining the buyer's ears, not his own mouth.

He'd ask himself: what is this person actually going to take away from that sentence?

The agent who controls what the buyer hears closes the deal the rep who only controls what he says never does.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
3. "I never sell insurance. I sell what insurance does."
Letterman never described the policy.
He described the widow who kept her house. The kid who finished college. The business that survived the founder.
The buyer doesn't want the product. They want what the product protects.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Give me 10 minutes. (If you're not interested, I'll leave and never call again."

Letterman's appointment-setter for 40 years.

The promise was specific. 10 minutes. A guaranteed exit.

The buyer who agreed to 10 low-risk minutes almost always gave Letterman an hour. The hard time limit was what got him in the door.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Let me ask you something I ask everyone."

Letterman used it to introduce his hardest question.

The phrase made the buyer feel like part of a pattern, not a target. Everyone gets asked this. It's normal. Answer honestly.

Relaxed. Then Letterman asked the question that surfaced the real need.
<!-- image 6 -->

@jackblairofficial

6. "You don't have to decide today. You just have to decide."

Letterman's response to a stalling buyer.

The phrase removed the pressure of "now" while keeping the pressure of "yes."

The buyer felt no rush. But the decision was still coming. By the follow-up, the buyer had decided.

7. "I'm not after your business. I'm after your trust. The business follows."

Letterman's closing philosophy in one line.

He played the 30-year game. The first policy was small. The trust it built was the asset.

By the end of his career, Letterman's clients had bought from him for decades and sent him everyone they knew
