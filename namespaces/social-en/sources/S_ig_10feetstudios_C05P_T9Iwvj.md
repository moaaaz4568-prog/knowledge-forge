Did you ever have some client say yes on a sales call and you might have even collected some cash, but just after a week or so they got cold feet and wanted to back out? That might have been your shitty onboarding process at fault. I guess the reason why nobody talks about this is because client onboarding is kind of this weird thing between client acquisition and client retention. But you'd be surprised if I told you what kind of a difference a solid and premium feeling onboarding process can make. Well, a bad one can destroy everything. What you guys call onboarding is probably just signing the contract, collecting the cash, and then maybe getting them into your Slack for communication, but letting them wait for like a week or two before delivering anything. Now compare that to a really solid onboarding process. The client pays and signs a contract. You book in a strategy call in the following two days where you come ready and loaded to tell them the exact plan for the next couple of weeks and listen to their opinion as well. But even before the call, the client is not only already part of your Slack, but also got to watch a whole welcome video where you make them feel very good about their purchase and lay out what the next few weeks with you are gonna look like. And then after the strategy call, once you have everything you need to get started, you deliver the first update in the next three days and don't just let them sit in silence. I mean, how would you feel if you pay somebody three grand upfront and hear nothing from them for the next two weeks?
