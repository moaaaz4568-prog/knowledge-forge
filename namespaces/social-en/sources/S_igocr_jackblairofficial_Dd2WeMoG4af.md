1. Give before you ask.

"Your last invoice bills you for the waiting room twice." Cialdini spent nearly three years undercover at car lots, fundraising rooms and phone banks, and the pros all did the same first thing: they gave something before they asked. I gave Claude my offer and asked for the gift that costs me nothing in the first five minutes of a call. What came back:

Three gifts, ranked by how much they put the buyer in my debt: a line by line read of her current contract with the double charge circled, a photo of her current contract with checklist, and the product her crew uses that voids the warranty. The handover line has no pitch in it.

3. Proof that sounds like her.

"A dentist with two chairs, not a hospital with two hundred." Social proof and authority: people copy people who like them and defer to them. On a call it is one story about someone the buyer recognises as herself. I asked Claude to rewrite my proof that way. What came back:

Three stories under 60 seconds each, blanks left for the real names, each ending on the moment that buyer decided: a failed inspection, a hygienist who quit over the bathrooms, a landlord walk through. Plus one authority line with a number in it and no adjectives.

"Six out of ten. You went to scarcity on a deadline you invented." The last prompt turns Claude into a skeptical buyer who gets colder every time I use a principle clumsily. I run the gift, the ladder and the proof on her and ask for the grade. Here's what it does:
