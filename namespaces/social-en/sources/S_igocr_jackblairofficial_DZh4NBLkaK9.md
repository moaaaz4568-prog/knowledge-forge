1. "You'll have to do better than that."

Dawson called this the vise, or the calm line
you say to almost any number the other
side puts on the table.

You don't argue or justify. You just hand
the pressure back and go quiet. More
often than people expect, the other side
moves against themselves to fill the
silence.

Say it, stop talking, and let them negotiate
against themselves.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "Always flinch at the first offer."

Dawson taught a visible reaction the moment a price or term lands, a wince, a pause, a quiet let that's too far.

Most people don't expect resistance, so the flinch makes them improve the offer before you've said a word. People who don't flinch leave money on the table every time.

React like it hurt, even when it didn't.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "I'd love to, but I can't."

Dawson taught reps to lean on a limit
instead of their own stubbornness, a
policy, a budget, a rule.

It's far harder to push against a wall than
against a person. Friendly on the outside,
immovable underneath, you hold the line
without ever picking a fight.

Blame the rule, not yourself, and the
pressure slides off.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. “Let me check with my people.”

Dawson called this the higher authority, a vague someone above you who has to approve the final terms.

It buys you time, takes the heat off, and lets you say no without being the bad guy. Never let the other side think you alone can sign, or they’ll pressure only you.

Keep an invisible boss in your back pocket.

@jackblairofficial
5. “If I do that for you, what do you do
for me?”
Dawson's rule was simple, never give a
concession without getting one back.
The moment you give freely, every future
thing you give feels owed. Trade instead of
donate and each concession raises the
value of the deal instead of shrinking it.
Never give anything away without trading
for it.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Never say yes to the first offer."

Dawson warned that snapping up the first offer makes both sides uneasy, the giver feels they offered too much, the taker wonders what they missed.

A little hesitation makes the final deal feel earned, and earned deals stick. The easy yes is the one people regret by morning.

Make them work for the yes so it holds.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

7. "Congratulate them when it's done."

Dawson ended every negotiation by telling the other side they did a great job.

A buyer who feels they won doesn't look for reasons to back out, they stay, they refer, they come back. The last words you say decide whether the deal survives the drive home.

Let them leave feeling like the winner.
