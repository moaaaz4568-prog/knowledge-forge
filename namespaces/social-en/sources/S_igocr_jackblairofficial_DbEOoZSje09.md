1. "Watch what this does, then tell me if you want it."

The man on the crate was Joe Ades, and he never described the peeler. He peeled a carrot in front of you. Ades sold by demonstration, not explanation, and the curl of skin did the talking. Stop explaining your offer. Walk them through one real result, live, before you ask for anything.

Nobody argues with what they just watched work.

2. "It's a small number. Say it out loud, plainly."

Ades said the price cheerfully, like it was the easy part, and set it next to something trivial. A price only feels heavy when you flinch. Name yours the same way, then anchor it to something they already spend without thinking.

Say the price like it's the easy part.

3. "Come in closer, this next part you have to see."
Ades gathered a crowd on purpose, because proximity built attention and attention built proof. On a call, pull them in. Ask them to share their screen for one second. Make them lean toward you before you ever pitch.
Attention is the sale before the sale.

4. "Let me show you that one more time."

Ades ran the same demo on a loop, and repetition built belief instead of boredom. Do not fear repeating your strongest proof. Show the one thing that matters, then show it again while they watch your hands.

Repetition is how proof turns into belief.

5. "Take one, or take the set and never think about it again."

Ades sold one for five or three for ten, and the set felt like the smart move, not the bigger spend. Offer the version that ends the problem for good, framed as less hassle, not more cost.

Sell the end of the problem, not the item.

6. "I'm not going to chase you. It's here if you want it."

Ades never ran after a walker, and the calm made people step back in. Drop the pressure on your call. A buyer who feels no pull stops bracing and starts deciding.

The rep who never chases is the one they follow.

7. "Hold it for a second. Feel the weight."

Ades put the peeler in your hand, because ownership starts the moment someone holds the thing. Get your buyer using it, clicking through it, picturing it theirs before the ask.

People keep what they already hold.
