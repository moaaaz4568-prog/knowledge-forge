1. "BEFORE WE TALK YOUR PRICE, HERE'S WHAT THIS USUALLY RUNS."
Kahneman proved the first number a person hears quietly sets the frame for every number after it. He called it anchoring.
Drop a real, higher reference point before the discussion and your actual price lands as a relief, not a shock. The first number wins.
Set the anchor before they set it for you.

2. "LET'S LOOK AT WHAT STAYING WHERE YOU
ARE IS ALREADY COSTING YOU."
Kahneman showed that losing something hurts
about twice as much as gaining the same thing feels
good. Loss does the heavy lifting.
Frame the decision around what they keep bleeding
by doing nothing, not just what they'd gain. The pain
of the leak moves people.
Make the cost of staying louder than the cost of
moving.

3. "IT'S NOT AN EXPENSE. IT'S THE THING THAT
PAYS YOU BACK."
Same fact, two frames, two different decisions.
Kahneman called it the framing effect, and it
changes the answer every time.
Never present a number raw. Wrap it in the outcome
it buys, and the brain evaluates the outcome instead
of the cost.
Control the frame and you control the choice.
