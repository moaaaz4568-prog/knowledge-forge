Why you should build a (psychology-driven) personal brand:
<!-- image 1 -->
I grew my personal brand to 60K in 8 months by analysing patterns in history and human behaviour
[and I turned it into a repeatable system for me and my clients]

This has nothing to do with posting more, or any new BS settings or trends. It all comes down to 5 timeless principles.
<!-- image 2 -->
PRINCIPLE 1
People Don't Pay for Value
When Van Gogh died, his paintings were worthless, people refused to buy them.

His sister-in-law published letters he'd written about each painting, the story behind every piece of art.

The value went from zero to millions overnight but his paintings didn't change. Their story did.

Posting just value, makes people value you less.
<!-- image 3 -->
PRINCIPLE 2
Trust is King
People don't buy from the most qualified person in the
room.
They buy from the person they trust.
In ancient Rome, merchants who actively
communicated their character, values, and track
record, outsold the merchants who just sold.
Sancinito, The Reputation of the Roman Merchant, 2024
<!-- image 4 -->
PRINCIPLE 3
Failure Builds Connection
PRATFALL EFFECT
A study found that making a mistake can actually make
highly competent people more likable and relatable.
Sharing your weakness builds connection, and people buy
from people they connect with.
With one caveat: this only works if the credibility is already there. If people
already see you as competent and authoritative, one human flaw makes
you significantly more likeable. If they don't, the mistake just confirms
their doubts.
<!-- image 5 -->
PRINCIPLE 4
Looks Matter
HALO EFFECT
Psychologists asked military officers to rate their soldiers on specific, unrelated traits like physique, leadership, and intelligence
If they thought a soldier had a great physique, they almost always rated that soldier as having high intelligence and leadership.
If people are put off by one thing about you, they'll trust nothing else you say.
Pay close attention to how you're being perceived: your visuals, your language, your value.
<!-- image 6 -->
PRINCIPLE 5
Create Open Loops
ZEIGARNIK EFFECT
The Zeigarnik Effect is simple: your brain remembers
unfinished things more than finished ones. It can't help it.
Open loops demand to be closed.
Every piece of content
your audience needs
to close.
When your offer is positioned as the answer, buying
becomes the only way to achieve the result they need.
<!-- image 7 -->
There are only 4 reasons your content isn't bringing in more clients:
• They don't trust you
• You're not speaking to their problem
• They're not seeing your content
• They don't know you have an offer

These 5 principles will help bridge these gaps, but you need to know which of these 4 gaps is yours first.
