Marketing Terms
<!-- image 1 -->
ROAS - Return on ad spend

Spend $1,000 on advertising and generate $4,000 in revenue.

ROAS = 4x.

A higher ROAS generally means your advertising is generating more revenue for every dollar spent.

But even a 4x ROAS doesn't automatically mean you're profitable, your margins and other costs still matter.
<!-- image 2 -->
LTV - Lifetime value

The total amount of revenue one customer generates during their time with your business.

A customer pays $29 per month and stays for 14 months.
LTV = $406.

It helps you understand how valuable the average customer is over time.
<!-- image 3 -->
CAC - Customer acquisition cost

How much it costs you to acquire one new customer.

Spend $5,000 on ads and gain 100 customers.

CAC = $50.

If you're spending $50 to acquire someone who only generates $30, the numbers don't work.
<!-- image 4 -->
Churn
The percentage of customers who stop using or paying for your product during a given period.
Start with 100 subscribers and 6 leave during the month.
Churn = 6%.
High churn is like constantly refilling a leaking bucket.
Acquiring more customers won't solve the underlying problem if retention is poor.
<!-- image 5 -->
ARPU - Average revenue per user
Your total revenue divided by the number of customers or users.
$50,000 in revenue from 500 users.
ARPU = $100.
It shows how much revenue the average user generates. Lower ARPU generally requires more customers to reach the same revenue level, while higher ARPU means each customer contributes more.
