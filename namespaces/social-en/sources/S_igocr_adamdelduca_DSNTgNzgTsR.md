How to Get 8 Hours of Work Done In 4 Hours (Seriously) >>
<!-- image 1 -->
By the numbers:
The average office worker
checks their email 74 times
per day.
The average person picks up
their phone 352 times per day.
The average American spends
close to 2.5 hours on social
media per day.
Most people don't lack time.
They lack focus.
<!-- image 2 -->
1. Use the 3-3-3 Method
From Oliver Burkeman:
- 3 hours of deep work
- 3 shorter tasks
- 3 maintenance/admin tasks
That's it. That's your day.
You'll get more done by doing
less — on purpose.
<!-- image 3 -->
2. Timeblock like a maniac
The best productivity hack I've
found.
Allocate specific time slots for
your tasks throughout the day.
Put them on your calendar and
stick to them.
<!-- image 4 -->
3. Set absurdly clear and specific goals
Instead of: "Work on pitch deck"
Try: "Draft 5 slides with bullet points"
Clarity removes the intimidation factor of starting a task.
<!-- image 5 -->
4. Banish distractions
Put your phone out of reach.
Turn off notifications.
Close extra tabs.
You don't suck at focusing —
you've just built an
environment that makes it feel
impossible.
<!-- image 6 -->
5. Use a timer (seriously)
If you want to lock in, set a timer.
I do 45 minutes on, 10 minutes off.
There's real power in committing time to one task and seeing it through.
<!-- image 7 -->
6. Eliminate, Automate, and Delegate
My rules of thumb:
The cost of a task outweighs the benefit.
→ Eliminate it.
If it's repetitive and low effort.
→ Automate it.
If it's not the best use of your time and can be done better by someone else.
→ Delegate it.
Your mileage may vary.
