1. Verify the offer before you take the calls

Most reps never check the offer before they take the calls. In remote closing the company books the calls on your calendar, so your job is picking an offer worth taking them for. Paste this to Claude before you say yes to any offer:

I'm a sales rep with [YEARS] years of experience evaluating a remote closing role for a [INSERT OFFER, e.g. a business coaching program] priced at [INSERT PRICE] commission per close against ticket size, how the leads are generated and whether the calendar is booked for me, realistic close rates for this offer type, refund and clawback risk, sales cycle length, and the biggest reasons reps fail on offers like this. Find whether you'd take this role today and the five questions I should ask the offer owner before I do.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. Reverse engineer what's already closing

Know the method before you copy it.
Reda sold 1,582 cars in one year without pressuring a single buyer, and most of them came back or sent someone. Paste this to Claude:

Reverse engineer how Ali Reda sells cars. Break his method into what he actually says and does at each stage of a sale: first contact, discovery, presentation, objections, the close, and follow up. Use only the lines and habits he has documented in interviews and profiles, and explain why each one calms a nervous buyer. Then rewrite every line for a [INSERT OFFER] Zoom call. No generic sales advice. Base every line on what he actually does.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. Build your objection bank

Here's the follow up prompt. Run it in the same chat:

Based on that breakdown, list the 20 objections a prospect is most likely to raise on a call for [INSERT OFFER]. For each one, write the response Ali Reda would give, adapted to my offer, in plain words a real person would say out loud. Then rank the 5 objections that kill the most deals and write a full word for word response for each, ending with the one question I ask next to keep the call moving.

4. Run the call before it's real

Twenty objections and 5 full responses are
now sitting in your chat. Read the 5 out
loud until they sound like you. Then paste
this:

Play a skeptical [INSERT WHO THE OFFER SELLS TO] on a
sales call for [INSERT OFFER]. Raise the objections one at a
time and don't let me off easy. When we're done, grade the call,
tell me the exact moment you stopped trusting me, and show
me what Ali Reda would have said there instead.
