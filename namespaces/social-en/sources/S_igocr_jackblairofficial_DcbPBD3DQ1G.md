1. "TELL ME WHERE I LOST YOU."
The researchers called the first trait empathy: the ability to feel the buyer's reaction as it happens and correct mid-sentence.
Ask the question the moment a call cools. It converts your monologue into telemetry and tells the buyer their signal matters more than your script.
Great reps steer by the buyer's face, not the deck's order.

3. "I WANT THIS DEAL. HERE'S WHY IT'S GOOD
FOR BOTH OF US."
The second trait, ego-drive, is the personal need to
win the close, for the conquest more than the
commission. The researchers argued it barely trains
in from zero.
Stop hiding your wanting. Sandbagged desire reads
as indifference, and indifference kills more deals
than pushiness ever did. State the want with the
reason attached.
Buyers forgive hunger. They never forgive apathy.

4. "THAT NO IS INFORMATION. WATCH WHAT I DO WITH IT."
Their sharpest finding: empathy without drive produces a loving rep who never closes. Drive without empathy produces a bulldozer who closes once.
After a rejection, run the pair: read exactly why the no happened, then let the drive book the next step anyway. One trait diagnoses. The other keeps the file open.
Diagnosis plus appetite is the whole profession.

5. "WHICH HALF AM I TODAY?"
The study was written for hiring managers. Its real
use is the mirror: every rep leans one way under
pressure.
Audit your last five losses. Too much empathy reads
as agreeing with the no. Too much drive reads as
deafness. Label the loss. Too much drive each loss and your training plan
writes itself.
Losses cluster around the trait you're missing.

6. "BEFORE I ANSWER, TELL ME WHAT'S BEHIND
THAT QUESTION."
The empathic read on an objection: the words are
the surface, and the worry underneath decides. The
study's great closers answered the worry.
Buy one beat before every rebuttal. The question
behind the question is usually cheaper to solve than
the one they asked.
Answer the worry and the objection files itself.

08 / 10
7. "TRAIN THE WEAK HALF ON PURPOSE."
They tested veterans who kept failing and rookies
who flew. In their testing, experience predicted
nothing. The two traits that predicted almost
everything.
Empathy trains with tape: replay one call a week
hunting for reads you missed. Drive trains with
stakes: public commitments, scoreboards, asks you
cannot quietly skip. One hour a week on the weak
half compounds.
The best rep you have ever met built the half they
were missing.
