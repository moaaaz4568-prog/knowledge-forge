1. "I hear you and I want to find a way to make this work. What I cannot do is move on the number."

Vic Mackey always separated what he was willing to do from what he was not
and he communicated that boundary with complete clarity and zero apology.

This phrase signals warmth and firmness simultaneously and the buyer feels both.
The buyer who was using relationship language to soften the price suddenly finds a boundary they cannot move emotionally.
You acknowledged the relationship. You held the number. Both things happened in the same sentence.
Hold the number with warmth. The buyer who cannot move it emotionally almost always accepts it logically.

2. "I understand you feel that way. My position has not changed."

Vic never let someone else's emotional state become his obligation to resolve through concession.
This phrase is the cleanest boundary available in any negotiation.

Four words. No explanation. No justification. No invitation to try again from a different angle.
The buyer who has been using frustration or disappointment as leverage suddenly has nothing left to push against.
The rep who does not fill that silence with a discount almost always keeps the price.

Say it once. Mean it completely. Do not add anything after it.

3. "I am not going to apologise for the value I bring. What I will do is show you exactly what that value looks like."

Vic Mackey never apologised for being effective and he never let anyone make him feel like he should.
This phrase does the same thing in a sales conversation without a single defensive word.

The buyer who was using the price as a character attack suddenly finds themself in a value demonstration instead of a negotiation.
You did not defend. You redirected to proof.
Proof is always more persuasive than justification and it never sounds desperate.
Redirect to proof when they attack the price. Proof closes what justification only prolongs.

4. "I want to be straight with you. The guilt trip is not going to change the number. But I am happy to talk about what will."

Vic always named the tactic before the person using it could weaponise it further.
This phrase does the same thing with enough directness to stop the tactic cold and enough openness to keep the conversation alive.

The buyer who was applying emotional leverage suddenly finds it named and neutralised in one sentence.
They either pivot to a real conversation or reveal that the guilt was the only move they had.
Either way you are now in control of where the deal goes next.

Name the tactic. The buyer who hears it named almost never uses it again in the same conversation.

5. "We have worked together for a long time. That is exactly why I am being straight with you about what I can and cannot do."

Vic Mackey always used relationship history as a reason for honesty not a reason for capitulation.
This phrase flips the buyer's use of the relationship as leverage into your use of it as transparency.

The buyer who invoked loyalty to get a discount suddenly receives loyalty delivered as directness instead.
You are not dismissing the relationship. You are honouring it in the only way that actually respects it.
The relationship that survives honest negotiation is always stronger than the one built on concessions nobody meant to make.

Use the relationship to justify honesty not complacency. It closes better and lasts longer.

6. "I am not going to make a decision under pressure that I am going to regret when the pressure is gone."

This is Vic Mackey's operating philosophy translated into a sales boundary and it is the most honest close on this list. It signals self-respect and strategic thinking in a single sentence.

The buyer who was applying pressure suddenly understands that the pressure is not working and never will. They either move to a genuine conversation about what works for both parties or they confirm that pressure was their only play. A buyer with no other play is a buyer who is about to accept the terms you already set.

Signal that pressure does not move you. The buyer who discovers this early stops wasting time and starts negotiating honestly.

7. "I think we can get this done. But it needs to work for both of us or it does not work at all."

Vic Mackey never accepted a deal that only served the other side. This phrase establishes the non-negotiable condition of any close with complete calm and complete certainty.

The buyer who was trying to extract a one-sided concession suddenly has to consider what works for you as well. That consideration is the beginning of a real negotiation rather than a one-sided extraction.

The deal that works for both parties is the only one worth closing and the only one that holds after the signature.

Make mutual benefit the condition. The buyer who accepts it is the buyer worth closing.
