1. "Help me understand what is driving that concern because I want to make sure I am solving the right problem."

Most reps hear an objection and answer over the objection. Littlefinger always answered the person, not the words they chose to say out loud.

This phrase buys you the intelligence every close actually requires. The buyer stops performing their hesitation and starts explaining their real position. Now you are not pitching against a decoy. You are closing against the truth. This phrase finds the rest of the story.

The objection is never the whole story.

2. "I get the sense there is something sitting underneath this that we have not talked about yet. Am I reading that right?"

This is the most disarming question available on any stalled call.
It signals that you see more than you are being shown, without accusation, without pressure.

The buyer who hears this almost always confirms it.
And what they say next is the most valuable information on the call,
the real reason the deal has not moved and the only thing that will move it.

Read between the lines out loud. The buyer will fill in what they find there.

3. "What would closing this deal actually mean for you personally, not just for the business?"

Littlefinger understood that every decision has a personal dimension. The professional language hides. Career protection. Internal credibility. the people watching. The need to look right in front of the business making the decision.

This phrase reaches past the business case and finds the human being. The buyer who answers honestly gives you the close nobody else in the market was even looking for. Personal stakes. Logical arguments never could.

Find the personal win. Build your close around it. Watch the deal move.

4. "If the [price, timing, resource] concern was not there, is this something you would want to move forward with?"

This phrase is a surgical diagnostic tool dressed as a simple question.
It isolates the real blocker from the stated blocker in a single sentence.

If they say yes, you have confirmed the objection is the only thing standing between you and the close.
If they say no, you have found the real reason for the first time.
Either answer moves you forward. Neither answer leaves you guessing.

Isolate the true objection. Everything else is a distraction you cannot afford to chase.

5. "I want to share something I noticed about how you have described this situation because I think it matters."

Littlefinger always used observation as leverage, not in a threatening way, in an illuminating one. This phrase signals that you have been listening at a level the buyer did not expect.

What follows it must be real, specific, and genuinely insightful about their situation.
When it lands correctly the buyer stops feeling managed and starts feeling understood.
A buyer who feels understood does not go looking for a different conversation.

Observe precisely. Reflect accurately. They feel seen.
The close follows the moment they feel seen.

6. "I am not going to pretend I know exactly what you need. But based on what you have told me, here is what I think is really going on."

This phrase positions you as a diagnostician instead of a salesperson.
Littlefinger always reframed the situation before anyone else had finished defining it.

The buyer stops defending their position because you have just offered a more accurate one. They evaluate your read instead of their objection. And a buyer who agrees with your diagnosis almost always accepts your prescription.

Reframe the situation before they finish building walls around the wrong one.

7. "I think the real question is not whether this works, It is whether you are ready to be the person who makes this call."

This phrase is Littlfinger at his most precise. It shifts the entire conversation from product evaluation to personal decision-making.

The buyer who was hiding behind process and policy suddenly sees the decision for what it is. A choice about them in this moment, not a spreadsheet exercise. That reframe closes deals that every rational argument in your arsenal could not touch.

Make it personal. Not manipulative. Personal. The close lives in that distinction.
