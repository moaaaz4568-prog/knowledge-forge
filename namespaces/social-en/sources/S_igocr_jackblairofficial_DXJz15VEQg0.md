1. "Let me ask you something. What does another ninety days of this actually cost you?"

Most reps create urgency by talking about what the buyer will gain.
Roma knew the sharper edge is always what they continue to lose.

This phrase does not push. It invites the buyer to do the maths themselves.
By the time they finish answering, the cost of waiting is their number, not yours.
You never applies the pressure. They felt it entirely on their own.

The close that lands hardest is always the one the buyer builds themselves.

2. "I am not going to tell you this is a limited time offer. I am going to tell you what changes next month."

Fake urgency is the most expensive mistake a rep can make.
Buyers have heard every version of it and they are armed against all of them.

This phrase signals honesty and then delivers a real consequence. It earns the trust that makes what comes next land with full weight.
Real urgency does not need a countdown. It just needs to be true. Truth closes faster than tactics every time.

Tell them what is actually changing. Truth closes faster than tactics every time.

3. "You told me earlier that this has been a problem for over a year. What makes right now different from six months ago?"

Ricky Roma listened to everything a buyer said and used it at exactly the right moment.
This phrase does the same thing. It plays their own words back to them.

The buyer admitted the problem is old. You are simply asking why they are still living with it. They have no clean answer. The silence after this question is the most productive silence on any call. Let it sit. Do not fill it. The close lives in that pause.

The best urgency a buyer feels is shame at their own delay.

4. "The people I work with who waited always tell me the same thing afterward. I wish I had done this sooner."

This phrase works because it removes you from the pressure entirely.
You are not pushing. You are reporting. The urgency belongs to someone else's story.

The buyer hears a pattern they do not want to become part of.
Nobody wants to be the person who waited and regretted it.
Roma never needed to ask for the close. He just made the alternative feel worse.

A story about someone else's regret is always more powerful than your best argument.

5. "If nothing changes today, where does this sit in three months?"

This is the quietest close on the list and the most devastating.

They paint the picture. They feel the weight of it.
You did not create the urgency. You just handed them the brush.
Roma understand that a buyer who imagines their own pain closes without being asked.
Give them the future. Let them decide they do not want it.
