1. "You're not buying a Chevy. You're buying me."

Joe Girard sold the same cars as every other guy on the floor. Same stickers, same inventory, same Detroit.

He figured out the product was never the product. The customer was deciding on the human, then letting the car justify it.

Stop selling features against a competitor's. Sell the fact that YOU pick up the phone after the money moves. Nobody else on the call offers that.

2. "I like you."

Every month, Girard mailed 13,000 hand-signed cards to every customer he’d ever had. Twelve times a year. The message inside was three words: I like you.

No pitch. No offer. Just presence. When anyone in that stack needed a car, there was exactly one salesman in their head.

Follow up when there’s nothing to sell. The rep who only shows up at deal time trains the buyer to see a wallet inspector coming.

3. "Who do you know that I should take care of the same way?"

Girard's Law of 250. He noticed weddings and funerals of about 250 people, which meant average about 250 people stood in front of him 250 more.

Treat one person badly and 250 people hear the story. Treat them right and the room refills itself.

Make the referral ask on every closed deal, framed as taking care of their people. Skipping this line is declining free pipeline out of shyness.

4. "Something will go wrong eventually. When it does, you call me, not a hotline."

Girard picked up the tab on minor repairs and fought to get his customers' cars serviced first. The sale was the start of the relationship, not the end of it.

Buyers aren't afraid of price. They're afraid of being abandoned after the check clears. Kill that fear and price objections shrink on their own.

Promise the after, out loud, specifically. It costs nothing on the call and it's the thing they're actually deciding on.

5. "Let me give you the real number, no games."

Girard sold in the most distrusted profession in America. He knew every buyer walked in braced for the trick.

So he took the trick off the table first. When the game they're expecting never shows up, the guard drops, and a guarded buyer never signs anything.

Name the fear in the room before they do. The fastest way past a defense is to make it unnecessary.

6. "Tell me about them."

Girard told the story of a sale he lost because the customer had been bragging about his son getting into medical school, and Joe wasn't really listening. The man bought elsewhere and told him exactly why.

He never forgot it. People rarely leave over price. They leave over feeling unheard for one sentence.

When the buyer mentions their kid, their move, their deal, stop. Follow it. The close is on the other side of that detour.

7. "Send me somebody and I'll make it worth your while."

Girard built a paid army. Barbers, bankers, mechanics, anyone who sent him a buyer got cash when the deal closed. Hundreds of people selling for him while he sat at his desk.

He never ate lunch with the other salesmen. While they stood in a circle complaining, his network was out hunting.

Your closed buyers and contacts are an unpaid sales force waiting for an offer. Put a bounty on introductions and watch what shows up.
