1. Do you have five minutes for me, in person?

Bohns and her colleague found a request made face to face was about 34 times more effective than the same request over email.

People are far more willing to say yes in person than on a screen, and far worse at saying no to your face.

Whenever the deal matters, get on video or in the room. Do not let email do your asking.

Bohns' research shows we badly underestimate how likely people are to help when we simply ask.

5. Most people say yes to this. Would that work for you?

Her research found people comply far more often than the asker predicts, because refusing feels socially costly.

Gently signaling that yes is the normal answer makes it easier for the buyer to give the one they were already leaning toward.

Name the norm, then invite them into it. You are lowering the cost of yes.

The title of Bohns' book is that you have more influence than you think, and the research backs it.
Reps shrink themselves on calls, assuming they have no leverage. That belief leaks into the pitch and the buyer feels it.
Walk in knowing your ask carries weight. Underestimating your influence is what costs you the deal.

7. If it's a no, just tell me straight.

Bohns found people find it surprisingly hard to say no, so they dodge, delay, and go quiet instead.

Giving the buyer clear permission to say no builds trust and flushes out the real answer faster than chasing a ghost.

Invite the honest no. It is worth more than a polite maybe that never closes.
