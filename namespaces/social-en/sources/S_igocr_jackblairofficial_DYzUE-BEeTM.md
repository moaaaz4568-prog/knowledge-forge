1. “Let me see this from where you are sitting.”

Pink found the best sellers are great at taking the buyer’s perspective.

He calls it attunement. Top performers step into the buyer’s chair and see the problem through their eyes. Get the buyer’s view and you bring back actually fits.

See it their way and you sell the right thing.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. “What is the problem you have not been able to solve yet?”

Pink found modern selling is about finding the problem, not just pitching the answer.

He learned buyers today have answers everywhere. What they lack is the real problem. Help them name it precisely and you become the one who showed them what to fix.

Find the problem and you own the solution.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
3. "Can I help this person? Yes."
Pink found top sellers prep with a
question, not a pep talk.
He calls it interrogative self-talk. Instead of
hyping yourself before a call, ask whether
you can genuinely help, then answer it.
The honest yes builds a steadier
confidence than any chant.
Ask if you can, then go prove it.

@jackblairofficial
4. “A no today does not mean a no forever.”
Pink found buoyancy, the skill of staying afloat through rejection, separates the best.
He learned a no is rarely permanent and rarely personal. Treat it as one moment, moving. The seller who recovers fast is the one still standing at the close.
Take the no lightly and keep your feet.

5. “Here is who this is really for, and it may not be you.”
Pink found honesty about fit makes a seller more trusted, not less.
He showed that today's buyer can check everything you say. So be straight about who the offer suits and who it does not. That candor suits and who it Is exactly why they trust your yes when it comes.
Honest about the fit, trusted on the close.

6. "How would solving this change your actual week?"

Pink found people buy when the purpose behind the purchase is clear.

He learned that buyers move for meaning, not features. Connect the offer to something that matters in their real life and the decision stops being a cost. It becomes a reason.

Give the purpose and the price gets quiet.

Jack Blair

7. "Let us make this about you, not the product."

Pink found selling works when it serves the buyer first.

His whole case is that selling, done right, is helping someone move forward. Aim the conversation at the buyer's outcome instead of your offer, and you stop pushing and start being useful.

Serve the person and the sale takes care of itself.
