1. Wartime mode is a different operator than peacetime mode.

In peacetime, the rep can build slowly. In wartime, every call carries weight.

The closers who win in slow markets aren't running the playbook from when leads were everywhere. They switched modes the day the market did.

Horowitz wrote that most management books teach the peacetime. Almost none teach the only mode that matters when you're 90 days from broke.

2. The shit sandwich kills more deals than honesty ever does.

The rep who softens bad news with two compliments before the truth gets caught every time. Buyers feel the setup coming.

Horowitz tried it once on a senior employee. She cut him off mid and said "spare me. Just tell me what I did wrong."

That moment ended his career as a polite communicator.

3. The Struggle isn't a sign you're failing.
It's the entry fee.

Every closer hits months where the pipeline dies, the calls go cold, and the doubt arrives uninvited.

Most reps treat that month as proof they don't belong. Horowitz treated it as proof they finally do.

The Struggle is where reps quit and closers compound. Same month, two different exits.

4. Tell the bad news first.

The rep who hides the offer's biggest weakness loses the deal the moment the buyer finds it on their own.

The closer who names it first disarms the entire objection cycle. Trust transfers immediately. The rest of the call runs at a different temperature.

Horowitz raised $350 million by being the founder who never softened the slide.

value forever.

The closer who builds the relationship first
closes the same deal plus the next 4 the
buyer refers in.

customer as the asset that compounds.

6. Best is the enemy of better.

The rep who waits to perfect the pitch never runs it. The closer who runs the 70% pitch 100 times more than the rep running the 95% pitch 10 times closes more.

Volume of attempts beats elegance of approach.

Horowitz rebuilt Opsware in public, on a public company filing, with a 35-cent stock price. Imperfect at scale beat perfect in theory.

7. Hold the line on price.

Every buyer pushes once to test if the rep flinches. The closer who holds gets the price. The rep who flinches teaches the buyer the price was never real.

HP came in at $13.50 to test if Horowitz would bend. He held at $14. They came back at $14.25.

The 25 cents wasn't the lesson. The hold was.
