1. "What's the first thing they're going to ask you when you bring this up?"

Allport and Postman ran messages down human chains and watched them warp toward whatever the listener already believed. The partner's objection already exists before your pitch arrives. Pull it into this call, where you're present to answer it, instead of there you're not.

2. "Before you go, give me your version. How would you explain this to them?"
Their finding: the first retelling sets the shape of every retelling after it. So make the buyer rehearse it in front of you. You'll hear exactly which details died and which got mangled, and you get to fix the message before it ships.
The first retelling decides all the others.

3. "Don't try to retell the whole thing. Just bring them these three numbers."
Seventy percent of details die in transit, so stop sending a pitch that needs all of them to survive. Hand over a payload built for the chain: three concrete numbers and one sentence. Nuance dies between here and their dinner table. Numbers make it home.
Nuance dies in transit. Numbers survive.

4. "When the price comes up, here's the sentence that goes with it."
Sharpening was their name for it: one detail outlives the rest and becomes the whole story. In your deal, that surviving detail is always the number. Never let the price travel alone. Weld it to the one line that justifies it, or the partner hears a cost with no case.
A price that travels alone arrives twice as heavy.

5. "What happened the last time you brought them something like this?"

Assimilation: the message bends toward what the listener already believes. Your pitch won't be heard fresh, it'll be heard through the last thing that burned them. Find out what that was, because that's the ghost your offer is actually negotiating with tonight.

6. "What would they need to hear from me directly, not secondhand?"

The chain only distorts what passes through it. This question makes the buyer name the exact piece too heavy to carry, and it plants the obvious conclusion: some of this shouldn't be retold at all. They start selling themselves on the three-way call.

Your best pitch is worthless delivered by an amateur.
