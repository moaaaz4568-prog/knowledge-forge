3. "I am not going to tell you everything is fine with your current approach because I do not think it is."

Baum never validated a position he believed was wrong just to keep the room comfortable. He called it and took the consequences and the people who listened to him made money.

This phrase is the most disarming thing a buyer hears from a rep who is supposed to be selling. The buyer who gets a challenge instead and challenges from credible sources land like nothing else. The rep who tells the truth when it is inconvenient is always the rep they remember.

Challenge the current approach when you genuinely see a better one. The room that argues is the room that is listening.

4. "The reason most people in your position are not solving this is not budget or timing. It is that nobody has shown them what it actually costs to ignore it."

Baum always traced the real problem back to its actual source rather than accepting the surface explanation. This phrase does the same thing for a buyer who has been using budget or timing as a comfortable reason to stay still.

You are not attacking their excuse. You are elevating the conversation past it.

The buyer who hears their real obstacle named accurately feels seen in a way that moves them faster than any feature demonstration.

Named truth closes what polished pitches leave open.

Name the real reason they have not moved. The buyer who recognises it has nowhere left to hide.

5. "I want to show you the numbers on this because I think when you see them the decision becomes a lot simpler."

Baum always brought the data when the room was operating on assumption and comfortable fiction. This phrase signals that what follows is not opinion but evidence and evidence changes rooms that arguments cannot move.

The buyer who was deliberating on instinct suddenly has a concrete basis for the decision they were stalled on. Numbers remove the subjectivity from a close and subjectivity is where delay lives. The rep who brings the right data at the right moment closes deals that were stalled purely on uncertainty.

Bring the numbers. The buyer who sees the evidence almost always stops deliberating and starts deciding.

7. "I could tell you what you want to hear. That is not why you should work with me."

This is Baum's philosophy in a single sentence and the most powerful positioning statement available on any call. It signals that your value is not in your agreeableness. It is in your accuracy.

This phrase does not close the deal. It makes the buyer decide they want to be closed by someone like you. And that decision is always worth more than any technique that follows it.

Position yourself as the truth in a market full of comfortable lies. The buyer always remembers who told them.
