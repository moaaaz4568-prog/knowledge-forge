1. "Help me understand your situation before I say anything."

Cohen taught that information is one of the three levers of every negotiation.

He believed the side that knows more controls the room. Ask early, ask wide, and listen. Every fact a buyer hands you about budget, pressure, and timing becomes a tool you can close with.

The one who knows more, wins.

3. "What is driving the timing on your end?"

Cohen taught that time and deadlines quietly decide most negotiations.

He knew most concessions happen near a deadline. Find out what clock the buyer is on, financial, personal, seasonal, and you can read when the pressure will tip them toward yes.

Whoever owns the clock owns the deal.

4. "Wow. That number is a real stretch for what we are doing."
Cohen taught the flinch, an honest visible reaction to a demand.
When a buyer pushes on price, do not absorb it smoothly. React. A genuine pause makes them question their own ask, and often then they soften it before you be more word.
A flinch can move a number on its own.
<!-- image 4 -->

@jackblairofficial

5. “I can do that for you, if you can do this for me.”

Cohen taught that a concession should never be given for free.

He found a free giveaway gets no respect and invites another ask. Trade instead. Every time you bend, get something back, and the buyer starts treating the deal as earned.

Never give. Always trade.

6. “Take the time you need. I am not going anywhere.”

Cohen prized patience as a weapon most people are too anxious to use.

He believed the restless negotiator loses. Silence makes amateurs nervous and they fill the gap with concessions. Sit in it calmly, let the buyer talk first, and let the quiet work.

The patient one rarely pays full price.

7. “Everything here can be worked out. Where should we start?”

Cohen built a bestseller on one belief, that you can negotiate anything.

He taught that almost nothing is truly fixed. Price, terms, timing, scope, it is all movable if you treat it that way. Walk in believing the deal is open and the buyer believes it too.

Treat it as negotiable and it becomes negotiable.
