1. "Help me help you."

Jerry's client is tanking his own deal, and Jerry stops pitching, stops managing, and says the five most honest words in the movie.

The line works because it flips the roles. The buyer stops being a target you're working and becomes a partner in their own outcome.

When a deal stalls, stop pushing and recruit them. "Help me help you the way here?" Resistance can't survive being a teammate.

2. "Show me the money."

Rod Tidwell refuses to let Jerry hide behind agent-speak. He makes him say the real thing, out loud, screaming it down a phone line.

Most calls die in politeness. Both sides dance around the number for forty minutes and call it rapport.

Get the money on the table early and without apology. The rep who's comfortable saying the number is the one the buyer trusts with it.

3. "The things we think and do not say."

That's the title of the memo that got Jerry fired. Twenty-five pages of what everyone in the building knew and nobody would put in writing.

Every sales call has one of these. The thing you both know: they're going to shop it around, the budget isn't really the issue, the spouse hasn't signed off.

Say it first. "Can I name what I think is really going on?" The unsaid thing somebody says it. Make sure it's you.

5. "The key to this business is personal relationships."

Dicky Fox, Jerry's mentor, says it straight to camera. It's the oldest line in the movie and the only one nobody argues with.

AI can quote a price. A CRM can send a follow-up. Neither one can be the person the buyer calls first when something matters.

Know the kid's name. Remember the thing they said in minute four. That's not soft skills, that's the moat.

6. "I will not rest until I have you holding a Coke, wearing your own shoe, playing a Sega game featuring you."

Jerry doesn't promise Rod "great representation." He paints the exact scene of Rod's win, brand names included.

Vague promises bounce off buyers because they can't picture them. Specific ones move in and start paying rent in their head.

Stop selling "results." Describe the Tuesday six months from now when the problem is gone. Detail is what belief is made of.

7. "The kwon."

Rod's word for the whole package: love, respect, community, and the dollars too. Not just the contract. Everything the contract stands for.

Buyers are never only buying the thing. They're buying what having it says about them, and who they get to be after.

Close for the kwon. Name what the yes means for their life, not just their invoice, and price stops being the conversation.
