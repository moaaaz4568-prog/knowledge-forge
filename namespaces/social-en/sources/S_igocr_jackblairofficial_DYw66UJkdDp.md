DANIEL KAHNEMAN WON THE NOBEL PRIZE FOR PROVING PEOPLE DO NOT DECIDE WITH LOGIC, THEY DECIDE ON EMOTION. HERE ARE THE 7 PHRASES HIS RESEARCH PROVED CLOSE:
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "Here is what staying exactly where you are will cost you."

Kahneman's Nobel work proved a loss hurts about twice as much as a gain feels good.

He called it loss aversion. People will move to stop a loss long before they will move to chase a win. Frame the cost of doing nothing and the decision suddenly has weight.

Fear of losing outpulls hope of gaining.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. “Most people invest somewhere between this and this.”

Kahneman proved the first number a buyer hears shapes every number after it.

He called it anchoring. Whatever figure lands first quietly sets the frame. Place a real, high anchor early and your actual price stops sounding expensive. It starts sounding reasonable.

Set the anchor or the buyer sets it for you.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "Think of it as a few dollars a day."

Kahneman showed the same fact lands differently depending on how it is framed.

A buyer can choke on a yearly figure and shrug at a daily one, even when the math is identical. The number did not change. The frame did, and the frame is what they feel.

Same price, different feeling, different answer.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. “How does this sit with you so far?”

Kahneman proved decisions run on fast emotion long before slow logic catches up.

He called it System 1. People feel the yes or no first, then build reasons for it. Check the feeling in the room early, because if it is off, no spreadsheet will save the close.

Win the gut and the head follows.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
5. “Most people in your spot just go with this one.”
Kahneman found people lean hard toward whatever feels like the default.
Every extra choice is friction, and friction stalls decisions. Name the option people like them usually pick and you have made saying yes the path of least effort.
The easy choice is the chosen choice.
<!-- image 6 -->

6. "By this time next month, here is where you will be."

Kahneman's peak-end research showed people judge a decision by how it ends.

A buyer is not weighing today. They are imagining the finish. Paint a clear, vivid ending where the problem is solved, and that picture is what they reach for when they decide.

Sell the ending and the start sells itself.

7. "You will know exactly what happens next, step by step."
Kahneman proved people will pay real money to remove uncertainty.
A vague process feels like risk, and risk freezes buyers. Lay out precisely what comes after yes, in order, with no surprises. Certainty is part of what they are buying.
Clear the fog and the yes walks through.
