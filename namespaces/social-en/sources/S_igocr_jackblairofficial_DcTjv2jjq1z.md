1. "HERE'S WHAT HAPPENS NEXT."
Pope never enters a crisis describing how bad it is. She enters sequencing it: first this, then this, you, do that.
When your deal hits a fire, an outage, a legal redline, a champion suddenly gone, the buyer is not waiting for your apology. They are watching to see if anyone owns the first sentence.
Panic spreads. So does sequence.

3. "I NEED TWO THINGS FROM YOU. THEN STOP CARRYING THIS."
Gladiators get assignments. Pope splo does what by when, and takes the disaster into whens off the client.
Give the panicking buyer a job, small, concrete and theirs. Then explicitly take the rest. Anxiety without a task turns into escalation emails at midnight.
A tasked buyer calms down. An idle one calls your boss.

4. "WE'VE SEEN THIS ONE BEFORE."
Half her power is pattern memory. The scandal walks in believing it is unprecedented. She has three files that say otherwise.
Keep your own files. Every implementation fire, every stalled legal review, every mid-deal exit you have survived is a precedent. Cite one, with its ending, the moment trouble starts.
Precedent is the fastest tranquilizer in business.

5. "THAT'S TOMORROW'S PROBLEM. TODAY WE FIX THIS."
In every episode the client wants to litigate how it happened while the fire is still burning. She refuses the autopsy until the patient is stable.
Buyers do it too: mid-crisis they want to renegotiate the whole relationship. Park it, out loud, with a date. Triage first, retrospective second.
You cannot run the post-mortem and the surgery at the same time.

6. “YOU’LL HEAR FROM ME AT FOUR, EITHER WAY.”
The scariest sound in a crisis is no news. Pope’s clients never sit in: the update arrives when promised, even when the update is nothing yet.
Set an update cadence the moment something breaks, and honor it when there is nothing to report. The call that says maybe they quit beats the gap that says nothing.
On-time bad news builds more trust than late good news.

7. “THIS DEAL ISN’T DYING. IT’S MID-STORY.”
Fixers exist because powerful people make a mistake a bad chapter for the ending. Pope’s job is holding the frame until the facts catch up.
Deals are the same. Procurement fights, security reviews, budget freezes: every big close has a chapter that looks like the end. The rep who narrates it as a step keeps the buyer walking.
Buyers borrow their confidence from yours. Stock up.
