MICHAEL CORLEONE NEGOTIATED EMPIRES WITHOUT EVER SHOWING HIS HAND. THESE ARE THE 7 PHRASES HE USED TO HOLD HIS PRICE WHEN BUYERS PUSHED BACK:
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "I understand what you are asking for. That is not something I am able to move on."

Most reps hear a demand and immediately start calculating how much to give away. That calculation is visible. The buyer sees it and asks for more.

This phrase closes the door on concession without anger, without apology, without explanation. It is calm. It is final. It is the sound of someone who decided this before the call started. Michael Corleone never negotiated terms he had already settled in his own mind.

Decide your line before the call. Then hold it like it was never in question.

2. "I want this to work for both of us. Here is what I can do, and here is where I need to stay firm."

Corleone always gave something small to protect something large.
That is not weakness. That is architecture.

This phrase signals goodwill while drawing a boundary the buyer can feel but cannot move. You are not stonewalling. You are structuring. The buyer feels heard and respected, and the number stays exactly where you put it.

Give on the things that cost you nothing. Hold on the things that cost you everything.

3. "I hear you on the price. Walk me through what you are comparing us to."

When a buyer says it is too expensive, most reps defend the price immediately.
Defending without understanding is the fastest way to lose the frame entirely.

This phrase buys you intelligence before you spend any position.
Nine times out of ten, the comparison is flawed, incomplete, or based on something entirely different.
Now you are not defending your price. You are correcting their maths.

Never argue about price until you know what price they are actually arguing about.

4. "If I move on the number, something else in the agreement has to reflect that. What would you like to adjust?"

Michael Corleone never gave anything for free.
Every concession had a cost attached. Every move had a counter.

This phrase keeps the value of your offer intact while appearing completely reasonable.
The buyer wanted a discount. Now they are restructuring a contract instead.
Most will pause, reconsider, and decide the original terms were fine.

Never discount. Trade. There is a significant difference between the two.

5. "I am not going to rush you. I do want to be transparent about what holds at this price and what does not."

Corleone understood that information, released at the right moment, shifts the entire room. This phrase uses transparency as leverage without ever sounding like a threat.

The buyer suddenly understands that waiting has a structural cost, not just a time cost. They are no longer negotiating against you. They are negotiating against the terms themselves. That is a completely different conversation, and one you are already winning.

Control the information. Control the negotiation.

6. "We have worked hard to get to this point. I would hate to see us lose it over something we can solve right now."

This phrase protects the deal without protecting the ego.
It reminds the buyer how much ground you have already covered together.
Sunk cost is real and it is ethical to name it when it is true. The buyer who remembers every conversation, every meeting, every version of this negotiation does not want to start over any more than you do.

Remind them what walking away actually costs. Do it with care. Do it once.

7. "I respect that you need to think about it. I want you to know the terms we discussed are specific to this conversation."

Michael Corleone never left an offer open indefinitely. Every agreement had a context and that context had edges.

This phrase closes the time window without an ultimatum.

It is not a threat. It is information delivered with complete closure.
The buyer who was planning to stall suddenly understands that the deal they want exists right now, not next week.

Scarcity does not need a deadline. It just needs to be real.
