3. "This is a business call. Do you have a quick minute?"

While other agents disguised why they were calling, Ferry said it outright.

Naming it as a business call respects the person's time and removes the suspicion that makes people hang up, so the honest opener gets more minutes than the clever one. Candor disarms.

Tell them exactly what the call is and ask permission to continue.

4. "When do you plan on interviewing

someone good, which positions him as
that someone without a hard pitch.

Frame yourself as the obvious next choice,

5. "I'm doing a survey of the homes for sale by owner in the area."

Ferry approached for-sale-by-owners as a researcher, not a closer.

Leading with a survey lowers the wall a FSBO throws up at every agent, and the questions that follow surface exactly why they are struggling to sell alone. Curiosity opens doors.

Open as someone gathering information, then let the answers make your case.

6. "I just sold a home nearby. Do you know anyone else thinking of selling?"

Ferry turned every sale into the start of the next one.

A fresh nearby sale is proof he can perform, and asking for referrals while the news is hot returns one closing into a stream of listings on the same street. Momentum is a tool, not a feeling.

Mine every win for the next three leads before it cools.

7. "If I could show you how to get it sold, would you list with me today?"

Ferry tied the close to a simple condition the seller could say yes to.

The "if I could, would you" frame gets a commitment in principle first, so once he proves the how, the listing is already half agreed. The yes comes before the proof.

Lock the agreement in principle, then go earn it.
