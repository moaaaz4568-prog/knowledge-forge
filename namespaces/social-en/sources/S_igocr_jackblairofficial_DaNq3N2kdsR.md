1. Appeal to their self-interest, never your need.

Greene's 13th law. Walk in needing the sale and they feel it and pull back. Frame the entire conversation around what they walk away with and the pressure flips. People don't move for your quota. They move for their own win.

Nobody acts on your need. They act on theirs.

2. Always say less than necessary.

Law 4. The amateur fills every silence and talks himself out of the deal. State the price, then stop. Let it sit. The quiet feels unbearable to you and twice as heavy to them.

The one who speaks next loses the frame.

4. Win through actions, never through argument.

Law 9. You will never logic someone out of an objection. Stop debating it and show the thing instead. A walkthrough of how it played out for someone exactly like them ends the argument without a fight.

7. Know who you're dealing with before you say a word.

Greene's first rule of power is reading the person in front of you. Don't pitch into the dark. "Before I show you anything, walk me through where you're actually at." Then build around what they just handed you.

Diagnose before you prescribe, or you're just guessing.
