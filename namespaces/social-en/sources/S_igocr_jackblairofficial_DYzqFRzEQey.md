1. "One loyal customer is worth $332,000."

Sewell's famous calculation, the one that reframed his whole business.

He did the math on a car buyer who stays loyal for life: the cars, the service, the referrals. It came to $332,000. After that, no single sale felt small, and no customer felt disposable.

Price one customer over a lifetime and you stop treating any of them cheap.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "Ask customers what they want, then give it to them."

Sewell on the simplest system he ever built.

He stopped guessing what buyers wanted and just asked them, constantly, through surveys and conversation. Then he actually delivered it. Most businesses guess. He removed the guessing.

The answer was never a mystery. He just bothered to ask.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "Underpromise. Then overdeliver."

Sewell on managing expectations.

He quoted a longer time and a fair price, then beat both. The customer walked away feeling like they won. Promise big and barely deliver, and even a good job feels like a letdown.

Set the bar where you can clear it, then clear it by a mile.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. “A complaint is the best free information you will get.”

Sewell on the customer most businesses dread.

A complaining customer is telling you exactly what to fix, for free, instead of just leaving and never saying why. Sewell trained his people to treat that person as a gift, not a problem.

The angry customer is doing your market research for you
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. “Do it right the first time.”

Sewell on the real cost of mistakes.

Every redo costs twice: once to fix it, once in lost trust. He built systems so the work was right the first time, instead of staffing up inspectors to catch errors after the fact.

The cheapest work is the work you only do once.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Measure everything that matters."

Sewell on running on facts instead of feelings.

He tracked numbers most dealers ignored: how long a service took, how often a car came back, how many customers returned. You cannot improve what you refuse to measure, so he measured all of it.

Feelings lie. The numbers tell you where you actually stand.
