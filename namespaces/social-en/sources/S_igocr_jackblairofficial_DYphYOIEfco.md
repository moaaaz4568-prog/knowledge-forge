1. “There’s no such thing as a born salesperson.”

Friedman’s foundational belief.

There is no born doctor and no born lawyer either. Selling is a learned skill, full stop. Friedman built his whole company on that, because a skill can be coached and talent can’t.

It is a skill. That means you can get better starting today.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair

2. “Never open with anything the customer can answer in one word.”

Friedman on the opening line.

“Can I help you?” gets “no thanks, just looking” every single time. Friedman trained reps to open with something friendly, interesting, and nothing to do with selling, so the customer actually talked back.

A yes-or-no opener buys you a no. Ask something they have to answer.

3. “The opening has nothing to do with the merchandise.”
Friedman on lowering resistance.
The first job is to drop the customer’s guard, not pitch a product. A little genuine, personal conversation makes them see a person standing there. Then the wall comes down on its own.
Connect as a human first. The product talk gets easier after.

4. "Find out why they really want it."
Friedman on the reason behind the
purchase.
Not just what they are looking at. Who is it
for, what is the occasion, what does it
mean to them. The real reason is what lets
you match the right product to the right
person.
The why behind the buy is what tells you
what to sell them.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
5. "Always show the better item first."
Friedman on never assuming a budget.
Start high and let the customer step down
if they want to. Start low and you have
capped the sale before it began. Friedman
never decided a customer's budget for
them.
Show the best first. Let them be the one
to reach lower.

6. "Every sale should have an add-on attempt."

Friedman on the moment after yes.

A customer who just said yes is in a buying state of mind, and that is the easiest second sale you will ever get. A genuine, helpful add-on suggestion belongs right there.

The 30 seconds after yes is the warmest a buyer ever gets
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair

7. "Handle the objection. Then ask again."

Friedman on what happens after the first no.

The close does not end at the first no. You answer the real concern, confirm it is actually handled, confirmed, then calmly ask for the sale a second time. Most reps just stop at no.

Answer it, confirm it, ask again. The second ask wins deals.
