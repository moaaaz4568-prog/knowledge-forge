You have not pitched a product. You have made yourself the most interesting person on their calendar today. Everything else is just noise competing for the same crowded space.

2. "I want to tell you a story about a company exactly like yours that almost made this mistake."

Reddington understood that stories bypasses every defence a sceptical buyer has ever built.
Logic is evaluated. Stories are experienced. And experienced things close deals that arguments never could.

This phrase wraps your value proposition inside a narrative the buyer lives through rather than judges.
By the time the story ends they have already felt the consequence of not acting.
The close was never a pitch. It was a story that ended with only one sensible conclusion.

Tell the story that makes the decision feel inevitable. Then stop talking.

3. "Most people in your position say exactly what you just said. The ones who moved forward said something different the second time."

The buyer who priced themselves on independent thinking suddenly wants to know what the second response sounds like.
That curiosity is the door and you are already standing on the other side of it.

Disrupt the pattern. The buyer who is thinking differently is the buyer who is ready to move.

The buyer who was braced for another standard pitch relaxes their defence. They become genuinely curious about what you brought instead.

And curiosity is the most receptive state any buyer can be in when your close arrives.

6. "I could give you references. Or I could show you something right now that makes them unnecessary."

Reddington never deferred to other people's opinions when his own demonstration was more powerful.
This phrase converts the social proof request into an immediate live proof moment.

The buyer was looking for a reason to trust you through someone else's experience.
This phrase gives you a reason to trust you through their own.
A buyer who sees it for themselves never needs to be convinced by someone who did.

Show them instead of telling them. Every time. Without exception.

7. "I want to leave you with one thought that I suspect is going to stay with you after this call ends."

Reddington always made sure the last thing he said was the thing that echoed longest.
