1. "IT IS THREE HUNDRED PENNIES. THAT IS THREE DOLLARS. IT IS A BARGAIN."
The line that doubled sales in the Arkansas study, word for word. A plain one, then a verdict. Eric Knowles found that either half alone did nothing. Together they went from 35 percent to 65.
Use it as a template, not a quote. Say the figure in a form the buyer has never heard, restate it plainly, and attach the verdict before they can attach their own.
The odd version opens the door. The plain version walks through it.

2. "IT IS ABOUT THE PRICE OF A SANDWICH A DAY. TWELVE DOLLARS. CHEAPER THAN THE MISTAKE IT STOPS."
Knowles argued that buyers run a resistance script the moment they hear a price. A small surprise interrupts the script, and the next sentence gets in before it restarts.
Convert the number into a unit the buyer does not expect, then give the real figure, then name what it replaces. The surprise is the wedge. The verdict is the sale.
A price the buyer has to re-hear is a price they have not yet refused.

3. "SAY THE NUMBER IN WEEKS, NOT DOLLARS. TWO WEEKS OF YOUR TEAM'S YEAR. THAT IS WHAT THIS GIVES BACK."
The disruption does not have to be about money. Any unexpected unit will do, as long as the plain restatement follows fast. One later repackaging sold cupcakes by calling them half-cakes.
Pick the unit the buyer actually feels. Hours, weeks, headcount, Mondays. Lead with the strange one, land on the plain one, and let the reframe do the closing.
Buyers do not resist a number they had to think about.

5. "YOUR LAST VENDOR WAS PROBABLY RIGHT ABOUT ONE THING. THE PROBLEM IS REAL. THEY WERE WRONG ABOUT THE FIX."
Conceding the other side is a disruption because buyers expect the opposite. In Knowles's model the window that opens after a disruption stays open for seconds, not minutes.
Give the concession first, plainly, and follow it in the same breath with the frame you want to stand. Never leave the gap open long enough for the buyer to fill it.
Agree first and you get to say what the agreement means.

7. "FORGET THE DECK. ONE SENTENCE. THIS TAKES THE MONDAY CALL OFF YOUR CALENDAR FOR GOOD."
The biggest disruption available to a rep is abandoning the pitch mid-pitch. It resets the buyer's attention completely, and whatever comes next becomes the frame.
Use it once, late in the pitch, when the room has gone flat. Drop the presentation, say one plain sentence about the buyer's week, and stop talking.
The reset is the disruption. The next sentence is the whole sale.
