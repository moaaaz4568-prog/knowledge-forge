1. "Here is what I am offering. Here is what it does. Here is what happens next."

Ron Swanson never dressed up a simple thing in complicated language to make it sound more impressive. He understood that clarity is the most impressive thing available in any room full of noise.

This phrase strips the entire sales conversation down to three sentences and closes in all three. The buyer who has been waiting for the pitch to end suddenly realises it already has. There is nothing to object to because there is nothing extra to pick apart.

Remove the excess. The close hiding underneath it is always cleaner than the one you were performing.

2. "I am not going to waste your time or mine. Do you want this or not?"

Ron Swanson respected his own time too much to spend it convincing people who were not ready. That self-respect was visible and buyers felt it as authority before he said another word.

This phrase is not aggressive. It is the most respectful thing you can say to a buyer who has had enough information to decide. It signals that you value the conversation enough to end it cleanly rather than drag it into another follow-up cycle. The buyer who hears it almost always answers honestly and moves deals forward.

Ask the direct question. The buyer who was ready to decide will always respect you for asking it.

3. "I have told you what I know. What else do you need from me to make a decision?"

Ron Swanson never repeated himself. He said the thing once, clearly, and then waited.
Repetition is what reps do when they do not trust the strength of what they already said.

This phrase signals complete confidence in everything that came before it.
It puts the next move entirely in the buyer's hands and signals that you are not going to fill the silence with more pitch.
The buyer who has been waiting for you to stop talking finally has the space to decide.

Trust what you already said. The buyer who needed more information would have asked for it.

4. "That is a fair point. Here is my answer."

Ron Swanson never took five sentences to respond to a question that deserved one.
Brevity in the face of an objection signals confidence in a way that length never can.

This phrase acknowledges the objection without validating its power over the conversation and delivers the response with the calm certainty of someone who has the answer before the question was finished.
The buyer who expected a long defensive response gets a short confident one and confidence always closes harder than explanation.

Answer in one sentence when one sentence is enough. It almost always is.

5. "I do not need you to like this. I need you to understand it."

Ron Swanson never sought approval and that absence of need was the most powerful thing in any room he entered. This phrase signals to be liked. You are here to be useful.

The buyer who was evaluating your likeability suddenly starts evaluating your substance.
That is the only evaluation that leads to a signature.
Reps who need to be liked always discount. Reps who need to be understood always close.

Stop selling your personality. Start selling the solution. The buyer will like you after they sign.

6. "I have said everything I have to say. The rest is your decision."

Ron Swanson understood that the hardest part of any close is knowing when to stop talking.
Most reps talk themselves out of deals they had already won by filling the silence after their best point.

This phrase marks the end of your pitch with complete finality and puts the full weight of the decision where it belongs.
The buyer who has been half-listening suddenly has nothing left to wait for.
The silence that follows is not awkward. It is the close working.

Know when you are done. Then stop. The silence you are brave enough to hold.

7. "No."

Ron Swanson’s most powerful word was also his shortest. The ability to say no to a bad deal, a bad concession, or a bad buyer is the most underrated skill in sales.

This phrase is for the moment a buyer tries to restructure your offer into something that does not work for you. One word. No apology. No explanation unless they ask for one.

The buyer who expected negotiation suddenly recalibrates the entire conversation around someone who has standards.

Say no when no is the right answer. The buyer who respects it was always worth closing.
