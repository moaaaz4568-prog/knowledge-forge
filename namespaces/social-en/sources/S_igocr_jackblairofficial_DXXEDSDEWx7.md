1. "I want to be upfront with you. I have done this enough times to know exactly how this conversation should go."

Ron Burgundy never walked into a room with doubt visible anywhere on his person.
He projected a history of success so completely that the room accepted it before asking for evidence.

This phrase signals experience and authority in the opening seconds before the buyer has formed a full opinion.
The buyer stops evaluating whether you belong in the conversation and starts listening to what you have to say.
Confidence announced early enough becomes the frame everything else is measured against.

Set the authority frame in the first sentence. The buyer will spend the rest of the call inside it.

2. "This is one of the strongest conversations I have had this week and we are just getting started."

Ron Burgundy made every person in his orbit feel like they were part of something significant.
That feeling is not flattery. It is energy and energy is contagious on a sales call.

This phrase lifts the temperature of the conversation before the pitch begins. The buyer who was half present suddenly feels the pull of a rep who is fully alive in the room.
Energy at the right moment converts a routine call into the one the buyer remembers.

Show up with more energy than the room expects. The buyer will match it without knowing why.

3. "I am going to give you my honest read of this and I want you to tell me if it lands."

Ron Burgundy always delivered his lines with such complete conviction that the audience forgot to question whether the content deserved it.

This phrase signals both confidence and genuine curiosity in a single sentence.
It tells the buyer you are secure enough to be wrong and that security reads as authority.
The buyer who was bracing for a pitch suddenly feels invited into a real conversation.

Confidence with curiosity is the combination that closes the rooms pure certainty cannot.

4. "I have looked at what you are dealing with and I have to tell you, this is very solvable."

Burgundy always made the people around them feel that whatever situation they were in was manageable because he was there and he had seen worse and he had always prevailed.

This phrase does the same thing for a buyer who has been carrying a problem they are not sure can be fixed. It signals calm expertise and the buyer stops feeling stuck and starts feeling possible. A buyer who feels possible is a buyer who is already moving toward yes.

Make the problem feel solvable by sounding like someone who has solved it before without breaking a sweat.

5. "I am not going to pretend I have all the answers right now. What I will tell you is that I will get them."

Ron Burgundy's greatest asset was not his knowledge. It was his commitment to the performance of certainty even in uncertainty.
This phrase does something more sophisticated than confidence alone. It signals integrity inside the confidence.

The buyer who expected you to bluff your way through a gap suddenly respects you for naming it.
That respect converts a potential credibility problem into a trust-building moment.
The rep who says I will find out and means it closes more than the one who pretends they already know.

Own the gap. Then close it before the next conversation. The buyer will remember both things.

6. "I want to make something clear. I would not be in this conversation if I did not believe completely in what I am bringing you."

Burgundy's conviction was never manufactured. At some fundamental level he believed everything he said and that belief helped misplace was the engine of every room he ever commanded.

This phrase transfers that conviction directly into the buyer's evaluation of your credibility.

A rep who says this and means it is impossible to ignore and very difficult to say no to.
Genuine belief in your offer is the one thing in sales that cannot be faked well enough to matter.

Believe it first. The buyer always knows the difference between conviction and performance.

7. "I think we both know this is the right call. Let me tell you what happens next."

Ron Burgundy never ended a broadcast with a question about whether anyone was still watching. He assumed the close with the certainty of someone for whom doubt was not an option.

This phrase assumes the yes without demanding it and proposes the next step before the buyer has finished deciding.

The buyer who was on the fence suddenly finds themselves in a conversation that has already moved past the fence.
Assumed momentum is always faster than requested permission.

Assume the yes. Move to the next step. The buyer who was ready will follow you there.
