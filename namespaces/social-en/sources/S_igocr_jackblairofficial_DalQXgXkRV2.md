1. "Before we hang up, I'm going to tell you the one thing that changes this decision."

Paul Harvey built his most famous segment on one device: the payoff held for the end. He called it The Rest of the Story, and 24 million people stayed for it. Open a loop in your first minute. A buyer who's waiting for something doesn't drift.

Open a loop early and they stay to the end.

2. "So the investment is eight thousand."

Then nothing. Harvey's pauses were as
famous as his voice. He knew silence isn't
dead air, it's emphasis. Say the number
and stop. The quiet tells the buyer the
number is normal. Rushing in to justify it
tells them it isn't.

The pause after the price does the selling.

3. "While we're on it, here's what I'd actually do in your spot."

Harvey read his own ads and wove them into the broadcast so smoothly listeners never braced for a pitch. That seamlessness built his sponsor loyalty. The second your voice shifts into pitch mode, the buyer's guard comes up. One voice, start to finish.

The pitch voice is what they're saying no to.

4. "That's page one. Page two is where this gets interesting."

Page two was Harvey's actual on-air device, a mid-broadcast turn that reset the room's attention right when it usually dies. Name the chapters of your call. A buyer who can feel the map moving forward leans back in instead of checking out.

Reset their attention before you need it.

5. "Let me give you that back in one sentence."

Harvey wrote in staccato. Short lines, one thought at a time, because a radio listener can't reread. After any explanation, compress it to a single line. That's the line your buyer repeats to their spouse or their boss, and that line closes the deal you're not in the room for.

If they can't repeat it, they can't buy it.

6. "I only sell what I'd put my name on. Here's why I took this one."

Harvey turned sponsors away and said it plainly: he was fiercely loyal to the ones willing to put their money where his mouth was. Advertisers waited in line because his yes meant something. Tell the buyer what you've said no to. Selectivity is the fastest credibility on a call.

A yes only counts from someone who says no.

Deals don't die on no. They die on drift.
