THE SEAT SCORECARD

THE MARKET MAP

"List the categories of companies that hire remote closers in [my vertical], what their sales cycles look like, and which are growing this year. Rank them by how often the seat leads to a bigger one."

The loudest job posts are rarely the best seats, and the best seats are filled by referral before a post exists.

The good rooms are mapped, not scrolled.

THE OFFER TEARDOWN
"Here is a role description. Write the questions that expose whether this seat actually feeds closers: where the calls come from, how many, what ramp looks like, why the last rep left."
A commission seat is a claim about lead flow. The teardown makes them prove the claim before you bet a year on it.
Vet the seat harder than the seat vets you.

THE STORY BANK
"Here are my five best deals. Turn each into an interview story: the situation, what I actually said on the call, what it achieved. Make them specific enough that nobody else could tell them."
Google compared tens of thousands of interview scores to how hires actually performed and found no relationship. What predicts is structured questions about real behavior, which makes prep a story problem, not a charm problem.
Interviews are not won live. They are won in the bank.

THE ROLEPLAY
"Interview me for this closing seat. Structured and behavioral: a deal I lost, a discount I refused, a quarter I missed. Push on every vague answer until each claim has a scene attached."
The moment an answer goes abstract, an interviewer stops believing it. Rehearse until the scenes arrive before the adjectives do.
Specifics get hired. Adjectives get thanked for coming in.

THE QUESTION FLIP
"Write the seven questions I should ask the hiring manager that reveal whether closers actually succeed here, without it sounding like an interrogation. Include the follow-up for every vague answer."
The rep who asks nothing is pricing the seat on hope. The interview view runs both ways or it runs against you.
You are not being chosen. You are choosing. You are choosing, under observation.

THE DECISION LEDGER
"Two offers. Here are both structures, lead flows and managers. Strip the company names and tell me which seat a stranger would take, and what I am overweighting because one logo is shiny."
Named companies drag borrowed prestige into a decision that should be arithmetic. Blind the logos and the real seat would declare itself.
Logos impress your friends. The structure decides your year.
