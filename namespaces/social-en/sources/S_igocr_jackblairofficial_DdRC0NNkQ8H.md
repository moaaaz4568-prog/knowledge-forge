GAGOSIAN GALLERY
THE WORLD'S MOST POWERFUL ART DEALER STARTED OUT SELLING $15 POSTERS WHILE PARKING CARS. NOW HIS GALLERIES DO OVER $1 BILLION A YEAR. HERE ARE 7 PHRASES FROM HIS PLAYBOOK THAT WORK ON ANY BUYER:

Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "I've got it until Tuesday."

His actual line. When a collector asked if a piece was for sale, Gagosian would say yes, he had it until Tuesday. No pitch. Just a date. When your buyer says they need to think, give them a real one: the start date, the price hold, the last open spot. Only if it's true.

Open-ended offers get open-ended answers.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "I'm calling for one reason."

He often made a hundred cold calls a day,
and every one was hunting something:
where a painting hung, who would buy it,
what the owner would take. Before you
follow up, find one new thing: a result
from a client like them, a date, the answer
to their question. Lead with it.

Nobody calls back "just checking in."
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "You said something last time that stuck with me."

Gagosian went to dinner at Marc Jacobs' apartment in Paris. Days later he called about two paintings he'd noticed on the walls, and the deal closed within three days. Dinner was the research. Write down the one thing your buyer said that mattered most, in their words. Open the next call with it.

Remember what they said and they'll remember you.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Honestly, the cheaper option won't get you there."

He told the painter Cecily Brown her work was priced too low for his customers, and pushed her prices up. Serious buyers read a low price as a warning. When yours reaches for the smallest package, hold it against the goal they gave you. If it can't get them there, say so.

A serious buyer hears cheap as risk.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "It might feel like a lot today. It won't in a year."

David Geffen, one of his biggest clients, once said that he may feel like he's overpaid for something, but in the end he's never overpaid for anything. Don't argue the number. Agree it stings today. Then take them to the day they'll judge the decision: a year out, when it's working.

People forget the price. They remember the result.

6. "I only do this with people who'll actually use it."

He's said he won't do business with people who buy art just to flip it. The most powerful dealer alive still turns buyers away. Say your version early and calmly. Tire kickers drift off. Serious buyers lean in, because now they're the ones being qualified.

The rep who can walk away gets believed.

7. "A no is fine. I'll follow up until I get one."

In SoHo they nicknamed him GoGo. If he couldn't reach someone, he'd call ten times. Say this on the first call, calmly, and every call after it is expected instead of annoying.

Now look at where all seven started. A parking lot, a stack of $2 posters, and a guy who could sell.

Sound familiar?
