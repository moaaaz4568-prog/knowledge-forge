SALESFORCE ASKED 4,050 SALES PROS WHY THEY WANT OUT. THE TOP ANSWER WAS NOT PAY. IT WAS NOT SEEING WHERE THE JOB GOES. THE 7 QUESTIONS TO ASK YOUR GM BEFORE YOU GIVE HIM ANOTHER YEAR:
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "Who got promoted off this floor in the last two years, and to what?"

Salesforce's own survey put career path above pay as the reason reps leave. So start with names. If your GM can list two people and the seats they moved into, a path exists. If he talks about opportunity and cannot name anyone, the path is a sentence, not a place.

A path you cannot name is not a path.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblair_official

2. "What does the seat above mine actually pay for, my closing or my managing?"

Most floors only promote reps into management. That is a different job with a different skill, and it takes the best closer off the phone. Ask which one the next seat rewards. If the only way up is to stop doing the thing you are good at, this floor has no next seat for a closer.

Up should not mean off the phone.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "If I double my number next year, what changes for me?"

Make him answer in specifics. Title, territory, lead flow, plan, anything. A GM with a real plan answers in under a minute. A GM without one says we will talk about it then. That answer means your double goes on his board, not yours.

Doubling should buy something you can name.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "What would I have to stop doing to move up here?"

A real answer sounds like stop running your own deals and start running the desk. That at least tells you the price. No answer means nobody has thought about you moving at all. You are being kept where you are useful.

If nobody can tell you the price, nobody plans to sell you the seat.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Is there a path here that keeps me selling and still moves me forward?"

Senior closer, key accounts, the big ticket desk, training the new reps on a split. Some floors have these seats. Most do not. If the answer is no, the honest version of your future here is the same seat and the same plan, with the number raised every January.

Forward without the phone is not forward for a closer.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
6. "How many reps here have five years in?"
Count them. Then ask what those five years bought them. If the long timers sit in the same seat on the same plan, that is your ten year forecast. If there are none, nobody stays long enough to forecast too.
The veterans on the floor are your future, written down.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

7. "If my next seat is not here, where would you tell me to go?"

A GM who respects you answers honestly, sometimes with a company name. A GM who needs you deflects. Either way you learn what you came for. He already knows whether this floor goes anywhere. He has been waiting for you to ask.

He knows the answer. Ask the question.
