1. "Your self-image sets the ceiling on your performance."

The discovery at the center of Psycho-Cybernetics.

Maltz watched patients fix their face and still act defeated. The problem was the picture they held of themselves, not the mirror. A rep who quietly believes they are average will sell average.

You perform up to the picture you hold of yourself. So change the picture.
<!-- image 2 -->

2. “The mind cannot tell a vividly imagined experience from a real one.”

Maltz on how the brain actually learns.

He found the nervous system responds to a clearly imagined event almost like a real one. That is why mentally rehearsing a call can build real skill and real calm before you ever pick up the phone.

Practice the call in your head and your body shows up ready.

3. “Rehearse the win in your head before you live it.”
Maltz on what he called the theater of the mind.
He had people vividly picture the situation going well, the calm voice, the smooth answer, the smooth close. Walk in having already seen it work, and the real version feels familiar instead of frightening.
See it go well first. Then go let it.

4. “You are never failing. You are getting feedback.”

Maltz on how to treat a miss.

He reframed a setback as course-correction data, the same way a guided system constantly adjusts toward its target. A lost deal is not a verdict. It is information telling you where to steer next.

A no is not the end of the story. It is a direction.

5. "Hold the picture of who you want to be."
Maltz on the daily mental discipline.
He taught people to keep a clear, steady image of the person they were becoming, and to act from that image now. Behavior tends to drift toward whatever picture you keep in front of yourself.
Decide who you are. Your actions will go catch up.

6. “Stop replaying old failures. They are not who you are now.”
Maltz on the cost of living in the past.
He found people carried old embarrassments and let them script the present. Maltz taught them to let the old reel go. The rep on today’s call is not the one who fumbled last year.
The past only runs the present if you keep pressing play.

7. “Confidence is built from a stack of small wins you actually remember.”

Maltz on where real confidence comes from.

He had people deliberately recall their past successes, not their failures, before a hard moment. Confidence is not a gift. It is a memory you choose to keep in reach.

Stack the wins where you can see them. That stack is your confidence.
