1. "If it doesn't make sense, it's not true."

Judy's most famous rule, delivered thousands of times across 25 seasons. A story with a hole in it is a story.

Buyers run them too. "Love it, but the timing's wrong" from someone who booked the call, showed up early, and asked about pricing twice. That story doesn't add up.

When the objection contradicts the behavior, don't handle the objection. Ask about the gap. The real reason is hiding inside it.

2. "Um is not an answer."

She never let anyone escape into vagueness. Mumble in her courtroom and she waits, repeats the question, and makes you commit.

"Maybe," "probably," and "we'll see" are how deals die politely. A vague answer is the buyer purchasing time, and time kills.

Ask questions that force a real one. "Is this a yes with a condition, or a no?" You can work with nice about? You can work with um. can't work with either. You

3. "Don't pee on my leg and tell me it's raining."

Her father's line. She liked it enough to make it the title of her first book.
Translation: I know a smokescreen when I'm standing in one.

Every rep knows the rain. "Send me some info." "We're definitely doing this next quarter." The use instead of the truth.

You don't say Judy's version on a call. You say yours: "Can I be straight with you? That sounds like a polite no. If it is, I'd rather know now." Buyers respect the rep who calls the weather.

4. "When you can't look at me in the eye and tell me the story, that means you're lying."

Judy read delivery, not just words. The stumble. The glance away. The rehearsed part suddenly going smooth.

On the phone you get tone instead of eyes. The pause before "yeah, the budget's approved." The energy drop the moment the spouse comes up.

Stop listening only to what buyers say. Track where the voice changes. That's where the deal actually lives.

5. "I'm speaking. When my mouth moves, yours stops."

Nobody took the frame from Judy in 25 years. Not once. Her room, her rules, her pace.

Most reps hand the frame away in the first minute. The buyer sets the agenda, the timeline, and the terms, and the rep just follows along hoping.

You don't need her bluntness. You need her frame. "Here's how I'd suggest we use the next twenty minutes." Where of the call decides the outcome of it.

6. "Do you know when a gift becomes a loan? When the relationship is over."

Judy watched thousands of handshake deals get rewritten the second feelings changed. Nothing explicit, everything disputed.

Deals do the same thing. The verbal yes that softens over a weekend. The discount the buyer "remembers" you promising.

Close the loop out loud, same day. "So we're agreed: X by Friday, agreement in your inbox tonight." What's explicit can't be renegotiated by memory.
