5. ASK 'JUST SUPPOSE.'
The stall: 'We need to think it over.' The move: 'Just suppose the numbers worked exactly the way you need them to. What would you do then?'
If they light up, the numbers are the only conversation left. If they stall again, the numbers were never the problem.
A hypothetical finds the real objection.

6. ASK WHICH, NEVER WHETHER.
'Do you want to move forward?' invites a coin flip.
'Would Tuesday or Thursday suit you better?' offers a choice between two yeses.
Hopkins called it the alternate advance. Two real options, and the buyer keeps the feeling of control the whole way down.
Whether is a trap. Which is a door.
