1. "Want me to add the onboarding package to that?"

Would you like fries with that. McDonald's never trained anyone to ask "anything else?" The line names one specific item, because a specific suggestion gets a yes and an open question gets silence.

Pick the one logical add for this buyer and name it out loud.

Name the add-on. "Anything else" sells nothing.

2. "Now that we're doing this, here's the one thing I'd add if I were you."

The fries question comes at the end of the order. Never in the middle. McDonald's asks when the decision is already made, because resistance dies the second someone commits.

The moment after yes is the cheapest selling window you'll ever get, and most reps spend it celebrating.

The cheapest yes comes right after the first one.

3. "It's a fraction more to make it the complete version. Want the complete one?"

Make it a meal. The upsize works because the step is small next to the money they already committed, so it never feels like a second decision.

Price your upgrade against what they just agreed to, not against zero.

A small step up never feels like a second decision.

4. "Let me put all of it under one number so nothing falls through the cracks."

The combo exists because one price is easier to say yes to than three items. Bundling isn’t about hiding cost. It’s about collapsing three small decisions into one the buyer already made.

Fewer line items, fewer places for a deal to snag.

One number closes easier than three.

5. "The main thing's handled. This is the piece that makes it run every day."

A burger without fries feels unfinished, and McDonald's built its margin on that feeling. The profit was never in the burger.

Frame your add-on as the completion of what they bought, not an extra bolted on top, and it stops sounding like a money grab.

Don't sell extras. Sell the missing piece.

6. "One suggestion before we lock in, and I'll only make it once."

The fries ask works because saying no costs nothing. No pressure, no repeat, no guilt. That's why nobody resents it after seventy years.

Make your attach ask once, make declining painless, and the yes rate goes up because the guard never comes up.

Attach asks work because no is easy. Keep it easy.

7. "While we're at it, want me to include the seats for your team?"

And a drink with that. The second small ask rides the momentum of the first one. Once a buyer adds one thing, the next small add is the easiest sale of the day.

Chain one more tiny yes while the pen is already moving.

Momentum loves one more small yes.
