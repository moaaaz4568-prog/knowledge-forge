IN 11 STUDIES, 4,806 PEOPLE GRADED THE ADVICE THEY WERE GIVEN. SAYING I'M NOT SURE COST THEM. PUTTING A NUMBER ON IT DID NOT. HERE ARE THE 7 PHRASES THAT SURVIVED IT:

1. "Can I give you a number instead of a feeling?"

Across 11 studies, 4,806 people were given advice about something genuinely uncertain, then asked to rate how good that advice was. The line the researchers tested was literally this: I am X percent confident. Every study was registered in public before the data came in.

A number is a position. A feeling is an apology.

2. "I'd put it at about seventy percent."

Here is the first of three ways to admit you do not know, that cost the advisor nothing at all. Putting a number on it. Not one of the studies found people rating an advisor worse for giving a probability instead of a promise. The uncertainty was identical. Only the form changed.

Doubt with a number on it stops sounding like doubt.

4. "More likely than not."

The third free one, and the cheapest.
Saying that one outcome is more likely than another costs the advisor nothing either. No percentage, no range, no maths. Four words that lean, and a buyer can act on a lean.

Nobody can act on maybe. Everybody can act on a lean.

5. "Cut the words I'm not sure. Keep everything else."

In the same experiments, one thing did cost the advisor, and it replicated the earlier research exactly. Advisors who said they were not sure were judged worse than confident ones. Same doubt as the number, the range and the lean. Different words. That phrase is the entire bill.

You are not paying for the doubt. You are paying for the phrasing.

7. "I'll sound sure when I am. Not before."

The authors put it in one line. Advisors benefit from expressing themselves with confidence, but not from communicating false certainty. Confidence in the delivery, precision in the claim. The rep who promises a Tuesday they cannot control is not being confident. They are writing a cheque the calendar has to cash.

Now look at what those seven actually are. Not one of them is more confident than the other. One is the same confidence, doubt, said in a form a person can act on.

Sound familiar?
