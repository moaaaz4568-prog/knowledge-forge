# G_ig_10feetstudios_C0z4jGbt3Wy — keeping clients

## Term

Keeping clients means holding on to the ones you already have, and the speaker opens with the economics: it is so much cheaper than acquiring new ones. The phrase appears 1 time, beside a solid $2,500 a month, difficult phases, and the long run, and his advice is to give a struggling high-paying client something — even covering a month of their costs and paying them $1,000 — rather than lose them. The math he gives is blunt: they keep paying $1,500, you save money over the long run, and you get past the phase to better results afterwards.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

> "Keeping clients is so much cheaper than acquiring new ones." — sources/S_ig_10feetstudios_C0z4jGbt3Wy.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as begging clients to stay: the gift goes to high-paying clients in a difficult phase, ideally before they even raise it, and the aim is better results afterwards, not a bribe for loyalty.
- Read as spending without limit: the example is bounded — $1,000 against a $2,500 monthly fee that keeps paying $1,500 — and it only makes sense while the client is worth keeping.
