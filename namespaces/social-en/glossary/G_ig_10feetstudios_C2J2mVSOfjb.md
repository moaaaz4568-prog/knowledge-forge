# G_ig_10feetstudios_C2J2mVSOfjb — time horizon

## Term

A time horizon is how far away you place a goal, and the speaker says the way you set goals heavily impacts where you actually end up. The phrase appears 2 times, beside confidence, stress, and wrong decisions, and his comparison is two owners at four k a month: one aims for ten k in six months, the other in two, and the confident one will most likely reach it faster even if it takes three. The rule is to set the goal super far away, almost impossible, on a larger horizon, because a huge goal due in a month only creates stress and wrong decisions.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "So set a big goal on a longer time horizon." — sources/S_ig_10feetstudios_C2J2mVSOfjb.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as aiming low to stay safe: the goal should be almost impossible to reach, and only a small part of you needs to know it is actually possible.
- Read as a deadline trick: the longer horizon is not permission to drift, it is what keeps a huge goal from forcing rushed, wrong decisions.
