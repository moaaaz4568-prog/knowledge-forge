# G_ig_theaustinschneider_DYFunKDCVG6 — cost of goods sold

## Term

Cost of goods sold is the speaker's pricing anchor: what it costs to fulfill an offer if you delegated everything inside the business — and exactly how pricing should be set. The phrase appears 5 times, beside "offer breakdown", "retail price", and "markup and a margin", and the calculation runs in steps: itemize everything done for the offer — editing, shooting, onboarding calls, client success check-ins — quantify how much time it takes, price what it would cost per hour to hire somebody to do it, and multiply time by rate. That product is the cost of goods sold, and multiplying it by three or four gives the retail price, with a markup and a margin built in. From the cost of goods sold to the actual retail price, that is how you know you can comfortably scale.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the price itself: the cost of goods sold is the fulfillment cost, and the retail price is three or four times that — confusing the two underprices the offer.
- Read as a fixed multiplier: three or four is the range he gives, not a law — the point is the margin the multiplier leaves, not the number.
