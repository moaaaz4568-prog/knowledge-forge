# G_ig_theaustinschneider_DYKZXd8lYAB — chief expectation officer

## Term

Becoming chief expectation officer first is the speaker's underrated move for teams: most of the teams he sees fail simply do not have proper expectations in place. The phrase appears 1 time, beside "proper expectations", "failing teams", and "weekly one on ones", and it sits in part three of his overrated-and-underrated team edition — treating the team like friends is overrated because boundaries create respect, micromanaging every task is overrated because it burns the team member out and makes them quit, and expecting hires to operate like you is overrated because they are not you and systems must create the clarity. Weekly one on ones are underrated because connection builds retention, and investing in infrastructure before scaling a team is underrated because tools make delegation actually work.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a job title to hire for: chief expectation officer is a role the leader takes on first — setting proper expectations — not a position to fill.
- Read as a substitute for systems: the speaker pairs expectations with systems that create clarity, so the officer role without systems still leaves the hire guessing.
