# G_ig_therealbrianmark_CtKySGXARV6 — churn rate

## Term

The churn rate is the share of clients leaving each month, and Brian teaches it as the number that decides whether a coaching business can grow from $10,000 to $25,000 a month: nine leavers out of 100 clients is 9%, and growth then needs either more than nine new sign-ups a month or fewer leavers. "churn rate" appears once in the source, beside "nine divided by 100", "sign more clients" and "decrease the amount of clients leaving". He notes the numbers matter less for a beginner, so the rate is a scaling metric — it only becomes the deciding number once the business is big enough that leavers can cancel out new sales.

## Live Example

> "Example, if you had 100 clients at the start of the month and nine clients left the program, you would do nine divided by 100. Your churn rate is 9%." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CtKySGXARV6.md
> "So if you want your business to grow, there's only two ways to do that." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CtKySGXARV6.md
> "Number one is sign more clients than nine per month, or number two is decrease the amount of clients leaving your program." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CtKySGXARV6.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Reading churn rate as a complaint about clients: Brian treats it as arithmetic — leavers divided by total clients — so the rate measures the business, not the loyalty of any single client.
- Treating 9% as a target: the example uses nine out of 100 to show the division, not to say 9% is good, so the point is the calculation and the two responses to it, not the number itself.
