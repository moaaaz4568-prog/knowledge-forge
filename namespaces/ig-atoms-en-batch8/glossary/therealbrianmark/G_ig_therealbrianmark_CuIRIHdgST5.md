# G_ig_therealbrianmark_CuIRIHdgST5 — branding

## Term

In this source, "branding" is the reason Brian Mark gives for why Coca-Cola can charge more than a no-name store brand: not taste, but the brand itself, and he warns coaches that without advertising like a high quality brand they will be paid no-name prices. The word appears once, next to "high quality brand", "no name prices", and "level up your brand", and the video turns the idea into three visible habits — matching fonts on videos, matching colors of clothes, and a personal story — so that viewers recognise the creator. His own example is the personal story of having been an addict, which he presents as the thing that sets him apart from everybody else on social media.

## Live Example

> "Why does Coca Cola sell for more money than no name bullsh*t from Superstore? Because it tastes better! No, branding." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CuIRIHdgST5.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When "branding" is read as a logo or a name alone: here it is the whole recognisable package — same fonts, same colours, a personal story — that lets a coach charge brand prices instead of no-name prices.
- When the Coca-Cola comparison is read as a claim about taste: the video explicitly rejects taste as the explanation and gives branding as the answer, so treating the price gap as a quality gap misses his point.
