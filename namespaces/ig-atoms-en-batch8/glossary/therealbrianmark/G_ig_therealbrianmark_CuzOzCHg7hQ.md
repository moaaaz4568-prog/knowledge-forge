# G_ig_therealbrianmark_CuzOzCHg7hQ — buyer's remorse

## Term

In this source, "buyer's remorse" is the moment a client asks for their money back because the program just isn't for them, and Brian Mark's prevention is to cement the sale and give a next step immediately after the purchase. The phrase appears once, and the fix is a script: ask the buyer how they feel, affirm every answer as a good emotion, recall that his client Don felt the exact same way and now makes over $20,000 a month, then send the welcome questionnaire by email and require it back within 24 hours so the program can be designed within seven days. The remorse is treated as a feeling to be steadied with a next step, not as a verdict on the program.

## Live Example

> "We've all had that client that says, "Can I get my money back? This program just isn't for me." It's called buyer's remorse." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CuzOzCHg7hQ.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When "buyer's remorse" is read as proof the client should be refunded: here it is a predictable emotion after the sale, handled by affirming the decision and assigning the questionnaire within 24 hours.
- When cementing the sale is read as arguing the client out of it: the video's move is to affirm their feelings and give a concrete next step, not to debate whether the program fits.
