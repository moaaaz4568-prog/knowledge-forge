# G_ig_therealbrianmark_CvGhSMMJUWd — perfect program

## Term

In this source, the "perfect program" is the hypothetical Brian Mark uses to smoke out a prospect's real objection: when someone says "I'm not sure if I want to move forward today", he asks "if this program were perfect, would you move forward today?", and they always say yes. The phrase appears once, and the follow-up — "what's the difference between the perfect program and my program?" — makes them name the real objection, which he can then overcome, or say nothing, in which case he goes for the close again. His concrete close after that is the choice between payment in full for 1500 or 500 per month.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "So what's the difference between the perfect program and my program?" — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_CvGhSMMJUWd.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When the "perfect program" is read as a product he is selling: here it is a question device — a program that does not exist — used only to reveal what is missing from his own.
- When the prospect's "yes" is read as a commitment to buy: the yes only answers the hypothetical, and the real work is the difference they name next.
