# G_ig_therealbrianmark_CvPiu8_g2ND — cash runway account

## Term

In this source, a "cash runway account" is the fifth of five bank accounts Brian Mark says you need to stop being poor: put 20% of your money in it until it holds six months of operating expenses, so that if anything ever goes bad, you are good to go. The phrase appears 2 times, in a list with a fun money account at 5%, a smart money account at 20%, a don't go to jail account for taxes at 20%, and an operating expenses account at 35%, and the runway is the safety buffer at the end of the split. His concrete rule is the six-month target, measured in operating expenses rather than income.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When a "cash runway account" is read as a savings account for a holiday: here it is a six-month operating-expenses buffer for when things go bad, distinct from the smart money account that saves for a down payment or a vacation.
- When the five accounts are read as five equal splits: the video assigns different percentages — 5, 20, 20, 35, 20 — and the runway's 20% is the one tied to the six-month rule.
