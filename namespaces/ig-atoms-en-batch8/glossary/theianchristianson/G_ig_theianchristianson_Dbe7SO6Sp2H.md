# G_ig_theianchristianson_Dbe7SO6Sp2H — predictions

## Term

Predictions are what the speaker says the brain actually cares about: not talent, but what it predicts you will do. The word appears once, in the sentence that the brain cares about its predictions, beside the singular prediction and the verb predicts — a brain that predicts failure makes its owner hesitate, stay silent, overthink, and watch opportunities slip by. The mechanism is behavioral: belief in yourself changes the behaviors that allow success to happen, so a slightly less talented person whose brain expects success takes more shots, learns faster, and recovers quicker. That is why the speaker calls confidence one of the biggest performance enhancers the brain has, and why identity matters more than most people realize.

- prediction

## Live Example

> "Your brain doesn't care how talented you are. It cares about its predictions, what it's predicting that you will do." — sources/S_ig_theianchristianson_Dbe7SO6Sp2H.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as positive thinking: the video's mechanism is prediction shaping behavior — hesitation, silence, and missed shots — not affirmations or mood.
- Read as a claim that talent is irrelevant: the speaker says the less talented person passes the more talented one because of behavior, not that talent itself has no value.
