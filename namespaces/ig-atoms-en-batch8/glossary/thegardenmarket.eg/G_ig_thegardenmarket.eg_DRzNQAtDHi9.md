# G_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DRzNQAtDHi9 — signature

## Term

'Signature' is what the copy says the scent is — 'It's not just a scent, it's a signature' — appearing once as the claim the whole passage builds on. The scent is described as 'a delicate blend of elegance and desire' where 'every note tells a story of confidence, of passion, of timeless beauty', so the term names the perfume as a personal mark: the thing that leaves a lasting impression and says who you are. Its neighbors are 'scent', 'note', and 'unforgettable', and the closing line spells it out — 'This is more than perfume. This is you, unforgettable'. The passage names no brand and no bottle; the signature is the idea, and the notes are its handwriting.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a review of a perfume: the copy calls the scent a signature, but it names no brand, no bottle, and no scent notes beyond 'elegance and desire'.
- Read as proof the wearer is unforgettable: the line 'This is you, unforgettable' is advertising copy, and the source tests nothing about who wears it.
