# G_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DBB_5aRtARC — curly-haired

## Term

'Curly-haired' describes the little baby of the sung line, paired both times with 'shine just like gold'. The line repeats word for word, so the term appears twice, and each time the curls come first and the shining follows — the hair is the thing seen, the gold is how brightly it shows. 'Little baby' and 'shine' sit beside it in both repeats, tying the hair type to a small, bright figure. The lyric says nothing about whose baby this is or where the gold comes from; the curls are simply the mark the singer keeps returning to.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "I'm a curly-haired little baby, but I shine just like gold." — sources/S_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DBB_5aRtARC.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim about a real baby's hair: the line is a sung lyric, and the curls belong to the 'little baby' of the song, not to a child the singer names.
- Read as a product claim about curly hair: the lyric says the baby shines like gold, and it promises nothing about any hair product or result.
