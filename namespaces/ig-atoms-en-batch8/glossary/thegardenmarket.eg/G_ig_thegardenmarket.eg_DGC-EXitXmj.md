# G_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DGC-EXitXmj — sugar and spice

## Term

'Sugar and spice' is the comparison the singer gives for feeling nice, appearing twice in the repeated line 'I feel nice, like sugar and spice'. The verse opens with 'I feel good. I knew that I wouldn't' and answers it with the sweetness comparison, so the phrase names the flavor of the feeling — sweet, warm, and pleasant — rather than any food. Its neighbors are 'I feel nice', 'so good', and 'so nice', and the doubling ('so nice, so nice') keeps the mood circling on the same sweetness. The song says nothing about what made the singer feel this way; the sugar and spice is the whole description of the mood.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a recipe: the phrase is a simile for feeling nice, and the song names no food, no sugar, and no spice beyond the comparison.
- Read as proof the singer feels good for a reason: the verse says the singer feels nice like sugar and spice, and it never says what made her feel that way.
