# G_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DBHSJBDN2wq — laid eyes

## Term

'Laid eyes on you' is the moment the song keeps circling back to: the first sight of the person addressed, named twice at the head of the line 'From the moment I laid eyes on you'. Each time it opens a little speech — 'this is what I said, I said' — followed by wanting, liking, and needing, so the phrase marks the start of the feeling rather than a description of looking. It appears twice, beside 'this is what I said' and 'a beautiful sight to see', and the second telling adds 'every little thing you do' as what the sight meant. The song never says where the meeting happened; the eyes-laying is the whole event, and everything after it is its echo.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "From the moment I laid eyes on you, this is what I said, I said, I see it and I like it and I want it, yes I do." — sources/S_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DBHSJBDN2wq.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a literal account of seeing: the phrase is the song's opening for a feeling, and the source gives no meeting, no place, and no first sight to check.
- Read as proof the two people are together: the song only says the singer saw the person and wanted them, and it says nothing about what happened after.
