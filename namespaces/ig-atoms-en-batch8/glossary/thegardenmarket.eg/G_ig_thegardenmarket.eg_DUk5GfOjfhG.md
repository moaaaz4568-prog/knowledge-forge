# G_ig_thegardenmarket.eg_DUk5GfOjfhG — up

## Term

'Up' is the state the singer has reached, appearing twice in 'I was down but now I'm up, yeah' — once at the opening, once at the close. The line credits the rise to God — 'This all God, this ain't no luck' — and contrasts it with being 'stuck in the mud', so the term names the recovery from down to up, not a direction in space. Its neighbors are 'down', 'mud', and 'ain't no luck', and the pairing is the point: up means something only because down came first. The song names no job and no win; up is the turnaround, and the mud is what it left behind.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim of success: the singer says he was down and now is up, and the song names no job, no win, and no measure of the rise.
- Read as proof luck played no part: 'This ain't no luck' is the singer's own credit to God, and the source offers no account of how the rise happened.
