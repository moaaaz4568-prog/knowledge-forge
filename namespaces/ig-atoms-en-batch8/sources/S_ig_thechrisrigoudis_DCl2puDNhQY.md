So you're pitching your product and the prospect asks a highly technical question. How should you attack it? Here are a couple horror stories I've heard before. Number one is you don't know the answer, you have to go back, ask a developer or a support person, and then the prospect waves bye-bye. The second scenario is you try to answer in a highly complicated way, the prospect doesn't understand, you go back and forth, and you lose the sale. Instead, try to figure out what problem are they trying to solve by asking that question in the first place. So simply tell them this: "I'm happy to elaborate. Can you give me an example where having that future will actually solve a problem in the business? " Or you can say, "Well, that's an interesting question. Can you share with me what problem are you looking to solve by having that? " This helps them refocus back to the actual problem. And in many cases, they don't even need that future in the first place. They're asking that because they think they need that future. But you, if you're a trusted advisor, you can shift their perspective and showing them that they can solve the problem regardless if they have the future or not. Make sure you follow for more tips.
