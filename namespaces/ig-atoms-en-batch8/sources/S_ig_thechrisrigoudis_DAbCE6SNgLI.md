I have reviewed hundreds of sales calls, and the biggest mistake I see people make is how they take notes. They ask a question to a prospect, they look down, they write it down, and they miss everything else the prospect just told them. And they have to ask again, and they break rapport. Instead, here's how to take notes effectively. Take a piece of paper, split it down the middle, and write problems and solutions. Under the problems, you're gonna focus on four things. You're gonna write why, the overt outcome. Why are they there? Two is their hell. What are the biggest problems they're struggling with right now? Right below it, you're gonna write heaven. What do they want to achieve? And number four is why should they take action now? What's the driving factor? What's the urgency behind making a change? Over the solution, all you have to write is likes and dislikes. What would they like to have? And what they would dislike, what they don't want to have in a solution. For example, if you're selling coaching, they only want one-on-one, or they only want group with a community and support. If you focus on only these few things, it will allow you to know exactly how to present your offer without getting too distracted and noting down irrelevant things that don't matter.
