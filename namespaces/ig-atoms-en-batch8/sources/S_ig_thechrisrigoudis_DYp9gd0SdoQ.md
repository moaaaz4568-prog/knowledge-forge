I've closed more than twenty two million in sales by preventing objections in the first place. An objection is not a rejection of you; it's just a concern your prospect hasn't had answered yet. When someone says, "I need to think about it" or "it's too expensive," they're saying, "I don't have enough information so I can say yes." Most salespeople hear an objection and they go straight into the sales pitch: more facts, more logic, and much more pressure. Do not do this because what they actually need is to feel understood. So instead, validate what they say and ask them to expand on it. Once you've handled their real concern, you can sell them on the outcome before you even pitch. The objection was never the real issue. Not understanding what's behind the objection was their real issue. If you want to learn how to prevent any objection from ever happening, make sure you follow for more sales tips.
