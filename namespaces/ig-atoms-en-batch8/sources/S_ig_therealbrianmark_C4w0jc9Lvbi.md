When you're on a sales call and a client asks you for something that you know you can't give them or you just don't want to, use what I call the authoritative redirect. So let's say your prospect is just on the edge of buying, but they're trying to shake you down for a better deal. You know, Brian, this all sounds great, but I think I can lose the 20 pounds in 12 weeks instead of 16. Can you do that for me? Now most of you would just conform to the shakedown, or you would respond with, well, Nick, we can do 16 weeks to help you lose that 20 pounds, but unfortunately we wouldn't be able to do the 12 weeks. This does two things. One, makes you look incompetent, and two, makes it seem like you're trying to convince them to do something that they don't want to do. Instead, I want you to command authority and redirect the conversation. You know, Nick, we don't actually do 12-week programs, but what we can do is, you know, if you give me 16 weeks of your time, I will help you get in the best shape of your life and I will help you lose that 20 pounds. Is that fair? Now you sound more direct and upfront with what you can offer, and they will be more likely to trust you so you can close the deal. Follow for more value.
