When you are talking to a prospect, you need to use assumptive language. You say, "When you become a client with us, then X, Y, Z," not "If you become a client with us, then X, Y, Z." Okay, so this is terrible advice because you don't want to assume anything. You don't know what's going on in the prospect's mind. When you make assumptions about what direction a prospect wants to go, it actually triggers more resistance than what's needed. And the reason for that is prospects need to have the illusion of control when they're buying anything. I want to show them that there are multiple paths in this journey, and I want to talk about the benefits of what our clients achieve when they go with us. So never use that advice, and you'll win more sales.
