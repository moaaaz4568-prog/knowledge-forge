You don't become charismatic by talking to more people. You become charismatic by teaching your brain and your nervous system that talking to other people is safe. The biggest communication myth is that experience automatically makes you better. Because if that were true, every single salesperson with 20 years of experience would be insanely charismatic. Every executive would have executive presence, married couples would have no problems whatsoever. And obviously that's not what happens, because your nervous system doesn't learn from experience. It learns from evidence and iteration. Think about two people who both spend five years in sales. The first gets rejected, overthinks every interaction, replays conversations on the way home, and they slowly start believing that people are difficult to talk to. The second reflects after every conversation, intentionally changes one thing, gathers new evidence, and intentionally over time starts teaching his brain that conversations are predictable and engineerable. Same amount of experience, but two completely different nervous systems. That's why more conversations don't automatically make you better. They just make you more of what you are already building. Most people don't have 10 years of communication experience. They have one year repeated 10 times. That's why I built Neuro-Charismatic Engineering. It's not for giving you just more exposure. It's built around engineering better evidence for your brain and nervous system. Because once your nervous system expects connection instead of danger, then your communication changes before you ever open your mouth.
