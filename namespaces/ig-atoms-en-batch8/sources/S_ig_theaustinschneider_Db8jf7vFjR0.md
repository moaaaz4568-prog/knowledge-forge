Seven things I know now that I wish I knew when I started my agency. Number one, the stress doesn't mean that you're failing; it means that you're right at the edge of the next version of yourself. Two, nobody who's built something actually real did it when they felt ready. They did it feeling scared and just did it anyways. Three, the people in your life that don't get it aren't necessarily wrong. They just don't see what you see. Four, working harder than everybody else you know and having very little to show for it isn't a sign that you should quit. It's a sign that something in the model is supposed to change. Five, you will outgrow people. Friends, clients, mindsets, and that's not betrayal. That's growth. Six, the loneliness of building something—the part where nobody really understands the weight of it—that's not a flaw in the journey. That's just what the journey can feel like. Seven, conviction did more for the agency owners that I've seen scale than talent alone. They decided it would work before they even had a single reason that it would.
