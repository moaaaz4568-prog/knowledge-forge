# G_ig_regaliscapital_DS8FTbQAHQu — SBA loan

## Term

In this source the SBA loan covers ninety percent of the deal for a three million dollar business: the seller finances five percent, the buyer puts five percent of their own cash — one hundred and fifty grand — and a business profiting around eight hundred thousand dollars a year nets around three hundred and fifty thousand annually after debt payments. The phrase occurs once, beside 'the seller finances five percent', 'you only have to put five percent of your own cash', and 'getting your entire investment back in the first year alone'. The speaker frames the leftover cash flow as the fuel for repeating the process and acquiring multiple other businesses.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "You get an SBA loan to cover ninety percent of the deal." — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DS8FTbQAHQu.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a guarantee of getting the investment back in year one: the speaker's own figure depends on the business profiting 800 grand a year, so a weaker business would not return the cash that fast.
- Read as the SBA paying 90% as a gift: the loan is debt the business must service, and the 350 grand net figure comes only after debt payments.
