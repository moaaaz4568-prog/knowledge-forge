# G_ig_regaliscapital_DRu1aIKgAef — cash flow bind

## Term

In this source a cash flow bind is what happens when fixed payments to a former owner choke the business just after the purchase: the buyer gives her seventy five upfront and then pays her every dime of profit above the buyer's own salary until she is repaid, with no fixed payments, so if the business makes zero she gets zero that year. The phrase occurs once, beside 'the former owner', 'seventy five grand', and 'every dime of profit'. The speaker presents the structure as protection for both sides: she doesn't get burned, the buyer doesn't get burned, and seventy five thousand stays saved.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "You don't get put in a cash flow bind by the former owner and it puts you out of business right after you bought it and gave her seventy five grand." — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DRu1aIKgAef.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the seller being paid nothing: she gets seventy five upfront plus all profit above the buyer's salary, so the deal only delays her payment, it does not erase it.
- Read as a fixed repayment schedule: the buyer puts no payments on it precisely because profit is uncertain, so the seller's timing depends on what the business makes.
