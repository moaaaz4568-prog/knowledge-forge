# G_ig_regaliscapital_DVHIMV_ERFr — money glitch

## Term

In this source the money glitch is the SBA-backed way of buying a business that pays for itself almost immediately: a small business making around $300, 000 a year sells for about a million bucks, and with an SBA loan covering up to 90% plus seller financing for another 5%, the buyer needs only about $50, 000 of their own money. The phrase occurs once, beside 'cash flowing assets', 'stack them', and 'start paying them almost immediately'. The speaker contrasts this with real estate, which takes years to generate meaningful returns, and calls the opportunity the biggest wealth building one most people have never heard of.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "But here's the money glitch. A small business making around $300, 000 a year often sells for about a million bucks." — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DVHIMV_ERFr.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a loophole with no repayment: the business must still cover the loan payment from its cash flow, and the owner keeps only the meaningful profit left after that.
- Read as a claim that billionaires are lazy: the speaker's hook is that they find a path of least resistance, not that they do no work, and the glitch is the deal structure, not idleness.
