# G_ig_regaliscapital_DSx4qOkkclF — pennies on the dollar

## Term

In this source pennies on the dollar is the price Floyd paid for small recession-proof businesses during the Depression: he held onto his cash while everyone else loaded up on debt, then bought steady-cash-flow businesses with strong assets for just pennies on the dollar. The phrase occurs once, beside 'recession-proof businesses', 'steady cash flow', and 'strong assets'. Floyd started with $40, 000 in 1923 and grew it to over $100 million by the end of the Depression fifteen years later, and the speaker presents the same playbook for today: use a steady 9 to 5 cash flow to buy a first business.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "so when the real crash came, he started buying small recession-proof businesses with steady cash flow and strong assets for just pennies on the dollar." — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DSx4qOkkclF.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a literal price of a few cents: the phrase means a small fraction of value, not a counted price, and the source never names what Floyd actually paid.
- Read as a promise that recessions make everyone rich: Floyd's gain came from holding cash while others took on debt, so the phrase describes his buying price, not a guaranteed outcome for any buyer.
