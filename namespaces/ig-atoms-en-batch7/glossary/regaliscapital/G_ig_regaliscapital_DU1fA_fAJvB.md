# G_ig_regaliscapital_DU1fA_fAJvB — mutual funds

## Term

In this source mutual funds are the slow alternative to buying a business: $200, 000 invested at 8% a year takes about nine years just to double, while the same money puts 5% down on a $4 million business that could produce around $600, 000 in yearly profit. The phrase occurs once, beside '8% year over year', 'a 5% down payment', and '15% net profit'. The speaker allows both have their time and place, but argues buying a business is one of the fastest ways for an entrepreneurial person to accelerate wealth.

## Synonyms

- mutual fund (the singular form the source uses for the same investment)

## Live Example

> "If you had $200,000, would you rather buy a business or invest it in mutual funds?" — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DU1fA_fAJvB.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that mutual funds lose money: the source says they grow 8% a year and double in nine years, so the criticism is speed, not safety.
- Read as a promise of $600, 000 a year from any $4 million business: the figure depends on 15% net profit and leaves two to $300, 000 in cash flow only after paying back the loan.
