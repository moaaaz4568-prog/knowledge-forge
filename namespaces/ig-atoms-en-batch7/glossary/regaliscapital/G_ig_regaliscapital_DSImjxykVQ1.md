# G_ig_regaliscapital_DSImjxykVQ1 — corporate takeovers

## Term

In this source corporate takeovers are what T. Boone Pickens became the face of: starting with a $2, 500 loan in 1956 to launch Mesa Petroleum, he bought substantial stakes in undervalued oil companies, showed shareholders management was underperforming, and used that leverage to demand changes. The phrase occurs once, beside 'undervalued oil companies', 'show shareholders that management is underperforming', and 'run the business more efficiently'. His greatest deal was Gulf Oil, the sixth largest oil company in America, acquired while Mesa was still mid-sized.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "He ended up becoming the face of corporate takeovers, became a billionaire, and gave away over a billion dollars to charity before passing in 2019 at the age of 91." — sources/S_ig_regaliscapital_DSImjxykVQ1.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as stealing companies: the speaker calls Pickens a thief only in the hook, while the method described is buying a stake and pressing shareholders for change, not theft.
- Read as a strategy anyone can copy at any size: the example runs from a $2, 500 loan to Gulf Oil, the sixth largest oil company in America, so the scale of the deals is part of the story.
