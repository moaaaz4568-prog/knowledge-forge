Your brain is just as afraid of success as it is of failure. There's this theory in neuroscience called predictive processing, which essentially means that your brain is in a constant cycle of trying to predict what happens next. When the world behaves the way it expects, processing is easier. And when something is uncertain or unexpected, the brain must devote more energy into figuring it out. Because the human brain is naturally wired to conserve energy, we take the path of least resistance. We avoid being out of our comfort zone. We don't like trying new things. We procrastinate. We like what's familiar. But the thing we don't often realize is that success is equally as uncertain to the brain as failure. In fact, it might even be more uncertain because the brain is better at imagining fear and loss. The same way your life will change after you fail, your life could drastically change after you succeed. So sometimes when people think of themselves as lazy or unable to execute, they're not actually avoiding work. They're avoiding an outcome, and sometimes they're avoiding the good outcome. This is your sign to stop subconsciously self-sabotaging.
