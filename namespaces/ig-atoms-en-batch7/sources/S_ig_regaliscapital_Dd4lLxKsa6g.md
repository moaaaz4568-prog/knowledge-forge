How is possible that private equity guys make so much money? Say you buy an HVAC company that makes $2 million a year in profit, and the retiring owner sells it to you at four times profit for $8 million. You put down $1.6 million of your own money, and a bank lends you the remaining $6.4 million, because the business makes more than enough to cover the payments. And over the next three years, you hire a general manager, so the business stops depending on you, and you raise prices 8% because the old owner hadn't touched them in a decade. And just like that, profit climbs from $2 million to $3 million. Now, at this point, most owners would either keep collecting the profit or sell. But private equity does something in between, and almost nobody outside that world talks about it. But here's the real money move. You go back to the bank and ask them to refinance, because you're no longer the risky borrower they lent to three years ago. You now have a $3 million profit business with clean financials, a manager running it, and a perfect payment history, so they're willing to lend around three times profit, or roughly $9 million. You use that $9 million to pay off what's left of the old $6.4 million loan, and the difference of about $2.6 million goes into your pocket. This is called a dividend recap, and it's how you take money out of a business without selling a single share of it. When you eventually sell at a seven times multiple for $21 million, the bank gets paid back first, and you keep the rest on top of what you already took out.
