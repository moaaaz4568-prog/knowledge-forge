You just can't build an interesting company if you're not an interesting person. I've noticed a lot of young founders treat their lives like there's only one objective: build your company, and everything else is a distraction. But the founders who feel truly magnetic, the ones who are unfathomably creative and see problems from angles that others simply don't, they deliberately put themselves in rooms and experiences that have nothing to do with their startup. Side questing unironically expands your depth in ways you cannot predict. Creativity isn't about grinding harder on the same idea. It's about increasing your number of inputs. When your life is narrow, your thinking is narrow. And when your experiences are repetitive, your solutions are too.
