One of the things about YC that people may not know is that we make our decisions in ten minutes, because of the scale at which we're trying to operate and the number of companies we try to see every year. And at first glance, you would say there's no way to make better than random decisions in ten minutes. And then you think about it a little more, and you say, okay, you can do better than random, but not that well. And that's what I would have said. But it turns out that in ten minutes, if the only question you're trying to answer is, does this person have the potential to be the next Mark Zuckerberg? Not are they definitely going to be, not should we put millions of dollars into this, but does this person have some seed of what it takes to be the next Mark Zuckerberg? You can answer that question in ten minutes. Not with 100 percent accuracy, obviously, but good enough that our business model works. And what we're looking for, the obvious thing is intelligence. That's really easy. You can tell that really quickly, much less than ten minutes usually. The hardest trait that we're looking for is determination. When we're really wrong one way or the other on a founder, it's because we were miscalibrated on how determined that founder seemed. And then there are a few other topics in the middle that we look at: clarity of vision, communication skills, sort of non-obvious brilliance of the idea we look at very hard. These are ones that you can't always be right on, but you can usually get quite a lot of data. And unlike determination, they're not quite as difficult to figure out, but not as easy as intelligence. And one of the surprising things about YC is that not only is it possible to figure this out, but you can train new YC partners to figure this out in a year or two years.
