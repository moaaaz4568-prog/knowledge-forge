Most people think buying the business is the hard part, but in reality, the first 30 days after acquisition are what make or break the deal. So here's what you do in the first 30 days after buying a business, or better yet, what you don't do. In the first 30 days, you don't reinvent anything, you don't disrupt anything, and you don't try to be the hero. You simply observe. You want to know how everything actually works and understand what you just bought. You watch how pricing decisions are made, you identify where the money leaks are, and you learn which employees actually move the needle and which ones don't. The business should run exactly the same as it did before. The only difference is that now you're controlling the cash flow. This matters because most acquisitions fail when new owners rush into optimization before they understand the machine they're operating. Yes, the first month is boring, and that's exactly how it should be.
