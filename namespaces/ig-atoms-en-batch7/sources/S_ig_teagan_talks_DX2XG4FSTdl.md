As somebody who literally left school to work full time in San Francisco, I think it is incredibly dangerous the way startup culture romanticizes dropping out. I promise you, all of these people posting announcements on LinkedIn about how they're moving across the country and joining a startup or building their own, like they have no idea what they're doing. I have no idea what I'm doing. And when I get DMs from people asking how I got the opportunity to leave school, like guys, leaving school is not the actual opportunity. Startups are a bubble and tech is a bubble. I think being surrounded by this bubble can make young people feel a lot of pressure to make these high conviction, high risk, life altering decisions because they think the decision itself is what makes them impressive. But there's nothing inherently impressive about dropping out of school. Honestly, I don't think there's anything inherently impressive about starting work at a younger age than everybody else. It's admirable to make sacrifices for the life you want. I think it's so cool when people take risks before they're ready. But I wish people were more honest about how scary and unstable and uncertain this path actually is. I think an underrated trait in startups is the ability to make decisions based on genuine conviction instead of outward pressure. Like you are not behind and don't make decisions out of fear.
