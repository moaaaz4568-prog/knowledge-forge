Non-technical tech roles that you can break into from other industry. This is one of those roles that I like to call it the sweet spot role because it keeps things interesting and it's low stress. Because you're not the sales team that has to deal with the quotas and you're also not the delivery team that has to do the heavy lifting and actually do the project build out. The role itself is called solution architect. As the name suggests, you design the blueprint at the very early stage. You build the proof of concept, the demo to show it to the client or the team that yes, this is what I think it's gonna look like and we can do it. For example, if an e-commerce business decides to buy Salesforce and you are a solution architect for Salesforce, you will step in during the pre-sales cycle and look at customer's requirement and say yes, we can build this. This is roughly what it looks like and how much it's gonna cost you. Most importantly, you are giving customer the confidence the software can handle their requirements. So you're basically backing up the sales team as a product side expert. See why I said this is a sweet spot role because you get to do the creative strategic side of the work without carrying the stress of doing the full execution or selling the product. However, this is not an entry level job, so you do have to build your way up there and here's how I would do it. First, start with getting certification from a bigger tech company like Amazon, Google, Oracle, Snowflake, HubSpot, Salesforce because they have very clear learning path or certification route that you can take. Then I will apply for adjacent entry level role like system administrator or junior consultant or business analyst to get hands on experience. Pro tip, there are a lot of admin or in person community group for these tech company. I would join one of those admin group and just meet people, network and find an opportunity. Once you have gained some product experience, I would then look for titles like solution architect to make the transition.
