# G_ig_jun_yuh_CqnuMkpqI8e — let me go

## Term

Let me go appears once in this 17-word source, in the line where the speaker tells the other person they could not save the speaker yet cannot let the speaker go. The phrase sits beside save me and the question about what the speaker is thinking about, so it names the grip the other person keeps, not a physical departure. In this use, letting go would mean ending that hold, and the speaker presents it as something the other person refuses to do. A concrete instance is the opening contrast itself: no rescue happened, yet the hold remains.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "You couldn't save me, but you can't let me go. You ask me what I'm thinking about." — sources/S_ig_jun_yuh_CqnuMkpqI8e.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a breakup line: the speaker is not ending the relationship here, only naming the grip that remains after a failed rescue.
- Read as a request for freedom of movement: let me go here means releasing an emotional hold, not letting someone walk out of a room.
