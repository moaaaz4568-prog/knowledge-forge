# G_ig_heyharut_DcDOCskR4Hb — time study

## Term

Time study is the exercise Harut prescribes for deciding what to delegate first: every day, every 30 minutes, put a timer on, and after it rings write down exactly what you were doing, and do it for a week. The phrase appears once in this source, beside least amount of the money, most amount of time, and hire someone, and the result is a list of what takes the most time while making the least money, starting with the first task on it. He calls it a very uncomfortable exercise but one of the most useful you can do in your life, because it shows exactly where your time goes. A time study here is a week of half-hour logs, not a guess about being busy.

## Live Example

> "So you do a time study. What should I delegate first? Things that make you the least amount of the money and take the most amount of time." — sources/S_ig_heyharut_DcDOCskR4Hb.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a productivity app: the study is a timer and a notebook for a week, so no tool is needed and no tool is the point.
- Read as advice to delegate everything: the study ranks tasks by time against money, so only the tasks that cost the most time for the least money go first.
