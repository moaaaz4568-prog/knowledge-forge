# T_CrTU-PkuaGf_01

"A big mistake I made earlier on in my business career is romanticizing business ideas. As it turns out, ideas are a terrible and pernicious thing. They're free flowing, very attached to your ego, and literally have no meaning until you connect the idea to an actual real world problem. It's much easier and more effective to start with the problem when we're talking about business ideas." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrTU-PkuaGf.md

# T_CrTU-PkuaGf_02

"And in order to find a worthwhile problem, you first need to know who you're serving. People, businesses, and markets are riddled with existing problems that other people are already trying to solve. So if we know who we're serving, then we need to isolate and identify the problem we want to solve, and then we clarify how we state it, because a problem well stated is half solved." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrTU-PkuaGf.md

# T_CrTU-PkuaGf_03

"You wanna find and uncover problems that are unworkable, unavoidable, urgent, and underserved. Then you ideate around the best way in which that problem can be solved for that market. Then you wrap it in a value proposition. If you're looking to start a new business, fuck your ideas and go find problems." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrTU-PkuaGf.md

# T_CrTU-PkuaGf_04

"If you have an existing business and you're trying to figure out how to make more money and build new products, stop looking for ideas and just go talk to your customers. They already have problems, they'll tell you what they are, and your job is to just solve the next one." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrTU-PkuaGf.md
