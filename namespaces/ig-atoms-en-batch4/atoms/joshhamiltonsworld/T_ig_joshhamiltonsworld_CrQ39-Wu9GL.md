# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_01

"When it comes to business, do what the market wants, not what you want. So something I learned pretty early on is the principle of channeling, not creating desire. You cannot create desire. You must find the existing desire and do one of two things. First, you put your offer in front of that existing desire." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrQ39-Wu9GL.md

# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_02

"Second, you can channel that existing desire into your offer by good marketing and message. Let's go down both of those paths. The first path is a direct service desire that you can place an offer in front of. For example, if we're tapping into an existing desire, the market desires good copywriting." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrQ39-Wu9GL.md

# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_03

"You can do the market research on existing copywriting services and there's an existing demand for copywriting. You model what works, which markets are underserved and undervalued, but you make it better, either faster or higher quality, more comprehensive, productized, and you monetize that direct service desire." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrQ39-Wu9GL.md

# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_04

"This is doing what the market already wants, just in a differentiated way through your model and through your delivery and through your experience. The second path is an indirect outcome desire that you can create a new opportunity for. For example, for channeling an existing desire into a new opportunity, let's say the market desires financial location freedom." — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrQ39-Wu9GL.md

# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_05

"Now that's not a service, that is a desire, an outcome desire. You place those copywriters in companies and take a piece of the recruitment fee. That's great, right?" — sources/S_ig_joshhamiltonsworld_CrQ39-Wu9GL.md

# T_CrQ39-Wu9GL_06
