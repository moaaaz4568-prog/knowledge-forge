# T_DdgVRgYMpll_01

"The biggest false belief in creative strategy and meta ads is that the unaware market is always the largest and most scalable portion of the market. The best example to illustrate this is creatine. Let's say you want to make some unaware ads for creatine and try to sell to people who don't already know what it is." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DdgVRgYMpll.md

# T_DdgVRgYMpll_02

"That would be completely unwise in my opinion because most of the market for creatine is going to already know what it is and they probably already tried some type of creatine. So the best angles for that market are likely going to be solution aware or problem aware. Which is why you see offers like creatine gummies and different ways of taking creatine popping up left and right." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DdgVRgYMpll.md

# T_DdgVRgYMpll_03

"The reason this matters is because if you're going to go waste time and money trying to test and scale unaware ads, they're going to have a higher CAC than problem and solution aware ads and they're going to be way harder to get right. You might be going down the completely wrong path." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DdgVRgYMpll.md

# T_DdgVRgYMpll_04

"Way easier to find ads that work in this market, you might just have a false belief that they're not going to scale. Like you might just be incorrectly thinking that because an ad is deeper in the funnel into problem or solution aware type people, that it's not going to spend as much money. But that can be the largest market." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DdgVRgYMpll.md

# T_DdgVRgYMpll_05

"So again, before you start trying to make ads targeting the most unaware people, try to think about your actual customers and what they currently think about the product. You don't need to go too unaware too quickly." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DdgVRgYMpll.md
