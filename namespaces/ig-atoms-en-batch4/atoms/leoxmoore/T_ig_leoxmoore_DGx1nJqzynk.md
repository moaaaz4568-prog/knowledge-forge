# T_DGx1nJqzynk_01

"Here's how to create about 120 ad variations in not that much time. It sounds like a lot, but what you need to do is just create variations of each segment of the ad and then mix and match those segments to make a full ad. So what you want to do is you want to split up the ad into the hook, the lead, the offer and the close. The hook is obviously the first five seconds of the ad." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DGx1nJqzynk.md

# T_DGx1nJqzynk_02

"The lead is what connects the hook to the offer. Quite often that'll just be explaining why it's important or why certain prospects need to listen to you. So the lead will often include things like the problem, the proof you have and why it's important pretty much. The offer will obviously just be explaining what it is that you do and how you're gonna help them." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DGx1nJqzynk.md

# T_DGx1nJqzynk_03

"And the close will include some objection handling and calls to action. So if you create 10 hooks, then you create three leads, then you create two offer sections of the ad and two close sections of the ad, that creates 120 variations. So you'll have ads that are like hook one, lead one, offer one, close one, and you'll have ads that are hook 10, lead one, offer two, close two." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DGx1nJqzynk.md

# T_DGx1nJqzynk_04

"You can use AI to generate all the variations and then you can literally just get a VA to mix and match all the clips. So if you want to scale an ad and you need to test more creative, I would 100% recommend this. Just write a bunch of hooks, write three leads, write two offer sections and two closes and you pretty much have all the ads that you'll need on a given month." — sources/S_ig_leoxmoore_DGx1nJqzynk.md
