The short answer to how I think about failure is that I don't. I never have once thought about what if this fails, what if it goes negatively. And I think the biggest thing is completely removing the self-judgment and the negative feelings that come with failure, and also like the loss in status that happens when you do fail. So like when you fail, you feel bad, you judge yourself, you feel like you're not good enough, you feel like people look at you differently, and all this stuff, like you almost feel evil. I'm bad and I'm less than because I didn't achieve X. That's what kind of the feedback loop is. But if you kind of get rid of that and you replace it with a positive feedback loop of if I fail a million times, I'm probably going to be closer to the mark than I was the first time. And you see that as if I just get a little bit closer every time, as long as I fail really quickly, as long as I get that failure out of the way, I'm going to eventually hit it.
