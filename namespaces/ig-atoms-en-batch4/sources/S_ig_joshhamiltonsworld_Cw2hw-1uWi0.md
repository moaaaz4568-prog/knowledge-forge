You ever ask a successful person for advice and you get a frustratingly simple answer? That's gold. Mine it. See, I've learned something big over the years. The single greatest signal of my own progression has been a shift toward simplicity. I used to make these big, detailed, dozen-step plans with countless components. I felt smart just making them. But here's the thing: complex plans can trick us. We feel we're doing a lot or planning to do a lot, but are we actually getting the result? Doing a task is good, but getting the right result is better. The best pros, they find the most effective way to get things done. No extra steps, no wasted moves. Now, the simple approach only comes from experience, from trying, failing, learning. Breaking things down to their first principles and asking, what can we remove and still get the same or better result? Or my favorite question, which I'm asking a lot lately: what are the few actions that, if executed for long enough, make success an eventuality? So when planning or seeking advice, aim for simple. Make a plan so basic any idiot could execute it, then go do so. Remember, if a plan requires luck, magic, or a table of contents, it's a weak plan.
