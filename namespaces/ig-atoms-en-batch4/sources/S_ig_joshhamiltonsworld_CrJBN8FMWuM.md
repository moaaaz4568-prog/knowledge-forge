Rational optimism. This is a term that I first saw articulated in the Almanack of Naval Ravikant, a book I just keep on my desk at all times. It's a handbook. It goes like this. If you're rational, you see the world the way it is, not how you want it to be. And if you're optimistic, you're bringing confidence to your work and are optimistic about your capabilities and your capability to get things done. You got one life on this planet, so why not try to build something worthwhile, but also accepting the fact that doing even the smallest of things takes a herculean amount of effort and time to achieve. So think big, but be rational about achieving it. The absence of the rational part is where I see a lot of problems in the coaching business, especially on Instagram, where it's all you can do it, but very little here's how you actually do it. Business is the lens through which I build brotherhood and bonds. I look for rational optimists to do business and life with. The reason is actually threefold. The first is action affinity. Rational optimists have a bias toward action. They just do stuff and they learn from trial and error. They take the path with the most promise. So it's easiest to discover if something is viable by just doing it. That leads to iteration, which is doing the same thing with slight variations to find out what works. The second reason is around conflict resolution. Rational optimists can be positive and honest at the same time. You're looking for the positives in reality. If you find a way to tell the truth while respecting the integrity of the other person, you usually find yourself as a friend and as a worthwhile partner. Some people just get insulted by the truth no matter how you deliver it, and I recommend distancing yourself from those people as fast as possible. Number three is risk analysis. The functional core of why I choose to be rationally optimistic is my ability to analyze risk. Rational optimists are able to make asymmetric bets, bets where the upside is unlimited, but the downside is capped. Pessimists often overanalyze the downside. They can convince themselves that the risk is too high on all opportunities and usually end up doing nothing. And I know so many brilliant people that are too smart for their own good and they overanalyze that downside and ignore the upside.
