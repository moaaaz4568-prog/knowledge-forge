Failing to do this one stupid simple thing actually killed one of my businesses. Skill crossover, mis expectations and false assumptions are a co founder's worst enemy in my experience. One of the worst mistakes I've made in the past is ignoring the importance of a documented areas of responsibility or AOR. It's basically just a spreadsheet document with rows detailing the different areas of the business that each co founder or head of department will own. Areas that are required for the business to actually run. There's supposed to be one name beside each of the areas of responsibility. Most often what happens is there's two or three and there's crossover here and there. It gets to be a bit of a mess. I found that the wrong way to do this is to partner with somebody and then figure out all this later. How I'm approaching it now is a little bit different. First, I outline all the areas of responsibility required for the business and model to thrive. Number two is I put my name beside the areas that I'm best at and the areas that I want to own, my zone of genius. And then I select co founders based off of their complementary skill sets and their ability to fill the gaps of responsibility left over. Typically you have promotion, product, and people, or you have growth, delivery, and operations. Three main buckets. Obviously those buckets can break down into different areas, but somebody needs to own each of those areas. Maybe you own two at first or all three at first if you're solo, but eventually like those three buckets need to be broken out and people need to own those divisions. And this is how business works. It works because of people. So don't do what I did and go kill your business because you sleep on this.
