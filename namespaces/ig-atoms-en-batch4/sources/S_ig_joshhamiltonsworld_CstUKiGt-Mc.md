Over the past year, I've become obsessed with the concept of never losing a customer. So if you think about it, it's the greatest business growth strategy out there. If you just never lose a customer, then it's very difficult to lose money, especially when you're running a recurring revenue business. So when you add a customer, you just add, you're not worried about losing them, right? It's a great fantasy. As it turns out, there's certain metrics and KPIs that can tell you how well you're doing in the customer success department. Here are seven metrics and the formulas to go with that I'm personally keeping a close eye on this year. Number one is net dollar retention or NDR. This tells you how much your business has grown or shrunk over a period of time. It's based on your new clients acquired and clients churned. The NDR formula is starting MRR plus expansion minus contraction minus customer churn divided by starting MRR equals NDR. Number two, average customer lifespan. So this tells you how long your customers stick around before they churn. The ACL formula is one month or year divided by your churn rate. Number three is average purchase value or APV. This is how much each customer is actually paying you. The APV formula is just total revenue divided by the number of orders or the number of sales. Number four is gross revenue retention or GRR. This measures how much revenue you're retaining from your customers. The formula is churn recurring revenue plus downgraded recurring revenue divided by the starting recurring revenue. That equals your GRR. Number five is customer retention rate. This goes together with GRR. This calculates the percentage of customers who keep paying you month to month. So lower customer churn rate is higher GRR. The formula is one minus customers lost in the period divided by customers at the start of the period. Number six is customer satisfaction score or CSAT. This measures how happy customers are with your service or your product. It's a decent indicator of how good your product or service is, and a high CSAT score equals lower churn rates, etc. Usually measure this by surveys, and so the formula is really just number of satisfied customers divided by total number of responders multiplied by 100. The last one, which is super important for economics, is your customer retention costs or your CRC. This tracks how much it costs to keep customers or retain them inside of your business, which has to do with gross margins. So the CRC formula is total cost of customer success plus retention activities divided by the number of active customers. So those are the seven KPIs that I'm keeping watch of this year, and so should you.
