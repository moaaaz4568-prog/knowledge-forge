Here are three things I've noticed separate the filmmakers who work with big brands versus the ones who don't. First, nothing beats certainty. You don't need the perfect idea, you need the conviction in the one you're presenting. Clients aren't judging if your vision is right, they are judging if you believe it is. If you're unsure, they're going to feel it too and they're going to move on to someone who isn't. Second, move fast with what you know. Big brands usually reach out last minute and they're comparing you against a few other people. The filmmaker who replies fast, is flexible to hop on a call, and sends a clear execution plan is the one that gets picked. So if you take a week to respond, that means that you've already lost the job. And number three, send one sharp offer. I used to offer clients options, different price packages, creative concepts, and crew sizes. But options don't feel like flexibility. They're actually more work to the client to figure out what they need. Filmmakers who learn how to diagnose the problem and send one clear direction that says here's what we're doing, why, when, and how, get hired more often and get paid more for it.
