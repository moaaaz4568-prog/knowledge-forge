We work with 400 plus businesses, and without a doubt, the scariest times in business are when you don't know what you need to do to grow. And probably one of the best and easiest way to fix that I learned at one of Alex Hormozi's workshops back in about February of 2024. It actually did teach me one thing that was pretty transformational for me in my career. And that thing was the theory of constraints. Basically, the idea is you just look at basically the pipeline of your business from sales to delivery to operations, marketing, all that stuff, and you just think, what is the thing that's stopping me from getting the exact result that I want? So for example, if I want to hit a million dollars a month, what is the thing that's constraining me from achieving that? And likely there's gonna be a few things, but what's the biggest thing that's constraining me? Then pretty much the idea is that you just focus on that individual thing and fix that before you fix anything else. But the thing that most people get wrong when they do this, including me when I first started trying to do it, is you'll go ahead and identify maybe one constraint that you think is just gonna be the solution to your entire business. And then when you forecast that out and you understand that, oh, if I try and fix the wrong constraint, that's gonna be three to six to 12 months of completely lost progress that if I had just found the right thing in the first place, it would have been avoided. So when you start going down this rabbit hole of really understanding the importance of decision making, the number one takeaway that I really got as a flow on effect of those earlier lessons is the need for the right data in my business. And also not just the right data as in tracking the right numbers, but also in the precision of those numbers. Because for example, you might have a way of tracking show rate, of tracking close rate, of tracking your ad metrics and all those things. But if those things aren't precise and organized in a level of hygiene, you're gonna be absolutely screwed because you're gonna make these decisions that are impacting your 12, 24, 36 month goals based on shit that's not even true. It's like having a compass that is broken and you're using that compass to decide where to go, but the compass is fucking janky and it's pointing you in the wrong direction. So if there's one thing I wish I did earlier in my business, it was getting data that is true, accurate, precise so that I can make decisions easily. Because once you know what to do, you can pretty much figure it out. The problem is when you don't know what the constraint actually is.
