Quick tip from my vault of lessons hard learned: information does not equal utility. Just because I'm educating people doesn't mean it's useful. That's why the first filter I use in my actionable bucket of content and my products I build is, is this useful? Does this have utility? Specifically meaning, can somebody consume this and gain some insight or instruction they didn't already have to take an action they had not already taken? I found that learning about something does not make it valuable. What's important is what somebody can do with that knowledge immediately that gives it utility. And the value comes from experiencing the results of implementing that utility. So if you have a course product, for example, your question should be, how can I make this as short as possible while giving my students every tool they need to shrink the gap between start and success? Now what that maximizes is a metric called TTV or time to value, which is the time it takes for somebody to experience your value of your product or content for the first time.
