Having an ad budget is one of the dumbest rules in marketing, and I know that sounds weird, but budget assumes that ads are a cost, but ads are not a cost if your funnel is making money. The only reason people set budgets is because they do not know their number. So last week I asked one of our clients, the founder, why they arbitrarily capped ad spend at $100, 000 a month. The response was, "That's our budget. " Now I looked at the data, and for every dollar they spent, they got back $2. 40 within 22 days of initially spending that dollar. So by arbitrarily capping ad spend at 100, 000, they were literally turning off profit because a spreadsheet told them to based off of some arbitrary ROAS data that one of their counterparts gave them. We need to stop asking, "What's our ads budget? " and start asking, "How fast do we get our money back? " One of the only rules that matters is your payback period, your cost to acquire versus cash collected within those first 30 days. If you get your money back that you spent in under 30 days and your profit margins are healthy, you're not really spending money, you're recycling it. It's house money. And when a funnel is profitable, the answer to ad spend is not necessarily be careful or this is our roof. The answer is more. Instead of just constantly trying to optimize this and optimize this and optimize this, eventually you got to start maximizing. The answer to budget is more until one of these three things break. Payback gets longer, which causes cash flow issues in the business.
