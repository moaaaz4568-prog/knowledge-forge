# G_yt_bB_CM418O70_en — extrinsic motivation

## Term

The speaker ranks the weakest types of motivation as extrinsic motivation — doing something for a reward or because of other people. Extrinsic motivation appears once, set against the things that actually move him: crossing items off a list, and the difficult things that really matter, which he would rather procrastinate and avoid. His wider point is that big changes are more sustainable when you find balance, not isolation, and checking his phone in bed — scrolling the feed and wasting the entire morning — is the habit the whole routine is built to break.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The weakest types are extrinsic motivation, like doing something for a reward or because of other people, right ?" — sources/S_yt_bB_CM418O70_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as rewards never work: the speaker calls extrinsic motivation the weakest type, not a useless one.
- Read as a claim about everyone: he frames it as his own finding about what sustains his changes.
