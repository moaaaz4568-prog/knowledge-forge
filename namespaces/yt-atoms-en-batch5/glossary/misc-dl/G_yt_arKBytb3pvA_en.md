# G_yt_arKBytb3pvA_en — no-shows

## Term

No-shows are the speaker's extraordinary problem lately: prospects who book a call and never appear, named as the specific trouble behind a wider sales worry. The phrase appears twice, beside extraordinary problem, lately, and specifically, in a discussion of dealership show rates. The contrast is the dealers with the best show rates, whose teams call back within 20 seconds. The speaker's fix is a setter for the 2,500 prospects who never showed up or showed up and did not buy — and he warns against copying the high-volume dealers who require people to answer the call before booking, a tactic only safe at a million dollars in sales a month.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "So, we've had an extraordinary problem lately , I don't know if it's exactly what it is , but specifically our no-shows." — sources/S_yt_arKBytb3pvA_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as people who never booked: a no-show here is someone who booked and did not appear, which is why the 2,500 prospects are a follow-up list, not a cold list.
- Read as a small annoyance: the speaker calls it an extraordinary problem and ties it to the speed of decision making, which he calls terrible — it is the bottleneck, not a footnote.
