# G_yt_cboXyBcJ2NY_en — prole drift

## Term

Prole drift is the speaker's named danger, and he presents it as the worst consequence to avoid when choosing where to live. Prole drift appears three times, next to high-trust enclaves, low cost-of-living areas and welfare states, and the speaker argues from an American perspective while insisting it is a global problem. His example is the low cost-of-living area with a generous welfare state: it attracts people who experience more prole drift because they can make it financially viable to be there. The defence he offers is mobility itself, the ability to call a broker and buy a house in another city or country when a community goes downhill.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a slur about poor people: prole drift here is the speaker's term for low trust behaviors spreading in a community, not a judgement of any one person.
- Read as a local problem: the speaker says it is global, and his American examples are only the ones he knows best.
