# G_yt_bDDlyRw3tW0_en — soft landing

## Term

In the 1994 rate hike cycle, the speaker notes, the economy had a soft landing and the Fed timed managing that well. Soft landing appears once, as one episode inside a tour of the great economic booms — the Soviet Union's collapse completing the official defeat of communism, ERP systems like SAP and Oracle going mainstream, deregulation in telecom and financial services, most notably the repeal of Glass-Steagall. The same period, he adds, is when real wages stopped growing and the gap was filled with expanded leverage and credit. The landing names a managed slowdown that avoided a crash.

> "Even like when they did have a major rate hike cycle in 1994, the economy had a soft landing and the Fed timed managing that well." — sources/S_yt_bDDlyRw3tW0_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as no pain at all: a soft landing is a rate-hike cycle that avoided a crash, not a boom without cost.
- Read as the Fed's doing alone: the speaker credits the timing of the management, inside a decade of deregulation and wage stagnation.
