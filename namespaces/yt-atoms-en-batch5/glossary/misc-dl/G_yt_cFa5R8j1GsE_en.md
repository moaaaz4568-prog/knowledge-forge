# G_yt_cFa5R8j1GsE_en — tariffs

## Term

The cost of the tariffs, the speaker says, is not going to be instantly passed on to the consumer, for a variety of reasons — and the talk is about what happens next. Tariffs appears twenty-four times as the policy under review: budget deficits above five percent of GDP lead governments to tighten the deficit to show fiscal credibility, to prevent inflation or a potential fiscal crisis. The same talk weighs the dollar and the market: if the economy is good, revenue rises, but in a recession revenue drops even faster than income tax revenue, and if the US goes back to two-thousand levels — half of all the world's market cap — that leaves a lot of rotate room away from US stocks and into international stocks.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a tax on you: the tariffs here are not instantly passed to the consumer, and the talk is about the delay.
- Read as the deficit being the tariff: the deficit and the tariff are different pressures; the deficit forces the tightening.
