# G_yt_bNQ76-HjoDs_en — tailwind

## Term

When housing is relatively affordable, the speaker says, it is a lot bigger of a tailwind than it would be in the United States. Tailwind appears once, as the push an affordable-housing era gives an economy: the help started in the '80s, but in the '90s is when the fruit of that truly came into fruition. The same era, he notes, is when inequality really started to accelerate in the UK — the tailwind and the inequality are two readings of the same period, and the speaker holds both.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "So, when you have an era where housing is relatively affordable, it's a lot bigger of a tailwind than it would be in the United States." — sources/S_yt_bNQ76-HjoDs_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a forecast: it describes a past era's push, not a prediction.
- Read as housing being good for everyone: the speaker pairs the tailwind with accelerating inequality in the same breath.
