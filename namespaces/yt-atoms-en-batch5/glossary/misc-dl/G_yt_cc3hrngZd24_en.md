# G_yt_cc3hrngZd24_en — psychology of achievements

## Term

The psychology of achievements is the lesson the speaker takes from Brian Tracy, and he defines it as the idea that success and failure both stem from the self-concept. Psychology of achievements appears three times, next to self-concept, compass and unlimited realities, and the speaker illustrates it with a planted teacher who conditioned average students to believe they were high achievers. His example is the class that became one of the best performing in the university, solely because the professor made the students think they were the best. Psychology of achieving is the same lesson in the form the speaker uses when he names what Tracy talks about.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as positive thinking: the psychology of achievements here is about the self-concept setting real boundaries, not about wishing.
- Read as a guarantee: the speaker says winners expect rather than hope, but the expectation is a stance, not a proof the result will come.
