# G_yt_b0drEg4TqC0_en — reverse engineer

## Term

To reverse engineer here is to take someone's success apart into the path and the steps they took, and then walk that path yourself instead of jumping to the end. The phrase appears once, beside success, path, and steps, in a video about what it takes to be worth a hundred thousand. The speaker's warning is the shortcut that fails: people try to jump to the end and ask why it is not working for them, when they have not done the steps. The sweet spot, he says, comes from repetition — repeated exposure to the same problem, trial and error, learning and applying best practices — and the process repeats at each new level.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "we have to reverse engineer their success and quite literally the path and the steps that they took so often what we try to do is we try to jump to the end and say well why isn't working for me well you haven't done" — sources/S_yt_b0drEg4TqC0_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as copying the result: reverse engineering here means recovering the steps and the path, and the speaker says jumping to the end is exactly why it fails.
- Read as a one-time analysis: the process is a loop — each level is reached by repeating the same exposure, trial, and practice on the next problem.
