# G_yt_awe5yCp7mjA_en — surface level thinking

## Term

Surface level thinking is what a real designer does not do: the speaker opens with it as the shallow approach, and a real designer takes things a bit deeper than that. The phrase appears once, beside real designer, deeper, and take things, in a design lesson built around an urban inspiration board. The deeper work is shown in the same video — a template board that turns a brand from a design into an actual language once it stays consistent. The contrast is the whole lesson: the surface is where the thinking stops, and the brand only becomes a language when the thinking goes past it.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

> "That is just surface level thinking because a real designer will take things a bit deeper than that." — sources/S_yt_awe5yCp7mjA_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a beginner's mistake: surface level thinking is presented as the default approach anyone can fall into, and the video's job is to show the deeper move past it.
- Read as a style choice: the speaker treats depth as the difference between a designer and a decorator — the board and the brand language are the evidence, not the decoration.
