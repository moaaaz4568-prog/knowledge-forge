# G_yt_bDB2bBk_Ujs_en — randomization

## Term

At two, three or four thousand emails per day, the speaker says you need good randomization — or a lot of variation — because sending five or ten thousand identical or very similar emails makes them start to deviate before they even get into the inbox. Randomization appears three times as the guardrail of high-volume cold emailing: inexpensive and useful even for beginners, but 10, 20, 50 or 100 meetings per month demand high volumes, and high volumes demand flexibility with the numbers. The same caution runs through the whole talk — an email with no reply or booking does not necessarily mean something is broken.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "I see that at the level of two, three, four thousand emails per day, if you don't have good randomization or, say, a lot of variation, and you just send 5 or 10 thousand identical or very similar emails, they will start to deviate before they even get into the inbox ." — sources/S_yt_bDB2bBk_Ujs_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as send more of the same: the speaker's point is that identical volume is what gets filtered out.
- Read as a deliverability guarantee: randomization keeps varied mail from deviating early; it does not promise replies.
