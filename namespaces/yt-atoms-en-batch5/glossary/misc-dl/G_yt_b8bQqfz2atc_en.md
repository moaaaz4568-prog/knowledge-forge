# G_yt_b8bQqfz2atc_en — brain drain

## Term

Portugal's graduates move to other parts of Europe where there is more economic opportunity, and the speaker names the pattern a brain drain — a spiral of the best academically talented people leaving. Brain drain appears once, at the end of a chain: the country kept pace with OECD averages for college graduation rates, yet more than 2 million Portuguese, about 20 percent of the population, now live outside the country, in Spain, Luxembourg, Belgium, Switzerland, Germany and the UK. The word names the loss of the educated young, not emigration in general.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as all emigration: the speaker reserves it for the academically talented leaving for opportunity elsewhere.
- Read as a cause: in the source it is the result of the opportunity gap, the spiral that follows.
