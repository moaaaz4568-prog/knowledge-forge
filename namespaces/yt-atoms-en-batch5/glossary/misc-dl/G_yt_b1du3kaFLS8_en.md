# G_yt_b1du3kaFLS8_en — color scheme

## Term

A color scheme here is the small set of colours a design is built from: having one is a great idea for your design work, and two to four colours is the ideal number. The phrase appears twice, beside prototyper, design work, and two to four, in a list of numbered tips for graphic designers. The speaker's rule is restraint — a few colours, chosen deliberately, rather than a new colour for every element. The same list's other discipline is simplicity: a simple and refined design does not have to be boring, which the speaker calls a misconception worth correcting.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as any colours in a design: the scheme here is a deliberate set of two to four colours, chosen for the design work, not whatever colours happen to appear.
- Read as a trend: the speaker presents the scheme as a basic working rule for anyone becoming a prototyper, beside aligning text left and keeping simple designs engaging.
