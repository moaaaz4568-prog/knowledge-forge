# G_yt_b2wSN1w5QH4_en — storyboard

## Term

A storyboard here is how Cooper describes a scene before it exists: the way the birds and the fluttering of their wings, the sound that would make, and how it would cast a shadow onto the typography, or how someone opening a door would look. The word appears once, beside Cooper, describe, and birds, in a video about what separates a working designer from someone with the same drawing skills. The speaker's point is that drawing the Nike logo, the Apple logo, or the new Verizon logo proves nothing — the creator was paid a lot for something else. The storyboard is the example of that something else: describing motion, sound, and shadow so the design can be felt before it is made.

- Read as a sketch: the storyboard here is a spoken description of movement and sound — wings, shadow, a door opening — not a drawing on paper.
- Read as a beginner's tool: Cooper's storyboard is presented as the mark of the designer who gets paid, the thing that separates the professional from the person who can merely draw a logo.
