# G_yt_alisJIebfkk_en — meat packers

## Term

Meat packers here are the four buyers every rancher's animal has to pass through, and the source blames them for the squeeze on ranchers: with fewer packers to sell to, they can collude and pay less for cattle while still charging more at retail. The phrase appears five times, beside four buyers, squeeze, ranchers, and retail, in an account of the lowest herd size since 1955. The packers sit in the middle of the rancher's cycle — when grain prices rise, ranchers slaughter early because feeding costs more than the extra fattening gains. The speaker's contrast is chicken, which replaced beef as the primary meat in the early 2010s because it stayed more affordable.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "And every animal a rancher raises basically has to pass through one of these four buyers, and that creates a huge squeeze because the ranchers have fewer meat packers to sell to, the packers can collude and pay less for the cattle, while still charging more at retail." — sources/S_yt_alisJIebfkk_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the ranchers themselves: the packers are the buyers between rancher and retailer, and the squeeze comes from ranchers having only four of them to sell to.
- Read as a free market with many buyers: the source's point is the opposite — four buyers control the passage of every animal, which is why they can pay less and charge more.
