# G_yt_-HDvfJW28Hg_en — properties panel

## Term

The tutor uses "properties panel" for the Illustrator CC panel that puts almost everything in one single location — the panel that can literally save heaps of time for a graphic design workflow. The phrase appears 14 times, across a tour of the panel Adobe revamped in Illustrator CC 2018 to 2019: you open it from the Window menu, and the first thing to know is that its interface and options change depending on what you have selected. Its neighbours are "artboard", "fill", "stroke", and "appearance panel": with nothing selected the settings relate to the document itself, with a shape selected you can add fill and stroke, change the opacity, and add effects, and even the appearance panel lives inside the properties panel. The concrete proof is the click: when you click a square on the artboard, the options in the properties panel instantly change, because you can literally find almost everything within one single panel.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a fixed toolbar: in this video the properties panel is context-sensitive — its interface and options change with the selection, from document setup with nothing selected to paragraph settings with text selected — so it is not one static set of controls.
- Read as a shortcut for experts only: the tutor's point is the opposite — the panel saves heaps of time precisely because everything sits in one easy-to-access single location, and the video ends by asking viewers to start using it in their workflow.
