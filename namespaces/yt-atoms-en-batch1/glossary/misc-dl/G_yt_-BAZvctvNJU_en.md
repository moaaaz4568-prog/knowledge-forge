# G_yt_-BAZvctvNJU_en — paid to pitch

## Term

The speaker uses "paid to pitch" for the better way top agencies work: instead of sending proposals and getting ghosted, they get paid to pitch and close bigger deals way faster. The phrase appears 1 time, in the video's opening promise: stop working for free — if you are tired of getting ghosted on proposals, the endless follow-ups, and "we'll think about it" as a response, there is a better way. Its neighbours are "proposals", "ghosted", "follow-ups", and "capacity": the top agencies are not sending proposals at all, and the video later talks about what to do when you do not have the capacity because you are maxed out. The concrete contrast is the free pitch: working for free means proposals, follow-ups, and silence, while getting paid to pitch means the client pays for the thinking before the deal closes.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as charging for proposals: in this video the top agencies are not sending proposals at all — getting paid to pitch replaces the proposal, it does not put a price tag on the same document.
- Read as a promise of instant bigger deals: the claim is comparative — bigger deals, way faster, than the proposal-and-ghosting route — and the video's own framing is a free strategy resource at agencymastery360.com/strategy, not a guarantee.
