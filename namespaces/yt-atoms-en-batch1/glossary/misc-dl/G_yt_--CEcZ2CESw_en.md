# G_yt_--CEcZ2CESw_en — outliers

## Term

Create Content Club uses "outliers" for the videos that earn five to 10 times the average views of their account — the speaker's working definition of a viral content idea, applied first to competitors' accounts and then to the creator's own profile. The word appears 12 times, always tied to spotting rather than guessing: the first strategy finds outliers on medium-sized accounts in the niche, and the second strategy finds them on the creator's own profile after 100 posts. Its neighbours are "average views", "hook", "format", and "topic": once an outlier is spotted, the creator writes down its hook, its format, and its topic, then mixes and matches those three elements into new videos. The concrete payoff the speaker promises is that anyone who posts 100 videos will almost certainly have five outliers, and those five are enough raw material to generate unlimited new content ideas.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as any video with a big view count: in this source an outlier is strictly relative — five to 10 times that account's own average — so a 5,000-view video on a 500-view-average account counts, while a 100,000-view video on a million-follower account may not.
- Read as a guarantee of going viral: the source treats outliers as proven raw material to mix and match, not as a promise — the creator still has to test new combinations of hook, format, and topic, and the speaker warns that formats die out and audiences tire of repeats.
