# G_yt_-FSIq213UJE_en — methodology

## Term

John Jantsch uses "methodology" for his Duct Tape Marketing way of doing marketing for small to mid-size businesses — a simple, straightforward, systematic approach, distilled in his 2007 book. The word appears 7 times, across an interview about the agency he started about 30 years ago: the book was really a distillation of his methodology, how he thought marketing should be done for small to mid-size businesses, and a lot of other people agreed. Its neighbours are "small to mid-size businesses", "license", "know, like, and trust", and "AI": the methodology is practiced in his agency today and licensed to well over 400 agencies, consultants, and fractional CMOs, and the job it serves is getting someone who has a need to know, like, and trust you enough to exchange money for what you promised to deliver. The concrete advantage is the small-business one: smaller businesses have a real advantage if they actually choose to leverage it and use it, rather than trying to play at looking bigger.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a generic process: in this interview the methodology is a specific, named thing — Duct Tape Marketing, a book from 2007, licensed to over 400 agencies — not any agency's internal process.
- Read as a growth hack for looking big: John's own point is the opposite — smaller businesses have a real advantage when they leverage being small, and the methodology's job is trust (know, like, and trust), not looking bigger.
