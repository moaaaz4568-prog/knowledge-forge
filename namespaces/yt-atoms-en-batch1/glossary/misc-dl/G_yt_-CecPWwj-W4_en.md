# G_yt_-CecPWwj-W4_en — paradox of thrift

## Term

Nick uses "paradox of thrift" for the idea that raising the savings rate may be good for an individual, a family, or a business, but hurts the economy when everybody does it at once. The phrase appears 11 times, as the video's whole topic: if everybody as a whole decided to save money, it would hurt the economy — and if everybody decided to shop at home for an entire month, it would be the equivalent of having the lockdown in 2020. Its neighbours are "savings rate", "macro level", "micro level", and "restaurant": Nick questions whether the paradox is really as strong as it looks, because saving does not necessarily mean the money is not being put to use — a bank should lend it out to borrowers — and paying down debt is negative lending. The concrete verdict is the mid level: the paradox makes the most sense not on the micro level and not on the macro level, but on the mid level, like the restaurant example, where everybody in town deciding to save would destroy a business.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a rule against saving: Nick's main counter-argument is that saving can help the economy grow when you act as an investor instead of a consumer, and that paying interest on debt is money lost out of the system — so the paradox is not a ban on saving.
- Read as a macro law: he concludes the paradox makes the most sense on the mid level, not the micro or macro level, and only in an economy with excess savings — in an economy without excess savings it does not make sense.
