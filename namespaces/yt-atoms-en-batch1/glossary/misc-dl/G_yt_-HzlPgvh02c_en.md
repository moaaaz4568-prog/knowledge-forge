# G_yt_-HzlPgvh02c_en — proof of concept

## Term

The creator uses "proof of concept" for the paid evidence that people want the product — a sale made before anything is built. The phrase appears 13 times, across a video about her brand-new business built on a YouTube channel: she started in January with 354 subscribers, made a couple of coaching sales, had a couple of people join her brand-new school community, and did a total of 3,244 pounds — and she is currently in what you call proof of concept phase. Its neighbours are "product", "beta offer", "triangle", and "pointless churn": step one is the product, and you need proof of concept before you build it; the beta offer is a paid beta offer, because you cannot get proof of concept if it is free; and without it you are pointlessly churning away at something. The concrete rule is the question: does anyone want it — that is what you need to figure out before you build anything, so you put a post out, have conversations, speak with people, make an offer, make a sale, and then build it from there.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a free pilot: in this video you cannot get proof of concept if it is free — anybody will take anything for free — so the proof has to be a paid beta offer, a real sale, not a signup.
- Read as building first and validating later: her rule is the reverse — do not waste time building before you have the proof, sell something you have not created yet, and build it while it is flying — the proof comes from the sale, not from the product.
