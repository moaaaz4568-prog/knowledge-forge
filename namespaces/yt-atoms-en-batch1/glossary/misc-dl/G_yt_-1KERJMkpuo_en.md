# G_yt_-1KERJMkpuo_en — world view

## Term

Jonathan Lewis uses "world view" for the frame an AI — or a company — needs before it can do useful work: what you see, what matters most, what you put at the top. The phrase appears 8 times in three forms, "world view" 4 times, "worldview" 3 times, and "world views" once, as the top of McKe Wallwork's 2018 move upstream: from making things, to writing the prompt, to defining the world view the prompt serves. Its neighbours are "AI", "tribe", "prompt", and "The Rebuilders": an LLM asked deep questions without knowing you starts from the average of all human knowledge, a tribe needs a world view to cohere, and his book The Rebuilders is about owners taking the rebuilder identity in a cultural malaise. The concrete payoff is the training rule Jonathan gives: first train your AI companion on your world view, so all of its work comes back framed within how you see the world — and that, he says, is a sustainable, anti-fragile business model.
