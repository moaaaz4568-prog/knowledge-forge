# G_yt_-I4UsbshJCk_en — rich black

## Term

The tutor uses "rich black" for the layered black of print design — K combined with the other C, M, and Y colors — which has a deep tone that pops from the page while regular black can appear flat. The phrase appears 4 times, as the first of the quick Satori tips for a perfect print every single time: color variations of black can be very difficult to detect on screen, but in print the difference shows. Its neighbours are "standard black", "CMYK", "body text", and "headings": standard black is printed strictly with the black toner, the K cartridge, and his own rich black recipe is CMYK values of 50, 40, 40, 100. The concrete rule is the split: use rich black for large areas of black and large uses of typography such as headings, and use standard black on body text, because rich black can create a loss of crispness on smaller fonts and detail.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a richer-looking black for everything: in this video rich black is only for large areas and headings — on body text it loses crispness on smaller fonts, so standard black is the rule there, not the exception.
- Read as a screen setting: the whole tip is about print — the variations are very difficult to detect on screen — so rich black only matters when the design is printed, and the CMYK values are the recipe.
