# G_yt_-C01FC27Jg0_en — major goal

## Term

Chris uses "major goal" for the outcome worth a major effort — the client's real problem with real money behind it — set against the minor goal that deserves only a minor effort. The phrase appears 2 times counting its opposite: "major goal" once and "minor goal" once, in the one lesson of the session: you do not want to spend millions of dollars to buy a suit, and conversely you do not want to make a minor effort for a major goal. Its neighbours are "minor effort", "live client", "2,000 pounds", and "branding": the client is live, meaning they have a real problem to solve and real money, they are going to pick somebody, and the branding budget on the table is 2,000 pounds. The concrete question is the close: do you stand to lose millions of dollars of business, and does 2,000 pounds seem an appropriate amount when every single day it is going to cost you money.

## Synonyms

- minor goal (opposite form used in this source)

> "just give me the room with a live client a live client meaning they have a real problem they need to solve and they have real money and they're gonna pick somebody and I will close a good percentage of those jobs" — sources/S_yt_-C01FC27Jg0_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a pep talk about trying harder: in this session the major goal is priced — the client stands to lose millions of dollars of business — and the effort has to match the stakes, so a minor effort for a major goal is a budgeting error, not a motivation problem.
- Read as a rule that big budgets are always right: the suit side of the same lesson says the opposite — spending millions of dollars to buy a suit does not make sense — so the point is proportion between effort and goal, not spending more.
