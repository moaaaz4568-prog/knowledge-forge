# G_yt_-0kt008QsUY_en — shape psychology

## Term

The speaker uses "shape psychology" for the way a shape's meaning steers what a logo says to its audience — circles read as inclusion, triangles as speed, shields as protection — and treats it as a designer's toolkit, not a theory. The phrase appears 3 times, at the point where the video shifts from rules to audience: after the 2026 rules of simplicity, versatility, memorability, scalability, and working without color, shape psychology is how you match a mark to the tribe it must speak to. Its neighbours are "color psychology", "tribe", "circles", and "shields": circles communicate inclusion, friendliness, and community, triangles and sharp V-shapes communicate speed, direction, and attack, and shields like GRRI say protection and stability, while color psychology sits at the top of the same audience-first thinking. The concrete test is the mismatch: a shield shape for a playful toy brand makes no sense, and a hyper-aggressive sports mark for a financial planner might scare clients away.
