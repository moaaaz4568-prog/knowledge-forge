# G_yt_--9yW5APyy0_en — soft problems

## Term

Ben Childles uses "soft problems" for the human and organizational difficulties that sit around the actual marketing work: internal politics, procurement delays, tangled approvals, and clients who cannot get decisions through their own companies. The phrase appears 3 times, set explicitly against "hard problems" — the execution work of SEO and paid media — which enterprise clients treat as assumed competence rather than the real benefit. His concrete claim is that his own group never discusses how to do SEO or paid media, because the soft problems are the work that remains, and getting good at them only grows more valuable as execution becomes commoditized. The term also travels with "trusted partner": advising clients through their soft problems is how an agency becomes the partner a client trusts when money and jobs are on the line.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a synonym for poor execution or weak skills: in this source the soft problems are not failed SEO or bad paid media but the organizational work — politics, procurement, and approvals — that surrounds execution, and Ben treats them as the durable, valuable part of the job.
- Read as a claim that technical skill no longer matters: the source keeps hard execution as the baseline clients assume, and argues only that soft problems grow more valuable as execution gets commoditized, not that an agency can skip the hard problems.
