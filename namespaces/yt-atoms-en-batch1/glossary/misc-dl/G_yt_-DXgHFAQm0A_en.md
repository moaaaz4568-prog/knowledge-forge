# G_yt_-DXgHFAQm0A_en — anchor points

## Term

The tutor uses "anchor points" for the control points of a vector logo — the things to keep to a minimum, because less is always best for professional and good logo design. The phrase appears 9 times, across an Illustrator tips video about the common mistakes designers make: first you want to keep the number of anchor points to a minimum, and the first step in cleaning up a logo is the Simplify option, played with until the shape and style stay the same while the anchor points decrease in number. Its neighbours are "stroke", "smooth tool", "cleanup", and "expand": the smooth tool also eliminates unnecessary anchor points with every stroke, there is a cleanup option as well, the second common mistake is forgetting to expand the design, and the stroke tip is that whatever size you make the logo, the stroke stays relevant to the design itself. The concrete check is the preview: select the original option and it will show you the anchor points being removed in red, then zoom into your design and see the changes up close.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a count to hit exactly: the video's rule is directional — keep anchor points to a minimum, less is always best — and the Simplify preview shows points removed in red, so the number is judged by the shape staying the same, not by a target count.
- Read as a one-time cleanup: the smooth tool eliminates unnecessary anchor points with every stroke, and the cleanup option is part of the same habit, so reducing points is something you do throughout the design, not once at the end.
