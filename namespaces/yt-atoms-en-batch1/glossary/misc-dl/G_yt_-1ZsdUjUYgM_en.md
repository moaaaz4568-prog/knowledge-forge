# G_yt_-1ZsdUjUYgM_en — consultant

## Term

The speaker uses "consultant" for the person who gives consultation from real experience — and the whole 135-word clip argues that reading and certificates do not make one. The word appears 3 times, always to draw a line: you can teach a teacher, not a consultant, and a consultant doctor cannot be someone without a year of internship and years of work. Its neighbours are "teacher", "consultation", "certificates", and "real experience": the speaker's question is whether reading a lot, studying a lot, and obtaining many certificates makes someone a teacher of a subject, and his answer is that taking a curriculum, simplifying it, and putting it in your mind has nothing to do with consultation. The concrete test is the consultant doctor: someone who has not completed a year of internship, then worked for 100 years in some unknown place, cannot be a consultant doctor, because they will not have enough real experience.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a title anyone earns by studying: in this source the speaker says reading a lot, studying a lot, and obtaining many certificates does not make a consultant — consultation needs real experience, and the consultant doctor without an internship is impossible.
- Read as the same job as teaching: the clip insists you can teach a teacher, not a consultant — teaching is taking a curriculum, simplifying it, and putting it in a mind, while consultation is something else entirely, built on lived experience.
