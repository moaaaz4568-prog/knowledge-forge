# G_yt_-7WKqSgY6Dk_en — vector

## Term

The tutor uses "vector" for the logo format that keeps its quality constant at any size — the first of five ways to ruin a logo design is not making it one. The word appears 2 times, both in the first point: a logo must be a vector because that will ensure the quality stays constant scaled up or down to any size, and a vector-based program such as Adobe Illustrator should be used when designing a logo. Its neighbours are "raster image", "kerning", "brief", and "typeface": the opposite of vector is a raster image such as a design made in Photoshop, kerning is the spacing between the letters of the logotype, and the other ruinous mistakes are straying from the brief and choosing the wrong typeface. The concrete scale test is the business card against the billboard: a logo must work on a small business card and as large as a billboard, and only a vector survives both.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a file format choice among equals: in this video vector is the only correct choice for a logo — a raster image made in Photoshop is named as the opposite, and the reason is the size range from business card to billboard.
- Read as a stylistic preference: the speaker frames vector as the first of five ways to ruin a logo, so a non-vector logo is not a taste issue but a quality failure that shows the moment the logo is scaled.
