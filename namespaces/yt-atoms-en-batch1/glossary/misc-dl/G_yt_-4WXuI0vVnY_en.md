# G_yt_-4WXuI0vVnY_en — VAS

## Term

Thomas uses "VAS" for the program that predicts where a viewer's eye will travel across a design in the first three to five seconds — a tool plugged into Photoshop that gives real-time data on whether the design is effective and attention-grabbing. The name appears 5 times, always as the tool itself: VAS is a really nifty new program that uses complex algorithms to determine where the eye goes and how the viewer perceives the design in that short period at the very beginning. Its neighbours are "three to five seconds", "area of interest", "face", and "Photoshop": the first three to five seconds are the initial hook, you can highlight an area and tell VAS it is a face or draw a box around an area of interest, and the whole point is that faces are universal — ingrained into human psychology and hardwired into our brains across every culture. The concrete use is the fix: when VAS shows the viewer travels left to right, you make the text more visually contrasting and appealing.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a guarantee of good design: VAS only predicts where the eye travels and gives real-time data on how the design measures up — the designer still has to change the text, the contrast, and the layout in response.
- Read as a Photoshop filter: VAS is a separate program plugged into Photoshop, and its job is measurement in the first three to five seconds, not editing — the design work happens after the data comes back.
