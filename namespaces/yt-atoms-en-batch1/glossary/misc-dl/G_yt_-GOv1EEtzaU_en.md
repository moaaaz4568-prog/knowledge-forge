# G_yt_-GOv1EEtzaU_en — predictability

## Term

The speakers use "predictability" for the agency state where you know what is coming next — a solid cadence, no guesses, no surprises — and profitability follows from it. The word appears 6 times, as the interview's thesis: if you can get things to be really consistent, you can move in the direction of becoming more predictable, and profitability will come if you understand what is coming down the pike. Its neighbours are "profitability", "cadence", "onboarding", and "revisions": predictability leads to profitability and you cannot really decouple those, a solid cadence is what a predictable business runs on, and onboarding has a huge impact on churn and on predictability itself. The concrete application is revisions: revisions are going to be a part of agency life, but what you can do to tame the chaos is bring some predictability to it — transparency over how many times, how you present them, and how you work through them.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as forecasting revenue: in this conversation predictability is operational — a solid cadence, transparency over revisions, onboarding that cuts churn — and the profitability it leads to is the ability to confidently reinvest, not a revenue prediction.
- Read as the absence of change: the speakers treat revisions as a permanent part of agency life, so predictability here means taming the chaos with transparency, not eliminating the revisions.
