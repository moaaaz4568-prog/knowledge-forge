# G_yt_--IcW24zGM0_en — open society

## Term

The reviewer uses "open society" for the philosophy behind George Soros's philanthropy as explained in "Soros on Soros: Staying Ahead of the Curve" — not a generic label for left-wing funding but Soros's own project. The phrase appears 2 times, both in the reviewer's correction of the common view: most people think of Soros as a generic financier of left-wing progressive ideas, while his open society is, in the reviewer's words, the most radical form of social liberalism. Its neighbours are "philanthropic goals", "investment philosophy", "black wednesday", and "The Alchemy of Finance": the book's first part covers his investment philosophy and his billion-pound short on Black Wednesday, and the reviewer finds the open-society philosophy the more interesting half. The concrete contrast is the reviewer's own: classical left-wing politics on one side, and an open society that turns all human interaction into transaction on the other.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a synonym for left-wing politics or progressive funding: in this source the open society is a distinct philosophy — the most radical form of social liberalism — and the reviewer explicitly contrasts it with classical left-wing politics.
- Read as the name of a charity alone: the source treats the open society as both Soros's group and the philosophy behind it, so reducing it to an organization name loses the reviewer's point that the philosophy itself is what he found most interesting in the book.
