# G_yt_-JYiXyzDEFI_en — golden ratio

## Term

The tutor uses "golden ratio" for the proportion system he checks a logo against — the discovered ratio a lot of designers use to find a great sense of balance. The phrase appears 6 times, across a live logo critique: he lays the golden ratio over the logo just to see if it is on scale or not, and the logo is a little bit off scale by the proportions of the golden ratio. Its neighbours are "scale", "proportions", "ink wells", and "experience": the quick fix is to rescale everything, using the same golden ratio but using the proportions to scale the elements like the type, and the same session explains ink wells — designed because the ink would bleed into the letterforms in print. The concrete payoff is the before and after: he made the logo a bit wider, showed the before and after side by side, and called it an improvement on the mark.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a rule that makes a logo good: in this session the golden ratio is a checking tool — lay it over the logo to see if it is on scale — and the fix is rescaling, so the ratio diagnoses the proportions, it does not design the logo.
- Read as a fixed template: the tutor uses the same golden ratio but different proportions to scale the elements, and the type has to sit between the cap height and the baseline when scaled, so the ratio is applied flexibly to the mark, not stamped on it.
