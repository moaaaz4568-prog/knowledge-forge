# G_yt_-EV-P4CQYCE_en — visual language

## Term

The speaker uses "visual language" for what a design says — the message carried by the image itself, as opposed to how good it looks. The phrase appears 6 times, across a video that opens with the test: look at this design, it looks fairly good, but what is this design saying, what is the message here — and many designers will not be able to give you an answer, and that is the problem. Its neighbours are "design thinking", "category", "message", and "concept": most people are stuck in the category of making things look good without saying anything with their visual language, and design thinking means looking deeper into why the design should exist in the first place. The concrete rule is the key idea: get the idea across with your visual language, and do not stack layers and push the boundaries of the software — rather, push the boundaries of your concepts.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as decoration: in this video visual language is the message itself — a design that looks good but says nothing has failed, and the fix is to push the boundaries of your concepts, not the boundaries of the software.
- Read as a matter of adding more: the speaker says more effects or more details do not equate to a better design, and that more does not correlate to a better outcome — the visual language gets clearer by removing, not by stacking layers.
