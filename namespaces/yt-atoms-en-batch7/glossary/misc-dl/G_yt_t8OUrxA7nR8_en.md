# G_yt_t8OUrxA7nR8_en — Big Mac index

## Term

The Big Mac index is the idea that you can compare the relative price levels of different countries from the cost of a Big Mac, because McDonald's is in most countries in the world. The phrase appears twice, beside purchasing power, The Economist, and fractional reserve banking, and the speaker presents it early in part two of his iceberg of economics series. The Economist magazine tracks it most popularly, and the comparison runs across the US, South Africa, Brazil, Mexico, France, Japan, and China. One concrete use in the source is the price of a Big Mac in the US versus South Africa: the same burger costs different amounts, and that gap is read as a price-level difference between countries.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a price guide for burgers: the index uses the Big Mac's price to compare countries' price levels, not to say where a burger is cheap lunch — the burger is the measuring stick, not the point.
- Read as a precise exchange-rate forecast: the speaker presents it as a rough popular gauge tracked by The Economist, and the video moves on to other concepts rather than defending any exact valuation.
