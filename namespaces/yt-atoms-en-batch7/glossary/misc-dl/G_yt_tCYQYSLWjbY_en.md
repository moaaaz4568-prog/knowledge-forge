# G_yt_tCYQYSLWjbY_en — golden ratio

## Term

The golden ratio is the first of three design myths this video debunks: the claim that the ratio is useful, and that the Apple logo adheres to it. The phrase appears eleven times, beside Apple logo, myth, and graphic designer, and the speaker's verdict after 15 years in design is that the ratio really is not that useful. His argument is mathematical: the decimal is recurring and never ends — 1.6182033 and so on forever — so the numbers do not add up properly for real-world application. One concrete case he cites is the Apple logo, which people say follows the ratio and actually does not, with a link below to a detailed proof.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a ban on ever using the ratio: the speaker's claim is that the ratio is not useful and cannot be applied cleanly in the real world — he never shows a design that failed because of it, so the video debunks the myth, not every use of the proportion.
- Read as a claim the Apple logo is badly designed: the point is only that the logo does not adhere to the golden ratio — the video says nothing about the logo's quality, only about the false ratio claim.
