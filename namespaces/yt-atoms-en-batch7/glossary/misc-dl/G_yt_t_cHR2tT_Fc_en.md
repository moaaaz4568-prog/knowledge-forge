# G_yt_t_cHR2tT_Fc_en — cliche

## Term

A cliche is one of the seven worst logo design cliches this video warns against: a tired idea — tree people, windows on buildings, double letters — that stops a logo from standing out. The word appears seven times, with the plural cliches seven times, beside logo design, simple iconic, and overdone, and the rule is that logo designs should stand out for all the right reasons so the brand can be remembered. The tree people logo is the first example: it might have been novel once, but today it is so overdone it has become an eyesore, and no brand that takes itself seriously will want one. One concrete test the speaker gives is simplicity: a logo design should be simple, iconic, and memorable, and the fix for a cluttered design is to refine it down to a simple iconic solution.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a style preference: a cliche here is a commercial failure, not a taste issue — the video's test is whether the brand can be remembered and interacted with, and cliches prevent that.
- Read as a ban on every common idea: the video says a double letter logo can be done pretty well with creative flair — the cliche is the unthinking copy, and flair can bring it out of that cliche.
