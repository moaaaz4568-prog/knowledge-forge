# G_yt_twwbFd9LcsE_en — retouching

## Term

Retouching is what the speaker asks ChatGPT to do with his studio product photos: keep the product and its printed data exactly as photographed, place it on a new background, and adjust the lighting and shadows so the result looks real. The word appears four times, beside backgrounds, lighting and shadows, and the workflow is always the same: crop the product so the model can read its small print, upload a background from Shutterstock, and tell the model in plain words to retouch the first image and put it in the second. His concrete proof is an espresso box photographed dirty and damaged, which comes back with the same 250 grams, the same text and the same font, and a sauce bottle where only the 170 grams slip slightly. The speaker also shows the limit of the trick: he refuses to lift a finished design from Pinterest or Behance and swap in his own product, calling it unfair to the designer who spent the time on it.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a filter that changes the product: the speaker's retouching must keep the product data exactly as photographed, and he praises the result precisely when the text, the grams and the font stay the same.
- Read as a shortcut that replaces design skill: the speaker ends by warning against copying other designers' work as a reference, and says the difference in the next stage is a trained eye and good taste, not the tool.
