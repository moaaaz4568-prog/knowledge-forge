# G_yt_t2kjdS0Up_g_en — consumer welfare

## Term

Consumer welfare is the legal standard Robert Scott says antitrust law has used: the test is the impact on the consumer, mainly through pricing, and simply being a monopolist is not grounds for a case. The phrase appears six times, beside pricing, public welfare, and the Supreme Court, and Scott presents it as the previous standard the Biden administration wants to replace. The rival standard in the source is public welfare — a broader test covering market concentration, supply chains, climate, and political power — which Scott says would force courts to overturn Supreme Court precedent. One concrete case shows the stakes: under the consumer welfare standard, cases are hard to bring because the law requires a negative impact on the consumer through pricing, which is why the standard favors big-cap tech.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a synonym for public welfare: the source opposes them — consumer welfare tests pricing impact only, while public welfare would open merger review to concentration, climate, and political power.
- Read as a rule that bigness itself is illegal: Scott is explicit that being a monopolist is not grounds for a case under this standard without a negative impact on the consumer through pricing.
