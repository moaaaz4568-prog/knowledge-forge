# G_yt_tVzWz-_zwjM_en — retainer

## Term

A retainer is the monthly payment model this video argues against: the agency charges $2,000 a month, and the client is always one bad month away from letting it go. The word appears nineteen times, beside churn, $10,000 up front, and infrastructure, and the problem is structural — clients churn in month one, two, or three, often through no fault of the agency, because payroll forces them to choose between the agency and their employees. The alternative the speaker sells is building an infrastructure, a system, instead of a retainer — and charging $10,000 up front instead of $2,000. One concrete claim is about stickiness: a client who paid up front is far more likely to stick for 12 months than one started on a retainer offer.

## Synonyms

- retainer model (the fuller form used in this source)

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the video endorsing retainers: it does the opposite — the retainer is the model that leaves agencies exposed to churn, and the pitch is infrastructure plus an upfront fee instead.
- Read as a claim that churn is the agency's fault: the speaker says most of the time it is not the agency's fault at all — a client's bad month forces the choice, and the retainer makes that choice easy.
