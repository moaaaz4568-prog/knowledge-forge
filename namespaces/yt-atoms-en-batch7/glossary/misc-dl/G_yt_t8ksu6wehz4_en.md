# G_yt_t8ksu6wehz4_en — hourly billing

## Term

Hourly billing is the practice Jonathan Stark is on a mission to rid the world of: charging clients by the hour for agency work, which he argues punishes efficiency and caps income. The phrase appears five times, beside ditching hourly, value pricing, and sales meeting, and the whole podcast is framed around Stark's break from it — he wrote a book called Hourly Billing Is Nuts and self-published it in 2016. His alternative is value pricing: scope the work last instead of first, and price the outcome rather than the hours. One concrete habit he describes from his 2004 dev shop in Atlanta is billing everybody out hourly with 10–15 developers — the model he later escaped by flipping the sales meeting to questions about why the client wants the work at all.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a ban on tracking time: Stark still talks about a transitional phase where agencies track time — the target is pricing by the hour, not the clock itself, and he warns that tracking after the transition means the agency is not really doing value pricing.
- Read as a claim that hourly billing never works: the source is one practitioner's break from a model he ran for years, and its case rests on his own dev shop and clients — not a study of every agency.
