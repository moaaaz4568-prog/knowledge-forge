# G_yt_tKY634THsbU_en — inspiration

## Term

Inspiration here is not a mood that strikes — it is a technique for getting ideas from other disciplines when a deadline demands results now. The word appears thirteen times, beside cinematography, industrial design, and gesture drawing, and the video is the first of a multi-part series on borrowing from related disciplines. The reason is competitive: if all industrial designers look to the same sources, everyone's work starts to look similar and standing out gets hard. One concrete case he gives is a designer who got inspiration from a house cat for a car design, which is why he says no source of inspiration is too banal or too basic — and his first borrowed discipline is cinematography, the visual storytelling of movies.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as waiting for a muse: the video's method is active borrowing from other disciplines under deadline pressure — inspiration is something you go and get, not something you wait for.
- Read as copying other designers: the point is borrowing from related disciplines like cinematography, not from other industrial designers — looking to the same sources as everyone else is exactly what the video warns against.
