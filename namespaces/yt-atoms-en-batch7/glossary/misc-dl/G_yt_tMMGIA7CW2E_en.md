# G_yt_tMMGIA7CW2E_en — new customer acquisition

## Term

New customer acquisition is the goal Scott Degrossier says agencies get wrong: they optimize ROAS, the return on ad spend, when the number that mattered was the cost of acquiring new customers. The phrase appears seven times, with new customer acquisition cost twice, beside ROAS, Wicked Reports, and scoreboard, and the argument is that new and repeat buyers get blended in the data, so ROAS looks good while the real goal is missed. His framework replaces ROAS with a scoreboard: for new customer acquisition, the measurement window might be 30 to 90 days, and the cost has to sit in an agreed range. One concrete target he gives is a new customer acquisition cost between 50 and 70 bucks for 30 days, and that range is the agreed scoreboard.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a synonym for ROAS: the source opposes them — ROAS latches onto blended new-and-repeat data, while new customer acquisition cost isolates what a new customer costs, and Scott says the cost is what mattered.
- Read as a cost to minimize at all costs: the framework sets an agreed range, 50 to 70 bucks for 30 days, not a race to zero — below the range is flagged as its own problem, not a win.
