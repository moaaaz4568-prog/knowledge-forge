# G_yt_tjjKO1QkIG4_en — use case

## Term

A use case is the specific job a hardware product must do for a specific user — and this video says the biggest mistakes are made on the first day of the project, when teams skip it. The phrase appears seven times, beside product development, user, and niche, and the warning is about other disciplines: they come up with an interesting or novel technology and then try to figure out what a good use case might be for it. That method can work, the speaker says, but it is problematic if you do not really understand who your user is. One concrete rule he gives: pick a specific use case, pick a specialized niche, and solve that really, really well — good product development starts with an intimate understanding of the user and the use case, and only then do you generate solutions.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a feature list: a use case is who the product is for and what it does for them — the video's failure mode is a novel technology hunting for a use, and the fix is starting from the user, not the feature.
- Read as a claim that technology-first development always fails: the speaker says there is nothing wrong with it and many successful companies did it — the problem is only when the team does not understand the user.
