# G_yt_tBE-ke9_51o_en — logo type

## Term

A logo type is the wordmark part of a logo — the lettering itself — and this video judges four designs by whether their lettering fits the brand. The phrase appears five times, beside font choice, serif font, and kerning, and the Rolex example is the lesson: the correct font choice is an established serif font, but the logo type still looks slightly off because the kerning is a tiny bit incorrect. The speaker walks through four different designs, not all of them logos, to show how to solve design problems and come up with the right font choice. One concrete test he gives is fit: a font that does not fit the brand will not work no matter how good it looks on its own.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a synonym for logo: the logo type is only the lettering — the Rolex crown symbol is a separate part, and the video faults the type, not the symbol.
- Read as a purely aesthetic judgment: the video's test is fit with the brand — the serif choice is right for Rolex and the fault is the kerning, not the font family.
