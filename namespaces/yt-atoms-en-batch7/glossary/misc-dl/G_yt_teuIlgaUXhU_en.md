# G_yt_teuIlgaUXhU_en — flying blind

## Term

Flying blind is the speaker's name for running a business without data: not tracking things, not tracking habits, making decisions by feel. The phrase appears three times, beside data, tracking, and habits, and the claim is that most entrepreneurs live this way — one of his top months did over $300,000, and it took him so long to look at a data first mindset. The cost is distraction: living blind leaves you where you do not want to be in life, while competitors who go off what feels right stay stuck in the trenches. One concrete result of switching: over the last three months he consistently beat his competitors by using data and capitalizing on opportunities they all missed.

## Synonyms

- fly blind (the base form used in this source)
- fly blindly (the adverbial form used in this source)

> "And most entrepreneurs, most people, everyone is just flying blind. They're not tracking things. They're not tracking their habits" — sources/S_yt_teuIlgaUXhU_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that data removes risk: the speaker says you can beat anyone with data if you try hard enough — data is the edge over competitors who fly blind, not a guarantee, and he admits he is not perfect at it.
- Read as a synonym for being a beginner: flying blind is a habit, not a stage — the speaker did it himself after his peak month at 20, and the fix is tracking, not experience alone.
