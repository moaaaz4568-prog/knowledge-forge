# G_yt_tw1VBrz_JIo_en — mosaic balance

## Term

Mosaic balance is a technique that builds a larger composition from a collection of small individual elements — shapes, images or typography — arranged so the whole holds together, and the speaker introduces it as one of the lesser-known balance techniques a master uses on purpose. The phrase appears three times, beside white space, symmetrical balance and coordinate balance, and the speaker's rule for it is that the placement must be thoughtful and functional: randomly scattering objects and hoping for the best does not work. His concrete example is a design that looks balanced left to right, with a purple area on one side and the other elements on the right, where the objects on the right form the mosaic that draws the viewer's attention. The designer can spread mosaic balance across an entire design or confine it to one section with white space around it, which is how the technique stays deliberate instead of looking like a mistake.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as any scattered arrangement: mosaic balance needs small elements deliberately placed into a larger composition, and the speaker says random scattering just looks like a mistake.
- Read as the same as symmetrical balance: mosaic balance is a separate technique the speaker teaches alongside coordinate balance, and it works by intrigue from many small pieces, not by mirroring left and right.
