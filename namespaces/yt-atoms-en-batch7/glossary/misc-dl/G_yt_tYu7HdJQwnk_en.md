# G_yt_tYu7HdJQwnk_en — payoff

## Term

A payoff is the third of the four core components of a banger piece of content: after the hook and the build, you give the audience a punch line, a tool in their hand, or an insight they can leave with. The word appears thirty-seven times, beside hook, build, and curiosity loop, and the test is blunt — without one clear payoff, there is nothing the audience can grab onto and nothing they can leave home with. The speaker learned the theory from Lego and breaks down three pieces of content — one video, one email, one carousel — to show the elements in action. One concrete number behind the method: the 12 hours a month he spends making content produces $80,000 a month in workshop ticket sales plus $100,000 a month in recurring revenue.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the ending of the piece: the payoff is point three of four — after it comes the next step, telling people what to do, and a piece with a payoff but no next step is still incomplete.
- Read as a vague promise of value: the payoff must be one clear thing — a punch line, a tool, or an insight — and the video's test is whether the audience leaves home with something they can grab onto.
