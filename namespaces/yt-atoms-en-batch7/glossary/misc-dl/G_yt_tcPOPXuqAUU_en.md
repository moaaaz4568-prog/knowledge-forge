# G_yt_tcPOPXuqAUU_en — show rate

## Term

A show rate is the percentage of booked appointments that actually show up — one of the numbers this video says an appointment setting business must track. The phrase appears seven times, beside close rate, appointments, and sales rep, and the lesson comes from two years working with Charlie Morgan, after paying $48,000 for his masterminds. The point is comparison: a 50% show rate means nothing on its own, because you cannot know if it is good unless you know whether the industry average is 30 or 80 percent. One concrete use is cost: if your show rate and close rate are bad, you pay more to acquire a client than the client pays you — and the business is simply a collection of numbers, so the numbers decide whether the work is in the right direction.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a vanity metric: the show rate decides cost — a bad show rate means paying more to acquire a client than the client pays, so it is a money number, not a scoreboard.
- Read as a number that speaks for itself: a 50% show rate is meaningless without a comparison — the video's point is that you need the industry average before you can call any rate good or bad.
