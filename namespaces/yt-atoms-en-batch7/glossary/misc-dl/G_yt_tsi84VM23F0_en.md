# G_yt_tsi84VM23F0_en — cold outreach

## Term

Cold outreach is the speaker's name for approaching strangers for work with a pre-packaged portfolio — captions and designs sent to people who never asked for them — and he treats it as the method that fails when the sender has done nothing specific for the recipient. The phrase appears three times, beside pre-packaged portfolio, permission and marketer for 20 companies, and the speaker's contrast is sharp: a person who did ten days of work for one company at their own expense gets heard, while a sender of generic portfolios gets ignored. His concrete test is the job seeker who claims there are no opportunities: the speaker asks whether that person has played the role of the marketer for 20 companies, doing real work and presenting the solution for free, before concluding that no opportunity exists. The permission line at the end draws the boundary: content outreach works only with permission, and the speaker says he got permission rather than jumping in like a parachute.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a ban on all outreach: the speaker does not reject outreach itself — he rejects sending pre-packaged portfolios to strangers, and his own alternative still involves presenting work to 20 companies.
- Read as advice to work for free forever: the free work here is a one-off proof for a specific company, ten days for one employer, not an open offer to work unpaid for anyone who asks.
