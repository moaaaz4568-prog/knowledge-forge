# G_yt_t7mUP6BJA6w_en — samurai q a

## Term

The samurai q a is John Benson's name for the question-and-answer section of a sales letter — the transcript spells the spoken Q&A as q a — and he calls it a completely different animal from an ordinary Q&A. The phrase appears five times, beside the sales letter, the video sales letter, and Copy Pro, and the whole final part of his 50 million sales letter blueprint is built around it. Benson warns it is gray-area persuasion that pushes the envelope, and says the tip lifted his video sales, though he also shows how to do it squeaky clean. One concrete placement he gives is that the samurai q a comes in at the end of the sales letter, in the P.S. or in a VSL, where it answers the objections that would otherwise kill the sale.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a normal FAQ: the samurai q a is not a list of common questions — Benson frames it as a persuasion device placed at the end of the letter, in the P.S. or VSL, to close objections.
- Read as a trick anyone should copy blindly: Benson himself warns it is gray-area material some people will not feel comfortable with, and he shows a squeaky-clean version for those who want it.
