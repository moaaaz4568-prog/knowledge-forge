# G_ig_createcontent.club_C1PN8jwgjet — precise in your speech

## Term

Precise in your speech is Jordan Peterson's tenth rule from 12 Rules for Life, quoted here as the reason the creator felt depressed without knowing it: failing without defining the terms of winning or failing. The phrase appears once, in the rule's wording be precise in your speech, next to depressed, define exactly and set goal. The clip's point is that undefined terms make failure invisible — without a set goal or guide, a person keeps failing without knowing what they are aiming at. A creator applying the rule would write down the exact terms of winning and failing in each realm of life, so the goal is visible before the work starts.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When the rule is read as a speech tip: the clip uses it to define the terms of winning and failing, not to polish delivery, so treating it as public-speaking advice misses its point about goals.
- When avoiding the realization is read as harmless: the clip says not defining terms feels good in the short term but causes long-term pain, so comfort now is exactly the trap the rule warns against.
