# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DSn_AiHDSQu — cliff

## Term

Cliff is Daniel's image for how government assistance disappears: earn a little more and the help falls away faster than the raise replaces it. The word appears twice, beside government assistance, SNAP or EBT, and step ladder, and his example is exact — a job at $35,000 a year, a raise to $45,000, and the subsidized benefits taken away because you now make too much money. The differential of the raise is less than the lost benefit, so you effectively make less money, and the same tiered system runs as a step ladder all the way up to $100,000 a year, below which he says you live in poverty.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The way that government assistance works is it works on a cliff." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DSn_AiHDSQu.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that raises always hurt: the cliff bites where the lost benefit outweighs the raise, and the clip's own ladder tops out around $100,000 a year.
- Read as an attack on all assistance: Daniel describes how the tiers work, and his point is the trap in the structure — earning more and ending up with less — not that help should not exist.
