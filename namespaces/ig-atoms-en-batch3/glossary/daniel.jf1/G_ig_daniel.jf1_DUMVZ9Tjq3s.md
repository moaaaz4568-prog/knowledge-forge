# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DUMVZ9Tjq3s — 99 to 1

## Term

99 to 1 is Daniel's correction of the 80/20 rule: the real split in business is not eighty-twenty but ninety-nine to one. The phrase is used once, beside 80/20, bidding, and monopoly, and his evidence is his own feed — he gets the ads of about eight people regularly, which tells him those few are bidding so astronomically higher than everybody else that only they are seen. His conclusion is the monopoly line: if they can afford to bid higher, their offer is so substantially better that they win all of the business, all of it.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "People think, particularly in business, they think 80/20 is correct, but it's not. It's literally 99 to 1." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DUMVZ9Tjq3s.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a measured statistic: 99 to 1 is Daniel's own figure for how lopsided the split is, and the clip offers his ad feed as the evidence, not a study.
- Read as advice to outbid everyone at any cost: his point is that the winners' offers are substantially better, and the clip never says bidding alone creates the advantage.
