# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DXnM8nIq1ww — ninety nine to one

## Term

Ninety nine to one is Daniel's ratio for who actually wins: the ads he sees belong to about eight people regularly, and that tells him those few are bidding so astronomically higher than everybody else that only they are seen. The phrase is used once, beside eight people, astronomically higher, and own a monopoly, and his logic is the affordability test — if they can afford to bid higher, it is not that their offer is a little bit better than someone else's, it is so substantially better that they win all of the business, all of it. His authority for the endgame is Peter Thiel's line: you want to own a monopoly.

## Live Example

> "People think eighty twenty is correct, but it's not. It's literally ninety nine to one." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DXnM8nIq1ww.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a count of real advertisers: the eight people are the ads Daniel sees regularly, and the clip never claims only eight advertisers exist.
- Read as a license to bid without a better offer: the bidding power follows the substantially better offer, and the clip never says the bid creates it.
