# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DW80z4lDLmB — free lead magnet

## Term

A free lead magnet is Daniel's test for who will ever pay: he watches Pyros to see if anyone who came in from one ever buys anything at all, and his answer is that they never do, because they are non-buyers. The phrase appears three times, twice in the singular and once as free lead magnets, beside $10,000 a day on ads, LTV, and don't buy, and his rule from the data is to never promote a free lead magnet to cold traffic — warm traffic on a YouTube video is the only exception, because it doesn't cost money. His dollar fix is the filter: charge $1 for the magnet, and the LTV magically works out, since the people who buy stuff continue to buy stuff and the only thing you need is for them to pay any amount at all.

## Synonyms

- free lead magnets (the plural form Daniel uses for the people who get them and never buy)

## Live Example

> "I can see if someone came in from a free lead magnet, did they ever buy anything at all ever, and I can figure out their LTV." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DW80z4lDLmB.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a verdict that lead magnets never work: Daniel's target is the free magnet offered to cold traffic, and warm YouTube traffic is explicitly allowed.
- Read as proof that free leads are worthless people: non-buyers is his label for the behavior — they just don't buy — and the clip never says the people themselves are worthless.
