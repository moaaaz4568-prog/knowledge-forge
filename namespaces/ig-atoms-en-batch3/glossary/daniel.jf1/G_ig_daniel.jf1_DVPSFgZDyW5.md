# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DVPSFgZDyW5 — invent something

## Term

Invent something is the mistake Daniel says will most likely break you: trying to invent something carries a severely higher probability of going broke than selling something that already sells. The phrase appears twice, beside probability, misconception, and profound, and his misconception is the premise itself — that to make a lot of money you need to make something new or profound or invent something. His verdict is blunt: that is literally not what you need to do at all, and you will make many hundreds of millions of dollars by not inventing a single thing in your entire life.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The probability that you go broke by trying to invent something is severely higher than if you just sell something that already sells." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DVPSFgZDyW5.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a ban on all invention: Daniel says selling what already sells beats inventing, and the clip never says no invention ever made money.
- Read as a promise of hundreds of millions: the figure is Daniel's rhetoric for the safe path, and the clip never shows anyone reaching it.
