# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DRx9TlSjbwI — multiply one into five

## Term

Multiply one into five is Daniel's test for signing a client: the buyer must believe you can turn one dollar of theirs into five. The phrase is used once, beside confident, personal ability, and sign people, after his image of every human clicking the casino button infinitely when a dollar in returns five out. His concrete numbers are the retainer itself — a client with their own business and their own money, asked for five thousand dollars a month — and the sale fails unless the client trusts you to make them more money than that.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The problem, if you can't sign people, is they're not confident in your personal ability to multiply one into five." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DRx9TlSjbwI.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a literal investment return: the one-into-five figure is Daniel's image for the trust a client must have, not a guaranteed ROI the seller promises.
- Read as a pricing formula: the clip never prices the multiplication itself; the five thousand dollars is the retainer asked for, and the multiplication is the belief that justifies it.
