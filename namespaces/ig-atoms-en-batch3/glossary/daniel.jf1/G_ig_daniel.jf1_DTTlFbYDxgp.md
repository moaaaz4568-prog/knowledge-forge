# G_ig_daniel.jf1_DTTlFbYDxgp — customer acquisition

## Term

Customer acquisition is the priced step Daniel standardizes for agencies: for digitally rendered service-based companies under $10 million per year, your CAC is typically around $3,000 plus or minus 1,500. The phrase is used once, beside standard rate, running ads, and CAC, and the range that follows is the working number — anywhere between $1,500 and $4,500 in most cases. His rule from it is about the client's LTV: it should be at least $15,000 for the economics to work properly, ideally more.

## Synonyms

- CAC (the abbreviation Daniel uses for the same rate when he prices it at around $3,000)

## Live Example

> "If you're running ads for clients, there's a standard rate for customer acquisition in most industries." — sources/S_ig_daniel.jf1_DTTlFbYDxgp.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a price Daniel charges: the $3,000 is the typical CAC for the client's business, not Daniel's fee, and the clip prices the acquisition, not the agency.
- Read as a guarantee that ads pay off: the standard rate only sets the math — LTV at least $15,000 against a $3,000 CAC — and the clip never promises the ads will hit the standard.
