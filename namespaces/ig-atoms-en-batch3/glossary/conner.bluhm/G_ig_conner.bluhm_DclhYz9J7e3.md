# G_ig_conner.bluhm_DclhYz9J7e3 — believe in them

## Term

Believe in them here is what Conner says a prospect must feel from you on the call: that you believe in them more than they believe in themselves. The phrase appears once, beside genuinely, wants them to succeed and buy into that, and the contrast is with family whose encouragement follows the traditional path and friends whose encouragement is hollow because they do not want you to do better than them. When a prospect genuinely feels someone believes they can succeed and wants them to succeed, people buy into that more than literally anything. That feeling, he says, is what makes it extremely easy to get people to pay five, ten, fifteen or twenty thousand dollars.

## Live Example

> "You need to show the prospect that you believe in them more than they believe in themselves, and I'll kind of explain what this means." — sources/S_ig_conner.bluhm_DclhYz9J7e3.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as flattery: believing in them here must be genuine and felt by the prospect, not praise said on the call.
- Read as a sales technique: the belief is about the prospect's success, not about the offer, and Conner ties the payment to that feeling, not to the pitch.
