# G_ig_conner.bluhm_DcEcAE-J2Sb — cost of inaction

## Term

Cost of inaction here is the guarantee you make to yourself when you decide not to act: you will not get what you want. The phrase appears once, beside guaranteeing, risk and loan, opening a video about the risk of not taking risks. His own proof is a $15,000 loan he had no way to pay back, spent almost all of it before signing a client, and still did his first $100,000 month within 90 days. The choice he offers is between guaranteeing failure by inaction and giving yourself a chance of it working by taking the risk.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "Did you forget? The cost of inaction is guaranteeing to yourself that you will not get what you want." — sources/S_ig_conner.bluhm_DcEcAE-J2Sb.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a price tag: the cost of inaction here is a lost outcome, not money spent; the $15,000 loan is the cost of the action he did take.
- Read as a promise that risk always pays: Conner says taking the risk gave him a chance, not a guarantee, and the video is about the chance, not the result.
