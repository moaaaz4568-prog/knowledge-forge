# G_ig_conner.bluhm_Dci_igcpTBD — EBITDA

## Term

EBITDA here is what actually matters when selling a business: how much you are profiting, not your revenue. The word appears once, beside revenue, profit and vanity, as the first of three takeaways from selling his company to private equity. The trap is optimizing for revenue, feeling awesome about $100,000 a month while walking away with $10,000, when someone making $15,000 and keeping $12 is doing better. Revenue is purely vanity and profit is what actually matters, whether you want a living or a sale.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The thing that actually matters is your EBITDA, so how much you are profiting." — sources/S_ig_conner.bluhm_Dci_igcpTBD.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a tax term: EBITDA here is simply how much you are profiting, and Conner never defines it beyond that.
- Read as revenue being worthless: revenue is called vanity only against profit when selling; the point is what you take home, not that sales do not matter.
