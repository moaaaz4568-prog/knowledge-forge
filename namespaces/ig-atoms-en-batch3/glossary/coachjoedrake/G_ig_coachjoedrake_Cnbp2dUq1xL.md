# G_all_your_eggs_in_one_basket — all your eggs in one basket

## Term

All your eggs in one basket is Joe's image for committing completely to a difficult goal instead of hedging or waiting for permission. The phrase is used once, beside go after your dreams, never compromised, and difficult to accomplish, in a source that cuts off mid-sentence after 'There's no'. He frames the commitment as refusing to let anybody say what he was capable of, and the basket is the whole of his effort going after one thing. The sentence that carries it makes the condition explicit: when many people think something is difficult to accomplish, total commitment is what he prescribes.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "If you want to accomplish something or do something that a lot of people think is difficult to accomplish, you have to put all your eggs in one basket." — sources/S_ig_coachjoedrake_Cnbp2dUq1xL.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as financial advice about investing: the basket here is effort and belief aimed at one dream, not money, and the source says nothing about savings or risk.
- Read as a promise that commitment guarantees success: Joe describes what he did and what he refused to compromise, and the cut-off source never states an outcome for the commitment.
