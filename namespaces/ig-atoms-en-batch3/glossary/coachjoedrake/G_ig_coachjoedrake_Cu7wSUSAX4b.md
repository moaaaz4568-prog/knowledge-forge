# G_barefoot_warm_ups — barefoot warm-ups

## Term

Barefoot warm-ups are the few minutes Joe spends getting clients out of their shoes before training, and he loves them for the awareness they restore. The phrase appears once, beside proprioception, turf, and ankle and foot problems, and he says people lose awareness of what is happening down there inside narrow shoes. The benefits he names are muscles working, proprioception, and information from the ground, and the cost he names is comfort: concrete and hard gym floors are not comfortable for everyone, and the change from shoes is major. He does not do all his training barefoot, because the most common gym injury is a dropped weight, so the barefoot work stays a small dose before workouts, after workouts, and on recovery days.

## Live Example

> "Love doing barefoot warm-ups, just getting people outside of their shoes, even just for a couple of minutes." — sources/S_ig_coachjoedrake_Cu7wSUSAX4b.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a recommendation to train barefoot always: Joe says he does not do all his training barefoot, and the barefoot work is a short warm-up dose, not the whole session.
- Read as a cure for ankle and foot problems: Joe wishes he had done more of it as a kid, and the source presents it as a little-goes-a-long-way habit rather than a treatment.
