# G_spatial_programming — spatial programming

## Term

Spatial programming is Joe's name for thinking about how a session flows through the gym's space, and he says most coaches are blind to it. The phrase appears once, beside superset, triplex, and primary zone, and his test is simple: are the two or three exercises of a set in one zone, or scattered across different corners of the gym. Keeping the work in one place creates a better flow and a better training experience, and it shows the client that thought went into the session rather than just pairing exercises. Even a big machine counts: the other movements should happen right there in the same space, with equipment brought over as needed.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that supersets must stay in one spot: Joe asks whether the exercises share a primary zone, and the point is flow and time saved, not a ban on moving.
- Read as a program template: spatial programming is a planning idea about space and flow, and the source gives no list of exercises for it.
