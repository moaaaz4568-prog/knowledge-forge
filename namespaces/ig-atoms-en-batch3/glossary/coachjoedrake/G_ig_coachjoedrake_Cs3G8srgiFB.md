# G_yielding_isometric — yielding isometric

## Term

A yielding isometric is a hold where the muscle gives way slowly to a load — here, holding a glute bridge while yielding to gravity — and Joe uses it to build isometric control and stability in the glutes. The phrase appears once, beside glute bridge, motor learning, and mind-muscle connection, and he contrasts it with the overcoming isometric he adds by driving the opposite hand in hard. The hold teaches the client where their glutes are: hold the bridge for 20 to 30 seconds and, he guarantees, they will know exactly where the glutes are. The same position can carry both kinds at once, yielding on one side and overcoming on the other.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a passive stretch: the yielding hold is loaded work against gravity that builds control and stability, and Joe pairs it with an overcoming drive rather than with relaxing.
- Read as the same as any isometric: Joe separates yielding from overcoming by the direction of effort against the load, and the source uses both words for different jobs in one position.
