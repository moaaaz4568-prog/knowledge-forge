# G_ig_coachjoedrake_Cxfp5-cga59 — retraction

## Term

Retraction here is the first shoulder blade motion Joe teaches: squeezing the shoulder blades back together around the rib cage. The word appears three times, beside protraction, elevation, and upward and downward rotation, as the anchor of a five-motion tour of what the blades can do. He contrasts it with protraction, where the blades push apart, then adds elevation, depression, and rotation, showing each one at the top and bottom of a pull-up. The point is that trainers should know what the blades can do even when an exercise asks them not to move.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "First one, as I squeeze my shoulder blades back together, right, those shoulder blades come back around the rib cage and squeeze together, that's retraction." — sources/S_ig_coachjoedrake_Cxfp5-cga59.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a cue to always retract: Joe teaches retraction as one motion among five, and notes that exercises may or may not want the blades moving.
- Read as the whole shoulder lesson: retraction opens the tour, and the source also defines protraction, elevation, depression, and rotation.
