# G_axis_of_rotation — axis of rotation

## Term

The axis of rotation is the point the body hinges around in an RDL, and Joe puts it at the hips: the dumbbells stay close to it, and the further they drift in front, the more torque lands in the wrong place. The phrase appears four times, beside RDLs, hinging, dumbbells, and torque, and the lesson is built on comparing two reps side by side. In the first rep the hips go back cleanly, while in the second the dumbbells push out front and the lifter starts feeling it in the low back. Pulling the weights in tight brings the feeling back to the glutes and hamstrings, which is how Joe tells viewers they have found the right axis.

## Live Example

> "So when we're doing an RDL, we want, I say want because it doesn't always happen, we want the axis of rotation to be here at the hips." — sources/S_ig_coachjoedrake_Cq5BY3Ygc66.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that more torque is better: Joe says extra torque from drifting dumbbells lands in the low back, so more torque here is the fault, not the goal.
- Read as a rule about weight rather than position: the source blames the dumbbells' distance from the axis, not how heavy they are, and the fix is pulling them in tight.
