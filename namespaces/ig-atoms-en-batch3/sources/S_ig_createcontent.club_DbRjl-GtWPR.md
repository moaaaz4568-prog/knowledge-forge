Blockbuster, Kodak, Nokia, MySpace. These four giants were worth hundreds of billions. All gone. And everyone says they didn't adapt, but that's not really what happened. They all made the same mistake. They fell in love with their vehicle instead of the outcome they gave people. Blockbuster could have bought Netflix for fifty million dollars but passed because they thought they were in the DVD rental business, but they were in the 'give me something great to watch tonight' business. Kodak invented the digital camera in 1975 and buried it. Because they thought they were in the film business, when in reality they were in the 'capture the moments matter' business. Nokia sold more phones than anyone on earth, but they thought they were in the 'bottom phone' business, when in reality they were in the 'keep me connected to the world' business. Myspace had every person online, they thought they were in the music page business, when in reality they were in the 'let me be somebody online' business. All of them fell in love with the vehicle, not the outcome they provided. Your personal brand dies the same way. You think you're in a niche, but you're not. You're in an outcome. You're not the fitness coach, you're the guy who gives a tired dad his energy back. While your audience wants will change, a trend changes, a platform changes, but the outcome you give them never changes. So you switch the topic, the angle, the platform, but you keep giving them the outcome they want to achieve. Never marry your vehicle, marry the outcome. The vehicle changes, but the outcome stays.
