One of the greatest advertising books of all time, "Cashvertising" by Drew Eric Whitman. 40 advertising rules in 90 seconds, let's go. Every person is wired with eight core desires, and every ad you make must hit one of them. Staying alive and living longer is the strongest desire we have. Nobody has to be taught to want food and drink. People will pay to get away from fear, pain, and danger. Sex sells because attraction is built into us. People want comfortable living, so show them how to make their life easier. Everyone wants to win and do better than their friends. Protecting the people we love is one of the strongest motivators we have. Social approval drives more buying decisions than people will ever admit. Those eight hardwired desires hit harder than learned wants like saving money or being efficient. Fear works when you give the reader a clean and easy action that makes the threat go away. Get people to picture themselves as the person using your product. Borrow credibility from experts, celebrities, and symbols people already trust. Show how many people already bought because nobody wants to be first. Keep asking, "So what does that get them? " until you hit the real emotional payoff. Long copy signals more value, even to people who never read it. Specific numbers are more believable than round ones. Handle objections before the buyer has a chance to raise them. Make it so simple people don't have to think to get it. Break big chunks of info into small pieces so people can easily digest it. Show people what they lose by waiting, since losses hit harder than gains. Once someone takes a small step, they are way more likely to take the next one. Scarcity and deadlines get people to act now. Real customer testimonials sell harder than anything you say about yourself. A strong guarantee takes the risk off of the buyer. Your headline does 90% of your work, so spend 90% of your time on it. Put your biggest benefit right in the headline. Write to one person and use the word "you" in your copy. Write the way people actually talk. Short sentences and short paragraphs keep people reading. Use subheadlines so skimmers still get the message. Bullet points make your benefits easy to scan. Captions under photos get read, so put a selling point there. Photos of real people grab more attention than illustrations. Before-and-after shots prove results instantly. Ads that look native get watched more than ads that look like ads. Avoid white text on a black background for body copy because it kills readability. Break the price down to a daily cost so it feels small. Tell the reader exactly what to do next and make it dead simple to respond. And test everything because the market decides what works.
