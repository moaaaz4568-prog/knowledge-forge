If you're just jumping into it because it seems like a business opportunity, I don't know many people that have done it that way and had long-term sustainable success. I use the example of the statue of David. At cocktail gathering, someone asked Michelangelo, "It's amazing how you made David out of one piece of rock. How'd you do it?" And his response was, "Well, I just carved away everything that wasn't David." If you're a generalist, you probably have your productized service offering. It's just clouded and covered by scope creep that happened because you didn't know where the next client was coming from, or the scope seep, which most people overlook is you suggesting more work to the client out of fear of losing them, but not getting paid for said additional work. You just start adding stuff on because more is better, but not really, and then you can't see that thing anymore. And so it's kind of just like chipping away to find what your productized service is.
