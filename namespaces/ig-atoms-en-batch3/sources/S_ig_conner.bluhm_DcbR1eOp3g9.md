Something I learned from a mentor of mine that was doing $1.7 million a month is that everybody sets up their lead forms wrong. Now, the standard way that someone builds out a lead form is the first question asked is name, email, and phone number, and from there they proceed into some sort of general qualifying question. Now, the problem with this is that in people's minds, name, email, and phone number is the same level of friction as entering their credit card information. And because of that, it is very rare that people are actually going to input their real information immediately. And because of this, you are going to get less opt-ins and you're gonna get more frequently fake opt-ins. So the way to hedge against this is very, very simple. You need to start off every single lead form you do with a very simple yes or no question. Now, the reason for this is to get them hooked in and actually proceed with finishing the form. Because once someone starts a form, the likelihood of them finishing it massively increases. And from there, we want to run them through an increase in the difficulty of questions, leading to the single most difficult question as the last question on the form. And what is the most difficult question you ask? Name, email, and phone number, because it's the highest level of friction. So an example of what this would look like in reality is the first question would be, are you a business owner? Yes, no. The second question might be, how much is your business doing per month? And then a multiple choice selecting how much they make each month. From there, the question might be, explain in one sentence what your business does. And the question after that finishes with name, email, and phone number. Because when you do this, you'll average a 2x increase on your form submissions, because people will actually want to put in their real information because they have already committed to the form, and they are more likely to do it because they started the form off on an extremely easy question that got them hooked on finishing the rest of it out.
