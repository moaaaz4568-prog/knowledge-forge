If your sales team needs constant pressure to perform, then you don't have a high performance culture. What you've got is more like a pressure cooker, and yes, there's a difference. A lot of managers wear high standards a bit like a badge of honour, and they focus only on trackable metrics: pipeline velocity, KPIs, numbers, deal flow, all of which are important. If that's the whole culture, then you haven't built a sales team. What you've got is more like a reporting department that gets paid commission. And the really uncomfortable bit is that when your numbers or your figures take a shit, most managers won't look to even fix the culture. What they do instead is tighten the thumb screws, and they'll implement more meetings and more training and more tracking, which in my experience of managing multiple seven and eight figure sales teams creates less trust and less ownership. And then everybody acts surprised when team churn goes through the roof. The bit that will piss most managers off is that fun is not a nice to have. It's actually part of the performance. People will sell better when they've got good energy, and they will compete harder when they enjoy the environment. And I'm not talking about turning work into some kind of fucking holiday camp, but I am talking about the fact that misery is not a management strategy. So sure, keep the standards high, keep the numbers visible, but if nobody enjoys being on your team, don't call it high performance. Call it what it is. It's a bad culture with good spreadsheets.
