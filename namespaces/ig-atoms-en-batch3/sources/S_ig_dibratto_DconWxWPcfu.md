This is how anyone can get disgustingly good at writing LinkedIn posts. For context, I've personally written five thousand plus for founders backed by these VCs and more. Every single post myself or my team writes follows the CPIO framework. C stands for convey. All you have to ask is what are you trying to convey with this post? Is it a story? Is it an opinion? Is it a framework? Is it a process? Is it a case study? Whatever it is, figure out what you are trying to say. Next is P for packaging and it's arguably the most important. You have to ask yourself how you can package whatever you're trying to say or show or convey in the most compelling way possible for your reader. So for your context, if you're doing a really long list of points, a carousel is probably your best option. Or if you're doing industry commentary, I would take a screenshot of a headline or a tweet and then obviously write your reaction to it. Or maybe you're writing a really dense process, that's a great candidate for an infographic or a cheat sheet. You'll build a muscle for this over time, but it's very important that your post is packaged the right way. Next is I for information. You know what you're trying to convey, you know how it's gonna be packaged, what information needs to be in the post to make your point? Also very importantly, what does not need to be in the post to make your point? And finally O is for order. You have all of those steps done, how do you order that information in the most logical and compelling way possible?
