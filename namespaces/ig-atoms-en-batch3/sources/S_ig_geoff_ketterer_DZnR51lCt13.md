There are five major things that a million per month sales organization does very differently to the ones that are at six figures. I've personally worked with over 300 businesses on their sales and their sales systems, so here's the five and a couple of them you're not going to like. So number one is that they have a defined, repeatable sales process. Six figure businesses often rely on talented individual closers who can just figure it out. A million a month business runs on a documented step by step sales process that any rep can follow. So the process is the asset, not the person. Number two is that they obsess over objection handling at a system level. At six figures, objection handling is largely improvised. At a million a month, there is a documented objection handling library, call reviews and trainings in place every week for everybody. Number three, and I know some people are going to push back on this, they separate the setter and the closer roles definitively. Six figure businesses often have their closers doing everything. They'll do a bit of outbound, they'll do a bit of outreach, they'll be digging into the pipelines and stuff like that. And essentially that role is fine, but at a million a month, the setters are doing the outreach, the closers are closing deals. You should have your people focused on their job. Number four is that they manage metrics, not feelings. At six figures a month, the owner kind of feels like sales are either going well or poorly. At a million a month, there are clear KPIs that are tracked weekly. Show rates, close rates, cash collection per call, but also things like pipeline value, pipeline velocity, lead scoring, and then tracking those scores back to the specific lead channel. So decisions around hiring, firing, training are made based on data, not gut feeling. Number five, and again, you're probably going to hate this, but they have a very high converting offer architecture. Six figure businesses often sell a somewhat decent offer. A million a month business has engineered their offer to do things like remove perceived risk, increase perceived value, justify premium pricing. And after personally being inside hundreds of sales organizations, the difference is not the value that's on the back end, it's how that value is packaged and explained on the front end. The offer essentially should do half the selling before the call even starts.
