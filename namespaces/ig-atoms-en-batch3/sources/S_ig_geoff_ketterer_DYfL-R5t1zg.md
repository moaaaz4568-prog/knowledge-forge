I recently took a rep from a fourteen percent close rate to a fifty three percent close rate by teaching them something called inverse incentive. See, no two people buy the same way, and if you sell a more sophisticated prospect, that person does not respond well to you overpitching, overhyping, and overselling like a less sophisticated person would. Which is why everybody knows if you work B two B or B two C, you can't sell those two groups the same way. And the inverse incentive is really just the phenomena of you doing the thing that doesn't benefit you. To give you an example, think about a mechanic that phones you up and says, hey, you actually don't need that more expensive part. The cheaper one will do just fine. You didn't ask them to do that. He could easily have taken your money, but he didn't. So now there's a level of trust and you can engineer this into your sales calls as well. Let's say you're selling a high end financial service. I want you at some point in that conversation to turn around and say, I mean, John, I really don't think you need to use us. It looks like everything's going pretty well with your portfolio.
