Three neuroscience principles that will make you disgustingly good at sales. Number one is mirror neurons. Discovered almost by accident when researchers noticed that the same brain cells fired whether a monkey grabbed food or just watched someone else grab it. Humans do this constantly, but it's unconscious. We mirror others. This is why certainty and conviction on sales calls is so important because the prospect will literally experience the same thing. Number two is cognitive dissonance. This is when the brain physically dislikes holding two conflicting beliefs at once, so it works overtime to try and resolve that tension, usually by changing the belief that's easiest to change. So on a sales call, if someone says out loud, yeah, I need to fix this right now, early in the call, and their brain will start fighting to stay consistent with that statement for the rest of the conversation. Number three is priming. John Bargh's research shows exposure to a word or an idea quietly activates the behaviour attached to it. So if you tell a closed off prospect, I appreciate you being this open and honest with me, it says a lot about you. You haven't just complimented them, you've handed them an identity to live up to for the rest of the call. Most people will keep being open and honest just to stay consistent with this version of themselves that you named out loud.
