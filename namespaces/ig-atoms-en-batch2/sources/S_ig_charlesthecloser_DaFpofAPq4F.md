Three principles to become disgustingly good at sales. Number one, detach from outcomes. The Bhagavad Gita, the Tao Te Ching, Marcus Aurelius all held the same perspective. You control your actions, you do not control the outcome. So stop focusing on the close, start focusing on helping the person sat in front of you. If you listen and engage, the close takes care of itself. Number two is congruency. Jim Rohn, who was Tony Robbins' mentor, plus a load of other successful entrepreneurs, speaks about if you back up your words with actions, people have a much higher likelihood of following you. So you are gonna really struggle asking a prospect to make a bold, brave decision and invest in themselves if you've never done it yourself. Your words mean nothing if your life doesn't back it up. Number three is being in the pursuit of mastery. The best closers didn't just stop learning as soon as they finished a program or as soon as they closed the first deal. They're always studying psychology, language, reviewing their calls or investing in further coaching. Whilst the most basic salespeople in the world just focus on the sale and the close, they've never invested a single pound, a dollar into their own skill development or even personal development. They do one month of training at the beginning of a job and that's it. They stop developing completely. That's their ceiling and that's why they stay average forever.
