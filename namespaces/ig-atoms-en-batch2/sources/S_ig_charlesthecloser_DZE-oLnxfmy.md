Ten years in high ticket sales has taught me that you can deal with every objection in pretty much the exact same way. So, the first thing we're gonna do is actually clarify that they believe the solution we are presenting will actually get them the outcome that they're looking for. But throughout this section of the framework, we want to be disarming the prospect and really aiming to get the guard down through our tonality and through our body language. The next step from here is we're actually gonna ask and isolate. This is our way to ask questions and probe to figure out what is the actual real objection. Because nine times out of ten, the objection that a prospect gives you first and foremost is never the real thing. So we need to be understanding, okay, but what's really behind that? And isolate the real objection. Then we want to reframe. A lot of people believe that reframing someone is arguing with them and changing their mind, but it's not. It's just about showing them a different lens to look at their situation with, or a different belief that might change the way that they're thinking about the decision. So we might tell a story, give an example, challenge them, or even just reflect their own words back to them. Finally, we're looking to empower. The last thing we want to do in sales is force someone to make a decision that they don't wanna make. So we wanna tie them to the outcome and the decision, not the investment. And call forward the version of themselves who they actually want to become. There you have it. That's what I call the CARE framework for handling almost any objection.
