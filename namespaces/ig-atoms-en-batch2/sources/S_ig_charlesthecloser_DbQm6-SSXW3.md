Three psychological laws that will make you disgustingly good at sales. Law one is loss aversion. Daniel Kahneman proved something that most closers get completely wrong. Losing something hurts so much more than gaining something feels good. People don't buy because of what they can gain. People buy because they can finally see what it's costing them to stay the same. So stop selling the dream and start selling what they've lost already and what they stand to lose every month that they don't decide. Law two is the Zeigarnik effect. He found that waiters could memorize an entire unpaid bill right up until the moment that the bill was paid. Then they forgot it completely. Open loops stay in the prospect's mind. Closed loops don't. That's why if your prospects are checking out, it's because you're answering too many questions. Use open loop questions in the discovery and in the pitch to keep your prospects engaged. And thirdly, commitment and consistency. People will move mountains to stay consistent with something that they've said out loud. So in your discovery, getting your prospects to explain why this is so important to them, why they can no longer wait, why they can't delay, and holding them to a new identity. By the time that it gets to the real decision, saying no feels like they're contradicting themselves and causing cognitive dissonance, which nobody wants to do. None of this works without actual skill built underneath it. Comment roadmap if you want my sales system that's built around laws like these.
