The greatest lesson in getting people to do exactly what you want didn't actually come from a psychology book. It came from Pokémon Go. If you don't know, Pokémon Go has been collecting 30 billion images of the entire world just from users playing the game. The main purpose of the game wasn't just to make cash from microtransactions; it was to collect these images to train AI robots to deliver things to precise locations because, well, regular GPS just wasn't accurate enough to do it. Now, obviously, if they'd just told people to go around and take pictures of landmarks and roads, no one would have done it.

But if they make it so the user benefits or is entertained by it, you bet your damn shit they're going to do it. It's called the Trojan Horse. So if you want people to listen to you, buy from you, watch your videos, whatever it is, you can't just hand them the raw, shitty, boring information. You need to wrap what you want inside what they actually want, whether it's a laugh or a story or something they're just dying to know. This is the same reason why I'm much more likely to get you to watch an educational marketing video when I wrap it in a Pokémon story.
