This is one of the most valuable lessons that I ever learned in business, and it's something that I had to learn the hard way. I had to learn how to basically act the same whether times were good or bad. Because what we all have the tendency to do is like when times are bad, when our back is against the wall, when we're under pressure, we work harder, we hustle, we grind, we take a lot of action, and we do that out of fear basically. And then when times are good, we take our foot off the gas, we relax, we stop working as much, we procrastinate more, we go on more vacations, we spend more money. But if you keep doing this, what this leads to is a roller coaster. Because in good times, you take your foot off the gas, and then you eventually go back to bad times because you spent too much money, you took too much time off, you stopped doing the things that got you where you are. And then in bad times, you start hustling again, and then you burn yourself out, and you go through this emotional and financial roller coaster. And what I realized is that it's much better to just have a slow and steady approach, to do the same actions, actions that are sustainable, and just be extremely consistent over a long period of time, and act the same during bad times as you do during good times. And if you do that for a long enough period of time, you won't have that roller coaster anymore. You'll just have steady upward growth.
