This sign was put up in a national park to combat theft, but it actually ended up quadrupling it. Because the man who put up this sign forgot this core damn piece of human psychology that's being used on me and you and all of us every single day. And it's the Scarecrow Effect: when the thing you put up to scare something off actually ends up inviting them in.

And this effect states that people subconsciously copy whatever they think feels normal. And by this sign saying 'many past visitors,' it's basically saying, 'It's normal to steal here.' So people kept stealing.

But if you flip this effect to your advantage, getting people to change their behavior becomes a lot damn easier. Like if I tell you 80% of people don't floss their teeth in the morning, that's not going to make you think, 'I should floss.' No, it's going to make you think, 'Oh, so not flossing is normal; I shouldn't do it.' But if I instead say everyone who still has teeth by the age of 70 does this one habit, you're probably going to be like, 'Shit, I should probably floss.'

The rule is: never make the bad behavior sound normal. Instead, make the desired behavior seem like the thing everyone's doing, so doing it feels like the obvious move.

When researchers swapped in this sign, theft basically disappeared.
