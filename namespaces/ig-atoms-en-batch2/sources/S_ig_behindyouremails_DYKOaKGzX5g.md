Working in email and SMS marketing has actually taught me what retention really means: keeping customers with your business for the long haul. And here's the catch: if your product or business model is also bad, then your retention is also gonna act poor. The two have to move together. I've worked with community-driven brands that have it all: a loyal customer base, a great product, strong customer experience, and engaged community. The email opens, click-throughs, repeat purchase rate, and AOVs are all through the roof. Retention marketing works for them because the foundation is solid. And on the flip side, I've worked with brands that think they are creative, and they might be, but their product and customer experience are weak. We've built out every retention flow imaginable, but no amount of clever copy is gonna save a business that is not worth coming back to. Retention marketing amplifies what's already there. It does not fix what's broken. For brands with low AOV and low repeat purchase rate, the game is brutal.
