How we sabotage ourselves in business and life without realizing it. The tricky thing about self-sabotage is it often feels like progress. How often have you had a new idea and felt excited about it, thinking, "This is it. This is exactly what I need to do," only to lose motivation a few weeks later and move on to the next shiny thing? Hey, no shame. We've all done it. But that's how we sabotage ourselves without realizing it, by following our emotions instead of our logic. Emotions tell you to follow what feels good, even if what feels good is bad for your life or business. Emotions tell you to do what you love and avoid anything that feels boring or uncomfortable. Logic, however, tells you that you'll be far more successful if you learn to focus on one thing and concentrate your energy. It's like a magnifying glass under the sun. The power to light a fire depends on how steady you hold its focus. So how do we avoid shiny object syndrome so we can achieve our goals in less time? One thing I think is important is to stop consuming so much, because spending even an hour on social media bombards your mind with dozens of ideas for what you can do with your life or your business. The problem is you can't possibly implement every good idea or strategy you come across. If you're lucky, you can properly implement half a dozen in your entire lifetime. When it comes to business, in most cases, you don't need more than one business. You don't need more than one social media account, and you don't need more than one product to sell. But the world will try to convince you that you do, and that's the problem. Stay focused, my friend.
