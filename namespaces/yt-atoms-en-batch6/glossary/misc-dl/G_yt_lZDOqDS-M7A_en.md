# G_yt_lZDOqDS-M7A_en — pricing model

## Term

In this solo episode of the Agency Profit Podcast, a pricing model is one of the ways an agency charges for its work — hourly, value-based, project, or retainer — and the speaker's point is that every model can be compared with the same math through one pricing formula that normalizes margin. The phrase appears 14 times, beside delivery margin, value based, pricing formula, and client, and the concrete move is to run the formula on an hour of time to see what it should cost the client and what margin it leaves, instead of arguing about hours times rate. Its use here is a billing choice, not a price itself: positioning as a graphic design agency puts the firm in a low value category, while moving up the value ladder to high value, high-risk work is where abstracted time and materials appear.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the price itself: the speaker's pricing model is the billing structure, and the price comes from running the formula on margin, so quoting a model without the math proves nothing about profitability.
- Read as proof that one model is always best: the framework exists to pick the ideal model per situation, and the same formula is what lets hourly, value-based, project, and retainer work be compared apples to apples.
