# G_yt_l_FUhnQnDtA_en — wrong problem

## Term

In this video, a wrong problem is the problem an inexperienced person focuses on while the real one goes unsolved, and the speaker's point is that effort spent on it is wasted no matter how hard the trying is. The phrase appears 4 times, beside solution, identify, and trying, and the concrete move is to sort every situation into four cases — right problem with the right solution, right problem with the wrong solution, wrong problem with the right solution, and wrong problem with the wrong solution — and fix the diagnosis first. Its use here is a diagnostic error, not a lack of effort: the speaker says you learn far more by trying and doing a hundred times than by thinking about something for a hundred hours, but only once the problem itself is the right one, which is where the leverage of an expensive coaching program comes in.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a call to think longer before acting: the speaker's remedy is to identify the problem correctly and then try things out, and endless thinking is the habit he argues against.
- Read as a label for any failure: a wrong problem is specifically a misidentified problem, and a failure with the right problem and the wrong solution is a different case in his four-way split.
