# G_yt_k32dp-knfvw_en — compartmentalization

## Term

In this source compartmentalization is the brain's trick for handling multiple editing projects: the brain can sort thoughts into its working memory, its working bucket, so that one project at a time keeps all resources on one tab instead of ten. The word appears twice, beside working memory, working bucket, one at a time, and parallel, and the speaker offers it as the advantage of the one-at-a-time philosophy over working on projects in parallel. His concrete case is the computer analogy itself — ten open tabs slow a machine, and one tab lets all its resources go into that one tab, just as one project lets the editor's whole working memory serve a single cut.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as multitasking: compartmentalization is the one-at-a-time advantage, keeping working memory on a single project, not doing several at once.
- Read as a brain fact: the ten-tabs analogy is the speaker's picture of working memory, not a measured result about how brains store thoughts.
