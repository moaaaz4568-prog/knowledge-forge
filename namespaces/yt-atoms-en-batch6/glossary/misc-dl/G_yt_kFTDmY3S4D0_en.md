# G_yt_kFTDmY3S4D0_en — golden rules

## Term

Here the golden rules are the thirteen rules of logo design the speaker compiled from sixteen years of graphic design: dynamic application, appropriation, and the rest, each essential before designing the next logo. The phrase appears 8 times, beside logo design, dynamic application, appropriation, and hierarchy, with the singular Golden Rule 16 more times, and the speaker walks them as a list — a logo must work in motion graphics, must be appropriate for its brand and market, and must be memorable rather than descriptive. His concrete case is the line-length rule: forty-five to ninety characters per line of text, spacing included, with magazine layouts as the exception that proves it.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as thirteen suggestions: the speaker calls them essential before designing the next logo, and the list includes dynamic application and appropriation as rules, not tips.
- Read as rules for logos only: the compilation covers golden rules of graphic design more broadly, and the thirteen are the logo-design subset.
