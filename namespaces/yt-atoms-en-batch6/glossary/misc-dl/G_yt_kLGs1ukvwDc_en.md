# G_yt_kLGs1ukvwDc_en — this line

## Term

In this source this line is the dividing line the speakers draw through all their work: below it sits the work that keeps life running — the job, the bills, the rent — and above it sits the work that compounds, like a portfolio or a new skill. The phrase appears 12 times, beside above the line, status quo, portfolio, and financially free, and they define it before anything else because the whole system depends on knowing which side a task falls on. Their concrete case is their own story: they quit their jobs as medical doctors and built a business by focusing only on work above the line, until they worked only on what they wanted.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a line on a chart: this line is a sorting rule for work, not a graph, and a task counts as above it only if it keeps building value over time.
- Read as a promise that quitting a job makes you free: the speakers say they were once afraid they would be homeless, and the line only works when the work above it actually compounds.
