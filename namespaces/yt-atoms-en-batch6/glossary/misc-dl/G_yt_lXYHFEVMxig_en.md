# G_yt_lXYHFEVMxig_en — EBITDA

## Term

In this video about turning industry knowledge into a paid AI service, EBITDA is the number the whole deal is judged by: Dino Watt's client wants to sell her orthodontic practice in five years, make at least 1.2 million from the sale, and for that it has to have a certain EBITDA figure. The term appears 3 times, beside practice, appraisal, goal, and consulting, and the concrete math is that the practice's EBITDA should be around five hundred thousand dollars, and showing her how to get a hundred thousand back with one change brings her that much closer to her EBITDA goal. Its use here is a valuation target: the AI seminar and the paid assessment exist to move the practice's EBITDA, not to teach AI for its own sake.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the price of the practice: EBITDA is the earnings figure the sale price depends on, and the video treats the 1.2 million as the sale goal, not as the EBITDA itself.
- Read as a synonym for revenue: the EBITDA goal is about what the practice keeps after costs, and the hundred thousand back is a saving on that figure, not new sales.
