# G_yt_loX1Sa9fH84_en — associations

## Term

In this memory video, associations are the links the brain makes between things — the method is to tie an idea to a single image that is fastest for the brain to interpret and understand. The word appears 8 times, beside imagine, lungs, and straw, and the concrete move is to picture a smoker breathing through a straw for damaged lungs, or to simplify big words until a fifth grader would understand them, so the association does the remembering. Its use here is a technique, not a coincidence: the speaker calls the method visualization, and says associations can happen in many ways, but fundamentally it is about joining things together in one image.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as memorizing by repetition: the method replaces repetition with a single image, and a list drilled over and over is the opposite of what the video teaches.
- Read as free association with no target: every association in the video is built to remember one specific thing, like what damaged lungs mean, so a wandering chain of images is not the method.
