# G_yt_lTpms_lIPYs_en — typeface psychology

## Term

In this graphic design video, typeface psychology is how real designers use the personalities of typefaces: ignoring the way typefaces look and seem to have personalities is one way to totally ruin a design. The phrase appears twice, beside typeface, sans serif, project, and graphic, and the concrete cases are two designs — one using a typeface called yapari, a sans-serif, and a tennis design where the psychology of the project and the psychology of the people interested in it match the psychology of the typeface. Its use here is a matching exercise: the typeface must fit the feeling of the project and its audience, and the video shows the match working on real client work.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a scientific claim about fonts: the video calls it the psychology of typefaces as designers use it, and the evidence is client designs that match, not a study of readers.
- Read as a rule that sans serifs mean technology: the video says sans serifs are often associated with technology and innovation, but the lesson is to match the typeface to the project, not to apply one association everywhere.
