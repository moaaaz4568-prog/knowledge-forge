# G_yt_kmyEdIkJFOk_en — moral hazard

## Term

In this source moral hazard is the policy mechanism Nick blames for social change: governments pass economic policy that incentivizes morally bad behavior, so people respond to the incentives rather than to any new moral enlightenment. The phrase appears twice, beside economic policy, degeneracy, and social conservatives, and his test case is the cost of substance — gambling and similar activities becoming cheaper and easier to reach. His concrete remedy is blunt: simply eliminate the policies right now that incentivize the bad behavior, and the social change follows the economics.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that people simply became less moral on their own: the speaker says the incentives changed first, and the policies that reward bad behavior are what moved the morals.
- Read as another word for degeneracy: degeneracy is the social outcome he describes, while moral hazard is the policy mechanism — the incentives — that produces it.
