# G_yt_lQZTuURHAP4_en — duality

## Term

In this John Benson lesson, duality is his own term for speaking to both sides of the buying brain: duality is the concept of push pull or pull push, something positive followed by something negative, and it is usually found in sales bullets and benefits. The word appears 15 times, beside push pull, sales copy, bullets, and benefits, and the concrete use is a single sentence that carries both sides at once, which Benson says he uses all the time and which his copy tool gives naturally. Its use here is a copywriting device: duality patterns let one line hold the positive and the negative together, instead of selling only the upside.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that buyers are literally two-brained: Benson says the two sides of the buying brain are pretty close to literal but not literal, so the duality is a copy device, not a neuroscience claim.
- Read as a synonym for contrast: a duality pattern needs the positive followed by the negative in the same line, so a list that only praises the product is not duality here.
