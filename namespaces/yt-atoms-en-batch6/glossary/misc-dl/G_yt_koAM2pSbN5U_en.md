# G_yt_koAM2pSbN5U_en — student loan bubble

## Term

Here the student loan bubble is the crisis the second part of the series tries to solve: policy changes in the late 1970s expanded access to student loans and then prevented them from being discharged in bankruptcy, and that combination drove up the cost of college. The phrase appears once, beside bankruptcy, student interest rates, and moral hazards, and the speaker measures schools by whether their students can pay off their debts. His concrete case is the generation of underclass wage slaves stuck because student interest rates run much higher than trades, mortgages, or any non-credit-card debt.

## Live Example

> "the late 1970s involving expanding access to student loans and then preventing them from being discharged in bankruptcy that created the student loan bubble that we were going through today and the over the expensive cost of college education and so in this promise go into how can we solve that" — sources/S_yt_koAM2pSbN5U_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as blaming students for borrowing too much: the speaker blames the policy incentives — expanded access plus no discharge in bankruptcy — not the borrowers.
- Read as a claim that college has no value: he says plainly there is value in a university degree, and the fix is cleaning out the moral hazards, not closing colleges.
