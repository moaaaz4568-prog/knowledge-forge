# G_yt_k22HKpzDMg4_en — shut up

## Term

Here shut up is the speaker's rule for the moment a client says yes: once the client says yes, shut up, just shut up, stop — because people keep talking and talking and talk themselves out of the sale. The phrase appears 4 times, beside client says yes, sale, talking, and stop, and he frames it as a discipline, not rudeness: the work is done, the idea is sold, and every extra sentence risks undoing it. His concrete case is the pattern he sees all the time — a seller who has already won the yes keeps pitching until the client backs away.

> "Going past the cell, Wanna tell you about this, okay people do this all the time, because we put so much work into our idea or effort once the client says yes shut up just shut up stop, people don't realize this they just keep talking and talking and you'll talk yourself out of the sale." — sources/S_yt_k22HKpzDMg4_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as rudeness to the client: the speaker's shut up is a rule for after the yes, when the sale is already won, not a way to handle objections or questions.
- Read as a ban on all follow-up: stopping the pitch after a yes is the point, and it does not mean the seller should never speak to the client again.
