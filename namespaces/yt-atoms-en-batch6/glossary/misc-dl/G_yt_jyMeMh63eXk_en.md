# G_yt_jyMeMh63eXk_en — intent

## Term

Here intent is the reason behind each design choice, set against work that only looks good: experts back every choice with intent, while beginners pick colors, typefaces, or layouts because they look quote unquote good. The word appears 3 times, beside strategy, research, font, and layout, and the speaker makes it the test that separates expert from beginner — a choice counts as expert work only when its reason can be stated. His concrete case is a CTA button chosen because it is proven to boost conversions, a font chosen because it speaks to the brand's audience psychology, and a layout chosen because it directs the eye where the designer wants it to go.

## Live Example

> "only really the beginning. Experts, on the other hand, don't just rely on what looks good. Every single design choice is backed up by intent. Why this font? Well, because it speaks to the brand's audience psychology. Why this layout? Because it directs the eye where we want it" — sources/S_yt_jyMeMh63eXk_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as taste: intent here is a stated reason for a choice — a font for audience psychology, a layout for the eye — not a feeling that the design looks good.
- Read as a rule that kills intuition: the speaker keeps intuition as the beginning for designers, and intent is what the choice must grow into, not a ban on instinct.
