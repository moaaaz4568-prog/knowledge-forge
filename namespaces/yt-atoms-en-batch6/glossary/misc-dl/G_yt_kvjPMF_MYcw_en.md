# G_yt_kvjPMF_MYcw_en — proprietary system

## Term

In this source proprietary system is what Taki calls the three-part structure every one of his programs is based on: a problem, a promise, and a process. The phrase appears once, beside 6-week course, problem, and promise, and the video is a short lesson in building a 6-week course priced between $500 and $8,000 to sell services one-to-many. His concrete case is the problem step itself: grab a wall and some stickers, write down the ideal client's fears and frustrations on the left side, and the problem is the list that results.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "depending on the outcome you provide to clients, there are three main components to keep in mind. Each program is based on what we call a proprietary system. It has three main components: a problem, a promise, and a process. Here's what I want you to do. First, think about your ideal customer" — sources/S_yt_kvjPMF_MYcw_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a patented product: proprietary here means his own named system — a problem, a promise, and a process — not a patent or a piece of software.
- Read as a course outline: the three components are what every program is based on, and the 6-week course is only the first way he sells it.
