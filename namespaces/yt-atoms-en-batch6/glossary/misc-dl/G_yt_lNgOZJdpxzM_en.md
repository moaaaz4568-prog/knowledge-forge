# G_yt_lNgOZJdpxzM_en — jet ski

## Term

In this coaching business video, a jet ski is the metaphor for a small, nimble team: the speaker would take a jet ski over a cruise ship every single time, and warns that hiring and hiring turns the team from a speedy, fun jet ski into something slow, heavy, and sluggish like a cruise ship. The phrase appears 5 times, beside cruise ship, team, bloat, and Zoom, and the concrete moment is a monthly all-hands call where page one of Zoom fills up and the meeting spills to page two — the sign the team has become a two-page Zoom company. Its use here is a hiring warning: the speaker says you did not grow talent, you grew bloat, and the fix is to stop throwing people at problems.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that small teams are always better: the video's point is about speed and fun in the team, and the cruise ship is what happens when hiring outruns the work, not a verdict on every large team.
- Read as a literal travel preference: the jet ski and the cruise ship are both team metaphors here, and the choice between them is about how the business feels to run.
