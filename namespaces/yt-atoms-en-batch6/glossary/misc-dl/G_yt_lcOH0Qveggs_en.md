# G_yt_lcOH0Qveggs_en — old money

## Term

In this video, old money is the wealth of families who have held it for generations, and it names a lifestyle and a set of habits, not an amount in a bank account. The phrase appears 7 times, beside lifestyle, wealth, and family, and the concrete move in the discussion is to look at how old money clients live — a fifth-generation family, a single family office, a wealth management firm — and what lessons a wealth advisor draws from them. Its use here is a social category, not a compliment: the speaker tells stories of people who lived as trust fund babies, sold shares to LVMH during a quiet takeover attempt of Hermes, and budgeted carefully on a couple of billion dollars.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as simply being rich: the speaker's old money is generational — fifth-generation families and inherited fortunes — so a newly wealthy person is not old money no matter how large the fortune.
- Read as a guarantee of good judgment: the stories include reclusive lives and family fights over the money, so the label describes where the wealth came from, not how wisely it is used.
