# G_yt_kwmm6PCQJHg_en — retention marketing

## Term

In this source retention marketing is the discipline most brands get wrong: they set up a few flows, send a couple of email campaigns a week, and rarely check in on profits — maybe once a month — while their customer acquisition cost climbs and profits shrink. The phrase appears twice, beside retention system, cohorts, and click-through rate, and the video is a customer retention audit of a brand doing over a million dollars in sales a month, so the mistakes are shown on a real account. His concrete case is the contrast he draws: brands with a customized retention system analyze the account constantly and launch new tests every week, while the rest chase acquisition and watch profits shrink.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as send more emails: the audit is about analyzing what works and launching new tests every week, not about increasing volume — most brands already send a couple of campaigns a week.
- Read as a claim that email is dead: the failure he documents is a low click-through rate hurting deliverability over time, which is an email problem with an email fix.
