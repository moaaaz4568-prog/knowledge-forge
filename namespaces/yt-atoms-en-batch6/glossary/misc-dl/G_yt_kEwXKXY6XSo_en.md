# G_yt_kEwXKXY6XSo_en — reserve currency

## Term

Here a reserve currency is the money of the dominant empire, and the speaker walks the last five hundred years through the Dutch guilder, the British pound, the American dollar, and the Chinese currencies: the reserve currency regime tends to shift slower than the great power regime. The phrase appears 4 times, beside empire, dollar, financial center, and capital markets, with the plural reserve currencies once more, and he measures relative power by economic output, world trade share, military strength, financial-center strength, and the currency's strength as a reserve currency. His concrete case is the American position: financial-center status is probably the last thing the US gives up, because its capital markets are the deepest, and that is what keeps the dollar's reserve role.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the strongest economy's money: a reserve currency is the money of the dominant empire, and the speaker measures power by trade, military strength, and financial-center strength together.
- Read as a claim that the dollar is finished: the speaker says financial-center status is probably the last thing the US gives up, so the regime shifts slower than the great power regime.
