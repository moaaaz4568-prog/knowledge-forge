# G_yt_khDDNeLszxA_en — shrink rate

## Term

In this source the shrink rate is the speaker's defined term for the percentage of revenue lost to theft: shoplifting, employee embezzlement, and lost items in transit from shipping inventory all count toward it. The phrase appears 4 times, beside shoplifting, theft, employee embezzlement, and lost items in transit, and he introduces it as a term — a term called the shrink rate — because it explains why retailers raise prices where theft is higher. His concrete case is Best Buy: its CEO said large-scale organized theft and shoplifting hit margins, and the stock went down 16 percent on that report.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as shoplifting alone: the shrink rate here covers shoplifting plus embezzlement plus lost items in transit, so one kind of theft is only part of it.
- Read as a reason to blame customers only: the speaker lists employee embezzlement beside shoplifting, so the loss comes from inside the store too.
