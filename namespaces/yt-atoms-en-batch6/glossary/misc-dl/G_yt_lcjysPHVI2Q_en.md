# G_yt_lcjysPHVI2Q_en — value assignment

## Term

In this long book discussion, value assignment is the brain's job of deciding what matters — the author's claim is that it is the brain's first and most fundamental function, and everything else in the book hangs off it. The phrase appears 72 times, beside brain, effective action, and values, and the concrete move is to trace how a high-capacity discriminatory system assigns values to what it detects, so that effective action determination becomes possible and the organism can act on what benefits it. Its use here is a mechanism, not a moral judgment: values are made and used by subjective entities for subjective purposes, and the book argues that this subjectivity is exactly why the same system cannot apply the same values to everything it detects.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a price tag on things: the speaker's value assignment is the brain assigning significance to what it senses, and no market price or dollar figure is involved.
- Read as proof that values are objective: the book's own hypothesis is that values are made by subjective entities for subjective purposes, so treating the assignment as a measurement of something out in the world reverses the argument.
