# G_yt_lPBTfQyV3Lo_en — time to value

## Term

In this training on scaling past thirty thousand a month, time to value is how fast a client gets activated after launch: the speaker says to start tracking time to launch and time to value, because if either is really bad, the business lacks clarity on the fastest ROI activities. The phrase appears 3 times, beside time to launch, ROI, activation, and fulfillment, and the concrete test is how fast people get some level of leads or qualified appointments — not time to ROI, which the speaker says can be much later. Its use here is a diagnostic: a slow time to value means the fulfillment is not delivering speed to activation, and that is why companies get stuck at the thirty to fifty thousand a month mark.

## Live Example

> "To know the reason why you need to know this is because if if your time to launch and time to value is really bad, then it's because you don't have clarity on what are the fastest ROI activities that you do that actually impact this speed to value, speed to activation on your fulfillment." — sources/S_yt_lPBTfQyV3Lo_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the same thing as time to ROI: the speaker separates the two, and time to value is about activation and first leads, while ROI can come much later.
- Read as a marketing metric: time to value is measured on fulfillment — how fast the client gets activated — not on how fast the ads are launched.
