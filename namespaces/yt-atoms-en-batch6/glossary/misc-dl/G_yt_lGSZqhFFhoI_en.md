# G_yt_lGSZqhFFhoI_en — joint venture

## Term

In this interview with Five Guys Europe chief John Eckford, a joint venture is the ownership structure behind the British expansion, set against a plain franchise: Eckford says the company would never have made its property decisions without being a joint venture as a franchisee, and would not have paid the premium for a ten thousand square foot property on the Champs-Elysees. The phrase appears 5 times, beside franchisee, premium, property, and the Morel family, and Eckford describes structuring the joint venture himself, hiring the first employees, and opening the first restaurant with what he calls amazing momentum. Its concrete case is the Covent Garden opening, where a study had said the number one criteria for customers across the UK was freshness, and the venture was signed just before that first restaurant opened outside the US.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a franchise agreement: the venture here is a joint ownership deal with the Morel family, not a franchise contract, and Eckford contrasts the two when he talks about who decides on property.
- Read as proof that joint ventures always pay a premium: the Champs-Elysees premium is one property decision in this story, not a rule about what every joint venture costs.
