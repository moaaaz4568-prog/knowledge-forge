# G_yt_TZzV9WbB7FU_en — direct selection tool

## Term

In this Illustrator tutorial, the direct selection tool is the white arrow tool, pressed with A, that moves individual shapes inside a group without ungrouping them, and the phrase appears 3 times in the source. The first of seven tips to improve your workflow is to use it instead of ungrouping: pressing V, the standard selection tool, only moves the group as a whole, while the direct selection tool moves individual shapes and even edits them by moving the anchor points despite the facts they are locked together in a group. The speaker demonstrates with three vector shapes grouped by command G or ctrl G, then changes their order or style without breaking the group apart. In use, the tool is the way to edit one shape inside a busy design — selecting every object with a white fill color, for example — without disturbing the rest of the artwork.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the standard selection tool: pressing V moves the whole group at once, while the direct selection tool — the white arrow, pressed with A — moves single shapes and anchor points inside the group, so the two tools do opposite jobs here.
- Read as a way to ungroup: the tool exists so you do not have to ungroup at all — the shapes stay locked together in the group while you edit one of them — so reaching for ungroup first defeats the tip.
