# G_yt_SdNsh4kaCZg_en — message

## Term

Here the message is what a design is trying to deliver, the second and largest level of the speaker's pyramid: before details or aesthetics, the designer must understand the message, because small details are useless if the message is lost. The word appears 12 times, beside "understand", "always", "behind", and "tailor", and the speaker repeats the rule twice — always understand the message you are trying to deliver through your design. In his use the message is a language like the written word: a poster for a sale must carry the sale as its message, and a clothing-store design must evoke the sleek look its buyers want to buy into. The message also depends on the end user, so it is tailored to the specific group the design targets rather than to the designer's own taste.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as the words on the page: here the message is what the whole design delivers, not the copy alone — the article's language and the design's look both carry it, and either can lose it.
- Read as decoration: the speaker's rule is that aesthetics without the message are useless, so a pretty design that drops its message fails by the standard of this video, not by taste.
