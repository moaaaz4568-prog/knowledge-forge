# G_yt_Thx3o_rA8ZM_en — gross margin

## Term

In this video, gross margin is the agency metric the speaker watches because it decides what the business can afford: the phrase appears 19 times in the source. He wants a vacuum gross margin well above 70 because he expects at least 10% decay in real life on a P&L, with full-time resources still costing money while no one is 100% utilized, and he links it to cash flow, project based work, and the customer acquisition cost payback period. His concrete rule is to get paid in advance so he can redeploy the money, and to keep the margin high enough to make big OPEX investments. In use, gross margin is the buffer that lets an agency owner invest ahead of revenue instead of scrambling after it.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as profit alone: the speaker's gross margin is the buffer for OPEX investment and advance payment, so a high margin with no cash discipline still leaves the agency unable to redeploy money.
- Read as a reason to cut costs: he wants the margin high enough to invest in the right people, so squeezing the team to protect the number breaks the growth the margin is meant to fund.
