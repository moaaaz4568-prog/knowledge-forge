# G_yt_T_aXgB4H-Ag_en — fill it in

## Term

In this story, to fill it in is what the speaker's nonverbal son Ethan is asked to do when a sentence is left unfinished, and the phrase appears 2 times in the source. The therapists told the family to start a sentence, leave the last word off, and wait for Ethan to fill it in — it did not have to be the right word, just an attempt, even a sound. The family would point at people and name them — dad, Jotham, Zechariah, then mom — and wait, and wait, and wait for what seemed like an eternity, sometimes 30 or 40 seconds, hoping for a grunt or some kind of effort. In use, filling it in is the child's attempt at the missing word, and the waiting is the point: the first trick was to get down to the child's level, on one knee at eye level, and the second is to leave the space and let the child try to fill it.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as finishing someone's sentence for them: here the adult stops and waits — sometimes 30 or 40 seconds — so the child attempts the word himself, and supplying the word yourself removes the whole exercise.
- Read as a test with a right answer: it did not have to be the right word, only an attempt, so correcting Ethan toward the exact word mistakes the goal, which is the effort to speak.
