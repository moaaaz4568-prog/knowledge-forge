# G_yt_TB0yceuXmrI_en — self-doubt

## Term

Here self-doubt is what Brian Armstrong felt when everybody he talked to thought Coinbase was a bad idea: like any reasonable person would have quit, he was filled with self-doubt, and then Coinbase was valued at a billion dollars. The phrase appears 3 times, beside "filled", in a long Diary of a CEO interview that returns to the feeling twice. In his use self-doubt is the state before the glimmer: he thought maybe he was crazy, because all his friends who he talked about the idea with thought it was a bad idea, and the thing that gave him a glimmer was seeing technology as a way to try to have a big impact on the world in a scalable way. His concrete counterweight is the superpower the host names at the start: it is a real superpower to care less what other people think, and the same feeling ran through the early years — a tutoring business as his first real company, a spark of inspiration while paying the bills, and lines out the door of customers trying to sign up.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as weakness: here self-doubt is the honest state before the idea worked — the entry records the doubt and the glimmer, not a verdict on the founder.
