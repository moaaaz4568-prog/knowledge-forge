# G_yt_SjGeVqhkKPw_en — market

## Term

Here the market is the first thing successful direct-to-consumer brands master: the market you choose to participate in is the biggest determinant of how high you can go once you are successful, more than the product or the ads. The word appears 18 times, beside "choose", "active", "targeting", and "total addressable", and the speaker gives it three levels — the industry's general trend, demand measured with Google Trends and keyword volume, and the business model that fits. In his use the market sets a ceiling: even the best product and the best ads hit a limit in who can buy, and that limit is known as TAM, the total addressable market, the total number of people who would actually buy. His concrete warning is that TAM misleads beginners — it feels great at first because the content, message, and product are all focused, until the brand outgrows the people the market holds.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as any large market: here the market must be active and growing, because even competitors' ads can boost your sales — a stagnant or declining category fails the test even if it is big.
- Read as TAM alone: the speaker says TAM can mislead when you are starting out, so the entry records the market as three levels of checking, not as one number.
