# G_yt_TOHQXdCAo5k_en — intro

## Term

Here the intro is the first 20 seconds that decide whether a video blows up or wastes its potential: This is how to make a killer YouTube intro to blow up your channel — and while your next video could be the one that blows up your channel, all of this potential could be wasted on the first 20 seconds. The word appears 26 times, beside "youtube", "killer", "blow", and "watch", in a video that walks through four simple steps the best YouTube intros follow. In the speaker's use the intro starts before the video: step one is to nail your title and thumbnail, because you could have the funniest, most beautiful, most perfect video in the world, but if no one clicks on it in the first place, no one will ever see it. His concrete measures are the pacing numbers: in his intros Mr. Beast changes visuals every 1.4 seconds and Mark Rober changes visuals every 1.6 seconds — and when it comes time to edit together the different shots and visuals you've created for your intro, pacing is crucial, so watch your intro again and again, in a noisy environment, with no sound, and get your 7-year-old cousin to watch it.
