# G_yt_SqCjRUT7JW8_en — headline

## Term

Here a headline is the top line of a landing page, judged by whether it names the exact buyer and the exact result: the example the speaker calls a damn good headline reads "get 60 exclusive home buyer appointments that are guaranteed to show up and are ready to have a sales conversation". The word appears 10 times, beside "sub", "create", "damn", and "never", and its plural "headlines" appears too. In his use a good headline is not flashy — it starts with the power word "get", states the offer plainly, and puts the page in a club of its own because the appointments are guaranteed to show up. The sub headline does the second job, naming the reader's number one problem: "never speak with a bad lead ever again", because the mortgage loan officers' problem is bad conversations and leads that do not show up.

## Synonyms

- headlines (the plural form, used for more than one top line across the pages)

## Live Example

> "get 60 exclusive home buyer appointments that are guaranteed to show up and are ready to have a sales conversation that's a damn good headline" — sources/S_yt_SqCjRUT7JW8_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as clever copy: here the headline is judged by specificity — the exact buyer, the exact number, the guarantee — not by wordplay, and a flashy line that drops the specifics fails the test.
- Read as the whole page: the headline is only the top of the landing page, so a good headline does not save a page whose title is too long or whose mobile version repeats the same problem.
