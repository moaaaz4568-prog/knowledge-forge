# G_yt_T-BljCx4LFM_en — logo type

## Term

Here a logo type is the wordmark part of a logo, as opposed to the logo symbol: the video asks whether you fully understand the connection between a logo symbol and the logo type, and then looks at real-life examples of awesome logo type and horrible logo type. The phrase appears 11 times, beside "horrible", "awesome", "designers", and "different", in a video that spends most of its time on the typefaces behind the type. In the speaker's use the logo type carries the brand's feeling through its font: serif fonts typically promote feelings of class and heritage, making them ideal for a company that wants to feel established, while a bold sans-serif choice signals a different move. His concrete examples are Bacardi, whose classical serif fits a long-lasting company with a historical background, and a 2014 rebrand that swapped a serif font for a bold sans-serif to add professionalism.
