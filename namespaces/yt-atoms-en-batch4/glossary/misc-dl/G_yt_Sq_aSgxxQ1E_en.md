# G_yt_Sq_aSgxxQ1E_en — road trip

## Term

Here a road trip is the speaker's test for buying, selling, or entering a strategic partnership: if you cannot spend a road trip with this person, do not do the deal with them. The phrase appears 2 times, beside "spend", "person", and "full day trip", in the advice he says he always gives people — go over their house, or better, go on a road trip, a full day trip, and if you leave better off than you were, it is going to work out. In his use the trip is a character test stretched over hours: a couple of hours at someone's house shows something, but a full day on the road shows whether you can stand the person. The concrete rule is the feeling at the end: leave better off than you were, and the deal can work; if not, it will not.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as travel advice: here the road trip is a test of a business partner, not a holiday — the entry records the deal test, not a recommendation to travel.
- Read as a guarantee: the speaker says leaving better off is the sign it will work out, but the entry records his rule of thumb from experience, not a promise about any specific deal.
