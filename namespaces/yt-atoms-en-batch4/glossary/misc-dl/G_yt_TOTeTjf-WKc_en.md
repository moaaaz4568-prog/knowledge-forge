# G_yt_TOTeTjf-WKc_en — volatility

## Term

Here volatility is the one input the option trader can't know: of all the inputs that go into the replication of an option, the one that's not very well known is the volatility, because we don't know how much a stock is going to move around in the future. The word appears 21 times, beside "that's", "there's", "finance", and "returns", in an Analyzing Finance episode with Chris Adam, an options trader professionally for over 20 years. In the speakers' use volatility is what separates the two traders: a directional trader has an opinion about whether an asset is going to go up or down, while a vol trader believes the underlying is fairly priced and has a view that the option is mispriced and the volatility is either too cheap or too expensive — so by delta hedging they sterilize the position against movements in the underlying and try to isolate the volatility. His concrete lesson is the Twitter puts: he bought Twitter puts for six months out, the implied vol was so high that those puts were worth 20% of the whole company, and the stock went up about 19% in those six months — so he didn't make any money even though it pretty much moved the way he expected, because he lost on the fall trade.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a risk: here volatility is the unknown input, not the danger — the entry records the unknown, not the fear.
- Read as a guess: the speaker's vol trader isolates volatility by delta hedging, so the entry records the isolation, not the bet.
