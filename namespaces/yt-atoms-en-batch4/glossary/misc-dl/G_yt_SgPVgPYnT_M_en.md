# G_yt_SgPVgPYnT_M_en — mental health

## Term

Here mental health is what Reshma Saujani says she sacrificed while building Girls Who Code and trying to have a baby: the work is never done, and for a social entrepreneur it always comes at the sacrifice of others, so she got that really wrong. The phrase appears 5 times, beside "personal", "crisis", and "how", in advice that runs through the whole interview — put your personal mental health and your personal health first, and do not wait for rock bottom to hit. In her use the threat is hustle culture plus intensive parenting: working mothers are expected to be completely on at work and constantly on top of their children, and that double expectation is why we have a mental health crisis, with 51% of mothers saying they are anxious and depressed. Her concrete rule is to see the signs early and take breaks — take time off, take a nap — instead of living in the up-and-crash cycle until rock bottom arrives.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as self-care tips: here mental health is the cost of a specific way of working — building something while never stopping — not a list of wellness habits, and the entry records her warning, not a treatment.
- Read as a claim about all mothers: the 51% figure is a study she cites about mothers' anxiety and depression, so the entry ties the crisis to the double expectation on working mothers, not to women in general.
