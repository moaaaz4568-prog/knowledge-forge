# G_yt_IlBqA2eCWzM_en — pricing

## Term

Colin uses "pricing" for the money rules of his agency, when to raise prices and when to charge again for the same client. The word appears 4 times, next to "re-engagement fee", "redo", "double", and "prices". His example is the re-engagement fee: after a year, a client who never sent the needed information must agree to new pricing before work resumes. The same thinking shows up when he tells prospects that a service normally at 5,000 a month is 10 when the agency has no availability to deliver.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "have a re-engagement fee and then after a year you have to redo the pricing that they agreed on if they haven't got you the information" — sources/S_yt_IlBqA2eCWzM_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a fixed price list: the pricing here is situational, a fee for re-engagement and a higher price when availability is short, so no single number in the interview is the agency's standard rate.
- Read as advice to double prices for every client: the doubling story is about one situation where the agency could not deliver, and the speaker says the worst case is that the prospect says no, so it is a negotiation move, not a rule for all clients.
