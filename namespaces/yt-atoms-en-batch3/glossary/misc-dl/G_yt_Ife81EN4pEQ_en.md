# G_yt_Ife81EN4pEQ_en — grand

## Term

In this video "grand" is the speaker's unit for a thousand dollars, used whenever he prices a result. It appears 15 times, in amounts like "10 grand a month", "20 grand", "300 grand", and "50 grand". He uses it to draw the line between slow linear income and the exponential growth he is arguing for, where the goal is getting rich fast rather than inching from 10 grand a month to 20. The clearest use is his story of a client who made over 300 grand, offered as proof that a good solution the market wants pays at that scale.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a precise financial figure: "grand" here is spoken shorthand for a thousand dollars, so "10 grand a month" is an approximate income band the speaker uses to argue about growth speed, not an audited revenue number.
- Read as a promise that anyone can jump to 300 grand: the 300-grand client is one example of a good solution the market wants, and the speaker ties the result to the offer and the market, not to copying the amount.
