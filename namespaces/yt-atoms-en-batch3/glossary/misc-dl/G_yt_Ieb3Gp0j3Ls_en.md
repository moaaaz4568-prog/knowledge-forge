# G_yt_Ieb3Gp0j3Ls_en — white collar

## Term

Nick uses "white collar" for office and digital work done on a computer, the kind he says AI will hit hardest. The phrase appears 6 times, always set against "blue collar" work, and next to "wages", "employment", and "labor". His point is that the pay and status gap between blue collar and white collar work is already narrowing, and AI speeds that up because it devalues digital work that is low on creativity. He illustrates it with lawyers drafting boilerplate contracts and copywriters, both named as white collar work AI can already do.

## Synonyms

- white-collar (the hyphenated form used in this source)

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a job that AI will erase outright: Nick's claim is narrower, that AI devalues low-creativity white collar work such as boilerplate drafting, while he still treats high-creativity and physical work as far less affected.
- Read as a claim about all office workers earning more than tradespeople: the source says the income and status gap between blue collar and white collar work is already narrowing, with some tradespeople earning more than office workers.
