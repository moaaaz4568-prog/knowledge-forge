# G_yt_Jh5xKbuvMlA_en — data

## Term

In this source, data is the measurements companies collect to design products that fit real people, and the video is about using it well and knowing when to stop. The word data appears 51 times, next to design, headset, and Microsoft, as the speaker explains why a headset is hard to fit: individuals have radically different facial features, so if one person's brow ridge is zero millimeters and another's is 15 millimeters, Microsoft had to scan faces to make it fit. Nike, he adds, invests heavily in data to determine the outsole structure of its highest performing shoes. Data here is therefore not a spreadsheet but the evidence that shapes a product, and the lesson is that companies must be careful about how the data they collect affects their customers.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as numbers in a spreadsheet, data misses the design: here it is the measurements that shape a headset to fit every face, and the data decides the shape.
- Read as a resource to collect endlessly, it ignores the limit: the speaker says making a headset more comfortable stops being useful when the data stops being useful after a certain point.
