# G_yt_IviIMAItx2s_en — font

## Term

The speaker uses "font" for the typeface chosen for a design, and choosing it well is the whole lesson. The word appears 19 times, with the plural "fonts" also used 17 times, next to "design", "pairing", "harmony", and "audience". His rule is that the font must match the emotion and style of the design: a playful font fits a preschool nursery flyer, while a lawyer's business card needs a professional, important, strong look. The practical method is pairing, a sans-serif heading with a serif body, or two fonts that share a style, and he points to a bundle of licensed fonts in the description.

## Synonyms

- fonts (the plural form used in this source)

## Live Example

> "the font is just one aspect of the design as a whole and so needs to play into this style and the emotion of the design in general" — sources/S_yt_IviIMAItx2s_en.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a rule that any font works if it looks nice: the font must play into the design's style and the audience's expectation, so a beautiful font on the wrong card is exactly the mistake the video warns about.
- Read as a shopping list: the bundle in the description is where to get the fonts, but "font" here names the choice itself, and the lesson is how to choose and pair, not which bundle to buy.
