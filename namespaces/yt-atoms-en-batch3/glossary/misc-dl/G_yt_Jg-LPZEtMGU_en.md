# G_yt_Jg-LPZEtMGU_en — pareto

## Term

In this source, pareto is the distribution pattern the speaker uses to explain why success compounds. The word pareto appears 21 times, next to percent, top, and distribution, as he describes how good news happening to someone makes more good news more likely, and misfortune generally brings more misfortune. The numbers are stark: one person's net worth is 480 times what an even distribution would give, and 192 times the net worth of the bottom 80 percent of the distribution. The speaker's warning is kind: do not feel down about yourself when looking at this, because a lot of Pareto distributions apply, but it is not always the same. Pareto here is therefore not a formula but a lens on inequality, and the video's point is how compounding works.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a maths rule, pareto misses the human point: here it is the pattern that good news compounds into more good news, and the speaker warns not to feel down about not being in the top.
- Read as a fixed law, it ignores the exceptions: the speaker says a lot of Pareto distributions apply, but it is not always the same, and luck versus skill is a question for a future video.
