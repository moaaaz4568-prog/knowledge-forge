The next time you're on a sales call and a prospect tells you, 'I just found out about you yesterday,' or 'I didn't even know that this existed,' here's exactly how you're going to handle it. One thing that's really important to understand about your prospects is they're going to fit into one of two categories. They're either going to be in the camp of internalizing responsibility or externalizing responsibility.

Let me explain. So the person who internalizes responsibility is the person who says, 'The reason I'm not making good money... is because my choices.' This is my responsibility, and I'm willing to do whatever it takes at all costs to get what I want. The other person is going to say, 'The reason I'm in a bad financial position is because my parents, they just didn't teach me enough,' or 'My education didn't give me what I needed,' or 'Inflation is too high.'

These are all wrong. Because ultimately, if I can't get the prospect to take personal ownership and responsibility for their life, I cannot help them. They will be stuck where they're at. Because truly, when they say these things and they externalize such as, 'I just found you yesterday' or 'I didn't even know this exists,' what they're really saying is, 'Someone should have told me what to do,' or 'You should have spent more money on advertising and I would have seen you months ago.'

If these behaviors are not addressed, your prospect will not purchase. If they think they're right and their way of going about living life and making decisions is correct, they will not listen to you. Everything that you say will be through the lens of, 'This is a salesman trying to take from me.' So handle this in discovery and it won't come up at the end of your call.
