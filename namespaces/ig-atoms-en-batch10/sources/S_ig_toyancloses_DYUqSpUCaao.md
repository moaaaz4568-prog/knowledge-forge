The moment you open your sales call by telling the prospect how the call is gonna go step by step, you've communicated something to them that you didn't intend to, and you've just told a serious buyer that they're about to be managed. This advice is common amongst online sales training. You set a really hard frame early, you take control of the call and you lay out the structure. And I'm not saying the logic behind that is fully wrong. There are closers who should do it if they're a beginner, and there are offers where it makes more sense to do that. Structure helps, but there is a version of this that backfires completely when you've got an experienced buyer. When you tell a confident, capable, decision-making adult exactly how the conversation is gonna go, what you're actually communicating is, I'm here, I'm leading, you are being evaluated. And that dynamic threatens the identity of someone who sees themselves as a capable decision maker. It creates resistance before you've even got started. What works instead is collaborative language. For these types of prospects, the way that I open my call after the rapport and the niceties is very simple. So we can focus on what's important to you. What do you need to get out of this conversation so we can move towards a decision, if any? And that is a completely different energy than just trying to guide someone through a process. You're inviting them to think alongside you, instead of just making them feel like they're someone that's being processed in a lab. That's the difference.
