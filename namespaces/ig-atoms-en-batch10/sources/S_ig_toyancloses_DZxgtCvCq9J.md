The most excited closer in the room is almost always the one that goes home empty-handed, and nobody talks about why. Because we've been sold this idea that energy sells, that enthusiasm sells, and that if you walk into a conversation with enough enthusiasm, that the prospect will just buy. And that works in certain rooms with certain buyers, usually the desperate ones. But the moment that you bring that into a conversation with a serious buyer, a wealthy buyer, someone who's been around long enough to recognize when something is a performance, they pull back. Not consciously, just that instinctive step away from what they feel is a sales rep that needs the outcome more than they do. Because the energy of a closer who is trying too hard is readable. It tells the buyer that this person is invested in winning instead of invested in helping them. And the moment a buyer senses that, they know that you're not thinking about them, you're thinking about you and your commission. That is the single fastest way to lose a serious person's trust. The closers that I've seen operate at the highest level are not the most energetic in the room. They're actually the most grounded and the calmest. They're the ones that feel like they don't need the sale, who clearly seem like they've had this conversation thousands of times and they're not in a hurry. That energy, that stillness, is what makes a serious buyer lean in and trust them. Because calm says, I am certain, and certainty closes deals that hype will never be able to close. Kill the hype. Calm is control. Calm is confident. And calm is what actually closes serious buyers.
