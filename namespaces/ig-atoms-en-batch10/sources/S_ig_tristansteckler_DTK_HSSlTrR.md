Did you know the world's greatest life insurance salesman used one analogy to sell millions in life insurance from his small Ohio office? He would just ask people who he saw on the street or in an elevator randomly. He'd say, "Hey, if you had a money-printing machine in your basement, would you insure it?" And they would always respond, "Yeah." And so then he would say, "Well, aren't you that money-printing machine for your family?" Bam!

Literally outsold all the big New York life insurance agents simply with that one analogy.

Analogies crush in sales because a lot of the times, sales is all about leadership. The people coming to you have problems that they don't know how to solve. And a lot of the times, especially in high-ticket, the person you're talking to is the problem themselves. They're not resourceful; they don't take action on their goals; they're in analysis paralysis and we can go on and on and on. Oftentimes in... sales process, you may feel like you and your prospect are not on the same page. Analogies are the perfect way to get them on your team where they recognize that you're right and they're wrong. And the only way to help them is if they're following your lead. So a simple example of an analogy that I teach sales reps is imagine the prospect comes to you and they've wanted to make $10,000 a month online for the last two years. And all they've really done over the last two years is they've done research and they've looked into different online...

Coaches and different PDFs and downloads on YouTube, maybe they watched a bunch of videos, but they haven't implemented anything and they haven't been on any calls, they haven't taken any action, and they haven't made any money. They're still working the same job. So what we would do in that scenario is we would use an analogy of an overweight person to convey the idea that what they're doing isn't enough and they need to take different actions to lose weight. So here's how it goes: Let's say I came to you two years ago and I told you I wanted to lose 50 pounds...

But over the last two years, all I've really done is look into different diets and maybe some types of workouts to do, but I haven't really implemented anything and I haven't lost any weight. I also never reached out to a personal trainer to get more accountability and more of a structured plan. Like if I told you that, would you really feel that I had done everything in my power to lose that 50 pounds or do you feel I could have done more? Prospect is always going to agree, and then that gives us leverage once they say, "You're right, I'm wrong."

To ask them a deeper question, which is what's prevented you from getting the outside help or what's prevented you from doing everything in your power to make $10,000 a month? That's typically where fear's going to come out and you can handle it from there. But anytime you're not on the same page with your prospect, use an analogy or a personal story to reframe them to get leverage in the conversation.
