Mistake number three is following a script rather than following a framework. So what do I actually mean by this? They follow a script word for word and they have no idea why they're saying what they're saying. They also have no idea if they have the correct answer from the prospect in answer to the questions that they're asking either. So they're learning words rather than learning principles. And as soon as the prospect takes them off of that script, they panic because they haven't got a pre-written line for it. A good sign of this is usually when students say, well, what do you say when a prospect says this objection? What do you say when they say that objection? It's not about what do you say, it's about what's the principle behind how you're going to respond to that. And it's the same no matter what the objection is. Hence why you should be able to freestyle with your objection handling. You shouldn't be relying on preset phrases or questions to be saying.
