Why do indie games have such incredible stories? Some of the most gut-wrenching and memorable storytelling are found in games made by a small indie team or just a single developer. Like a lot of independent writers, they wear many hats during the creative process. But why does the storytelling hit different in comparison to AAA titles? It's not trying to explicitly tell you a story. Games like The Outer Wilds or No Man's Sky don't give you explicit objectives or tell you exactly how the story will unfold. They allow you to uncover it for yourself. You feel more attached to the narrative elements because you're getting a chance to explore and discover the world as the story is evolving. You have room to ask questions about the character, and there's a mystery to why you're doing the actions the game is implying you to do. The plot resonates differently with players, and what you take from the story is a totally unique experience. The instinct for indie developers to not over-explain is something writers can learn from for their novels. Allow readers to investigate, explore, and discover the meaning in their own terms. Because the stories that stay with us the longest are the ones that let us believe we were along the journey the whole time.
