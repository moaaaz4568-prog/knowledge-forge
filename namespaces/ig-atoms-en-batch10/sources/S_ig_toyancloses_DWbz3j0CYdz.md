Most salespeople think that the close happens at the end of the call. It doesn't. The close happens before you've even told them about the product, the deliverables, the length of the program, even the price. And if you get in objections afterwards that are anything over than money, logistics, or a little bit of fear, that is a pre-commitment problem. This is not about what to say, it's about how to think. Because if you think correctly on a call, then you will naturally say the right things. Pre-commitment architecture is extremely simple. It's getting the prospect committed to the decision before they even hear the details. Most people think a sales call is let me show them how this works and they will then decide. That's not what's happening. In 99% of cases, the prospect already knows the results and that it works. They didn't come to the call to learn, they came to the call to decide. So your job is not to convince them, it's to help them decide. And what you're helping them decide is who they want to be. The how does not decide the what. So in sales, the decision to change comes first. This includes being committed. What does commitment mean? Being willing to do whatever it takes. And then the details come after, not the other way around. There are two key moments which will define if you have solidified the pre-commitment. Moment number one is the final objection suite. So first you clear any remaining resistance and you say, I feel like we've got alignment here. We can definitely get you to ABC goal. Before I run you through how this works, is there anything we haven't spoken about that you think I should know? You're giving them a chance to give you an objection. Then comes moment two, the line in the sand. So this is where we're forcing the decision. So you're simply asking them, are they ready to do whatever it takes to be that person? Then I get a strong yes. Yes, I am willing to do that. Okay, now I'm going to show you the menu because you've confirmed that you're looking to eat something now because you are hungry. If that answer is not a clear yes, then you are not ready to pitch. Now, this is where the prospect will usually try and take control back. So they'll move into logistics. You know, how does this work? What are the details? Now, this is not curiosity. This is avoidance. They're trying to move from, am I doing this? To let me analyze it. Your job is to bring them back to the decision. Once they say yes, the pitch becomes easy. You're saying, here's what you've just committed to. Here's how it's going to work. If you do it properly, the only objections you should get are money, logistics, a little bit of fear. Anything else means you didn't build enough pre-commitment. So moving forward, stop trying to close the end. Start engineering the decision before the pitch. The sales call should feel like a natural conclusion is that it closes.
