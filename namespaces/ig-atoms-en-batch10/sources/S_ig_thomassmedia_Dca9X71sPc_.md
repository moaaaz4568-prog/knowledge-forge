I spent two years thinking software was the reason I wasn't a good editor. Six editing packs, every single plugin I could find, tutorials for hours every single day, and I was genuinely doing the work. None of that mattered. The thing that actually made me a better editor has nothing to do with visual graphics at all. When I started, I thought that being good at editing means knowing every single shortcut. I bought courses, I was putting in real hours, doing cold outreach, and it just wasn't working. Clients weren't impressed, and my own stuff wasn't performing. I genuinely thought I just needed to learn more effects. And then I had this one project with a really huge client, did a paid trial video, and I got rejected. He said I wasn't at that level yet. And it changed something inside of me because the person who got picked instead of me had way less software knowledge, and his edits were way simpler than mine. But every cut he made just felt right. And that's when it actually hit me that I've been optimizing for the wrong thing this entire time. Nobody hires you because you know how to create all these dopamine-inducing visual graphics. They hire you because you know when to cut and when not to. And that's it. That's the whole skill. Pacing, timing, restraint, and a clear understanding of the client's goal, not software. The second I stopped chasing graphics and started studying pacing and what makes the video addicting, everything changed. Same footage, same software, even, just a lot better decisions. And that's the advice nobody gave me early enough.
