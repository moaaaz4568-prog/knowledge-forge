It took me way too long to learn this lesson in sales. Challenging people builds trust way faster than building rapport. When I first started taking sales calls, I thought rapport was everything. My sales manager would tell me to be friendly, be likable, get them to feel comfortable. And my calls were great conversations, but they never closed. People don't trust the person who agrees with them; they trust the person who leads them. Most closers confuse rapport with trust-building. Rapport makes people like you; leadership makes people follow you. Prospect says on a call, 'Things are going okay. I just want to make more money. I'm doing all right.' And instead of challenging it, you just nod, you smile, you move on, and that kills your authority because subconsciously the prospect is thinking, 'If this person can't challenge me on my BS, how are they going to help me to grow and change?' And to be clear, challenging is not being aggressive or condemning or demeaning somebody. It's holding the mirror up to the person and holding them to the standard of who they actually want to become. So it would sound something like this: Hold on, what does 'all right' actually mean? Can you get specific with me for a second? Help me understand that. Or... What did you mean by that exactly? In that moment, you're going to create tension in the conversation. But the tension is going to lead to clarity, which is going to lead to certainty, and that's what builds trust. And high performers, they don't respect someone who makes them feel comfortable; they respect honesty, direction, and someone who's actually willing to shove their foot in the door and slow them down if it's to help them grow. And the ironic thing is that when you really... Challenge them, they're actually going to feel more comfortable after having that conversation with you because now they feel validated, understood, and led in the right direction toward getting what they want, which is why they're on the sales call in the first place.
