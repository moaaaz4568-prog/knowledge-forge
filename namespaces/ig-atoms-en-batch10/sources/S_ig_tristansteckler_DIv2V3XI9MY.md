Do not do what I did on my first ever sales call. So this was about three and a half years ago. I get on this call, this guy hops on, he's super monotone, like down in the dumps. And I'm like, "Hey man, how's your day going so far?" And he looks at me like, "Yeah man, it's not going too well. Like I just found out that I lost my job because of COVID." And I'm like, "Do you still have 45 to 60 minutes for the call?"

What I see oftentimes with sales reps is they try and win over the prospect by building rapport and getting them to like you, but then you get to the end of the sales conversation and now we're talking about the program, the logistics, the price of the program, and all of a sudden the prospect starts to backpedal. They start to have anxiety about taking the step 'cause it's uncomfortable. And then that's where the sales rep rushes in and starts trying to handle all the objections and use all the frameworks and the work tracks to get them in the door. But you have to realize that now your prospect thinks, "Well who is this guy? I thought he was nice, I thought he was friendly, I thought he wanted to help."

And that's where the resistance comes in. So what you should do differently is you should ask questions seeking to see if you can even help the person in the first place. And that's gonna be uncomfortable 'cause you're gonna have to confront certain identities that they currently have that are incongruent with the outcome that they want. That might cause you to sweat a little bit, that might cause you to feel uncertain and nervous. But that's a part of the process. And the better you can get with leaning into discomfort in your sales process, the more leverage you're going to have to close the deal.
