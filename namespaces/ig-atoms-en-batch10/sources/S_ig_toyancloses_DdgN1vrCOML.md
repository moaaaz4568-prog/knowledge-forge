When a prospect says I need to think about it, they haven't actually told you what the objection is. They've given you a container because six different prospects can mean six different things. They don't have the money, they have it but they're scared to spend it. A partner that's not in the room, they don't trust themselves to get the result. There's still something they don't understand. They don't think it's worth the price. So I do understand why most closers ask immediately, what do you need to think about? But what they don't realize is they're trying to uncover the objection before they've diffused the resistance around it. And the danger of that is the prospect can feel like you've ignored their request for space and gone straight into objection handling mode, which unfortunately means they become less guarded, they give you even less information, and you end up trying to handle an objection that you haven't even diagnosed yet. So instead you want to diffuse first, make them think it's completely fine to go away and think about it. Then separate the solution from whatever is stopping the decision. Confirm that putting everything aside, they genuinely believe that your solution can get them to their desired outcome. Then get them to explain why. So what then happens is they've just reconfirmed the value in their own words. The resistance comes down and you can now diagnose what I need to think about it actually means. Because once they tell you the real reason, you now finally have an objection that is worth handling. So would you rather hear I need to think about it, guess what the problem is and start throwing random reframes at them that you've heard on Instagram, or diffuse, get them to reconfirm the solution and then find out what the real objection is?
