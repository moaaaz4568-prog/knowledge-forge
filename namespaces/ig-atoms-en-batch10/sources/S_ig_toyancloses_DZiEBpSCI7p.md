Pain-based selling does work on a specific type of buyer, but the serious money doesn't get moved by pain. It gets moved by the idea of being left behind. The buyers that I work with in the property investment space, they weren't struggling and they definitely weren't broke. They definitely didn't have a pain point sharp enough to push them into a decision either. What they had was an awareness that the decisions that they'd been making had not been the right ones to get them into the rooms that they wanted to be in. They were watching from the outside, they were watching their friends' portfolios grow. So they didn't have pain, they had status anxiety. Because what is the definition of status? A person's position in society, the esteem in which people hold that person. And that whole concept moves people differently to pain. Pain creates urgency that feels desperate. Status awareness creates urgency that feels purposeful. The buyer's not running from something, they're running towards something. They're running towards the version of themselves that they already believe that they should be. And that distinction changes everything about how you actually sell to them. You don't make them feel worse about where they are, you just simply make the gap between where they are and where they should be so big that it's impossible to ignore. That's a different conversation entirely. And I'll be honest, it's one that most online sales coaches have never even had heard of or been trained on. If you take anything away from this video, take this. More serious buyers are not in pain, they are simply aware. Awareness of being left behind will always move more money than desperation ever will.
