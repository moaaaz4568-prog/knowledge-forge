# T_Db3MDqbMS2Y_01

"The emotion in your edit was never coming from the footage. One shot, a guy's face, totally blank. Cut after the soup, people said he looked hungry. Cut after the coffin, people said he looked heartbroken. Cut after the woman lying on the couch, people said he looked in love. Same face, every time, nothing changed. The emotion wasn't on his face, it was in the edit. Your brain just built it." — sources/S_ig_thomassmedia_Db3MDqbMS2Y.md

# T_Db3MDqbMS2Y_02

"And here's why that actually matters for your edits. Your client is not an actor. Half the footage you get is flat, stiff, and with barely an emotion on the camera. Normally that's a dead end. You cannot reshoot the footage, but you don't need to. Cut a neutral shot of them right next to the right context, and the viewer's brain fills in the emotion for you." — sources/S_ig_thomassmedia_Db3MDqbMS2Y.md

# T_Db3MDqbMS2Y_03

"For example, if your client gave you a footage of him interviewing his case study, and in the hook he talks about his struggles. Working around 12 to 14 hours a day. And what he had to go through with no emotion. But you can fix that by using B-roll that indicate the struggle. For example, no money in the bank, or his social media with very little followers." — sources/S_ig_thomassmedia_Db3MDqbMS2Y.md
