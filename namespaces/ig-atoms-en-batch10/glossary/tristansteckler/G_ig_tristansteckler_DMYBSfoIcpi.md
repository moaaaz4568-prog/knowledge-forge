# G_ig_tristansteckler_DMYBSfoIcpi — smokescreen

## Term

A smokescreen is what Tristan calls the I'll think about it objection: the customer is not thinking at all, and the line is cover for the real objection underneath. The word appears once, beside money, value, and guard, and it names the video's central move. He forbids the usual reply of sure, let me know, because that gives the prospect permission to disappear forever, and instead tells the rep to stay calm and ask what specifically needs thinking about. Nine times out of ten the answer is money, and the test is whether setting money aside would get a yes today.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "And when you drop the tone, you drop the guard." — sources/S_ig_tristansteckler_DMYBSfoIcpi.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that every prospect who asks for time is lying: Tristan says the line is a cover for an uncovered objection, not a verdict on the person's honesty.
- Read as permission to push hard once the smokescreen is named: his move is to drop the tone and lower the guard, not to raise the pressure.
