# G_ig_tristansteckler_DNn237bIgtf — trust yourself

## Term

To trust yourself is the question Tristan puts to a couple in debt: whose choices put them in the position, and what has kept them from getting resourceful enough to get out. The phrase appears once, beside debt, wife, and fear, and it is the video's whole turn. The wife can be the reason they do it or the reason they do not, but she cannot be both, and the block underneath is the fear of failing. The close is the cost of holding on: if they stay the guy who does not take the risk at five thousand a month, how will that feel, and what must die off inside for him to step into the fifteen-thousand-a-month man.

## Synonyms

- trusting yourself (the form the source uses when the fear is named)

## Live Example

> "She can either be the reason why we do it or the reason we don't do this, but she can't be both." — sources/S_ig_tristansteckler_DNn237bIgtf.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as advice to ignore the wife's stress: Tristan names her stress about money at home, and the question is who is responsible for getting out, not whose feelings count.
- Read as a claim that debt is only a mindset problem: the couple's debt is real, and the trust question is about the resourcefulness to act despite it.
