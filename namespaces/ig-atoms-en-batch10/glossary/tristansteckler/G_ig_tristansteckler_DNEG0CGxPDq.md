# G_ig_tristansteckler_DNEG0CGxPDq — fold like a lawn chair

## Term

To fold like a lawn chair is what a prospect is testing for when he demands the price and gets angry at a refusal: he is looking for weakness and insecurity, to see if the rep folds. The phrase appears once, beside price, trust, and weakness, and it is the video's whole test. Tristan's rule is to sit in the discomfort and trust the process, because the rep who cannot do that should not be in sales. The answer is to hold the frame instead: the solution is custom to the person, and he is not yet confident it can help, so the prospect must show the problem first.

> "If you don't have the internal self-control to be able to sit in that moment and embrace the discomfort and continue to trust your process, you should not be in sales." — sources/S_ig_tristansteckler_DNEG0CGxPDq.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as advice to withhold the price forever: Tristan withholds it only until he knows the person and the problem, then walks through what the work does and how it works.
- Read as a claim that angry prospects are always testing you: the video says the anger has everything to do with them and nothing to do with you, and the test is whether you fold.
