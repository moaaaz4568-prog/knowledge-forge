# G_ig_thetravisstephenson_CME-T4wDXm6 — lead

## Term

A lead here is a person who opts in from a Google click, and Travis prices one by simple math: a click costs about a hundred dollars, and at 20% conversion two leads cost five hundred dollars each. The word appears four times — twice as lead and twice as leads — beside hundred dollars, Google click, conversion, and 20%. He walks through a plastic surgeon's numbers — ten clicks for a thousand dollars, two leads from them — to show why a lead is worth so much to her, and why the eight people who did not opt in are the audience she cannot reach without ads.

## Synonyms

- leads

## Live Example

> "She would have two leads and that would average out to five hundred dollars a lead." — sources/S_ig_thetravisstephenson_CME-T4wDXm6.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a fixed price for a lead: the five hundred dollars is the arithmetic of this one example — a hundred-dollar click at 20% conversion — not a rate Travis charges.
- Read as proof the surgeon profits: Travis shows what she spends per lead, not what she earns from the two leads.
