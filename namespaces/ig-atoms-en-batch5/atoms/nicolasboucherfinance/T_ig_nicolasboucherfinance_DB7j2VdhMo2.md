# T_DB7j2VdhMo2_01

"FIFO versus LIFO, by Nicolas Bouché. FIFO stands for first in, first out. The oldest inventory items are sold first. LIFO stands for last in, first out. Under LIFO, the newest inventory items are sold first. The choice between FIFO or LIFO also affects the accuracy of financial statements." — sources/S_ig_nicolasboucherfinance_DB7j2VdhMo2.md

# T_DB7j2VdhMo2_02

"In FIFO, inventory sold consists of the oldest units, while the inventory remaining consists of the most recent purchases. On the other hand, cost of goods sold in LIFO consists of the most recent inventory costs, while the ending inventory is composed of the earliest costs. FIFO can be used for perishable goods." — sources/S_ig_nicolasboucherfinance_DB7j2VdhMo2.md

# T_DB7j2VdhMo2_03

"By selling the oldest units first, FIFO helps to minimize the risk of obsolescence or spoilage. FIFO is also best for inflationary environments. Since cost of goods sold is based on older, lower-priced inventory, the remaining inventory is valued at more recent, higher prices. LIFO is suitable for non-perishable goods. These goods do not have a limited shelf life or expiration date." — sources/S_ig_nicolasboucherfinance_DB7j2VdhMo2.md

# T_DB7j2VdhMo2_04
