1. "How many sales consultants left this store last year?" (كم مستشار مبيعات غادر هذا المتجر العام الماضي؟)

NADA's number is the industry. Yours is the store. A desk that knows its own turnover and says it out loud is running a seat. A desk that shrugs is running a door. Ask for the count, not the vibe. The store already reports it.

Every store knows its number. Ask for it.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "What is the pack on a car before my split is calculated?"

The pack is the amount the store takes off the top before your percentage touches the deal. It is legal, common, and rarely explained on your first day. Ask for the figure and when it last moved. A pack that grows every year is a quiet pay cut nobody announced.

You cannot protect a split you cannot see.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "How many times has the pay plan changed in the last 24 months?"

Once is a business decision. Three times is a strategy. Every rewrite lands after the reps have figured out how to win on the old one. Ask for the dates. If the plan changes more often than the model year, the store is managing you, not paying you.

A plan that keeps moving was never built for you to win.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
4. "Who decides which ups and internet leads I get?"
On most floors the desk assigns the walk ins and the internet leads, and the desk has favourites. Your close rate cannot fix a lead flow that is decided before you stand up. Ask who allocates, on what rule, and whether it is written anywhere.
Your seat is only as good as the traffic sent to it.

an unwind, a refinance, or a
chargeback?"

customer came back. Which of those
come out of your check, and how long
after the sale can they? Get it
in writing. The store already has the answer in a
document you have never been
shown.

A deal is not yours until you know what
can take it back.

6. "Do I participate in F&I, and at what rate?"

The F&I office is the part of the store still growing. If you hand the customer to the box and your part ends there, you sell the half of the deal that is shrinking and someone else gets paid on the half that is not. Ask for the participation rule. Ask if it exists.

The growing half of the deal should have your name on it.

7. "Who sat in this seat before me, and where did they go?"

The desk knows. If the last three people in your seat went to other stores, the seat is the problem and the store already knows it. If they went to F&I, the desk, or a better plan across the street, that is the map. Ask for names.

The seat has a history. Read it before you give it another year.
