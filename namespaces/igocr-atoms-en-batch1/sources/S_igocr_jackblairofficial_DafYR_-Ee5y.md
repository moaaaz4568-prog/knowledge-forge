1. "How do you want this to feel six months from now?"

Gerald Zaltman ran buyers through neuroimaging at Harvard and found the decision forms in the emotional system first. Logic arrives later to explain it.

So ask for the feeling before the specs. Whatever they answer is the real buying criteria. Everything else on the call is decoration.

The feeling decides. Logic signs the paperwork.

2. "Walk me through a normal Tuesday with this problem."

Zaltman found people don't think in bullet points. They think in pictures. His whole method was built on pulling those images out.

Get them describing the problem as a scene. The commute. The spreadsheet. The call they dread. Now you're selling into a picture they built themselves.

Buyers think in pictures. Get theirs first.

3. "Most people in your seat feel a little doubt right about now."

Zaltman's core finding: buyers can't tell you their real reasons. The 95% is hidden even from them. It runs the call anyway.

Name the feeling out loud and let it surfaces where you can work with it. Leave it buried and it votes no in silence.

Surface the doubt or it decides in silence.

4. "What happened with the last one of these you bought?"

Zaltman showed memory quietly scripts the current decision. The old vendor. The tool that flopped. It's all sitting in the room with you.

Ask early. Find the old wound or the old win. You're negotiating with their history whether you know it or not.

Every buyer brings a ghost. Find it early.

5. "What's the version of this you've already pictured?"

By the time they booked the call, Zaltman's research says the movie has already run in their head. Most reps talk over it with their own.

Pull their version out first. Then sell that one back to them. People rarely reject a future they authored.

Sell their movie. Never screen yours over it.

6. "Here's what that your mornings look like once this is handled."

Zaltman found abstract benefits barely register. Lived, sensory detail is what the deciding part of the brain actually processes.

So drop the feature list. Give them the 7am with no fire to put out. The inbox that stopped growing. The wing. Make the after feel like a memory.

Paint the morning, not the feature list.

7. "And here's the part you can take to your boss."

Zaltman's law in one line: emotion decides, reason justifies. The gut says yes, then goes looking for evidence to defend it.

Once you feel the yes land, arm the yes land, arm it. Hand them the three clean numbers they'll repeat upstairs. You're not convincing them. You're equipping them.

They buy on feeling. Arm them with reasons.
