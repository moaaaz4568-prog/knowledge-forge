Vagueness sends people looking for a second opinion before they act. Mileti's research found the counterintuitive fix. Longer, more specific messages made people search less, not more.
So split it out loud. Name what you would stake your name on, then name what you honestly don't know yet. Precision keeps everything else believable.
Say the doubt before they go find it.

"HERE'S THE EXACT NEXT STEP, IN ORDER."
Guidance is the element most warnings leave out,
and the model treats it as mandatory. Without a
specific action, people default to gathering more
information instead of moving.
End on the sequence. What happens first, who does
it, what it needs from them. Not "I'll follow up." A
named step, in order.
People stall in the gap where instructions should be.
