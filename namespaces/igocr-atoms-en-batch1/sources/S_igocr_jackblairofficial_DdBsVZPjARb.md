1. "IT LOOKS LIKE PRIORITIES SHIFTED OVER THERE. DID THEY?"
Reps chase ghosts with warmer and warmer check-ins, and every rests with warmer and warmer check-ins, and every rep knows the pattern by heart. The dealmaker posture does the opposite. It names the silence on message one.
No recap, no pitch, no apology for writing. One observation, one question, send. You are not reminding them you exist. You are asking what changed.
Silence is an answer waiting for permission.

4. "THIS COMES OFF MY BOARD FRIDAY UNLESS YOU STOP ME."
Deadlines were his native language: public, specific, final. A message with a date in it is a decision request. A message without one is decoration.
Pick a real date and honor it. The point is not pressure. The point is that ignoring you now has a price and a clock.
Deadlines are respect. Open-ended is invisible.

6. "SILENCE USUALLY MEANS NO OR NOT YET. WHICH ONE IS THIS?"

The binary is the mechanism. Every message in the sequence collapses to two doors, and either door beats the fog.

Never ask ghosts open questions. Give them a two-word reply that resolves the thread. Not yet comes with a date. No comes with your time back.

Named silence breaks. Unnamed silence calcifies.

7. "I'M GOING TO TAKE SILENCE AS A NO AND WISH YOU WELL."
The walk-away was his signature move, and it only works if you mean it. Sent without spite, and it is the single highest reply-rate message in the sequence.
Send it once, then actually walk. A surprising number of dead deals answer this one inside an hour, because losing access is the first cost they believe.
Walking away first is the last form of leverage.
