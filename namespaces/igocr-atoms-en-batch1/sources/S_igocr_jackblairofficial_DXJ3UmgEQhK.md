1. "I know you were not expecting much from this call. Let me change that in the next five minutes."

Some buyers pick up the phone already checked out.
They booked the call out of obligation and they are already planning their exit.

This phrase names the dynamic and flips it before they can act on it.
It signals self-awareness, confidence, and a timeline they can commit to.
Now they are watching to see if you deliver instead of looking for a reason to leave.

The rep who acknowledges the low bar and clears it owns every call like this.

2. "I may not be the most senior person you have spoken to this week. I am probably the most prepared."

Peggy Olson never pretended to be something she was not.

It replaces the credential they are looking for with one that actually closes deals.
Preparation beats pedigree on every call that matters.

You do not need their respect handed to you. You earn it in the first sentence.

4. "I have heard that objection before. Here is what I found when I looked into it properly."

When a buyer dismisses you with a surface-level objection they expect you to fold. Most reps do. They apologise, they soften, they start over from a weaker position.

This phrase signals that you have seen this play before and you came prepared for it. It does not argue. It does not push back. It simply shows the buyer that you are several moves ahead.

Preparation is the only authority nobody can take from you.

5. "I am going to say something that might surprise you given how this call started."

This phrase is a pattern interrupt built for the room that underestimated you. It creates anticipation in a buyer who had already written you off.

What follows as it lands twice as hard because you lowered their guard first. Peggy Olson never made her biggest move when the room expected it. She waited, she built, and then she said the thing nobody else in the room could say.

Timing is a skill. Surprise is a close.

6. "I am not asking you to trust me yet. I am asking you to give me one more minute."

This phrase is built for the moment the buyer starts to disengage.
You feel it on a call. The answers get shorter. The energy drops.

Most reps push harder when they feel it. That push finishes the deal for good.
This phrase pulls instead. It asks for something small and signals complete confidence.
One more minute from a buyer who was leaving is worth more than an hour from one who was never there.

Ask for less. Get more. Every time.

Confidence in your timing is the close most reps never make.
