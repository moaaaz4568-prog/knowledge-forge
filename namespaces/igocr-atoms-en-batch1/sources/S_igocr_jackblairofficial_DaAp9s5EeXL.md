1. "Walk me through how you're handling this right now."

Rackham found the best closers ask before they pitch. He called this is the situation question, and weak reps skip it to start selling.

You cannot solve a problem you do not understand yet. Get the lay of the land first, in their words, and you earn the right to ask the questions that actually move them.

Map the ground before you plant the flag.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blain

2. "Where does that break down for you?"

This is the problem question. Rackham's research showed top performers spend the early call hunting for the crack, not presenting the fix.

A buyer who has not named a problem out loud has no reason to buy. The moment they say where it hurts, you stop being a salesperson and start being the person who might fix it.

No admitted problem, no sale.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "And when that happens, what does it cost you down the line?"

This is the implication question, the heart of what Rackham found. Average reps stop at the problem. Closers make the problem grow.

You walk them from a small annoyance to the real, stacking cost of leaving it alone. The pain they into is the pain they will pay to end.

Don't fix a small problem. Reveal the big one under it.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Who else feels it when this goes wrong?"

Rackham found one problem rarely closes a deal. Spread across a team, a number, a reputation, it becomes urgent.

This question widens the damage past the one person in front of you. Now it is not a personal gripe, it is a cost the whole operation is carrying, and those get fixed fast.

Make the problem bigger than the person you're talking to.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "If this were handled, what would that actually be worth to you?"

This is the need-payoff question. Instead of you listing am proved it is far stronger to make them say the value out loud.

When the buyer describes the upside in their own words, they are no longer being sold. They are selling themselves, and nobody argues with their own conclusion.

Let them say the value. They'll believe it then.

6. "Just so I'm clear, what are you hoping this changes?"
Rackham found weak reps assume they know what the buyer wants. Closers confirm it, out loud, before they ever present.
This locks the target before you pitch, so your offer lands as the exact answer to the thing they just named. You are no longer guessing. You are reflecting their own words back as the solution.
Confirm the target before you take the shot.

7. "Based on what you've told me, where do you think we go from here?"

Rackham's data killed the hard close. The strongest reps handed the next step back to the buyer instead of forcing it.

When they say the path forward is their decision, not your push, and people do not back out of their own ideas. The close stops being a fight and becomes a handoff.

Stop closing them. Let them close themselves.
