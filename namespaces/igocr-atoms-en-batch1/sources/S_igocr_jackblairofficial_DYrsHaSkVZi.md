1. "Wealth is ideas on the job."

Prevette's central teaching.

He believed money follows a useful idea that has actually been put into action. Not luck, not your background. An idea applied. The idea sitting in your head, unused, pays nothing.

An idea you act on creates the result. One you just have doesn't.

2. "Take stock of yourself first."

Prevette on the inventory before the work.

What are you genuinely good at? What do you already know? Prevette had reps inventory their own abilities before anything else, because most people badly undervalue what they already bring.

Know your own strengths and you are selling from solid ground.

3. "Find out what people want, then show them how to get it."

Prevette's plain definition of selling.

You do not push the product at them. You find the buyer's real want, then position your offer as the path that gets them there. Do that and the buyer is already leaning in.

Connect the offer to a want they already have and they lean in.

4. “Once a decision is made, the law of averages goes to work.”

Prevette on the power of a decision.

Nothing actually happens until a decision is made. A clear decision sets the whole process in motion. Effort with no decision behind it just drifts and produces nothing.

Get a real decision and everything else starts moving.

6. "Enthusiasm is the most contagious thing you can carry."

Prevette on energy.

Genuine enthusiasm jumps straight to the buyer. A rep who is truly excited about the value they bring transfers that feeling across the call. A flat rep transfers the flatness just as fast.

Real enthusiasm is contagious. So is boredom. Pick one.

7. “Repeat the right action until it becomes habit.”
Prevette on the law of repetition.
A skill done once is fragile and falls apart under pressure. The same skill repeated until it is automatic holds when it counts. Prevette built reliability through pure repetition.
Repeat the right move enough and pressure can’t shake it loose.
