Prompt 1 | The Mirror

#ROLE:
Act as Chris Voss applying the Mirror technique to my next sales call, since mirroring is the cheapest concession you can give and the fastest way to get the prospect talking.

#TASK:
Identify the most loaded words my prospect is using and build the mirror responses that get them to keep talking without me ever pitching.

#STEPS:
1. Paste in the transcript or notes from my last 3 sales calls
2. Identify the 3 most loaded words or phrases the prospect repeated (price, timing, team, budget, risk)
3. For each loaded word, build a one-sentence mirror that repeats the last 1-3 words back as a question
4. Design the silence pattern that follows each mirror (3-4 seconds, no filler)
5. Plan the follow-up question if the prospect doesn't break the silence on their own

#RULES:
- A mirror is the last 1-3 words of what they said, said back as a question
- Tone goes up at the end. Slow. No filler words
- After the mirror, you say nothing. The silence is the work
- Never mirror more than 3 words. The shorter the mirror, the harder it pulls

#OUTPUT:
Loaded Word → Mirror Response → Silence Length → Follow-up Question → What to Avoid Saying

Prompt 2 | The Label

#ROLE:
Act as Chris Voss applying tactical labels to the emotions my prospect is feeling but not saying, since the unnamed emotion is the one that kills the deal.

#TASK:
Identify the 5 emotions my prospect is most likely sitting on during a call and write the Voss-style labels that bring each one into the open.

#STEPS:
1. Describe my offer, price point, and the typical buyer profile
2. List the 5 emotions a typical prospect is likely feeling but won’t name (skepticism, fear of regret, embarrassment, distrust, overwhelm)
3. For each emotion, write a label starting with "It seems like..." or "It sounds like..."
4. Build the silence that follows each label (5-6 seconds minimum)
5. Plan the recovery line if I label wrong and the prospect pushes back

#RULES:
- Labels start with "It seems like" or "It sounds like" or "It looks like"
- Never start a label with "I"
- No question marks. A label is a statement, said calmly, with a downward tone
- The label names the negative emotion. Naming it disarms it
- If you label wrong, they correct you, which is also information

#OUTPUT:
Hidden Emotion → Voss-Style Label → Silence Length → Recovery Line → Why This Works

Prompt 3 | The Calibrated Question

#ROLE:
Act as Chris Voss building the calibrated questions that flip the pressure back to the prospect without me ever saying no.

#TASK:
Generate the 10 calibrated questions I can use on a sales call when the prospect pushes back on price, timing, or commitment.

#STEPS:
1. Identify the 3 most common objections I hear on calls (price, timing, "I need to think about it.")
2. For each objection, build 3 calibrated questions that start with "How" or "What"
3. Make sure no question can be answered yes or no
4. For each question, identify what answer would tell me to keep going vs walk away
5. Plan how to use silence between the question and their answer

#RULES:
- Every calibrated question starts with "How" or "What"
- Never use "Why" (it sounds accusatory and triggers defense)
- No yes/no questions. The prospect must explain their position out loud
- The question puts the problem-solving on them, not you
- "How am I supposed to do that?!" is the highest-level written

#OUTPUT:
Objection → 3 Calibrated Questions → Answers That Mean "Keep Going" → Answers That Mean "Walk" → Silence Strategy

Prompt 4 | That's The Right Moment

#ROLE:
Act as Chris Voss building the summary that gets the prospect to say 'that's right' instead of 'you're right,' since 'you're right' is the polite no.

#TASK:
Write the 60-second summary I deliver at the peak of the call that restates the prospect's situation in their language and earns full agreement.

#STEPS:
1. Tell me the prospect's stated problem, their goal, and the obstacle they've named
2. Write a 60-second summary that restates all three in their words, not mine
3. Build in 2 labels and 1 mirror within the summary
4. End with a statement (not a question) that compresses everything to one truth
5. Plan what to do in the silence after they say "that's right"

#RULES:
- Use their words. Not your translation
- "That's right" means full understanding has been reached
- "You're right" means they want you to stop talking, and the deal is dying
- The summary must include both their pain and their goal, side by side
- After "that's right," you go silent. Then you close

#OUTPUT:
Prospect's Pain → Prospect's Goal → 60-Second Summary → 2 Labels → 1 Mirror Inside → Closing Line After "That's Right"

Prompt 5 | The Accusation Audit
(Run This Last)

#ROLE:
Act as Chris Voss running an accusation audit, since the objection you
name first is the objection that loses its weapon.

#TASK:
Generate the 5 worst things the prospect is silently thinking about my
offer right now, and write the lines I say out loud before they do.

#STEPS:
1. Describe my offer, my price, and the buyer's likely state of mind walking
into the call
2. Generate the 5 harshest unspoken objections (price, trust, timing, fit,
fear of failure)
3. For each, write a sentence I can say out loud, framed as "You might be
thinking..." or "It probably feels like..."
4. Order them from least painful to most painful (save the worst for last)
5. Plan the pause after each one and what to listen for

#RULES:
- The accusation audit happens early in the call, not for
- Name the objection so directly the prospect feels safe, not attacked
- Use their language, not yours
- The worst objection you said by you carries 1/10 the weight of the same
objection from them
- After the audit, the prospect's defenses drop because you've taken their
ammo

#OUTPUT:
5 Silent Objections → Voss-Style Audit Lines → Order (Light to Heavy) →
Pause Length → What to Listen For
