1. "I AM GOING TO STOP TALKING NOW. TELL ME WHAT YOU WOULD ACTUALLY DO WITH THIS."
Lewin's lecture groups got the full case for organ meats, delivered well. Three percent of them served it. The discussion groups heard the same facts and then talked. Thirty-two percent served it.
Make the pitch short and then hand the room the problem. A buyer who was told a conclusion owns it. A buyer who argued the conclusion rents it. A buyer who was told a conclusion rents it.
People defend the decisions they made. They forget the ones they were given.

2. "WHAT WOULD GET IN THE WAY OF DOING THIS AT YOUR PLACE?"
The discussion groups spent most of their time on obstacles. The smell, the family’s reaction, how to cook the thing. Lewin let the objections out in the open where the group could answer them.
Ask for the obstacles before the buyer volunteers them as a no. Named in a group, an objection becomes a problem to solve. Held privately, it becomes the reason.
An objection said out loud is half solved. An objection kept quiet is a decision already made.

3. "WHO HERE IS GOING TO TRY IT THIS WEEK?
HANDS UP."
The discussion ended with one question and a show
of hands. Lewin found that raising a hand in front of
the neighbours did more than any fact in the lecture.
End the meeting with a visible commitment, not a
summary. Ask who is doing what by when, and ask
it where the others can see the answer.
A hand raised in front of the room is worth more than
a yes said to you alone.

4. "LET US DECIDE THIS TOGETHER, NOT ONE AT A TIME."
Lewin's unit of change was the group, not the individual. It is easier to move a whole room a little than to move one person against the room.
Stop working the buying committee one contact at a time. Get them on the same call and let the decision belong to the group, because nobody wants to be the only one who said no to the room.
One person can resist you. A room resists itself.

5. "BEFORE WE TALK ABOUT THE NEW THING,
HOW IS THE OLD WAY WORKING FOR YOU?"
Lewin called the first step unfreezing. Nothing
moves until the old habit has been examined by the
people who hold it, so his discussions opened on
what they were doing now.
Open every call on the current way, in the buyer's
own words, before you mention yours. Change is
impossible while the present still feels finished.
Nobody replaces what they have not yet questioned.

6. "I AM NOT HERE TO TELL YOU. I AM HERE TO RUN THE CONVERSATION."
The discussion leaders in Lewin's study were not lecturers. They asked, they listened, and they kept the room on the question until the room answered it.
Swap the presenter role for the chair. Ask the question, hold the silence, and let the buyers do the persuading of each other. A room trusts its own voices more than it trusts yours.
The rep who runs the conversation wins it. The rep who dominates it loses it.

08 / 10
7. "CAN I CHECK BACK IN A WEEK AND HEAR
HOW IT WENT?"
Lewin's numbers came from a follow-up, not from
the meeting. He came back and asked who had
actually served the meat. Without the check, the
show of hands would have been theatre.
Book the follow-up inside the meeting, with a date,
and frame it as hearing the result rather than
chasing the order. The follow-up is what turns a
raised hand into a habit.
A commitment with a date attached is a plan.
Without one it is a mood.
