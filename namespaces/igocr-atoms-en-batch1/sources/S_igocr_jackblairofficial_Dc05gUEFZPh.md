1. "STOP ME THE SECOND I SAY A WORD YOU'D NEVER USE."
In 1990 a Stanford dissertation had tappers tap famous songs on a table. They predicted half would be recognized. Listeners caught three out of 120. The song only existed inside the tapper's head.
Your product runs in your head the same way.
Permission to flag jargon turns your blind spot into the buyer's job description.
Expertise is inaudible from the other chair.

2. "WANT THE WHOLE THING IN ONE SENTENCE
BEFORE WE GO DEEP?"

while they tapped. The curse of knowledge: once
you know a thing, you cannot imagine not knowing
it.

An analogy is a melody. Facts are taps.
