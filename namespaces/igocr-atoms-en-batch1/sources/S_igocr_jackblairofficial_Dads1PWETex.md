1. Kill the menu.

You present three packages and watch them say they'll think about it.

At the big jam table, shoppers flooded in, tasted, and walked away. 3% bought. Big menus feel generous to you. To the buyer they feel like homework.

Say: "I could show you six ways to do this. Based on what you told me, you only need to see one."

2. Prescribe. Don't display.

You lay out every option because you're scared of choosing wrong for them.

The small table converted 10x because someone had already done the eliminating. Curation is the value. A buyer drowning in options isn't served, he's abandoned.

Say: "If I were in your seat, with your numbers, here's exactly what I'd do."

3. Shrink the decision to one door.

The buyer stalls, overwhelmed by the size of the whole thing.

Overload doesn't kill desire. The shoppers wanted jam. It kills action. Cut the decision down until saying yes is smaller than saying maybe.

Say: "Forget the whole program for a second. The only decision today is whether we fix [X]. Yes or no on that one thing."

4. The two-door close.

Zero options feels like a trap. Twenty-four feels like a chore.

Two preserves their agency without triggering the paralysis. It's the smallest number that still feels like a choice.

Say: "There are two ways people do this. Fast and full, or slow and phased. Which one sounds like you?"

don't lose the deal. That's how you lose the
deal.

table. It's not built for your situation."

6. Name the paralysis.

"Send me everything and I'll review it with the team."

That's not a buying signal. That's a shopper at the 24-jam table asking for more jam. More information makes the decision heavier, and heavier decisions get postponed.

Say: "More information won't make this easier. Tell me the one question that actually decides this for you."

7. One next step. Ever.

You end the call with a recap and four action items. All four die.

Choice overload applies to next steps too. The close isn't a list. It's a single door with a date on it.

Say: "Ignore everything else we covered. Your only next step is [X] by Friday. That's the whole assignment."
