1. "I'm calling to ask a favor. Do you have a quick minute?"

Wickman opened cold outreach by asking permission instead of launching in.

A small yes at the start lowers the wall. The other person feels respected, not ambushed, and they actually listen to the next sentence instead of looking for the exit.

Get a tiny yes before you ask for a big one.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "If I could, would you?"

Wickman's signature line. If I could get you the price you want, would you list today?

It isolates the real condition before you do the work. You stop guessing at objections and find out exactly what it takes to get a yes.

Tie your effort to their commitment before you lift a finger.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "Let me make sure I understand what you're really after."

Wickman trained reps to play back the client's goal in their own words.

People buy from the person who clearly gets them. Reflecting their goal proves you listened and quietly makes you the only logical choice to deliver it.

Repeat their goal back and you become the obvious one to solve it.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "What's most important to you about that?"

Wickman pushed past the surface answer to the reason under it.

The first thing a client says is rarely the real driver. One more question gets to what actually moves them, and that is the thing you sell to.

Ask why once more and you find the real reason they'll buy.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Just so I'm clear, what's holding you back?"

Wickman taught reps to invite the objection out into the open instead of dodging it.

An objection you can hear is one you can handle. The ones that kill deals are the silent ones, so he made clients say them out loud where they could be solved.

Pull the objection into the light and it stops running the deal.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Would it help if I took that off your plate?"

Wickman closed by removing friction instead of applying pressure.

People stall when the next step feels heavy. Offering to carry the hard part makes saying yes feel like relief instead of risk.

Make the next step lighter and the yes comes easier.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair

7. "Let's get the paperwork started while we're together."

Wickman moved to action the moment the client was aligned, never after they left the room.

Agreement fades the second people walk away and start second-guessing. Locking it in while the energy is high is what turns a good conversation into a signed deal.

Close while the room is warm, not after it cools.
