1. "What number were you thinking?"
In July 2022 the Nationals offered 15 years and $440 million. Soto and Boras didn't counter against themselves with a lower ask. They said no and let the other side carry the next number. When a buyer says "what's the best you can do", don't answer with a number. Ask theirs. The first side to move against itself pays for the whole negotiation.
Silence after a number is not rude. It's the price.

3. "Forget the price for a second. What would make this an easy yes?"

The Mets didn’t win on the headline alone. The deal carries a $75 million signing bonus, no deferred money, a full no-trade clause and an opt-out after five years. Boras sold the shape, not just the size. Ask for the shape before you fight about the number: payment timing, start date, who’s on the call, what happens if it doesn’t work. Half the objections to price are objections to shape.

Buyers argue price when nobody asked about shape.

4. "If I do that, what comes off your side?"

Even the opt-out has a price. The Mets can cancel it by adding $4 million a year to the last ten seasons. Every concession in that deal bought something back. Never give a discount for free. Trade it for the start date, the case study, the referral, the longer term. A concession that buys nothing teaches the buyer to ask again.

A free concession is a lesson the buyer never forgets.

5. "That's a fair ask. It's not one I can take."

The $440 million no cost Soto two trades, Washington to San Diego to New York, and two seasons of waiting. A walk-away only counts when you actually walk. Say it once, calmly, without a reason. You don't owe an explanation for holding your price. The buyer who hears calm hears that the number is real.

Calm is what a real number sounds like.

6. "Take the weekend. I'd rather a yes on Monday than a maybe today."

Two years passed between the no and the record. Boras let time work for him instead of against him. The rep who needs the deal today gives it away to the one off the call on purpose. Set the date, name what changes by then, and let the buyer feel that you're not the one in a hurry.

Whoever needs it today pays for it today.
