1. "Let's put the real issue on the table."
His signature move, from Sicilian fishermen who gut the day's catch on the table, together and mess it all. Leave the fish under the table and it rots. The objection nobody names is the one that kills the deal three weeks later as a ghosting.
Name the mess early. Buried conflict rots into a lost deal.

2. "I'm on your side of the table here."

Kohlrieser's first law: you cannot influence someone you haven't bonded with, and you can bond with anyone, even someone holding a gun on you. He survived four hostage situations on that skill. The rep who treats the buyer as an opponent has already lost.

Bond first. No bond, no influence. Ever.

4. "Whatever comes up, we'll figure it out."

He calls it being a secure base. People only take risks, and buying is a risk, when someone in the room feels like solid ground. Your calm is contagious. So is your panic. The steadiest person in the room quietly runs it.

Be the solid ground. Calm transfers, and so does fear.

5. "Let's focus on where you want to end up."
His concept of the mind's eye: people move toward whatever their mind is locked on. A buyer staring at everything that could go wrong will find a reason to freeze. Redirect the picture to the outcome and the same person finds reasons to move.
Point their mind at the goal. Focus decides direction.

6. "You might be right. Help me understand."
The negotiator's rule under fire: never meet aggression with aggression. Pushing back on a heated buyer feeds the fight and loses the deal even when you win the argument. Softening first costs you nothing and hands you back control.
Never match their heat. Soft answer, strong position.

7. "Tell me where I've got this wrong."
Dialogue was the entire method that kept him alive. A monologue makes the other person a spectator, and spectators disengage. Inviting the corrections turns them into a participant, and participants talk themselves into the deal.
Invite the pushback. Participants buy. Spectators leave.
