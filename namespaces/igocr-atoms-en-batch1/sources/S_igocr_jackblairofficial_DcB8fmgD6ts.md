1. “CAN I HAVE NINETY SECONDS, AND THEN YOU TELL ME TO STOP?”
Selling takes time from people. Pitching asks for it. The difference is that one of them asks for it. It’s that one of them hands the other person a stop button, and almost nobody ever presses it.
Use it the second before you start. You are not being humble, you are removing the reason to brace.
People give more time to whoever offers to take less.

2. “YOU KNOW HOW MOST [PEOPLE LIKE YOU] END UP [THE BAD THING]? I FIX THAT.”
This is the whole clarity formula. Their problem first, your job second, in one breath. Most people run it backwards and open with their title.
Say it out loud before the room. If you cannot fit it in one sentence, you do not have it yet.
Nobody buys what you are. They buy what you end.

3. "LET ME TELL YOU ABOUT SOMEONE WHO WAS STANDING EXACTLY WHERE YOU ARE."
Her whole career started with a pen and a story. She didn’t talk about the ink or the price. She told a story, and that story is now worth a billion dollars. A story moves someone through a decision they cannot argue with, because it isn’t happening to them yet. Use it right after the one-liner, while they are still deciding whether to care.
Facts get checked. Stories get finished.

4. "THIS ISN'T FOR EVERYONE, AND IT MIGHT NOT BE FOR YOU."
Here is the part that feels insane until you watch it work. The moment you give someone permission to walk away, they stop looking for the exit and start looking at you.
Use it before you have been asked a single objection. It is not reverse psychology. It is just true, and they can hear that.
Pressure makes people leave. Permission makes them lean in.

5. "WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO BE WORTH IT TO YOU?"
Now they build the case instead of you. Every reason they say out loud is a reason they already believe, which is more than any reason of yours will ever be.
Use it when you feel yourself about to list features. Ask this instead and stop talking.
Your reasons convince you. Theirs convince them.

6. “HERE’S THE NUMBER. I’M GOING TO STOP TALKING NOW.”
The number is not the problem. The apologising after the number, the softening, the justifying, the discount you offer before anyone asks. Then close your mouth and let it be a normal thing you said.
Whoever fills the silence after the price pays for it.

7. "TELL ME NO IF IT'S A NO. I'D RATHER KNOW
THAN WONDER."
This is the opposite of chasing, and it is the only
close that never costs you the relationship. You are
not asking for the sale. You are asking for the truth,
and people will nearly always give you that.
Use it instead of "let me follow up next week."
A fast no is a gift. A slow maybe is a tax.
