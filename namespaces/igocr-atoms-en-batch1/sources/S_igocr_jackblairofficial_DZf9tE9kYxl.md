1. "If this isn't a fit, are you okay telling me no?"

Sandler once made 87 calls in a row and got 87 noes, then built a system around killing the pressure that caused them.

This line hands the buyer permission to say no, which makes them relax and tell you the truth instead of dodging you. Pressure creates resistance, permission dissolves it.

Give them the exit and they stop looking for it.

2. “I’m not sure we can help yet. Mind if I ask a few questions?”

Sandler taught reps to drop the eager pitch and take the posture of a doctor, not a salesman.

Admitting you might not be able to help flips the dynamic, now the buyer is the one trying to qualify for you. Chasing repels, mild detachment attracts.

The second you stop needing the sale, they start wanting it.

3. "How long has this been going on, and what have you tried?"

Sandler built the pain funnel because people buy emotionally and justify it later with logic.

These questions walk the buyer down from a surface complaint to the real cost of doing nothing, in their own words. People are moved by the pain they say out loud, not the one you point at.

Let them talk their way into why they need you.

4. “It sounds like fixing this maybe isn't a priority right now?”
Sandler called this the negative reverse, gently suggesting the opposite of what you actually want.
When you back off, a real buyer pushes against you and argues for the sale themselves. People defend what they might lose. Lean away and the committed ones lean in.
Take it away a little and watch who reaches for it.

5. "If the budget isn't there, it's completely fine to say so."

Sandler refused to demo for people who could never buy, so he put money on the table early.

Giving the buyer an easy out on budget surfaces the truth before you waste a single hour pitching. A fast no is worth more than a slow maybe.

Qualify hard up front and you only pitch people who can say yes.

6. "Besides you, who else weighs in on a decision like this?"
Sandler watched countless deals die because the rep pitched someone who could not actually sign.
This question finds the real decision makers before you build the whole case for the wrong person. The best pitch aimed at a non decoder closes nothing.
Find who signs before you spend your breath convincing anyone.

7. “So where do you think we should go from here?”

Sandler closed by handing the next step back to the buyer instead of pushing for it.

When they say the path forward out loud, it becomes their idea, and people do not argue with their own conclusions. The strongest close is the one the buyer talks themselves into.

Let them name the next step and the pressure to close disappears.
