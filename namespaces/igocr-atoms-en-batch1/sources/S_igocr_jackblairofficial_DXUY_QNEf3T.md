1. "I am going to tell you exactly what I will do and exactly when I will do it. Hold me to it."

Chigurh's power was rooted in one thing: no one else in the room could offer. Complete, unconditional consistency between what he said and what he did.

This phrase sets a standard that almost no rep in the market ever establishes explicitly. The buyer who hears it stops expecting the usual and starts paying attention to something different. And the rep who then delivers exactly what they promised, on time, without being chased, becomes the only rep that buyer ever calls back.

Say it. Mean it. Do it. The trust that follows closes everything.

2. "I do not overpromise. What I am telling you right now is exactly what you will get."

Most buyers have been burned by reps who sold the dream and delivered the reality.
That burn is the invisible wall between you and every close you are trying to make.

This phrase dismantles the wall before it becomes the objection.
It signals a different kind of rep, one whose word is the contract before the contract is signed.
The buyer who has been overpromised before responds to this like air in a room that ran out of it.

Promise less than everyone else. Deliver more. The close takes care of itself.

Chigurch never surprised anyone with an outcome they were not prepared for.

The buyer who hears this stops bracing for the unexpected and starts relaxing into the relationship.

4. "I am going to follow up on exactly the day I said I would. Not the day after."

The follow-up is where most reps reveal that their word means less than they implied.
They said Tuesday and called Thursday and wondered why the buyer's tone changed.

This phrase commits to a precision that signals respect for the buyer's time and calendar.
The rep who arrived when they said they would is already different from every other rep the buyer is comparing.
Precision in the small things is what makes buyers believe you in the large ones.

Be exactly where you said you would be. The trust compounds with every repetition.

5. "I want to make sure what I am telling you right now is something I can actually deliver. Give me a moment to confirm."

Chigurh never committed to something he had not already confirmed was within his control.
That discipline was the reason his word was worth more than anyone else's in any room.

This phrase signals a level of integrity so rare in sales that buyers almost do not recognise it at first.
You are not slowing down to protect yourself. You are slowing down to protect them.
The buyer who sees that distinction stops negotiating and starts trusting.

Confirm before you commit. Every rep who skips that step eventually pays for it.

6. "You will never have to chase me. If I go quiet, something has gone wrong and I will tell you what."

Most buyers chase their reps. They follow up on their own follow-ups.
They email to confirm the call they already confirmed.
That chase is the slow erosion of every trust a good first call built.

This phrase eliminates the chase before it begins and signals a standard of communication the buyer has probably never experienced from a rep.
The buyer who never has to chase you never thinks about replacing you.

Remove the chase. The relationship that does not require chasing never ends.
