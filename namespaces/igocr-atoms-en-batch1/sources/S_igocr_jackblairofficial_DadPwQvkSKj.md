1. Make the problem expensive before you solve it.

The buyer admits a small annoyance and you leap into the pitch.

Across 35,000 calls, average reps solved problems the second they heard one. The top closers made the problem bigger first, because a small problem never justifies a big yes.

Say: "What does that end up costing you by the end of a quarter?"

2. Let them say the value out loud.

You list benefits. They nod. Nothing moves.

The study found deals advance when the buyer states the payoff in their own words. A rep saying it counts for almost nothing.

Say: "If that were fixed tomorrow, what would it free you up to do?"

4. Never run a close on a big deal.

End of call. You reach for the assumptive close you were taught.

The study's most hated finding: closing techniques lifted win rates on cheap tickets and lowered them as the deal grew. The bigger the number, the worse the trick performs.

Say: "I'm not going to run a close on you. Tell me where you honestly stand."

5. Objections are self-inflicted.
You pride yourself on handling objections.
The research called that the tell.
The gap between top and average was
never how they handled objections. It was
how few they got. Objections spike when
you pitch before the need is explicit.
Say: "Before I show you anything, tell me
what's broken about how you do it now."

6. Never end a call on a vibe.

"Great call, let's keep in touch!" That deal is already dead.

The researchers split call endings into advances and continuations. Continuations feel friendly and go nowhere. Top closers left every call with a commitment the buyer had to act on.

Say: "Before we hang up, what's the next step you're taking on your side, and by when?"

7. Close on their sentence, never yours.

You translate their problem into your pitch language and lose them.

Buyers move on needs they voiced themselves. The winning reps repeated the buyer's exact words back and attached the fix to that sentence only.

Say: "You said [their exact words] is the priority. Here's the one thing that fixes exactly that."
