1. "What is doing nothing costing you every month you wait?"

Fisher's whole life circled one question: why do people refuse the thing that would help them? Because the cost of staying stuck is abstract, and abstractions move no one. Put a number on the status quo. The moment inaction has a price, it stops being the safe choice.

A no with no price attached always wins.

2. "Walk me through what next quarter looks like if nothing changes."

His nuclear idea forced a president to picture the death instead of reciting a code. Do the same. Don't argue for your offer, make them narrate the version where they pass. When a buyer describes the stuck quarter out loud, they feel it, and it get solved.

Make them live in the no before they choose it.

3. "Who on your team feels this the most right now?"

Distance is why buyers stall, so shrink it. A line on a report is easy to defer. A named person having a hard week is not. Move the problem from the spreadsheet to a face, because people get protected faster than numbers get fixed.

A problem with a face gets a budget.

4. "A year from now, is this the same conversation or a different one?"

Abstraction lets people postpone forever. Fisher collapsed "tens of millions the room. Collapse "someday" into a date. When the buyer pictures themselves twelve months out, still stuck, still explaining it, the waiting starts to cost something.

Someday is free. A date has a price.

5. "You keep calling this a small thing. What's it costing to keep calling it small?"

People shrink problems with language, the way jargon shrank nuclear war to a code. Words like "minor" and "later" are anesthetic. Gently take the anesthetic away. When a buyer has to defend why it's small, they usually hear that it isn't.

The words they use to shrink it are the wall.

6. "If this were bleeding money in plain sight, would it still be waiting?"

Most costs are invisible, so they feel survivable. Make the slow leak visible. Fisher's whole genius was turning a distant abstraction into something you couldn't look away from. Do that with the quiet monthly loss and the wait ends on its own.

Invisible losses are the ones people tolerate forever.

7. "Let's not decide the whole thing. Let's decide the first real step, today."

Fisher knew a decision too big to feel is one people flee. Shrink it. One concrete step in the room beats a grand yes they escape after the call. Motion kills the abstraction, because a step already taken can't be filed under someday.

A step taken today can't be postponed to never.
