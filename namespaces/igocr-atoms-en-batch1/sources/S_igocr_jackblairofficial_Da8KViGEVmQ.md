1. "Can I get ninety seconds to tell you why I called? Then you decide if we keep talking."

In the Freedman and Fraser study, one tiny yes changed what people agreed to weeks later. Homeowners who accepted a small sign took the monster billboard at four times the rate.

Never open on the big ask. Open with a request so small that refusing it feels absurd.

Small doors open big rooms.

2. "Before we touch price, can we agree the problem is real?"
The first yes builds a self-image: I am someone who acts on this. Every later request gets measured against that identity instead of standing trial alone.
Stack agreements on the problem before you present anything. A buyer who confirmed the pain three times cannot cheaply dismiss the cure.
People obey their own words first.

3. "Would you fill out a two-minute scorecard before our next call?"

Nobody in the study was argued into anything. The small act itself did the persuading. Effort invested becomes commitment banked.

Give homework that costs minutes, not hours. A buyer who spends effort on your deal starts quietly defending it inside their own head.

Effort spent is a deposit on yes.

4. "Earlier you told me this costs you every single week. Is that still true?"

Once people state a position, they defend it. That is the engine under the whole experiment.

So bank their words in discovery and replay them at the objection. You never have to push. You just quote them back to themselves and wait.

Their words outsell yours. Every time.

5. "Let's not decide the whole thing today. Let's decide the pilot."

Compliance climbs when each request reads as a continuation, not an escalation. The billboard was never the first ask.

Cut your decision into a step one person can approve today without a committee, and let the staircase do the heavy work.

Shrink the ask until yes gets easy.

6. "You've already done the hard part. This step is smaller than the last one."

Momentum is a real force in agreement, and it goes unnoticed unless somebody narrates it.

Point at the steps already climbed before you show the next one. A buyer who sees their own footprints on the staircase keeps walking.

Momentum only works if you name it.

7. "Who else needs to feel good about this? Let's get them their own small yes."

The staircase works on every stakeholder, not just your champion. Committees kill conclusions they had no hand in reaching.

So never send your champion uphill carrying your big ask. Arm them with one small, easy yes per voter.

Every voter gets their own staircase.
