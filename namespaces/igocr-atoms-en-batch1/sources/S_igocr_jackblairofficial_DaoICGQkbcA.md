1. "Let's meet Thursday, because that's when your trial hits two weeks and we'll have real data to look at."

Ellen Langer's Harvard team cut a copy machine line three ways. The naked ask got 60% compliance. Adding "because" plus a reason got over 93%. Most reps schedule follow-ups with naked asks all day long. Attach the reason to every next step you propose.

A naked ask invites a no. A reasoned ask invites a yes.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair

2. "It's $8,000, because two of that is the onboarding buildout. Here's the split."

The brain hears "because" and assumes legitimacy before it audits the logic. A bare price is a floating number, and floating numbers feel negotiable. Anchor your price to its anatomy the moment you say it, and it stops being an opinion the buyer gets to haggle with.

A price without a because sounds like an opinion.

Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "The price holds, because the work behind it doesn't change when the invoice does."

Reps cave on discounts because refusing feels rude. But Langer's findings cut both ways: people accept almost anything that arrives with a reason attached. Refuse with a because and the refusal reads as policy, not rejection. The buyer stops pushing on a wall that explains itself.

A no with a reason attached doesn't feel like a fight.

4. "This closes Friday, because our onboarding cohort locks Monday morning."

Deadlines without reasons smell like pressure, and buyers have smelled a thousand of them. A real mechanical because turns the same date into logistics. If your deadline has no true reason behind it, the fix is the deadline, not the phrasing. Never dress a fake clock in a because.

A deadline with a real because is information. Without one, it's a trick.

5. "Can I ask you something blunt? Because guessing wastes your time more than asking does."

Hard questions get denied at the door when they arrive alone. Ride the door with them and they get permission instead. The because tells the buyer the bluntness serves them, not you, and the question that would have stalled the call becomes the one that moves it.

The because is the passport that gets hard questions across the border.

6. "I'm asking you specifically, because you're the one who pushed this through on your side."

A because that names why THEM converts a favor into a role. People decline requests all day, but they step into parts that were written for them. When you need the intro, the internal push, or the referral, attach the reason they're asking specifically are the one you're asking.

Nobody refuses a part that was written for them.
