1. "I am going to be honest with you and I want you to know that honesty comes with a genuine smile behind it."

Stewy Hosseini delivered hard truths in ways that made the other person feel lucky to receive them.
That combination of directness and warmth was the most disarming thing in any room he entered.

This phrase signals that what follows is both honest and safe to receive.
The buyer who was bracing for another polished rep suddenly feels the ease of someone who means well and says so.
Charm is not the absence of directness. It is directness delivered with enough warmth that the buyer never feels threatened by it.

Lead with honest warmth. The buyer who feels both stops protecting themselves and starts listening for real.

2. "I have to say I think you are going to enjoy this conversation more than you expected to."

Stewy always set an expectation of enjoyment before the room had decided how to feel.
And because he believed it completely the room almost always caught up with him.

This phrase plants a positive expectation in the buyer's mind before you have delivered anything worth expecting.
The buyer who walks in with low energy suddenly has a prediction to either confirm or disprove.
Most choose to confirm it because Stewy was always right and the promise always is.

Set the expectation of enjoyment. The buyer who expects to enjoy the call almost always does.

3. "Look I am going to cut through the usual back and forth because I think we can both see where this is going."

Stewy never was one of a negotiation when the outcome was already visible to both sides. He named the obvious thing and the room exhaled and moved to the real conversation.

This phrase creates an instant sense of shared intelligence between you and the buyer. They feel like the one who respects them enough to skip the performance. The buyer who feels respected skips the defensive posture and starts engaging authentically.

Name the obvious. The buyer who feels smart enough to see it with you closes faster than any other buyer you will meet.

4. "I genuinely like the way you think about this. Here is where I see it slightly differently."

Stewy always made disagreement feel like a compliment. He validated the thinking before he redirected it and the person being redirected always felt better not worse for the experience.

This phrase is the most sophisticated objection reframe available on any call.
The buyer who was prepared to defend their position suddenly feels validated enough to consider yours.
And a buyer who feels validated never argues. They evaluate.

Validate before you redirect. The buyer who feels heard will always give you the room to move them.

5. "Between us, I think this is actually the easiest decision you are going to make this quarter."

Stewy always made the deal feel obvious and the buyer feel smart for seeing it.
This phrase does the same thing with a casual confidence that lands nothing like a sales pitch.

The buyer who has been overthinking the decision suddenly has a rep who is casually certain it is simple. That certainty is contagious and it spreads from genuine ease rather than rehearsed confidence.
The buyer who catches the certainty stops deliberating and starts deciding.

Make the decision sound simple. The buyer who feels smart for seeing it almost always takes it.

6. "I want to work with you specifically. Not just your company. You. And I think you can tell I mean that."

Stewy always made every deal feel personal because for him every deal was personal.
He chose his people and he made sure they knew they had been chosen.

This phrase converts a vendor relationship into a personal one before the paperwork has been discussed.
The buyer who has been treated like an account number suddenly feels like the person behind the decision.
People close deals with people who chose them. They renew with them. They refer them. They call them first.

Choose them out loud. The buyer who feels chosen almost never chooses anyone else.

7. "I think we are going to do a lot of good business together. This is just the first one."

Stewy never closed a deal. He opened a relationship and made the deal feel like the beginning of something better. This phrase does the same thing and the buyer feels the difference before they consciously understand it.

They are not signing a contract. They are starting something that has a future worth anticipating.
The buyer who sees a future with you stops comparing you to the competition and starts thinking about what comes next.
The close that opens a relationship is always worth more than the one that ends a transaction.

Frame the deal as the first one. The buyer who sees the relationship ahead never treats it as the last.
