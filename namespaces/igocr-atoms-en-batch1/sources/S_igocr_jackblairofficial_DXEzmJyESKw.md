1. "I want to make sure this is right for both of us before we go any further."

The moment you show need, the buyer owns the negotiation. Logan Roy never needed anything from anyone, at least not visibly. This phrase signals that you are evaluating them as much as they are evaluating you. The power shifts before they even realise it happened. Equal footing is still footing you control.

2. "That's an interesting position. Let me think about whether that works for us."

Most reps respond to every counter offer instantly. That urgency is the tell. Logan never rushed a response in his life because he knew that whoever speaks first after a number loses. This phrase buys you silence, signals strength, and makes the buyer question on whether they pushed too hard. Pause. Then answer.

3. "We have a few conversations at a similar stage right now. I want to focus on the one that moves."

This is not a bluff. This is positioning. Logan Roy never sat at a table where he was the only option and let the other side know it. This phrase reintroduces scarcity without aggression and reminds the buyer that your time has value. Scarcity is only a weapon when it sounds like a fact.

4. "I'm not going to be able to move on the price. What I can do is this."

Never defend the number. Redirect to value. Logan never apologised for what he charged because he never needed to. This phrase holds the line on price while giving the buyer something to move toward. You close the discount conversation and open a different one entirely. Hold the number. Shift the frame.

5. "If this doesn't close today I completely understand. I just want to make sure you know what changes tomorrow."

Urgency without desperation. Logan Roy applied pressure the way gravity does, quietly, constantly, without ever raising his voice. This phrase creates consequence without threat and lets the truth do the closing for you. The buyer feels the weight of the decision without feeling pushed. 6. "I hear you. That's not something we're able to accommodate, but here's what we can do."

The rep who folds on every ask trains the buyer to keep asking. Logan Roy never rewarded pressure with concessions and neither should you. This phrase acknowledges the request, closes the door firmly, and immediately redirects to what is possible. You stay in control of the deal without ever sounding defensive.

7. "I think we both know this makes sense. The question is whether you're ready to move."

This is the close Logan Roy would use. Calm, certain, and completely without apology. It assumes the logical conclusion has already been reached and simply asks for a decision. No final summary. No last ditch effort. No pitch. No final ask. Just a quiet, immovable certainty that the answer is yes. Certainty is contagious.
