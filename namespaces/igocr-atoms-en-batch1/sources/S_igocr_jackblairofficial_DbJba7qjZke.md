1. "Before we go any further, here's what would make this a no."

Rob Hall was the best guide of his generation, and his rule was absolute: 2 PM turnaround, summit or no summit, the air is thick and heads are clear. Name your deal breakers on call one, while yours is. Criteria set early cannot be argued with later.

Decide the no while you can still think.

2. "I want this to work. I need two things to be true first."
Guides screen clients before the mountain ever sees them, because wanting the summit is not the same as surviving it. Wanting the deal is not qualifying for it. State your two conditions out loud and check them like gear. A buyer who meets them climbs, you just saved.
Enthusiasm is not oxygen.

3. "If this slips past Friday, let's both agree to stop."

The 2 PM rule works because it is a clock, not a feeling. Feelings on summit day always say keep going. Deals drifting past their own deadline feel one push from closing, and that feeling is the death zone talking. Put a date on the walkaway when the deal is healthy.

The clock is set in clean air.

4. "I'd rather lose you now than watch this fail in month three."

In 1996, summits came as late as 4 PM. Every extra hour up high felt like success and was actually the disaster arriving. A bad-fit deal that closes is a summit in a storm: the celebration and the catastrophe are the same event. Losing the wrong buyer early is the save.

Some closes are the accident.

5. "Let's agree now on what proof ends the maybe."

A turnaround time only works because it is agreed before anyone is exhausted and in love with the view. Maybes survive on undefined evidence. Set the proof with the buyer in advance, and the maybe expires on its own, in either direction.

Undefined proof keeps dead deals warm.
