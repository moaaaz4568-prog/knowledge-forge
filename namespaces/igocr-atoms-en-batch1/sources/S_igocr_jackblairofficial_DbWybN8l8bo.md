1. "WHAT MADE THIS THE RIGHT CALL WHEN YOU PICKED IT"
In 1990 Marilyn vos Savant answered a game show puzzle in her column: three doors, one the car, stick or switch. She said switch. Ten thousand readers wrote in to call her wrong.
Open by honoring the first pick. People defend a choice harder when it's attacked, so make their original decision smart out loud. A buyer respected for door one will actually look at door two.
Nobody switches while they're busy defending.

7. "IF THE NUMBERS SAY SWITCH, WHAT WOULD STILL HOLD YOU BACK?"
Vos Savant kept her answer through a year of mail because the math didn't wobble when the crowd did. The final blocker is never arithmetic. It's the fear of being wrong in public.
Name it out loud at the finish. Ask the question, then make being wrong cheap: the exit clause, the rollback plan, the shared blame if it fails. Buyers don't fear switching. They fear doing it alone.
Make it safe to be wrong and they'll do what's right.
