1. "It seems like something about this isn't sitting right."

Voss called this labeling. You name the emotion you sense instead of ignoring it.

A buyer braced for a pitch drops their guard the moment they feel understood. Naming the hesitation out loud makes it shrink.

Name the feeling and it loses its grip.

2. "How am I supposed to do that?"
Voss called this a calibrated question. You
push back without pushing.
Instead of arguing the objection, you hand
it back and make them solve it. Now they
are working on your side of the problem,
not against it.
Make them solve it and they stop fighting
it.

3. Repeat their last three words. Then go quiet.

Voss called this mirroring. Three words, said back as a question, nothing more.

The buyer feels heard and keeps talking, and the longer they talk the more they talk themselves toward yes. You close less by saying more.

Say less. Let them convince themselves.

4. "You're probably going to think the price is too high."

Voss called this the accusation audit. You say their objection before they can.

When you name the worst thing they're thinking, you take its power. They can't use it against you once you've already said it out loud.

Say the objection first and you defuse it.

5. "Is now a bad time?"
Voss proved people feel safe saying no,
and safety opens them up.
A buyer terrified of being trapped relaxes
the instant you hand them the exit. The
no they get to say freely becomes the yes
they choose freely.
Give them the no and you earn the yes.

6. "What about this doesn't work for you?"

Voss used "what" and "how," never "why."
Why feels like an accusation.

These questions make the buyer explain
their real blocker out loud, and the real
blocker is almost never the one they led
with. Now you can solve the true
objection.

Ask what, not why, and the truth comes
out.

7. "It sounds like you're ready, you just need it to feel right."

Voss aimed for "that's right," not "you're right." That's right means they feel understood.

When you summarize their situation so well they say "that's right," the wall is gone. That moment, not a clever pitch, is where the deal turns.

Earn a "that's right" and the close is already done.
