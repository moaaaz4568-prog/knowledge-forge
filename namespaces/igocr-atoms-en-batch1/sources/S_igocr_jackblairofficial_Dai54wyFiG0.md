1. "You're clearly not looking. That's exactly why I'm calling."
Retained search firms charge a third of a first-year salary because the people worth having are never on the job boards. When a buyer says they're not in the market, stop treating it as a wall. It's the profile. Say it back to them as the reason you're here.
The best buyers are never in the market.
<!-- image 2 -->

2. "Nothing about this call requires anything from you."

Recruiters open confidential, zero-obligation conversations because pressure hangs up the comfortable. A buyer who feels the exit is free will wander surprisingly far from it. Build the container out loud at the top of the call, then explore like it's true. Because it is.

Comfortable people only talk when the door stays open.
<!-- image 3 -->

3. "I'm coming to you specifically, and here's why."
One named, checkable reason separates a targeted approach from spam in a single sentence. Recruiters cite the deal you closed, the team you run, the exact thing that flagged you. Give your buyer the real reason it's them. Generic praise reads as a script. Specifics read as research.
A named reason beats a hundred compliments.

4. "Before I ask you anything, here's what I'm seeing in the market."

Good recruiters open by paying the person in information. Comp ranges. Who's hiring. What their profile commands. The buyer leans in because the call is already worth something before any ask exists. Bring your market intel to every call and spend it first.

Pay them in information before you ask for anything.

5. "Walk me forward two years in the current setup. Where does it top out?"

Recruiters never trash the current job.
They walk the person down their own trajectory until the ceiling comes into view on its own. Do the same with the current vendor, the current process, the current plan. You don't have to sell the gap once they've seen it.

Don't attack the status quo. Ask where it peaks.

6. "Are you comfortable, or are you capped?"

The entire recruiting industry runs on the fact that those two feel identical from the inside. Most people have never been asked to tell them apart. The stalled middle of your deal is usually a buyer mistaking familiarity for satisfaction. This question splits them in one line.

Comfort and a ceiling feel identical from inside.

7. "If the timing's wrong, help me understand what right timing looks like."
A recruiter never closes a file on a no. They log the conditions that would change it and call back the week those conditions arrive. Convert every not now into named criteria plus permission to return. Then actually return.
A no with conditions is a yes with a date.
