1. "Before I show you anything, tell me what you'd want to be true a year from now."

Maria Konnikova, a psychologist who spent years dissecting history's great cons, found they all open the same way: the put-up. Learn exactly what the mark wants before the you say a word. The con does this to exploit the want. You do the same thing to find out if you can honestly serve it. Either way, the pitch that skips this step is already dead.

You can't sell to a want you never bothered to find.

2. "We might not be a fit, and I'll tell you straight if we're not."

Konnikova calls the second stage the play: the emotional foundation laid before any offer. The con manufactures it. You earn it, by being visibly willing to lose the sale. Nothing builds real trust faster than a seller who will disqualify you to your face. It is the one thing a con can never actually afford to merit.

The fastest way to be trusted is to risk the sale to earn it.

3. "Walk me through why you'd even consider changing this now."

Her most unsettling finding: in most of the persuasion a con, the mark does rearranges it so you talk yourself in. Strip out the theft and you have the best sales move there is. Stop pushing reasons at the buyer. Ask the question that makes them say their own out loud. The reasons they generate are the only ones that hold after you hang up.

The reasons they say out loud are the only ones that close them.

The next stage is the tale. Konnikova found belief is the tale. Konnikova rides not on the pile of evidence but on the coherence of the story the mind builds around it. The con hands you a fake story. You hand them a true, specific one. A vague better future moves no one, with dates and steps, is the thing they replay after the call.

People don't buy the facts. They buy the story the facts add up to.

Your most guarded buyer, the one who has heard it all, is not cracked by a slicker pitch. They are disarmed the second you set the pitch down and just talk to them like an adult.

The buyer certain they can't be sold is the one a pitch bounces off.

Every con ends the same way, the blow-off: take it and vanish before the mark notices. This is the one step you invert completely. The con disappears. Your entire reputation is that you don't. A con's last move is to vanish. Yours is to stay. That's the whole difference.
