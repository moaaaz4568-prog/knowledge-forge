No. 1
Kill your darlings

Your portfolio shouldn't be a timeline of every project you've ever done since you picked up a camera. It's a curated gallery that showcases your taste, style and capabilities.

No matter how emotionally attached you are to a project, ask yourself, if you'll be embarrassed when a potential clients clicks on it, instead of another piece that presents your current skillset better.
<!-- image 2 -->
Speak the brand's language
Don't sell just a video, focus on the business impact. How will your work increase their revenue, engagement or brand authority. Most clients you'll have are not creative people, so try and understand what goal they want to achieve with that project.
<!-- image 3 -->
No. 3
Ask high-level questions
Is this video part of a larger campaign? If so, what are the supporting assets and messaging? Where does this sit in the funnel - top of funnel awareness or bottom of funnel conversion? Are they running paid ads?

Don't be scared to ask these questions. It shows you understand the bigger picture beyond just delivering a beautiful video. Connect with their marketing team and position yourself as someone who gets strategy, not just production.
<!-- image 4 -->
No. 4
Show confidence
If you don't believe you're worth the budget you proposed, neither will they.
Establish a very clear process and walk them through it - discovery call, proposal, contract, pre-payment. High value clients respect structure and proactive communication.
