1. "BEFORE WE TALK PRICE, CAN I ASK WHAT YOU'RE COMPARING THIS TO?"
January 12, 2007, 7:51 AM. The Washington Post puts Joshua Bell, one of the finest violinists alive, at the L'Enfant Plaza metro with his 1713 Stradivarius. Rush hour prices him like a busker.
Nothing about the product changed, only the comparison set. Ask this first, because whatever sits next to you in their head is setting your number.
The comparison is the price.

7. "WHICH ROOM DO YOU WANT TO MAKE THIS DECISION IN?"

The Post picked rush hour on purpose. Same man, same violin, wrong moment, and the verdict never had a chance. Decisions inherit the room they're made in.

Stop taking verdicts in hallways. Book the decision its own room: time held, right people in, nothing else competing. You've watched deals die of context, not content.

A hallway no was never a real no.
