1. "This deserves better than email. Got ninety seconds?"

Juliana Schroeder and Nicholas Epley gave evaluators, including Fortune 500 recruiters, the same pitches spoken and written. Heard out loud, the same words rated smarter, warmer, more hireable.

When the stakes rise, stop typing and ask for seconds, not minutes.

Your voice is the upgrade your words need.
<!-- image 2 -->

@jackblairofficial

2. "I typed three replies and deleted them all. Can I just call you?"

Admitting the deleted drafts is honesty that flatters. It tells the buyer this conversation matters enough that writing failed it.

Use this the moment a thread starts turning tense or delicate. Tone dies in text and gets resurrected on a call.

Hard things go better out loud.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair

3. "Don't open the contract yet. Let me walk you through the two lines that matter first."

A contract read alone gets the buyer's coldest, most suspicious reading. Every clause sounds worse in silence.

Get your voice into the document before their doubts do. The two lines you explain out loud never become objections.

Narrate the contract or it testifies against you.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
4. "If this lands wrong in writing, it costs us a week. I'd rather say it."
Schroeder and Epley's finding, in plain terms: text strips the thinking out of your message and leaves only the words. Misreadings fill the gap.
Name the risk of writing it and the buyer takes the call. You've made the safe option.
A misread email costs more than a call.

5. "Send questions by email anytime. I'll always answer the big ones by phone."

This is a policy, stated once, and it quietly reroutes every important moment of the deal onto your strongest channel.

Small stuff stays in writing where it belongs. Anything that decides money gets your actual voice on it.

Route the deal through your best channel.

6. "Before you forward this to your boss, let's rehearse how you'll say it."

Your champion selling you internally by forwarded PDF is the study's finding working against you: your case, stripped of its voice.

Rehearse them out loud until they can pitch it like they mean it, because they'll be heard, not read.

Arm your champion with a voice, not a file.

7. "A no over the phone is worth ten maybes over email. I can take it."

Buyers hide behind text because text makes soft rejection easy, and soft rejections keep dead deals in your pipeline for months.

Invite the hard answer out loud. Either you get the truth, or the conversation that saves the deal.

Dead deals hide in writing. Flush them.
