"IF WE'RE INTERCHANGEABLE WITH YOUR OTHER QUOTES, TAKE THE CHEAPEST ONE."
Peter Kraljic's model grades every purchase on two axes, how much it affects profit and how risky it is to source. Land in the easy to replace corner and grinding your price is literally the buyer's job.
Say it early and mean it. Then find out whether they actually believe you are interchangeable, because that is the only question on the table.
You cannot out-argue being replaceable. You can only stop being it.

"I COULD BE WRONG ABOUT THE STAKES HERE. WHAT BREAKS ON YOUR SIDE IF THIS DOESN'T WORK?"

Supply risk is one of the two axes, and it is measured by what happens to the buyer when a supplier fails. Not by how much they like the product.

Get the consequence said out loud, in their words, before anyone opens a spreadsheet.

Your price is set by what your failure would cost them.

"WHERE ELSE DOES THIS TOUCH?"

Profit impact is the other axis. A purchase that affects one line gets handled as routine. A purchase that affects the operation gets handled as something worth protecting.

Widen the frame from the line item to the operation before price comes up, not after.

A line item gets negotiated. An operation gets protected.
