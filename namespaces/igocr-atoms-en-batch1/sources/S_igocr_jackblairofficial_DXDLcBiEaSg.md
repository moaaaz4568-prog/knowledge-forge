1. "Help me understand what's holding you back."

Most reps hear "I need to think about it" and go quiet.
That silence is where the deal dies.

This phrase opens the real objection without pressure.
The buyer feels heard, not cornered.
You cannot close what you cannot see.

Get them talking. Then you can close.

2. "That concern makes sense. Here's what my other clients said before they signed."

You are not arguing. You are surrounding them with proof.
Social proof hits harder than logic ever will.

The buyer stops feeling like they are taking a risk.
They start feeling like they are joining something.

Make saying yes feel inevitable.

3. "If that weren't an issue, would you move forward today?"

One question. Reveals everything.
Either the objection is real or it is a stall.

If they say yes, you have one job left: solve that one thing.
If they hesitate, there is a deeper objection hiding underneath.

Surgeons don't guess. Neither should you.

4. "Let me be straight with you, most people who wait end up paying more."
Not a threat. A fact delivered with authority.
Urgency with out evidence is noise. Urgency with truth is pressure.
Saul never begged. He informed.
Then he let the buyer feel the weight of the alternative.
Let the cost of waiting close the deal for you.

5. "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?"
This is the most underused phrase in sales.
It hands the buyer a pen and says: write your own close.
They tell you exactly what they need.
You deliver it. The deal moves.
Stop guessing what they want. Ask them.

7. "What happens on your end if this doesn't get solved in the next 30 days?"

Make them calculate the cost of inaction out loud.
Their own words become your most powerful closing argument.

Buyers say no to products. They never say no to solving problems.
You are not selling. You are holding up a mirror.

Let them close themselves.
