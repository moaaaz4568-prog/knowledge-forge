1. "Help me understand what matters most to you here."

Most reps argue with the position the buyer says out loud. The pro digs past it to the interest underneath, because the stated objection is almost never the real one. Find what they actually want and half the fight disappears.

You can't move a position. You can move an interest.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "Let's put the problem on one side
and the two of us on the other."

The amateur makes the buyer the
opponent. The pro makes the problem the
opponent and sits on the same side of the
table. The second it stops feeling like you
versus them, the defenses drop.

Beat the problem, not the person.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "If this doesn't work out, what's your honest plan B?"

Make them say their alternative out loud.
Usually it's weaker than they pretended,
and the moment they hear themselves
describe it, your offer starts looking like
the obvious move without you pushing at
all.

A weak alternative closes the deal for you.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "What if we could solve both of those at once?"

Weak negotiators split the difference.
Strong ones invent a third option neither side walked in with. You stop fighting over the size of the slice and quietly make the whole thing bigger.

Don't divide the pie. Grow it.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Let's agree on a fair standard first, then run the numbers through it."

Don't haggle ego against ego. Anchor to something outside both of you, the comparable, the market rate, the documented number. Now it's not you versus them, it's both of you versus the facts.

Take it off opinion. Put it on a standard.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Tell me where I've got this wrong."

Inviting the pushback is the move. It makes the buyer feel heard, surfaces the real objection you'd never have found, and people defend a decision far harder when they helped shape it.

Let them edit the deal and they'll defend it.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

7. "So it sounds like if we handle X, you're comfortable moving forward?"

Name the one thing standing in the way and get them to confirm it's the only thing. Now there's nothing vague left to hide behind. Solve that, and the yes was already agreed to a sentence ago.

Close the gap to one question, then answer it.
