Pacetta's first rule, drilled into every rep he led.

2. "Know more about the customer than they expect."

Pacetta on walking in prepared.

His reps researched the account hard before any meeting. The customer's business, their pressures, their competitors. Walking in already understanding their world made the rep a peer instead of a vendor.

Preparation is the difference between visiting and belonging.

3. "Outservice the competition after the sale."

Pacetta on where accounts are actually kept.

He taught that the sale is where the relationship starts, not where it ends. The rep who keeps showing up, solving problems, and following through is the one who still has the account next year.

Anyone can win an account. Service is how you keep it.

4. "Make the customer a promise, then keep it."

Pacetta on building trust fast.

He had reps make small, specific promises and then keep every one, exactly. A call back by Tuesday. An answer by noon. Each kept promise stacked into a reputation the customer could lean on.

Trust is just a pile of small promises you actually kept.

5. "Show up prepared or do not show up."
Pacetta on respecting the customer's time.
He had no patience for reps winging a meeting. An unprepared rep wastes the customer's time and burns the relationship. If you were not ready, he would rather you rescheduled than showed up empty.
An unprepared meeting costs more than no meeting at all.

6. "Treat every account like the competition is calling it tomorrow."

Pacetta on never coasting.

He refused to let his reps relax on a won account. Somewhere a competitor was preparing to take it. Staying a little paranoid kept the rep attentive and kept the account loyal.

The account you stop working is the account someone else is working.

The customer feels your energy before they hear your pitch.
