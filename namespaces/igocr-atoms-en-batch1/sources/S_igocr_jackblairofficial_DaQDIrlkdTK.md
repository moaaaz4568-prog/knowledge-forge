1. "Can I show you something you're probably not seeing?"

The study's core finding: the best reps don't ask what keeps you up at night, they tell you what should. You lead with an insight the buyer didn't have, not a question they're tired of. Teaching beats interrogating.

Walk in with an insight, not a discovery question.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "Most people assume X. Here's the cost."

Challengers reframe the problem before they ever mention a solution. You name the expensive assumption the buyer doesn't know they're making. The reaction you want is "I never thought of it that way."

Reframe the problem. Show them the hidden cost.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "For someone in your seat, here's what this means."

The "tailor" rule. The same insight lands differently on a CFO than an operator. You bend the message to the one person in front of you and what they personally answer for. Relevance is what makes them lean in.

Tailor the point to the person, not the room.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Let's talk money now, so it doesn't blow up later."

Challengers raise price early and without flinching. Weaker reps dodge it until the end and let it detonate the deal. Naming the number first is a control move, and it signals you're not afraid of your own value.

Bring up price first. Don't let it ambush you.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "That's exactly why we keep going. Next step is..."

Taking control means handling pushback without folding. You don't get defensive and you don't cave, you absorb the objection and redirect to the next action. Calm assertiveness, not aggression.

Meet resistance by pointing at the next step.

7. "Any reason we couldn't move forward today?"

Challengers create constructive tension instead of avoiding it. This surfaces the real objection while you're still in the room to handle it, instead of hearing "let me think about it" and losing the deal to silence.

Surface the real objection while you can still answer it.
