1. "I'm not sure if it's for you, but..."

Jones found that the fastest way to kill resistance is to remove the pressure first. That opener tells the brain there's no trap, so curiosity takes over and they lean in instead of bracing. Lower the stakes and people raise their interest.

2. "How open-minded are you about...?"
Nobody wants to call themselves closed-minded. Jones used that to float a new idea, because the question itself pulls a buyer toward saying yes before they've even heard it. Frame the ask so the only comfortable answer leans your way.

3. "What do you know about...?"
Instead of lecturing, Jones handed the
buyer the floor. Their answer shows you
their gaps and their objections, so you sell
into reality instead of guessing. Ask what
they know and they will tell you exactly
how to sell to them.

4. "Just out of curiosity..."

Jones used this to ask the blunt question without the bluntness. It softens what is really stopping you into something a buyer answers honestly instead of defending against. Disarm the hard question and you finally get the true one answered.

5. "Most people..."
People are wired to do what people like
them do. Jones would say most people in
your position choose this, and the buyer
feels safe following the crowd. Show them
the path others took and the scary
decision starts feeling normal.

6. "Before you make your mind up, let's run through it one more time."

When a no is coming, Jones slowed it down instead of pushing. One calm recap lets the buyer hear what they would actually be turning down. Make them see exactly what they are saying no to before they say it.

7. "What questions do you have for me?"

Not any questions, which invites none.
Jones assumed there were questions, so
the buyer surfaced the real hesitation
instead of leaving with it unspoken. Ask it
so the doubt comes out now, not after
they have gone quiet.
