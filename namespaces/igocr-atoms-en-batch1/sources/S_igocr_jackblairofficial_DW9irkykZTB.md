1. "I've said what I needed to say. The next move is yours."

Rhades never repeated himself in a courtroom. He made his case and let the weight of it sit.

Most reps panic the second a buyer goes quiet. They start talking again and undo everything they just built.

Stop rescuing them from the silence. That silence is doing your job.

Four words that flip the entire pressure dynamic.
Suddenly you're not waiting. You're watching.

The buyer who was stalling is now the one who feels observed.
Rhodes knew that patience looks exactly like power from the other side of the table.

The rep who seems unhurried always wins the room.

3. "I want to make sure I understand what you're actually saying."

Not an agreement. Not a pushback.
A pause dressed as a question.

This phrase slows the conversation to your pace.
It forces the buyer to clarify, commit, or expose the real objection.

Rhoades never let a vague answer stand.

4. "That's interesting. Walk me through that."

Five words that hand the talking back to them entirely.
While they talk, you think. While they fill the silence, you close.

Rhodes built entire cases on what people said when they thought he was just listening. Most reps talk when they should be extracting.

The buyer will tell you exactly how to close them. Let them.

6. "Before you answer, I want you to be sure."

This phrase does something almost no rep ever does.
It slows the buyer down before they give you a reflex no.

Rhodes never wanted a fast answer. He wanted the right one.
Because a buyer who is sure says yes once and means it.

Slow the close and it sticks. Rush it and it falls apart by Friday.
