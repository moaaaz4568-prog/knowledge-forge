FRANK BETTGER MADE $1M A YEAR SELLING INSURANCE ON A 1920S PHONE. WARREN BUFFETT STILL HANDS OUT HIS BOOK. THIS IS REMOTE CLOSING. HERE ARE THE 7 PHRASES HE USED:
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "Why?"

His most famous one-word objection
breaker.

When a prospect said no, he didn't argue.
He asked "Why?" in a soft voice. Then he
waited. The prospect would always start
explaining, and the real objection would
surface.

Most reps don't have the discipline to ask
one word.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. “In addition to that, is there any other reason?”

His Ben Franklin objection-isolation script.

Once an objection surfaced, he asked if there was another. He kept asking until the prospect ran out. Then he handled them in order.

Ben Feldman stole this from him 30 years later.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "What's behind that?"

Documented drill-down question.

When a prospect gave a surface objection, he didn't accept it. He asked what was underneath. The first objection is almost never the real one.

He'd dig until the real one came out.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
4. "If you were in my place, what would you do?"
Documented perspective-flip question.
When a sale stalled, he handed the problem to the prospect. They almost always gave him the next move themselves.
The prospect would tell him how to close them.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "Sir, may I tell you why?"

Documented permission opener.

Before launching into a pitch, he asked permission. The prospect always said yes. Then they listened actively instead of resisting.

Permission flips the dynamic.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. “I’m not here to sell you anything. I’m here to ask questions.”

Documented disarming opener.

He immediately removed the pressure.
The prospect lowered their guard and
started talking. Bettger listened, then sold
from what he learned.

Sales is asking, not telling.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

7. "Tell me about yourself."

Documented from his sincere interest principle.

His Chapter 1 lesson: become sincerely interested in the other person. He asked, then he listened. The prospect did 80% of the talking.

Most reps reverse this ratio. Bettger never did.
