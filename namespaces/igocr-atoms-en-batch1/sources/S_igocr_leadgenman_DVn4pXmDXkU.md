Large Language Models
A super-autocomplete engine that reasons using attention.
How it processes information:
• Breaks text into tiny pieces (tokens)
• Looks at all relevant tokens at once (self-attention = "focus where needed")
• Builds meaning layer by layer
• Predicts the next token
Why it works:
LLMs don't understand words, they understand relationships between pieces of text.
Think of it as:
A very smart "next-word predictor" that learned logic by reading the entire internet.
<!-- image 7 -->
Small Language Models

Compact, efficient models optimized for speed and specific tasks.

How it processes information:
• Same transformer architecture as LLMs
• Fewer parameters (typically under 10B)
• Often distilled from larger models
• Fine-tuned for narrow use cases

Why it works:
SLMs trade broad knowledge for efficiency, making them fast enough to run on-device or at scale.

Think of it as:
A specialist that knows one thing really well instead of knowing a little about everything.
<!-- image 8 -->
Masked Language Models
Reads sentences like a human: sees the whole thing, predicts hidden words.
How it processes information:
• Masks some tokens
• Looks at full left + right context
• Predicts missing text
• Builds contextual embeddings
Why it works:
Its bidirectional view gives it deep semantic understanding.
Think of it as:
A language model trained on "fill in the blanks."

Segment Anything Models
You point at an object → SAM outlines it perfectly, instantly.
How it processes information:
• Vision Transformer encodes the entire image
• Prompt encoder encodes your point/box
• Mask decoder merges both
• Output = pixel-accurate segmentation
Why it works:
SAM generalizes across images – no retraining needed.
Think of it as:
A universal "select object" tool for the real world.
