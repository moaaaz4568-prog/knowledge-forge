1. He never sold the product.

Most reps sell the product. Feldman sold what the product did. "Don't sell life insurance," he told his agents. "Sell what it can do." A single reframe built a billion-dollar career. Paste this to Claude:

Ben Feldman told his agents "Don't sell life insurance. Sell what it can do." I sell [INSERT OFFER] on booked Zoom calls to [INSERT WHO IT SELLS TO]. Rewrite my offer the way Feldman would: 10 one-line statements of what it does for the buyer, never what it is. Plain words a real person says out loud. Then pick the 3 you'd open a call with and tell me why.

2. Every meeting opened with a question designed to disturb.

Know this one: "Do you know anyone who has a lease on life?" Not a pitch. A pattern interrupt that forced the prospect to see the problem before the solution arrived. Disturbed buyers buy. Comfortable buyers don't. Follow up in the same chat:

Feldman opened every meeting with a question designed to disturb: "Do you know anyone who has a lease on life?" Write 10 opening questions in that style for [INSERT OFFER] sold to [INSERT WHO]. Each one has to make the buyer see the problem before I present anything. No pitch, no product name. Rank them by how hard they are to dodge.

3. He wrote a $30 million check to a billionaire who refused to meet him.

He sealed it in an envelope. Handed it to the secretary. The check was for the estate tax the man's family would owe when he died. The meeting happened that week. He made the cost of doing nothing visible.

Same chat:

List the 10 objections I’ll hear most on a booked call for [INSERT OFFER]. For each one, answer the way Feldman would: put a number on what staying the same costs the buyer, then ask one question. Word for word, in out-loud English. Then rank the 3 that kill the most deals.
