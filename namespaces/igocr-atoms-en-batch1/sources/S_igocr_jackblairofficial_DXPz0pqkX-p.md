1. "I am not going to take you through a presentation. I am going to tell you three things that matter for your business right now."

Tywin Lannister never performed for a room. He informed it.
Senior buyers have survived a thousand presentations and they are exhausted by all of them.

This phrase signals immediately that this conversation operates at a different level.
No deck. No features. No theatre.
Just three things that matter, delivered by someone who did the work to know what they are.

Strip the performance. The executive who stops waiting for the pitch starts listening for the insight.

3. "I want to be direct with you because I think that is what you prefer and what this situation deserves."

Tywin Lannister had no patience for indirection and every senior buyer in every industry shares that quality. This phrase signals that what follows will not be softened, spun, or buried in qualifications.

It earns the executive's attention before you have said the thing that requires it.
Direct language to a senior buyer lands like fresh air in a room full of rehearsed pitches.
The rep who speaks plainly at the executive level is always the rep they remember.

Match their communication style before they ask you to.

Tywin Lannister never debated conclusions he had already reached.
He moved the conversation to execution and let others catch up.

This phrase assumes the strategic correctness of your solution and pivots to organisational readiness. The buyer stops evaluating the product and starts evaluating their own capacity to move. That is a completely different conversation and one that closes at a completely different speed.

5. "I have done this enough times to know what happens when organisations at your stage wait on this."

Experience delivered without arrogance is the most credible thing available on an executive call. Tywin Lannister never needed to prove his track record. The room already felt it.

This phrase invokes pattern recognition without naming names or breaking confidence. The executive hears someone who has seen this situation resolve in both directions and is choosing which direction it usually takes.

Let your experience speak before your solution does. Executives buy track records, not products.

6. "I am not asking you to trust the idea. I am asking you to trust the evidence behind it."

Tywin Lannister never asked for faith. He presented facts and let them do the work.
Senior buyers do not buy on enthusiasm. They buy on proof.

This phrase removes the subjective entirely and puts the entire close on objective ground.
The executive stops weighing their feelings about you and starts weighing the evidence you brought. That shift is where the close becomes inevitable for every buyer who operates on data.

Bring the evidence. Remove the opinion. Close on what cannot be argued with.

7. "I think we have covered everything that needed covering. Here is what I propose we do next."

Tywin Lannister ended every meeting with a decision already made.
He never left a room open, never allowed ambiguity to survive the final minute.

This phrase closes the conversation with the quiet authority of someone who always knew how this would end.
No asking. No checking. Just a calm, certain proposal for the next step.
The executive who has been in a well-run meeting recognises the natural conclusion it is.

End every meeting with a direction. Ambiguity is where deals go to disappear.
