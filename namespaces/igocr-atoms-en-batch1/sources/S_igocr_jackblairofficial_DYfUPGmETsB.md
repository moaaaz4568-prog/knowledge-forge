1. "You'll have to do better than that."

His documented "flinch" response to any first offer.

Never accept the first offer. Always flinch. Even good first offers can usually be improved. The other side expects pushback and has built in margin.

Silence after the flinch closes the gap.
<!-- image 2 -->

2. “I’ll need to think it over.”
Documented buying-side stall (recognize
when prospects use it on you).
When you hear this from a prospect,
never let them go without setting a
specific next time and decision. “When
specifically can I expect your answer?”
Pin the calendar before they hang up.

@jackblairofficial

3. "I'd need to check with my partner."

Documented higher-authority tactic.

Always leave yourself an out by referencing an absent decision-maker. Removes pressure to commit on the spot. Gives you time to negotiate.

Even when you ARE the decision-maker.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
4. "Always ask for more than you expect."
Documented anchor principle.
Your opening number anchors the entire negotiation. Ask for more than you'd accept. Then concede slowly. You end up where you wanted to be.
High anchors close at higher numbers.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
5. "Never give without getting."
Documented concession rule.
Every concession you make must be tied
to a concession from them. "If I can do
that, what can you do for me?" Otherwise
you train them to keep asking.
Trade. Never give.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Let's set that aside and come back to it."

Documented set-aside tactic.

When you hit a sticking point, park it. Move to easier topics. Build agreement momentum. Return to the hard point when you're both invested in closing.

Easy yeses make hard yeses possible.

7. "If I can do X, can you do Y?"
Documented conditional trade close.
Frames every concession as conditional on theirs. Both sides give and both sides get. The deal closes because both feel they won.
Trade closes outperform compromise closes.
