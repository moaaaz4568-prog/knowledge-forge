1. "How are things going for you in that area right now?"

Tracy taught that the top closers ask questions while everyone else pitches.

He spent years failing at cold calls until he learned to open by understanding the buyer's world first. The questions, not the pitch, are what surface the real problem you can solve.

Diagnose before you prescribe.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "Just so I understand, what would you want this to do for you?"

Tracy built his whole method on knowing exactly what the buyer is really after.

Most reps sell features at a person who never told them what they wanted. He slows down and gets the buyer to name the outcome they are after, then sells only that.

Find the result they want, then sell that and nothing else.

3. "Does this make sense so far?"

Tracy taught checking for agreement all
the way through, not just at the end.

Every small yes along the way builds
momentum, and it surfaces hesitation
early while it is still cheap to handle. By
the close there are no surprises left.

Earn a hundred small yeses before you ask
for the big one.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Let's set the price aside for a second and make sure this is right for you."

Tracy refused to talk price before the buyer saw the value.

Quote a number too early and everything becomes about cost. Build the worth first and the price stops being the point. Value has to outweigh price in their mind before they ever say yes.

Never name the price until the value is already bigger.

Jack Blair

5. "Why don't you try it and see for yourself?"
Tracy taught that experience sells harder than any argument.
Once a buyer holds it, uses it, or pictures it as already theirs, giving it back feels like a loss. Let them feel ownership before they have paid for it.
Let them taste it and the close makes itself.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
6. "If we could make the investment work, is this what you want?"
Tracy taught isolating the real objection before answering anything.
This question separates a true no from a simple money concern, so you never argue with the wrong thing. If the only barrier is price, you now know exactly what to solve.
Pin down the one real objection, then handle just that.

7. "Then the only question left is how soon you want to start."

Tracy closed by assuming the decision was already made.

Once the buyer has agreed all the way down, he did not ask if, he asked when. A calm, assumptive next step feels natural instead of pushy, and most people simply step through it.

Assume the yes and hand them the next step.
