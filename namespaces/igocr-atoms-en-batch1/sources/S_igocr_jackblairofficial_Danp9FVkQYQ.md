1. "If you can find a better deal, take it. I mean that."

Lee Iacocca's trademark line was "If you can find a better car, buy it," and he said it on national TV about a company fresh off a government rescue. The dirty works because confidence is contagious and hedging is too. When you invite the comparison out loud, the buyer stops running it in secret.

Confidence dares the comparison. Doubt forbids it.

2. "Here's what I've got on the line with you."

While asking a country for sacrifice, Iacocca cut his own salary to a dollar a year. The number did more selling than any speech. Show your buyer your actual stake: you're commission only, your name goes on the rollout, your cell number is in the contract. Skin talks.

Nobody believes the pitch of a man with nothing at risk.

3. "Here's the choice as it actually is. I wish it were prettier."

The Chrysler rescue ran on brutal plain terms: pay cut, rescue, plant closures, or no company and no jobs at all. People accepted the hard version because it was the whole and true. When your deal has a real tradeoff, say it complete. Buyers forgive hard truths. They never forgive ones discovered later.

A hard truth stated whole beats a soft one discovered later.

4. "Start at the base. Build it your way."
Iacocca's Mustang launched at an
attainable base price, and buyers optioned

expensive. Nobody felt sold. Quote your
entry point clean, then hand over the
menu. An upgrade the buyer chooses
lands as their taste, not your tactic.
People resist being upsold. They love
upgrading themselves.

5. "Saying no to me costs more than saying yes. Look at the math."

To Congress, Iacocca didn't sell sympathy. He sold arithmetics: the cost of Chrysler dying, in jobs and unemployment checks, against a loan guarantee. He won, and the loans came back seven years early with interest. Price your buyer's no in their own numbers and let the sheet argue.

Every no has a price tag. Make them read it.

6. "I'll cut first. Then we talk about your side."

The sacrifice at Chrysler was sequenced: his dollar salary and executive cuts came before he asked the unions and suppliers for theirs. Concessions granted first buy the standing to request them. On your deals, make your give visible and real, then ask for the match by name.

The first concession buys the right to ask for the second.
