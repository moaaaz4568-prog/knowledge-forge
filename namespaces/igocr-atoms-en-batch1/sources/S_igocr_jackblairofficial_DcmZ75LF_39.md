1. "WHAT PART OF THIS FEELS UNCLEAR?"
Voss's whole thing is calibrated questions. Not "why" questions. Never why. Why puts people on the defensive.
"What" and "how" questions make the buyer feel like they're in control while you're actually steering. They have to think to answer this. And when they think out loud you find out what's actually blocking the deal.

2. "IS THIS A PRIORITY RIGHT NOW OR JUST INTERESTING?"
Most reps are terrified to ask this. Voss calls it tactical empathy. You're showing the buyer you respect their time enough to be honest with them.
Real buyers respond immediately. They appreciate the directness. And if they ghost it, you just saved yourself three weeks of pointless follow up.

3. "WHAT WOULD NEED TO HAPPEN FOR THIS TO BE A NO-BRAINER?"

Voss says the reasons someone won't do a deal are almost always more powerful than the reasons they will. So stop guessing and make them hand you the real blocker.

Whatever they say next is the actual objection. Not the one they gave you before. The one underneath it. Now you're solving a problem instead of pitching a product.

4. "HOW DOES STAYING WHERE YOU ARE RIGHT NOW WORK OUT FOR YOU?"

This is Voss's accusation audit in reverse. You're not defending your offer. You're not touching price at all. You're making them defend the alternative out loud to themselves.

Most buyers have genuinely never been asked to do this. The silence after this question is where deals get reopened.

6. MIRROR THEM.
This one isn't a question. It's even simpler. Repeat
the last three words they said. Just that. Nothing
else. Voss used this in hostage negotiations.
The other person feels heard and keeps talking. And
the more a buyer talks the more they convince
themselves. You're not closing them. They're closing
themselves.

7. "IT SEEMS LIKE YOU'RE NOT QUITE THERE YET."
This is labeling. Voss's most underrated tool. You name the emotion you're sensing. Calmly. Without accusation.
It sounds almost too simple but it does achieve on a stalled deal. It makes the buyer feel understood. And people move forward with people who get them. Not people who push them.
