1. "You never open your mouth till you know what the shot is."

Roma's actual rule, word for word, and he says it in fury after the office manager blurts one helpful-sounding fact to his client, guesses wrong, and detonates the biggest deal on the board. One sentence, spoken blind, costs six thousand 1992 dollars and a Cadillac. Every call has a shot: who this person is, what they're afraid of, what tonight is really about. Find it before you speak. Speaking first is guessing out loud.

Talking before reading is how deals die.

2. "Forget business for a second. What do you actually want out of the next five years?"

The famous restaurant scene runs long, and almost none of it is about real estate. Roma talks life, regret, fear, what a man's allowed to want. The lonely stranger across the table leans in, opens up, starts confessing. The property appears only at the very end, almost as an afterthought. Meanwhile, across town, the always-be-closing guy is screaming at salesmen. He closes nothing in the entire film.

The best pitch doesn't sound like one.

3. "Let me show you something. Might be nothing for you. Might be exactly the thing."

When Roma finally reveals what he's selling, he hands it over like a curiosity, not a quota. No urgency. No countdown. The buyer gets to discover it instead of being charged by it, and a discovery is something people defend. A pitch is something they resist.

Offer it like a curiosity, not a quota.

4. "We're fine either way. This only makes sense if it makes sense for you."

Here's Mamet's buried joke. Blake, the ABC guy, appears in one scene, dangles the Cadillac and the steak knives and drives off. Roma, who isn't even in the room for the speech, tops the board doing the exact opposite of it. Pressure telegraphs need, and need is the one smell no buyer has ever paid for.

Always-be-closing is how you never do.

5. "Sounds like the money isn't the real question here, is it?"

Roma doesn't drag his buyer up to salesman energy. He drops down into the man's register first, the doubt, the smallness, the bad day, sits it beside him, and only then starts to lift. A buyer who feels met will follow you anywhere. A buyer who feels performed at follows you nowhere.

Match their weather before you change it.

6. "When's the last time you decided something just because you wanted it?"

What Roma is really selling at that table isn't land. It's the feeling of being a man who acts. The signature on the contract is just the receipt for it. Your buyer is the same. Underneath the feature questions, they're buying who they get to be after they say yes. Sell that person.

They're not buying the thing. They're buying themselves.

7. "If it's not this, no harm. But it sounded tonight like this mattered to you."

Roma's close doesn't announce itself. The yes arrives as the natural last sentence of a conversation the buyer never wanted to end. No transition into pitch mode, no shift in voice, no moment where the trap becomes visible, because there is no trap. There's a conversation, and at the end of it, a decision.

The smoothest close feels like the conversation ending.
