1. "BEFORE I EXPLAIN ANYTHING, LET ME SHOW YOU THE WHOLE THING WORKING."
On a Chicago dime store floor, a teenage Popeil ran a full demonstration every six minutes, no lunch, no bathroom break. No speech about the chopper. The chopper, choppers its job, never for a man describing one.
Open with the finished result live on screen. Narrate what they're watching, not what they should believe. Explanations come after the thing has already moved.

itself if you give it the floor.

4. "YOU CAN HAVE THE PRICE RIGHT NOW IF YOU WANT IT. I'D WAIT."
Popeil held the number until the bird was carved. Not a trick, a sequence. A price named early gets judged against nothing. A price named late gets judged against everything they just watched work. 5. "BEFORE YOU ANSWER, THERE'S ONE MORE
THING YOU SHOULD KNOW ABOUT."
Popeil's pitches were more a
household phrase, and the add never led. It landed
after the offer stood whole, so each extra fell on a
decision already tilting his way.
Hold one real extra back on every call. A template, a
review session, an onboarding seat. Drop it after the
price, never bundled in front of it.
A bonus before the price is just a feature.

7. "IF IT'S A NO, GIVE IT TO ME TODAY. A MAYBE COSTS US BOTH."
Call now was never decoration. Popeil asked for the decision while the food still steamed, because a red is a demo dying. In 2000 he moved a million dollars of rotisseries in one live QVC hour, every yes landing inside the demonstration.
Ask while the proof is still on screen. Say the no out loud and mean it. Never bet the close on them remembering how this felt.
