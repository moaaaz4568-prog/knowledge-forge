1. "Before we wrap, there's one thing I found that I'm saving for Thursday."

Bluma Zeigarnik proved the brain holds unfinished business twice as tightly as finished business. A call that ends complete gets filed and forgotten by dinner. End every call with one thing deliberately unsaid and you stay open in their head all week.

A finished conversation gets filed. An unfinished one gets remembered.

2. "I ran your numbers. Half of them surprised me. I'll bring the rest next call."

The waiters didn't try to remember the open orders. The open state did it for them. Deliver your analysis at 80% and the missing 20% becomes a splinter in the buyer's mind that only your next call removes.

Never deliver everything. Deliver everything but one thing.

3. "Let's not touch price today. That number deserves its own conversation."

An unheard number is the loudest open loop there is. Reps rush to quote and close the loop for free. Sequence it. The buyer who's still waiting on your price is a buyer who cannot mark you done.

The number they haven't heard is the loudest thing in the room.

A buyer who owes you an answer can't ghost you.

Her follow-up finding: people interrupted mid-task fought to resume it. So never let a call complete, let it pause. A conversation with a hard next date isn't finished enough for the brain to throw away.
