1. “Enter the conversation already happening in the reader’s head.”

Halbert’s core rule of persuasion.

The prospect is already thinking about something before you ever show up. Halbert’s move was to join that conversation instead of starting a cold one. You meet their mind where it already is.

Walk in mid-thought and they feel understood before you’ve pitched a thing.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. “The only advantage you need is a starving crowd.”

Halbert once asked a room what edge they'd want if they opened a hamburger stand.

People shouted out the best meat, the best location, the lowest price. Halbert said he would give all of it up for one thing: a starving crowd. Demand beats every other advantage.

Pick a hungry market and the selling is half done before you speak.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "To figure out what people will buy, look at what they do buy."

Halbert's rule for reading a market.

He never guessed what people wanted. He looked at what they had already spent money on. Past purchases are the only honest signal you get. Everything else is a survey nobody answers straight.

The market already told you. Go read the receipts.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. “Sell people what they already want.”

Halbert refused to talk anyone into desire.

You cannot manufacture a want from nothing, and trying is the hardest job in sales. Halbert found a want that was already burning in the market and connected his offer straight to it.

Stop building desire. Go find it and point your offer at it.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. “Get the reader to read the first sentence.”

Halbert on momentum. Every sentence has one job: earn the next one.

The headline sells the first line. The first line sells the second. All the way down the page. He built every sentence to do one thing only, pull the reader into to do one after it.

Win one sentence at a time and they are still with you at the price.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. “Specifics sell. Generalities don’t.”

Halbert on proof.

Not “you will save money.” The exact number, the exact day, the exact result. A vague claim sounds like a sales pitch. A precise one sounds like a fact, and people buy facts.

Exact numbers get believed. Round ones get skipped.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

7. "Motion beats meditation."

Halbert's advice to his son, written from a prison cell.

He believed results came from action, not from endless planning. Start before you feel ready and fix it as you go. The rep waiting to feel prepared is just a rep waiting.

Ready is a feeling that arrives after you start, not before
