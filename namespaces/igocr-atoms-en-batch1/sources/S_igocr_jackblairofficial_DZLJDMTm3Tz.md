Action beats hesitation every time.

2. "Say yes, then learn how to do it later."

This is Branson's real rule for opportunity, how he leapt into records, then airlines, with no experience either time.

He never let "I'm not ready" cost him the chance. He committed first and worked out the how on the way up. Say yes to the bigger client, the harder deal, the new offer, and grow into it. Readiness comes after the leap, not before.

Take it first, master it after.

3. "Opportunities are like buses. There's always another one coming."

Branson's reminder, from a career of chasing the next idea, never to cling to a single one.

He never got desperate over one deal, because he knew another was always around the corner. That calm is leverage. When you stop needing one particular sale, you negotiate from strength and walk away clean when it is wrong.

Need it less and you close more of them.

4. "Set realistic expectations, then beat them in ways they don't expect."

Branson built Virgin's whole reputation on surprising and delighting customers across every business he touched.

He promised honestly and then over-delivered, so customers felt they got more than they paid for. Under-promise, over-deliver, and people sell you to everyone they know. The pleasant surprise turns a buyer into a fan and a referral.

Beat the expectation and they bring you the next sale.

5. "Look after your people and they'll look after your business."

Branson is famous for putting staff and customers first, convinced that happy people drive everything else.

He treated the relationship as the real asset, never the single transaction. Put the person ahead of the sale and their own. People feel whether you actually care, and they buy from, stay with, and refer the ones who do.

Care for the person and the business takes care of itself.

6. "You don't learn to walk by following rules. You learn by doing and falling over."

Branson, dyslexic and out of school at 16, built everything by doing, not by being taught.

He never waited to be trained. He got in, made mistakes, and learned faster than the cautious ever could. In sales, the reps on real calls beat the ones still memorising the script. Get in, get it wrong, and get good by doing, not by waiting.

You get good by doing, not by waiting.

7. "Fun is the most underrated ingredient in any success."

Branson built the entire Virgin brand on enjoying the work, certain that energy is contagious.

He knew people are drawn to someone who clearly enjoys what they do. A buyer would rather deal with the person who brings real energy than the one reading a flat script. Bring genuine enjoyment to the call and people want to be around you, and buy from you.

Enjoy it and they want in.
