PATRICK JANE SOLVED EVERY CASE BY SEEING WHAT EVERYONE ELSE IN THE ROOM WALKED PAST. THESE ARE THE 7 PHRASES HE USED TO CLOSE EVERY BUYER BEFORE THEY KNEW WHAT HAPPENED:

1. "I notice you paused on that point. Tell me more about what is sitting with you there."

Patrick Jane never let a significant moment pass without examining it.
The pause, the slight shift in tone, the answer that was just a little too quick, all of it was data.

This phrase tells the buyer you are paying attention at a level they did not expect.
They feel seen in a way that creates an almost involuntary urge to open up further.
The real objection, the one they were not planning to share, most always hiding in the pause.

Notice what others skip. The close is almost always hiding in the pause.

2. "Based on the way you described that, I think there is something specific bothering you about this. Am I close?"

Jane never asked broad questions when a precise observation would do the work faster.
This phrase demonstrates that you have been listening at a clinical level the entire call.
The buyer who hears an accurate read of their own thinking feels something shift immediately.
They stop protecting their position because the position has already been identified.
Now you are not guessing at what they need. You are confirming what you already observed.
Read the buyer out loud. Accurate observations close faster than any argument.

3. "You said yes but something in how you said it told me you are not quite there yet. What is the real hesitation?"
Patrick Jane trusted what he observed over what he was told. Most reps trust what they are told and miss everything they are being shown.
This phrase gives you permission to look past the stated answer and into the real one.
Buyers who say yes almost always confirm the observation when it is made with confidence. The yes you close on must be real. This phrase makes sure it is.
Never close on a yes that does not sound like one. Find the real answer first.

4. "I think you already know what you want to do. You are just looking for permission to do it."

Jane always saw the decision before the decision-maker did.
This phrase reflects that back to a buyer who has been circling the yes for longer than they realise.

It is not pressure. It is a mirror held up with complete calm and total confidence.
The buyer who hears this and recognises it as true stops deliberating and starts deciding.
You did not push them toward yes. You showed them they were already there.

Show the buyer where they already are. The close is recognition, not persuasion.

5. "You have mentioned [detail] three times now. That tells me it is the thing that matters most. Am I right?"

Jane catalogued everything and used specific details to demonstrate a quality of attention that felt almost supernatural.
This phrase does the same thing with information the buyer handed you voluntarily.

Reflecting a pattern back to someone signals that you were present in a way that almost no one ever is. The buyer stops and realises they gave themselves away and that you caught it without making them feel exposed. Now they trust you completely because you paid attention when everyone else would have moved on.

Track the repetitions. They are always the map to the close.

6. "I am going to make a suggestion and I want you to tell me if it lands correctly."

Patrick Jane never forced a conclusion. He offered one with the calm confidence of someone who already knew it was right.
This phrase signals precision without arrogance and invites the buyer to confirm rather than comply.
The distinction matters. A buyer who confirms your read feels smart.
A buyer who complies with your pitch feels sold to.
One of those buyers refers you to three more. The other one has buyer's remorse by Thursday.
Offer the insight. Let them confirm it. The close that follows is always stickier.

7. "Everything you have told me in this conversation points to the same answer. I think you already see it too."

This is Jane at his most precise and most devastating.
It connects every thread of the conversation into a single inevitable conclusion without a single moment of pressure.

The buyer reviews the call in their mind and realises you are right.
They did say all of those things. The answer was always the same.
You did not close them. You helped them see what the conversation had already decided.

Connect the dots out loud. The buyer who sees the pattern closes themselves every time.
