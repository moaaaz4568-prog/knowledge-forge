1. "I get why you'd feel that way. Walk me through it."

George Thompson built Verbal Judo on one law: empathy absorbs tension. He learned it on streets where the wrong sentence got people hurt.

When the buyer heats up on price, don't defend. Absorb first. A person who feels heard stops swinging.

Absorb the heat first. Then steer.

2. "Let me make sure I've got this right."

Thompson called paraphrasing the most powerful move in language. Their meaning, your mouth. Nobody can ignore their own words coming back at them.

Feed the objection back better than they said it. They correct you or confirm you. Either way, you now hold the real one.

Their words in your mouth make them listen.

3. "Let me tell you why I'm asking."
Thompson found people resist naked demands and cooperate with explained ones. Context first, question second.
Drop it before the budget question.
Before the decision-maker question. The why lowers the guard the question would have raised.
Give the why and the guard drops.

4. "Here's what I can do for you right now."

Thompson banned the phrases that spike resistance. "There's nothing I can do" starts a fight every time.

So never lead with the wall. Can't discount? Skip that sentence. Open with what's possible and the conversation stays alive.

Never sell the wall. Sell the door.

5. "There are two good ways to go here. Which one fits you?"

Options over ultimatums. Thompson proved people fight commands and defend choices, even under a streetlight at 2am.

Give two honest paths that both move forward. The buyer keeps control. The deal keeps moving. Everyone wins the same decision.

Commands get fought. Choices get kept.

7. "Is there anything I can say that would make this a yes today?"
Thompson asked one final question before he acted, because it forces the truth to the table. One clean chance.
Ask it when the call stalls. They either hand you the real objection or set you free. Both beat another week of maybe.
One clean question ends the maybe.
