ART SOBCZAK SPENT 40 YEARS PROVING YOU CAN PROSPECT BY PHONE WITHOUT FEAR OR REJECTION, AND TAUGHT THOUSANDS OF REPS THE WORDS THAT GET A YES. HERE ARE 7 OF HIS PHRASES:
<!-- image 1 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

1. "Open with them, not with you."

Sobczak's whole Smart Calling method starts before the pitch.

A dumb call opens with who you are and what you sell, which triggers an instant brush-off, while a smart call opens with something real about the prospect's own situation. Relevance buys the next sentence.

Lead with what you know about them, and earn the right to keep talking.
<!-- image 2 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

2. "It's a quality game, not a numbers game."

Sobczak spent forty years killing the myth that you just dial more.

Doing the wrong thing more often only gets you better at being rejected, so he traded volume for research and made fewer, sharper calls that actually connected. Aim before you fire.

Do the homework on a few right prospects instead of blasting a list.
<!-- image 3 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

3. "Never be rejected."

Sobczak refused to let a no count as rejection.

He set a secondary objective for every call, a win he could always get, so even a no left him with a referral or an open door instead of a wound. You decide what the call meant.

Define a win you control on every call, and rejection has nowhere to land.
<!-- image 4 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

4. "Answer the reason, not the symptom."

Sobczak hated canned objection rebuttals.

When a prospect pushes back, the words are just the symptom, so firing a memorized comeback misses what they actually meant, while one calm question uncovers the real concern. Diagnose before you respond.

Ask what's behind the objection instead of swinging at the surface.
<!-- image 5 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

5. "The important half of the question is the listening."

Sobczak taught that smart questions are worthless if you talk over the answer.

Most reps ask, then start loading their next line instead of hearing the gold the prospect just handed them. The answer is where the sale is.

Ask, then go quiet and actually listen to what comes back.
<!-- image 6 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial

6. "Here's what others in your position have gotten from this."

Sobczak replaced vague benefit-claims with a concrete possible value.

Instead of saying he could help, he named a specific result similar customers had gotten, which gives the prospect a reason to stay that a generic pitch never does. Specific outcomes create interest.

Lead with a concrete result like theirs, not a promise about your product.
<!-- image 7 -->
Jack Blair
@jackblairofficial
7. "Don't ask for the next step. Recommend it."
Sobczak closed by leading, not by hoping.
A weak rep asks what the prospect wants to do next and gets a stall, while a pro recommends the obvious next action with confidence, which is far easier to agree to. Certainty is contagious.
Tell them the logical next step instead of leaving it open.
