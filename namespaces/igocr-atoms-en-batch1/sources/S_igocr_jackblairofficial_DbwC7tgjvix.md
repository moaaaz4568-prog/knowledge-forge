TELL ME EVERYTHING, EVEN THE PARTS THAT SEEM IRRELEVANT.
Two psychologists rebuilt the police interview and found witnesses were holding detail nobody asked for. Their first rule: report everything. No filtering for relevance. Partial memories connect into whole ones.
Your buyer edits themselves the same way. They give you the version that sounds reasonable. This line removes the filter, and the real objection is always in the part they cut.
The deal-killer lives in the part they thought didn’t matter.

TAKE ME BACK. WHAT WAS GOING ON WHEN
THIS BECAME A PROBLEM?
Context reinstatement. Put a witness mentally back
at the scene, the sights and sounds and what they
felt, and recall jumps. Memory is filed by moment,
not by topic.
Stop asking what their problem is. Ask when it
became one. The story of the moment carries the
stakeholder and the urgency inside it,
unprompted.
Nobody remembers a pain point. Everybody
remembers a bad day.

WALK ME THROUGH IT BACKWARDS. WHAT HAPPENED RIGHT BEFORE THAT?
Reverse-order recall. Telling events backwards breaks the rehearsed script, and detail version skipped falls out. Police use it because a practiced story runs on rails.
Your buyer has a rehearsed story too: we're happy with our current setup. Run the timeline backwards from today and somewhere it stops being smooth. That's the real starting point.
A story that only runs forward has been rehearsed.

SILENCE. COUNT THREE BEFORE YOU ANSWER ANYTHING.
The enhanced interview's hardest rule isn't a question. Interviewers were trained to stop interrupting, because every interruption cuts off retrieval mid-reach and the detail is gone for good.
The average rep answers a buyer's pause like it's an objection. The pause IS the retrieval. Three full seconds. What comes after them is the sentence they weren't going to say.
Every interruption costs you the sentence that mattered.

PROBLEM?
Change of perspective. Witnesses asked to describe
the scene from another vantage point surface details
their own angle never held.

I'VE BEEN ASKING THE WRONG QUESTION. ASK IT BETTER THAN ME.
The whole method runs on one inversion: transfer control. The interviewer stops directing and the witness leads, because the person with the memory, not the person with the questions, knows where the information is.
When a call stalls, hand it over. What they ask you for next is the thing they actually need to hear before they buy.
The person with the answers should be running the call.
