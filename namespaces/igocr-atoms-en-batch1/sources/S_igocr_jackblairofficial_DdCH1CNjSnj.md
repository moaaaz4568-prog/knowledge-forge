1. "I'M NOT SELLING A COMPUTER. I'M SELLING TUESDAY'S PAYROLL, DONE."
The machine was the same machine every IBM rep carried. Perot sold what came out of it: the report on the desk, the payroll run on time, the month closed early.
Name the finished, not the feature list.
Buyers do not buy the machine. They budget for problems that stop being theirs.
Nobody buys a machine. They buy what that Monday looks like after it.

6. "ONE PRICE, FIVE YEARS, IN WRITING. NOW STOP WORRYING ABOUT IT."
EDS signed customers to long five year contracts at a fixed price while the industry billed by the hour. He sold certainty into a market that ran on surprises, and companies lined up to stop being surprised.
Price the peace of mind, not just the work. A buyer who fears the meter will pay a premium to shut it off. Fix in writing what your rivals leave vague.
Certainty is a product. Most reps forget to sell it.
