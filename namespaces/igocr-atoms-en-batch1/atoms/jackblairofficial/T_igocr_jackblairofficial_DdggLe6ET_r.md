# T_DdggLe6ET_r_02

"A problem question gets what Rackham called an implied need: the buyer admits something is wrong. In small sales that admission was enough to win. In big ones it wasn't. The buyer had a problem and still didn't buy. Get the problem in their words and then do the hard part: don't fix it yet. Solutions offered this early are where the research found objections get made." — sources/S_igocr_jackblairofficial_DdggLe6ET_r.md

# T_DdggLe6ET_r_03

"Implication questions. The hardest to ask, and the ones that separated the top performers in large sales. They take a small admission and make the buyer see its size. Follow the problem downstream: time, people, money, the meeting they had to sit through because of it. Let them do the counting. Your job is the next question, not the total." — sources/S_igocr_jackblairofficial_DdggLe6ET_r.md
