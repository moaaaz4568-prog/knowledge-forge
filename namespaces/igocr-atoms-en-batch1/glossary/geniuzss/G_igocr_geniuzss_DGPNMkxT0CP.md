# G_igocr_geniuzss_DGPNMkxT0CP — Spaced Repetition

## Term

"Spaced Repetition" appears twice in this source, as method 4 of five study methods in "How to Study Like a Harvard Student in 7 Days". Cramming fails because the brain forgets information quickly; spaced repetition reinforces learning by revisiting information at strategic intervals, helping you retain it long-term. The action step is to review notes after 1 day, 1 week, and 1 month to strengthen memory, and to use flashcards (Anki, Quizlet) for challenging topics. The words most often collocated with it are: memory, review, flashcards, intervals.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as cramming spread out: cramming fails because the brain forgets quickly — spaced repetition revisits at strategic intervals (1 day, 1 week, 1 month), it is not the same material crammed in longer sessions.
- Read as a replacement for active recall: the carousel lists five separate methods — spaced repetition is method 4, while active recall (quiz yourself after reading) is method 1, and they work together rather than one replacing the other.
