# G_ig_10feetstudios_C7ZbOe_Nnot — going all in

## Term

Going all in is the speaker's name for committing fully to the agency path: the downside of trying and failing a few times is much lower than the upside of taking the risk. The phrase appears once, in "actually going all in into something like this", beside "risk" and "upsides". His argument is that building an agency is a "baby business" with little real risk if you follow steps that work, and he backs it with his own past — delivering food on a bike for 400 bucks a month two years ago, now able to make that amount in a single hour. The fear he dismisses is the worst case: try, fail, try again until you make it.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "the upsides of taking the risk and actually going all in into something like this" — sources/S_ig_10feetstudios_C7ZbOe_Nnot.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as risk-free: he calls the downside much lower than the upside, not zero — failing a couple of times is part of the picture.
- Read as "anyone can do it because he did": his "I'm really not that special" is encouragement, not evidence that the path works without the steps he says to follow.
