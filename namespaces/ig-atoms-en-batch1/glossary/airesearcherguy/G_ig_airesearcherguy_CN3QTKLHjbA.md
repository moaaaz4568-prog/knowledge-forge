# G_ig_airesearcherguy_CN3QTKLHjbA — expectation

## Term

An expectation is what marketing sets before the customer ever tastes the product, and the speaker's claim is that this expectation can actually improve the experience itself. The word appears once, beside the phrase so good it will practically sell itself, blind tasting, and the same wine. His evidence is a neuroscience finding: people who blind tasted the exact same wine genuinely enjoyed it more when they thought it cost more. Marketing therefore does more than convince people to buy — it gives the seller a chance to set the expectation that shapes how good the product feels.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "It gives you an opportunity to set an expectation and actually improve the experience that a customer has with your product." — sources/S_ig_airesearcherguy_CN3QTKLHjbA.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as proof that expensive products taste better: the wine was the exact same in every glass, and only the believed price changed how much people enjoyed it.
- Read as a reason to skip marketing for quality products: even a high-quality product benefits, because the expectation marketing sets is part of the experience.
