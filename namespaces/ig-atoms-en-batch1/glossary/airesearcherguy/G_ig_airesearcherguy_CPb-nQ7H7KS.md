# G_ig_airesearcherguy_CPb-nQ7H7KS — third-party sellers

## Term

Third-party sellers are the speaker's evidence against one survey stat: their rapid growth, without physical stores, proves that stat wrong. The phrase appears once, beside delivery times, costs, and convenience of returning the products. The survey's telling points, in his reading, are the first and third ones: delivery times and costs, and how convenient returning a product is. His concrete takeaway is that an online store grows by solving those issues — fast, cheap delivery and easy returns — rather than by arguing with the survey.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The rapid growth of third-party sellers that are not represented in physical stores on Amazon proves that one to be wrong." — sources/S_ig_airesearcherguy_CPb-nQ7H7KS.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a claim that the whole survey is worthless: he only calls BS on the second stat, and says the first and third points are really telling.
- Read as proof that physical stores are dead: his point is that sellers without stores are growing on marketplaces, and the growth is the evidence about the one stat, not a verdict on stores.
