# G_ig_airesearcherguy_CPYrmXfHdUY — options

## Term

Options are what the speaker says a business owner should control: give customers a choice between three or four, and they end up with enough for every important room. The word appears once, beside choice, smoke detectors, and prospective customers, in a story about free smoke detectors in Britain. His lesson is that people asked how many they want will say one, because humans do not know what is best for them, so the limited set of options does the deciding for them. The concrete outcome is a decision good for both sides: enough detectors in every room, and a customer who chose without stalling.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "The lesson for business owners is when you control the options that your prospective customers have, oftentimes you can lead them to make a decision that's good for both you and them." — sources/S_ig_airesearcherguy_CPYrmXfHdUY.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a license to manipulate customers: the speaker's example ends in a decision good for both sides, and the options are limited to a sensible range, not rigged toward a bad buy.
- Read as a claim that customers want fewer choices in general: his point is about a number they cannot judge for themselves, and the three-or-four range is what makes the choice workable.
