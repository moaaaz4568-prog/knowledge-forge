# G_ig_airesearcherguy_CPbbMa3HgeG — brick and mortar

## Term

Brick and mortar is the physical store whose future the speaker is rethinking after the pandemic pushed boring shopping online. The phrase appears twice, beside in-person shopping, which appears three times, the experience, and socializing. His claim is that in-person shopping is now reserved more for special things, so stores like Lululemon add yoga classes and a restaurant, and Dick's adds a rock wall and a golf simulator. His advice to smaller owners is cheaper than that: take away the pain of shopping, which is the checkout process, and add to its most enjoyable aspect, which is socializing.

> "Even smaller brick and mortar owners can be mindful of this, but that means they need to really start thinking about the experience." — sources/S_ig_airesearcherguy_CPbbMa3HgeG.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a prediction that physical stores are dying: he says in-person shopping is not going anywhere, and the change is what it is for, not whether it survives.
- Read as advice that only big stores can follow: he explicitly says smaller owners can do it too, by fixing checkout and adding socializing rather than buying space or technology.
