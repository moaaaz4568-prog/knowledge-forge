# G_ig_airesearcherguy_CN5v742HfoV — survey

## Term

A survey is the speaker's way to gain valuable insights from customers, with a warning attached: most of the time self-reported survey data is not very reliable. The word appears three times, beside subconscious forces, brain scanning technology, and heat maps. His first rule is to ask what people have done, not what they think they would do — how many products they bought from Amazon this month, and how many they already reviewed. His second rule is to ask what they think somebody else would do, because people judge other people's actions more accurately than their own.

## Synonyms

- surveys (the plural form he uses for the valuable surveys a business can still conduct)

## Live Example

> "It's a great way to gain valuable insights, except that most of the time self-reported survey data is not very reliable." — sources/S_ig_airesearcherguy_CN5v742HfoV.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a dismissal of surveys: he says valuable surveys are still possible, and the fix is asking the right questions, not abandoning them.
- Read as a claim that customers lie: his reason is the human condition — we underestimate subconscious forces — not dishonesty in the answers.
