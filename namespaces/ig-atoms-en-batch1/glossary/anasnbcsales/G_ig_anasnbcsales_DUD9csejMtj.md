# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DUD9csejMtj — pay with your time

## Term

To "pay with your time" is the speaker's price for learning sales the slow way — free YouTube courses and trial and error over years — set against paying with money for a coach who compresses the journey. The phrase occurs twice in the source: once as the choice ("you're either gonna go pay with your time... or you go get a coach") and once as the warning that closes the video ("Please don't pay with your time"). Its neighbors are "outskill everybody", "in the trenches", "compress the time", and "go get a coach". Concretely, the speaker points at Yash — five or six years "in the trenches", 10 to 16 hour days, sleeping in the office — and argues that skill talk "casually glosses over" that hidden cost, so hiring someone six years in buys months or weeks what would otherwise take half a decade.

## Live Example

> "Please don't pay with your time because that is literally the most expensive valuable resource, even if you don't think so right now." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DUD9csejMtj.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as "time is money" in general: the phrase here is a specific fork — time or money, years or months — and flattening it into a proverb hides the video's actual claim, that a coach's compressed time is the cheaper purchase.
- Read as an attack on free learning: the speaker's target is not free courses but the underestimate of what getting great costs — "they grotesquely underestimate how much work, effort, energy is required" — so the warning is about the price, not about where the material comes from.
