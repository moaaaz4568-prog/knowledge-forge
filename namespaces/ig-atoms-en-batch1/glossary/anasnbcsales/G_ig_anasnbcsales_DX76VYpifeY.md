# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DX76VYpifeY — law of averages

## Term

The "law of averages" is the speaker's very simple rule for cold calling and door-to-door: make enough contacts and someone is always going to say yes. The phrase occurs once in the source, as the name of the rule, and the arithmetic is 70 contacts — "I spoke to 70 roofing businesses" — against the certainty that "no matter what, someone's always gonna say yes". Its neighbors are "1% interest", "70 contacts", "someone's always gonna say yes", and "basic". Concretely, even a basic pitch that earns "no, no, no, f off" from most coaches still leaves someone who doesn't hang up — "Ah, yeah, who are you?" — and that is "1% interest", which is all he needs: "As long as I have 1% interest, I can move the call".

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a guarantee of a yes: the law promises that someone says yes across the volume, not that any particular call closes — treating 70 contacts as a quota that must produce a deal confuses an average with a contract.
- Read as "the pitch doesn't matter": the 1% interest only moves the call because the rep then works it — the law justifies the volume, not a bad pitch, so dialing 70 with nothing to say earns 70 noes and calls it averages.
