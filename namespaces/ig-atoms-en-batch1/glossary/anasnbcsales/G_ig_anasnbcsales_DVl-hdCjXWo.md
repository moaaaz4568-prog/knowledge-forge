# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DVl-hdCjXWo — feedback close

## Term

A "feedback close" is the speaker's move for a deal that didn't close: call the lost lead back as a concerned citizen, apologize, and ask what the salesperson did wrong — and the real objection walks out on its own. The phrase occurs 3 times in the source ("you do the feedback close", "The feedback close has made me so much fucking money", "A feedback close is literally"), and the speaker credits it with almost 30% of his net earnings, through feedback closes and referrals. Its neighbors are "concerned citizen", "throw the sales rep under the bus", "what else?", and "low key". Concretely, the caller plays apologetic — "I know you had a call with so and so... what did Logan do wrong?" — and the lead either trashes Logan (revealing that certainty killed the sale) or defends him (revealing the true objection, like a four-month contract clashing with travel), which the caller then handles.

## Synonyms

- feedback closing (the form the speaker uses for the practice itself: "you have to do feedback closing")

## Live Example

> "When you don't close a deal, flip the lead to someone else. It could be your friend, it doesn't matter. Make sure you do the feedback close." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DVl-hdCjXWo.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a complaint call: the caller is not gathering a review but staging a confession — the apology and the "what did Logan do wrong?" are the bait, and treating the call as genuine customer service misses that its product is the objection.
- Read as throwing the rep under the bus: the speaker is explicit that the rep is justified "without destroying them" and the blame is shared ("I want to take full responsibility") — using the call to actually bury the rep turns the side-taking into the betrayal it pretends to be.
