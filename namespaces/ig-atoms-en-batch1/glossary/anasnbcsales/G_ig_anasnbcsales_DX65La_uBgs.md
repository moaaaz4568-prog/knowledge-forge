# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DX65La_uBgs — positioning the offer

## Term

"Positioning the offer" is what the speaker says the saturation complaint is always about: it is not that the niche is saturated, it is that the offer is positioned like everyone else's. The phrase occurs once in this cut — "It's always about positioning the offer" — and the prescription is threefold: "Unique name, unique approach, unique guarantees. That is where the novelty comes". Its neighbors are "sea of sameness", "saturated", "unique name", and "novelty". Concretely, the prospect who says "I've literally heard it all" is not being rude — they have literally heard the same copy from everyone — and the reason a saturation cannot be hit is that "all businesses always need new marketing", so the opening is a repositioned offer, not a new niche.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as renaming the offer: the positioning is name plus approach plus guarantees — a new label on the same pitch is the same sameness, so the phrase demands a different approach, not a different title.
- Read as blaming the prospect: "I've literally heard it all" is treated here as honest feedback, not rudeness — using the positioning line to dismiss the prospect as closed-minded keeps the offer in the sea while faulting the listener.
