# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DSbYf4DDAcQ — wild guess

## Term

"Take a wild guess" is the speaker's cold-reading move inside a sales roleplay with a prospect named Steve: the rep first feeds Steve his own objection — wanting to wait till the new year, when the holidays are over and things are calm — and then challenges him to guess how the rep knew it in advance. The phrase occurs 3 times in the source, always after "take a" and always as an instruction to the rep, never as a genuine request for a random guess. Its neighbors in the transcript are "he gets me", "Steve", and "in advance", and the move works because the prospect feels understood ("oh yeah, he gets me") rather than pitched. Concretely, the rep says the holiday objection back to Steve word for word, then drops "take a wild guess" to turn the recognition into the close.

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "They're almost like, oh yeah, he gets me. And you're gonna say, okay. Steve, you have to take a wild guess." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DSbYf4DDAcQ.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a genuine request for a random guess: here it is a scripted cold read — the rep already knows the answer ("How do you think I knew that in advance?"), and the line only lands because the objection was fed to the prospect first.
- Read as a one-liner that closes by itself: in the transcript it opens the close rather than finishing it — after "take a wild guess" the rep still has to ask whether Steve wants the shortcut now or the struggle later, so the phrase alone closes nothing.
