# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DWkF42OD_Ic — gatekeeper

## Term

A "gatekeeper" is the person who answers a B2B call first, and the speaker's rule is to treat them as the decision maker, not as an obstacle to bypass. The singular occurs once in the source — "The gatekeeper is the decision maker" — and the logic is respect: asking "can I speak to the boss?" disrespects them, "and they have no reason to help you", so "they're always going to say they're busy". Its neighbors are "decision maker", "B2B", "five, six minutes", and "lay down some of the details". Concretely, the rep opens by acknowledging the flood of calls, states the simple offer — help the clinic get more patients and make more money — and asks for five or six minutes to break down a small proposal, either now or at a better time, instead of demanding the doctor.

## Synonyms

- gatekeepers (the plural form the speaker uses for the role in general: "You're going to be calling a lot of gatekeepers")

## Live Example

> "Don't try to bypass them. The gatekeeper is the decision maker and do it as if you're selling them, not, hey, can I speak to the boss?" — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DWkF42OD_Ic.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as "the gatekeeper decides the purchase": the decision maker here means the decider of access — whether you get to speak to the boss — not the buyer of the service, so pitching the close to the gatekeeper mistakes the door for the deal.
- Read as flattery to get past them: the respect is structural — sell them on the help for the clinic, offer five minutes, accept a better time — and buttering them up while still treating them as a hurdle keeps the bypass, just politer.
