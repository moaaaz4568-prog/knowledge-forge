# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DUQS8UWjPL0 — pattern interrupt

## Term

A "pattern interrupt" is an opener so unexpected it drops the prospect's guard and buys the rep a few more seconds before the hang-up. The phrase occurs twice in the source in the singular, and its whole point is freshness: the classic "this is a cold call, do you want to give me 30 seconds" opener "has been beaten to death in so many industries that every other amateur calls" it, so repeated exposure numbs the prospect — "hedonic adaptation", as the speaker calls it. Its neighbors are "hedonic adaptation", "cold call opener", "NBC sales system", and "30 seconds". Concretely, his borrowed example for cold-calling gynecologists without a name — "My third favorite gynecologist of the week, it's an ass man, how the heck you been?" — stops the mind ("wait, ass third, wait, who is that?") and buys "an extra five to 10 seconds that they don't hang up".

## Synonyms

- pattern interrupts (the plural form the speaker uses for fresh ones the market hasn't been overexposed to)

## Live Example

> "Boom, that pattern interrupt, it buys you an extra five to 10 seconds that they don't hang up and you can get to your thing." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DUQS8UWjPL0.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as any surprising opener: the interrupt must be fresh to this market — the speaker warns that a pattern repeated across industries dies by hedonic adaptation, so recycling his gynecologist line everywhere turns the interrupt into the same dead opener.
- Read as a joke to open with: the laugh is not the product — the interrupt's job is the five to ten seconds after it, "right into the sale" — and an opener that amuses without leading to the next line wastes the window it bought.
