# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DWpCTySD3Pj — priority

## Term

A "priority" is the point at which the reality of a prospect's situation settles enough that shifting it becomes a decision, not a someday idea. The word occurs twice in the source — the reality "hasn't settled or became a priority", and the question of when "this becomes a priority to shift the current circumstances" — and the speaker's whole move is to name that moment before it arrives as a crisis. Its neighbors are "flippant", "settled", "tough and scary", and "hundreds of them a week". Concretely, he tells James, as an outsider who talks to hundreds of companies a week, that being in his position "is tough and it's a little bit scary", and asks when the point comes that he chooses to act — "when does that point come when you make the choice and decision" — before the situation reaches "oh shit".

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "It seems like the reality of your situation hasn't settled or became a priority." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DWpCTySD3Pj.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a task to schedule: the priority here is a decision point, not a calendar item — the prospect must choose to shift circumstances, so filing it as a to-do misses that the choice itself is the event being asked for.
- Read as pressure to decide now: the speaker explicitly opens with "You promise me you won't get mad at me" and "I don't say this to be flippant" — the question names the coming point without forcing it, and using it as a hard close turns a warning into the push it refuses to be.
