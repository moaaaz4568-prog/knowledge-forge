# G_ig_anasnbcsales_DWpXbBWDzt6 — involvement

## Term

"Involvement" is the question the speaker says to ask very early in the sale: who supports this person, and what is that person's involvement in the business or the relationship. The word occurs 3 times in the source, and the point is to learn it upfront, in some capacity, before the sale goes anywhere. Its neighbors are "the tail end", "ping them", and "the whole breakdown". Concretely, once the prospect says "I'm marketing their sales, I'm this, I'm that", the smart move is to invite the supporter in at the tail end of the call — "if you want to ping them... maybe you could just come at the tail end so they can kind of hear the whole breakdown and kind of ask their questions" — so the involved person hears the pitch instead of vetoing it second-hand later.

## Live Example

> "Very early on in the sale, you're always going to ask with the business, with your relationship, with your whatever, who supports, what's their involvement." — sources/S_ig_anasnbcsales_DWpXbBWDzt6.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a stakeholder-mapping exercise: the involvement question here is about one supporter and one invitation to the tail end — treating it as a formal org chart turns a quick discovery question into paperwork the sale never needed.
- Read as a reason to delay the call: the invite goes out upfront but the supporter only joins at the tail end — waiting for them before starting lets the sale stall on a person whose role is to hear the breakdown, not to open it.
