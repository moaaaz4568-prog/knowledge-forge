The pandemic has obviously pushed people into being more comfortable buying things online. But could it also be shaping the future of brick and mortar? Here's the thing. As social creatures, in-person shopping is not going anywhere. But as people have become more comfortable with doing their boring shopping online, buying things like toilet paper and groceries, now in-person shopping is reserved more for special things. As such, we see stores like Lululemon opening huge facilities that include yoga classes and a restaurant, or Dick's Sporting Goods, which has a facility with a rock wall and golf simulator. That doesn't mean that the in-person shopping experience has to be modified by either expensive technology or a lot of space. Even smaller brick and mortar owners can be mindful of this, but that means they need to really start thinking about the experience. Think about ways to take away the pain of shopping, which is the checkout process, and add to the most enjoyable aspect of shopping, which is socializing.
