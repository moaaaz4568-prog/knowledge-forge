Had somewhat of an epiphany today. I was listening to this book called Algorithms for Life, and it was talking about Amazon's algorithm they got a patent on for anticipatory shipping. All it is is a caching system. The way caching works in a library, for example, all of the returns go into the same area before they can get sorted and put back on shelves. That area is essentially the cached area. That's where the most recently used things go. And essentially it works the same way in Amazon's shipping algorithm. What they did was they built staging warehouses where all of the most recently ordered items would go. So it's not actually to predict what you're going to buy, but it simply stages where the most often bought things are based on geographical location. And the reason why this is significant is because a lot of speculation has gone into how rank is affected by geolocation. But essentially the conversation is always on, well, how do you affect geo rank? Do you need to make more sales in that area? And that's always been the discussion topic. How can you affect your geo rank? But it turns out if you look at what the algorithm is actually intended to do, it's unlikely that you're able to manipulate that on your own because it's based more on what people of that geographic location tend to buy in mass.
