How a car company got hundreds of thousands of views for basically the price of a print ad. Point of this story is to illustrate that effective marketing doesn't always have to cost an arm and a leg. This story is about the Mini Cooper's Porsche 911 challenge. So back in 2010, Mini in the United States issued a challenge to Porsche, challenging them to a race. The initial video got over 144,000 views, but they made subsequent videos that each got anywhere from 50 to 150,000. Not to mention the fact that the story got picked up by basically every auto blog, auto influencer, and essentially anybody in the automotive space. And all they really did was put a full-page ad out in the New York Times. But the Facebook post got over 150,000 likes, Twitter went crazy with it, social media did its thing. Now Mini is still a big company, so they had to follow through with something big, so they definitely did rent out an entire racetrack for a day and give 400 people free entrance into the event so that they could actually race their Mini versus a Porsche. But even before that happened, there was so much buzz, and the buzz itself really didn't cost that much money.
