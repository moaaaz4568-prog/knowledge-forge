Is marketing evil? A lot of people try to say that marketing does nothing more than convince people to buy stuff they don't really need, but it actually can and has done a lot of good. No better example exists than the designated driver. Did you know the DD didn't exist in America prior to the late 1980s? Thing is, at the time, college campuses were suffering from rampant incidences of drunk driving leading to injury and death. So the Harvard Center for Health Communication created the Harvard Alcohol Project. They worked with television networks and Hollywood to spread the message about designated drivers. There was a bunch of public service announcements, but the most effective form of the marketing came with their partnership in Hollywood. Basically, they got popular TV shows like Cheers, which was all about a bar, to seed the idea of designated driver. It only took about three years for the concept to take and pretty much everybody in America became familiar with it. And it was great because it attacked the problem on two fronts. One, it gave inebriated people a safe way to get home after partying, and two, it gave non-drinkers an out. See, at the time, not drinking at a party in college was not socially acceptable, but the DD program gave them a role, an important one at that, that ended up saving thousands upon thousands of lives. And this is a great example of how marketing can be used for good.
