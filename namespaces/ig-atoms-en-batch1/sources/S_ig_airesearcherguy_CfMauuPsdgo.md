Decentralized finance can be best explained by describing what it's not, and that's centralized. What we're used to in traditional finance is centralized. That's where you have your money in a bank. The bank grants you access to your money through their online system. Now it might be your money, but the banks are the custodians. That means if their online system is down, you aren't able to move your funds digitally. If you're trying to wire money and it's a Saturday, it'll stay in the queue until Monday when somebody's in the office. And that's just the stuff we deal with on a regular basis. But there are other implications. For example, if the government decides you did something wrong, they can have the bank freeze your funds. In contrast, DeFi is not centralized. You are the custodian of your funds. It's all run by smart contracts, which are computer programs. I'll explain those in another video. But there's nobody to report your earnings to a tax authority. There's nobody who can freeze your funds. And the reason why this is so amazing is because DeFi protocols offer many of the same financial instruments that traditional finance does. In DeFi protocols, you can borrow, you can lend, you can trade futures, you can trade options, but without the gatekeepers.
