This brain hack is really cool. So there's this behavioral scientist named BJ Fogg, and he came up with the Fogg Behavioral Model. And this basically describes how any habit is formed. All habit formation consists of three elements: motivation, ability, and prompt. Motivation is how badly you want the result of an action. Ability is how difficult or easy it is to do the thing that'll get you that result. And these work together in that the higher your motivation, the more difficulty you can overcome. The lower the difficulty, the less motivation is required. And then prompt is the trigger that reminds your brain, oh, I need to do this thing. So if you would like to adopt better habits, like maybe you want to start working out again or reading more, Fogg developed this thing called the Tiny Habits method. And this is where you would pick a prompt, say turning your coffee machine on in the morning, and then do two pushups or read one paragraph. That's it. It's a tiny habit, difficulty very low. But after that solidly becomes a habit, say after three weeks or whatever, then you can start stacking another paragraph or another two pushups. And then over time, eventually you'll find yourself working out or reading for an hour a day.
