Peter, how can I build my own AI startups and make millions? What does it actually take to build one from scratch? Alright, Stewie, it's not easy, but it's also not impossible. First, you need the right technology stack because everything depends on it. Start there. What tech do I actually need? At the core, you need four layers. First is the application layer, your front-end and back-end. Most AI startups today use React or Next. js on the front-end, and Node. js, Python, FastAPI, or Django on the back-end. Second is the AI layer. This is where most people get confused. You usually don't train your own large language model. That costs millions, requires massive datasets, GPU clusters, and months of tuning. Instead, use APIs from existing models like GPT-class models, Claude-style models, image models, video models, speech models, and build value on top. So most AI startups aren't actually inventing AI? Exactly. They're engineering products around AI. Third is the data layer. You need databases, PostgreSQL for structured data, Redis for caching, vector databases like Pinecone, Weaviate, or Qdrant for embeddings. This allows memory, search, personalization, and retrieval-augmented generation. Lastly, infrastructure and deployment. Cloud platforms like AWS, GCP, or Vercel, containers like Docker, CI/CD pipelines, monitoring, logging, and security. This is what makes your startup production-grade instead of a demo. That already sounds expensive. It's not that expensive at the beginning. You can launch an MVP for under a few hundred dollars. Cloud credits, pay-as-you-go, AI APIs, and managed services keep costs low. The real cost comes when usage scales. Why not just train your own AI model then? Wouldn't that make you unstoppable? That's the trap. Training your own LLM requires massive compute, specialized ML teams, constant retraining, safety evaluation, and infrastructure. It's slow, risky, and rarely necessary. Even billion-dollar startups mostly rely on existing models and focus on product differentiation. So that's how people actually build AI startups? That's the real blueprint. Not hype, not magic, just engineering, focus, and marketing the application after development.
