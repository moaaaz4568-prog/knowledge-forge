Hiring a closer is not gonna scale your agency. I've coached thousands of agencies and closers to this point, and the most difficult thing to understand is that you can't outsource the things that you are not good at yet. A lot of agency owners hate sales, and as soon as they start making a little bit of money, they're like, "I'm gonna get a setter, I'm gonna get a closer." They build out a whole team, and guess what happens? That team turns, they quit, they suck because there's not enough money to pay A plus talent. The agency owner never built the skills to actually coach and lead and build up the team, and when the team comes to them to ask them for some guidance and insight, they basically have no value to add. And now you have a founder that has no skills to coach on the thing, a teammate that's barely making money. It's a simple phenomenon. If you're a six out of 10 in something and you hire someone, you can probably only get them up to a three or a four. So you're only left with two decisions to scale the agency. You commit to a chapter, a season of growth and some suffering. Were you gonna go all in, become a master at sales as the best that you could, so eventually when you outsource it, you have competence and competence, you can pass it on and pay people and do all that fun stuff. Or you are fully, forever, indefinitely gonna outsource sales responsibility by getting a business partner, not a agency, because nobody's gonna be invested and give a shit about your business long term. In short, give up equity or commit to a season of growth with a bit of suffering. Either ways, you can't shortcut the path. You can't pass on what you're not great at yet.
