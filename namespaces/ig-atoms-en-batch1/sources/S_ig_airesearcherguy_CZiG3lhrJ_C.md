NFT stands for non-fungible token. What an NFT does is tokenizes a digital asset, and this can be any digital asset: an image, a song, an album, a movie, a document, a book. When it's tokenized by being minted into an NFT, that proves its rarity. It is the one and only instance of that particular item on the blockchain, and you can prove ownership. So an NFT is the digital version of basically a physical piece of art. There's only one Mona Lisa. There are lots of replications. There's even a couple of frauds, but you can test and prove which one the original is. So this of course has a lot of implications. Market efficiency, for example, this gives more rights and capabilities to the artists themselves. Obviously, this also has implications to protect from fraud. It gives digital assets more value because the rarity can be proven. And there's a bunch of other benefits being worked on by genius blockchain developers for how to utilize NFTs both in the digital and in the physical world. But that's essentially how they work.
