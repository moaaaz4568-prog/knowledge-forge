Peter, why is everyone suddenly panicking about RAM prices going up? My feed is full of "buy RAM now before it's too late" posts. Because this time it's not hype. RAM prices are expected to spike due to a massive shift in demand caused by AI infrastructure, and the supply side simply can't keep up. AI again? Why does AI even need that much RAM? Modern AI systems, especially large language models and video models, are extremely memory hungry. Training and running them requires enormous amounts of DRAM and high bandwidth memory. AI data centers don't buy RAM in thousands, they buy it in millions of modules. So who's buying all of it? Big AI players. Companies building massive AI clusters are locking in long-term contracts directly with memory manufacturers. That pulls a huge chunk of global RAM supply away from consumer markets like laptops, phones, gaming PCs, and servers for small businesses. So less RAM available, higher prices? Exactly. RAM is a commodity. When demand spikes suddenly and supply is fixed, prices rise fast. Memory fabs take years to expand, so manufacturers can't instantly respond. Is this the same kind of shortage we saw during crypto mining? Similar pattern, but worse. Crypto mainly affected GPUs. AI affects everything that uses memory, which is basically all modern electronics. And unlike crypto, AI infrastructure is long-term, not speculative. How bad could the price jump get? Analysts expect double-digit percentage increases, and in some segments, potentially much higher. Enterprise-grade and server RAM usually spikes first, then consumer RAM follows a few months later. How long is this supposed to last? Weeks? Months? Likely 12 to 24 months. Memory manufacturers are prioritizing high-margin AI contracts. Consumer supply won't normalize until new fabs come online, or AI demand stabilizes. Neither happens quickly. So this hits more than just PC builders? Absolutely. Higher RAM prices mean more expensive laptops, phones, cloud services, game consoles, and even subscription software, because cloud providers pass infrastructure costs down to users.
