Every viral video has a structure. Here are my favorite structures to use in my content.

Number one, the step-by-step structure. First, hook—state the dream result. Second, authority gap—state the results you've generated from this step-by-step process you're about to teach and why people should trust you. Third, introduce the name, framework, or acronym of the step-by-step process you're about to teach. Fourth, share the value—the actual step-by-step process—and fifth, add a CTA.

Number two, the comparison structure. First, you have your hook—you're going to introduce option A and B and pit them against one another. Second, you're going to introduce option A, share one to three facts about it, and make it seem like the better option. Third, you're going to introduce option B, state one to three facts about it, and make it seem like the worse option. Fourth, you're going to say insert time period later, whether it's days, months, or years, whatever that testing period is going to be. Fifth, you're going to share the results—share the results of option A, and then share the better results that option B got you. Sixth, break it down—explain why option B was better even though it seemed like it was going to be the worst result.

Number three, the 'my story' structure. First, introduction—introduce you and the problem. Second, inflection point—highlight the pain points from this problem. Third, rising action—explain the failed solutions/ attempts you tried in order to fix this problem. Then you have the climax—you're going to introduce the solution to this problem. Falling action—explain the success you have seen because of the solution. And finally, the resolution—the call to action to the solution which is going to be your business or your service.
