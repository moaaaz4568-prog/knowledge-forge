I spent 20 years in sales, and these are the three hard truths I had to accept to actually get good. First, you are the problem, not the leads, not the product, not the market. You. I spent years blaming everything around me when deals fell apart. Bad timing, wrong prospects, the product wasn't ready. And the moment I stopped looking outward and started looking at what I was doing on every single call, everything changed. Second, the pitch doesn't close the deal, the relationship does. I used to spend hours perfecting my deck and my talk track and my objection handling, and I kept losing to reps with worse products and worse pitches who just knew the buyer better than I did. People don't buy the best solution, they buy from the person they trust the most. And trust doesn't come from a perfect pitch, it comes from showing up before you need something. Third, and this one is hard, you have to be willing to walk away. I know you need the number, I know the quarter is ending, but the second a prospect feels how much you need this deal, you've lost all your leverage. Walk away from bad deals, walk away from bad fits. The ones worth having will respect you more for it. Most people avoid these truths. That's why most people stay average, and that's why you won't.
