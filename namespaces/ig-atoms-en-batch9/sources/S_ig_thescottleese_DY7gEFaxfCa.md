If you're selling to doctors, lead with evidence, not enthusiasm. They've been trained to question everything and trust nothing without proof. The hype will kill the sale before it starts. If you're selling to lawyers, expect to be cross-examined. Every word you say is being weighed for risk. You gotta be precise, be careful, and never promise what you can't defend. If you're selling to engineers, drop the marketing language. They want specs and logic and how it actually works under the hood. So you sell them the mechanism, not the magic. If you're selling to founders, skip the small talk and respect their time. They're solving five problems at once. If you can't tell them what you do in one sentence, you've lost them. If you're selling to creatives, don't pitch them, inspire them. They buy from people who get the vision, not from people who push the clothes. Make them feel seen and they'll sell themselves. If you're selling to accountants, every number better add up. They live in spreadsheets and they trust math more than people. One inconsistency and the deal is dead. If you're selling to teachers, lead with impact, not income. They didn't choose their profession for money and they don't make decisions based on it. Show them how it changes someone's life. If you're selling to salespeople, you're being watched the entire time. They know every trick because they've used them. Drop the playbook and just be human, or they'll close you before you close them. If you're selling to investors, you gotta bring pattern recognition, not passion. They've seen a thousand pitches that sounded just like yours. Show them why this one is different in numbers, not in words. If you're selling to consultants, frame everything as a framework. They think in models and they sell in models. If your pitch doesn't fit into one, they won't know what to do with it.
