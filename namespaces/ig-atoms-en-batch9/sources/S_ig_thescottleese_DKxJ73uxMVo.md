What most sales teams do is they just say, "I can't close any deals because we don't have this integration," and it just sounds like they're whining, right? So nothing gets done. I think it's really important for sales and product to have maybe like a bi-weekly kind of cadence where they're saying, "Hey, here is what I am running into. Here is why I am losing deals." Like product specific reasons why we're losing deals, but don't just say those things. Specifically tie it back to certain accounts. Give a dollar figure to those accounts in terms of what that deal size would be, and then do the overall calculation and say, "This is worth two million dollars in blocked revenue right now. How long would it take to build? How much is that going to cost? How many people need to be on the project?" If she can build that and it's going to cost a quarter million dollars and we have two million dollars of pipeline blocked by that thing, now I am going to say, "Okay, where does this request sit on your release queue? What other things do you have that are worth net one point seven five million?" Oh, nothing? Okay, let's raise that one up. Can we lower the thing that everybody thinks is neat and cute but actually customers don't care about? That's the type of dialogue that I would have with product all the time. And I've been doing this a long time and I can tell you very few sales leaders have that conversation with product. I think it's super important. And sales reps need to learn to communicate that way as well. Because if you just go bark to your sales leader, "I can't close any deals because we don't have this thing," you are not giving the sales leader enough information to do anything with it.
