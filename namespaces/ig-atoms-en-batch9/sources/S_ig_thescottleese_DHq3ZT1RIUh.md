No one wants to sit and listen to a hundred things that can be done. I was working with this company a few years ago. I said to them, "Write me a list of all the most important things that this product does." It was a list of a hundred things, and I said, "Great, good job. Now I want you to delete ninety-seven of them." And there was like a massive look of panic. "What are you talking about? That will never work." And I am trying to explain to them, "You've got to distill this down into three or four main things. It doesn't mean you never use the other ninety-six things. It just means don't lead with them because you are going to overwhelm somebody." They're like, "No way, I cannot cut any of these other things." We got to make some painful cuts because I can't pitch somebody on twenty different possibilities. But eventually when you get there, you are like, "Basically what we do is X, Y, Z." And then if you just focus on those things, the people you are talking to are like, "What about this? Can it do that?" And you are like, "Yeah, of course." And you just keep pulling these ace cards out of your back pocket. They could ask me ninety-seven more questions and the answer is yes to all those. And it gives them a feeling and a sensation of, "Cow, this guy does everything, right?" But if I led with all those things, I'd just scare somebody off and push them away. And that's the trick of it all.
