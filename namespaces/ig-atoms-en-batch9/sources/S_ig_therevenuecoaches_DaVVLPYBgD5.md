We have watched promising startups with great products and real funding run out of runway. Not because the product was bad, but because of one go-to-market mistake they all made. They build a go-to-market for the investor deck, not for the customer. Three ways that plays out. First, they define a massive, trollable, addressable market to impress investors and then have no idea which specific person to actually sell to first. Second, they pick channels that look good in a board update but have no evidence that the ideal customer profile is actually there. Third, they write messaging that sounds impressive in a pitch but does not speak to a single real pain point a real buyer actually has. A go-to-market built for investors does not close customers, and customers are the only things that keeps your business alive. The startups that scale fast are the ones that have one thing in common. They did not build this alone. They built it with experienced people who have done this before. People like Daniel and myself.
