Ten things I check before every sales call. Number one, the goal. What does this call need to achieve? A close, a next step, or just the truth about where this deal stands. Number two, the person. Who am I actually talking to? The decision maker, the user, or the person who reports to the real buyer. Number three, the pain. What problem brought them here? If I can't say in one sentence, then I'm not ready. Number four, their own words. What did they say last time? I use their exact words against them. I bring their exact words back to the call. Nothing builds trust faster. Number five, next steps. What am I asking for at the end? Every call ends with a decision, never with, "Sure, I'll send some info." Number six, objections. What are the two most likely reasons they'll say no? I try to handle them before they come up, not after. Number seven, proof. Which story or client do I mention if they get nervous? One good example beats ten product features every time. Number eight, the silence. Where in the call do I need to shut the hell up? After the price for sure, every time. Number nine, the walkaway line. If I don't know my no, I'll say yes to anything. Number ten, energy check. Am I calm? Buyers can feel a desperate rep through the call, and if I need this deal too much, then I've already lost it. A sales call is never just a conversation. It's prepared before it starts. A rep who checks these ten wins before they even call.
