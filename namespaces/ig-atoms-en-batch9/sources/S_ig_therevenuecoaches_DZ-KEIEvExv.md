The startups that survive is not the ones with the best ideas. It's not the ones with the best products. It's not even the ones with the best teams. It's the one that figured out how to close consistently before the runway ran out. We have watched genuinely impressive companies fail because they could not convert interest they were generating. The market liked them, investors were curious, the product worked, and then the money ran out before the close rate caught up. Ideas do not compound revenue. Compounds. The founders who make it, though, are the ones who treat closing as a skill they had to build, not a personality trait they either had or did not. They practice it, they measure it, and they got better at it on purpose.
