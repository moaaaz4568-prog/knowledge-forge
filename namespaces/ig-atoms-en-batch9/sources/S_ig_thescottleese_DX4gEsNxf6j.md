Recruiting, five out of five. This is the most underrated function in a startup. If you can't hire A players, regardless of department, you can't scale. Period. I see recruiting as the foundation of everything else. Bad hires destroy culture, kill deals, and burn cash. Great hires multiply your output. Sales, four out of five. The problem with sales is that it can't fix a broken product. Sales accelerates what's already working. It doesn't create something out of nothing. Customer success, three and a half out of five. If you're relying on CS to save bad sales or bad product, you're screwed. Great CS teams make good customers great, but they can't make bad customers good. Marketing, three out of five. I see it as top of funnel leverage, but most startups suck at it early on. They burn cash on ads before they even know their ICP. Good marketing is powerful, but bad marketing is just expensive noise. Finance, two and a half out of five, and I'm being generous. Look, you need somebody to make sure you're not running out of money, but I've seen too many finance-driven companies optimize themselves into irrelevance. They cut the wrong things, cut too many things, they slow things down, they kill off the right bets, they kill momentum in the name of efficiency. Finance keeps you alive, it just doesn't make you grow. What's the most valuable department? You need all the pieces in order for it to work.
