# G_ig_thesunnahguy_B29h06Mpnjr — the grave

## Term

The grave in this source is the first stage of the journey to the hereafter, and thesunnahguy uses it as a call to change now rather than later: he stands among gravestones in loving memory of fathers and mothers and says everyone will end up here one day. The phrase appears twice, beside Uthman ibn Affan, weep, beard, and the hereafter, and the speaker quotes Uthman's saying al-qabr awalul manazil al-akhirah. Uthman, he says, wept at the grave until his beard was soaked, yet many people bury their dead and take no heed from it at all. The warning is aimed at the listener who stays the same after burying the dead — upset for the loved one, but unchanged in his own life.

## Synonyms

- al-qabr (the Arabic form the speaker quotes for the grave)

## Live Example

> "Uthman ibn Affan, he used to stand at the grave and he used to weep, and he used to weep so much where his beard would get soaked." — sources/S_ig_thesunnahguy_B29h06Mpnjr.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a place of rest only: the source presents the grave as the first step toward meeting Allah, so treating it as an ending misses the journey the speaker describes.
- Read as grief for the dead being the point: the pain over a loved one's passing is real here, but the speaker says what is worse is burying the dead without changing oneself.
