# G_ig_thesunnahguy_C3sHFkINs6a — proud

## Term

Here "proud" is the standard the speaker holds up for Muslim women imagining the Prophet seeing them in the street. It occurs once, paired directly against "ashamed" in a single either-or question, with "sisters" and "streets" beside it. The point is not a description of pride but a test: would he be proud of you, or ashamed of you. He leaves the answer to the listener, using the imagined meeting to make her judge her own public conduct.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "My beloved sisters, I just want you to ask yourself one thing. Imagine the Prophet Muhammad Sallallahu Alaihi Wasallam saw you out on the streets today. Would he be proud of you? Or would he be ashamed of you?" — sources/S_ig_thesunnahguy_C3sHFkINs6a.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When it is read as the speaker claiming to know the Prophet's judgement: he only asks the listener to imagine it, and the verdict is left to her own conscience.
- When it is read as praise of the speaker's audience: the question is a challenge to self-examination, not a compliment, and it only works because the opposite answer, ashamed, stays on the table.
