# G_ig_thesunnahguy_C734kMQt8QN — fair

## Term

Here "fair" is the standard the speaker measures against the Prophet's life, and finds life unfair by design. It occurs 5 times, beside "unfair", "fatherless", "six of his children" and "sucker punch", and the losses are listed in order: born fatherless, mother lost at six, grandfather and uncle gone, a wife who was his confidant gone, then six children lowered into their graves. His anchor is his own six-year-old son Adam, held through a night after a month apart, and the thought of losing him. The conclusion is the refrain: if life was going to be fair to anybody, it would have been fair to him.

## When the Meaning Breaks

- When it is read as a complaint against God: the speaker uses the Prophet's losses to show that unfairness is the shape of life, not to accuse anyone of causing it.
- When the list of losses is read as biography alone: each loss is evidence for the refrain, and the point is the standard it sets, not the history itself.
