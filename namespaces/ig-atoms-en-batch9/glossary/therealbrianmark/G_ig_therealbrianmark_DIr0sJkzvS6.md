# G_ig_therealbrianmark_DIr0sJkzvS6 — elevate your position

## Term

To elevate your position is to move up to better-paid work instead of resenting someone else's success, and Brian Mark offers it as the only honest response to being triggered by an online coach's income. The phrase appears once, beside thirty five to forty an hour, construction worker and general manager, and his example is a construction worker taking school to become a general manager and one day own the company. His concrete charge is that anger and hate comments only expose the commenter's own insecurity, because the other person's success is not the problem.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "If you don't like the job that you are doing or you don't like the money that you are making, why don't you elevate your position?" — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_DIr0sJkzvS6.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a guarantee that schooling leads to ownership: becoming a general manager is offered as the path, and the reel never shows the years or the odds between the two points.
- Read as a dismissal of every complaint about pay: the target is anger at other people's success, not a claim that every low-paid job is the worker's fault.
