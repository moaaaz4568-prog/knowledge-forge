# G_no_finish_line — no finish line

## Term

No finish line is how the client describes the trap of making more money: once a coach showed him what was possible, he can never feel satisfied again. The phrase appears once, beside testimonials that can't be true, feeling super guilty when he makes less, and wanting to make more money and not being able to stop. The clip is framed as a call-out, a joke complaint that the program ruined him, because the new income level became the floor he cannot go back below. The finish line is the point where earning would feel like enough, and Brian's program moved it out of reach for good.

## Synonyms

- No variant form of the term appears in this source.

## Live Example

> "It's like there's no finish line. I can never be like satisfied anymore." — sources/S_ig_therealbrianmark_DJDIdVjN1Zr.md

## When the Meaning Breaks

- Read as a complaint about the program: the speaker is bragging through the call-out, crediting the coach for the higher income, not saying the program harmed him.
- Read as a claim that satisfaction is impossible for everyone: the line is one client's experience after a big income jump, not a rule about how all earners feel.
